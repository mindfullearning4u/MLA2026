Electromagnetic Radiation and Atomic Evidence

This page teaches the foundation. Read and study; do not answer questions while learning.

1. The wave relationship

c = λν, where c = 3.00 × 108 m/s. Wavelength and frequency are inverse: when wavelength decreases, frequency increases.

2. Photon energy

E = hν, where h = 6.626 × 10−34 J·s. Frequency and energy change together: higher frequency means higher energy.

3. Electromagnetic spectrum

radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-ray → gamma
Wavelength decreases →    Frequency and energy increase →

4. Atomic fingerprints

Electrons occupy quantized energy levels. A photon is emitted or absorbed only when its energy matches the difference between two levels. Therefore, each element has a distinctive line spectrum that can identify it.

5. Applications and safety

Spectroscopy helps identify elements in stars and samples. Different spectrum regions are also used in communications, imaging, and analysis. Higher-energy radiation requires stronger safety controls because it can damage matter and living tissue.
