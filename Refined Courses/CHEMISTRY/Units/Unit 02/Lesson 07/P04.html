P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: Compare red and violet visible light.

  1. Red has longer wavelength than violet.
  2. Longer wavelength means lower frequency.
  3. Lower frequency means lower energy.
  4. Violet has higher frequency and higher energy than red.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Why can spectra identify elements?

  1. Atoms have quantized energy levels.
  2. Electron transitions emit or absorb specific photon energies.
  3. Specific energies make specific spectral lines.
  4. Different elements have different line patterns.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Which has higher energy, radio waves or X-rays?

  1. X-rays have shorter wavelength.
  2. Shorter wavelength means higher frequency.
  3. Higher frequency means higher energy.
  4. Answer: X-rays.
Common Mistake: Thinking longer wavelength means higher energy because the wave looks bigger.
Incorrect example: Radio waves have the highest energy because they have the longest wavelength.
Correct example: Radio waves have low energy compared with X-rays because long wavelength corresponds to low frequency, and low frequency means lower energy.

Teachable Explanation

Each worked example shows the same learning pattern: identify the evidence, choose the correct atomic model or rule, apply it step by step, and interpret the result in context.
