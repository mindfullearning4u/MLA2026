P01 Lesson Overview

P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: Atomic Theory and Experimental Evidence
Lesson Purpose: Explain how evidence changed atomic models over time.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.CHEM.ATO.01
Support Standards: MLA.CHEM.SCI.03; ELA.K12.EE.1.1

What You Will Learn

  • You will explain how atomic models are revised when evidence no longer fits older explanations.
  • You will use the required model, data table, diagram, or spectrum stimulus from the Unit 2 mapping.
  • You will write Chemistry reasoning that connects evidence to atomic structure or atomic behavior.

What You Will Do

  1. Learn the vocabulary before using it in explanations.
  2. Read the model or data display step by step.
  3. Study three worked examples.
  4. Complete independent notebook work and a checkpoint.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by explaining the mapped Unit 2 atomic concept with accurate vocabulary, evidence from a model or data display, and step-by-step reasoning.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you understand atomic evidence and atomic patterns so later bonding and reaction lessons make sense.

Science Safety and Resource Note: Lab/Data/Safety Connection: Atomic model evidence timeline investigation
Safety: Use diagrams, historical data, and simulations only. No radiation or particle experiments are performed.

Required Direct Simulation Link

Open this exact simulation/resource: PhET Rutherford Scattering

Direct URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/rutherford-scattering/latest/rutherford-scattering_all.html

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. After the simulation opens, start with the atom model shown on the screen and turn on alpha-particle firing if it is not already moving.
  2. Observe several alpha particles and separate their paths into mostly straight, slightly deflected, or strongly deflected.
  3. Switch or compare available atom-model views if the simulation provides more than one model.
  4. Record which observation supports a small dense nucleus and one limitation of the model for the lesson focus: Atomic Theory and Experimental Evidence.

Evidence check: Observe the model, table, data, image, or source section carefully. Your notes must include the requested observations, data, or model evidence before you answer the lesson questions.
