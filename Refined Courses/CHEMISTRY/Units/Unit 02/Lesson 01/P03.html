How Evidence Changed the Atomic Model

This page teaches the lesson. You do not submit work from this page.

1. Dalton: atoms explain consistent composition

Dalton used patterns in chemical reactions to propose that matter is made of tiny atoms and that atoms combine in whole-number ratios. His solid-sphere model explained many reaction patterns but did not describe internal structure.

2. Thomson: atoms contain electrons

Cathode rays bent toward a positive plate and away from a negative plate. Thomson inferred that the rays contained negatively charged particles—electrons—present in all atoms. The atom could no longer be treated as indivisible.

3. Rutherford: positive charge is concentrated

In the gold-foil experiment, most alpha particles passed straight through, a small number deflected, and very few bounced back. If positive charge were spread throughout the atom, strong deflections would be extremely unlikely. Rutherford inferred that most of the atom is empty space and that positive charge and most mass are concentrated in a tiny nucleus.

Evidence → inference: rare large deflections → a very small region must contain concentrated positive charge and mass.

4. Bohr and energy levels

Atoms emit only specific colors of light. Because each color corresponds to a particular energy, line spectra support the idea that electrons occupy allowed energy levels. A continuous range of electron energies would produce a continuous spectrum instead.

5. Models improve; they are not final pictures

A newer model keeps successful parts of earlier models and explains evidence the older model could not. A model is not a photograph of an atom. It is a tool with strengths and limitations.
