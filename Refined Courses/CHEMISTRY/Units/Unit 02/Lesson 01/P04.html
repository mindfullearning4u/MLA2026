Reading Scattering Evidence Step by Step

Study this worked example. You do not submit work from this page.

Model situation

Suppose 1,000 alpha particles are directed at a thin metal foil. The observations are:

PathCountMeaning
Straight or nearly straight982Most of the atom is empty space.
Moderately deflected16Positive alpha particles sometimes pass near concentrated positive charge.
Strongly deflected/backscattered2A tiny, dense, positive nucleus can produce rare large deflections.

How the conclusion is derived

  1. Prediction: Thomson’s spread-out positive charge predicts no rare, very large deflections.
  2. Compare: The evidence contains two strong deflections, so that prediction fails for those observations.
  3. Revise: Concentrating positive charge and most mass in a tiny nucleus explains both the many straight paths and the rare strong deflections.
  4. Limit the claim: The data support a small dense nucleus; they do not show an exact photograph of electron locations.
Model answer: “The nuclear model is better supported because 982 of 1,000 particles pass through, showing mostly empty space, while two rare backscatters require a small region of concentrated positive charge. The model does not show exact electron positions.”
