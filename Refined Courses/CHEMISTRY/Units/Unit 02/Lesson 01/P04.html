Worked Example 1

Problem: Why did Rutherford's gold foil evidence challenge Thomson's model?

  1. Step 1: Thomson's model spread positive charge throughout the atom.
  2. If that were fully correct, alpha particles should pass through with only small changes.
  3. The evidence showed a few particles deflected sharply or bounced back.
  4. Large deflections require a small, dense, positive region.
  5. Conclusion: the atom must contain a tiny positive nucleus.
  6. Interpretation: Explain what the chemistry evidence shows and why it supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 2

Problem: What does cathode ray evidence support?

  1. Cathode rays were deflected by electric and magnetic fields.
  2. Deflection showed the rays had charge.
  3. The direction of deflection showed negative charge.
  4. Conclusion: atoms contain negatively charged electrons.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Why are line spectra evidence for energy levels?

  1. Atoms emit only certain colors of light.
  2. Each color corresponds to a specific energy difference.
  3. If electrons could have any energy, the spectrum would be continuous.
  4. Line spectra support quantized energy levels.
Common Mistake: Thinking that newer models prove older scientists were careless, instead of understanding that models improve when better evidence becomes available.
Incorrect: Rutherford's model replaced Thomson's model because Rutherford liked a different picture of the atom.
Correct: Rutherford's model replaced Thomson's model because scattering evidence showed that positive charge and most mass are concentrated in a tiny nucleus.

Teachable Explanation

Each worked example shows the same learning pattern: identify the evidence, choose the correct atomic model or rule, apply it step by step, and interpret the result in context.
