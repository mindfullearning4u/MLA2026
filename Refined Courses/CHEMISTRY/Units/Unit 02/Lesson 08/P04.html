P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: An atom has 11 protons, 12 neutrons, and 11 electrons. Identify the element and mass number.

  1. Protons determine element identity.
  2. 11 protons means sodium.
  3. Mass number = protons + neutrons = 11 + 12 = 23.
  4. Answer: sodium-23.

Worked Example 2

Problem: A periodic table excerpt shows sodium left of chlorine in Period 3. Which usually has higher ionization energy?

  1. Identify direction: across a period left to right.
  2. Ionization energy generally increases left to right.
  3. Chlorine is to the right of sodium.
  4. Answer: chlorine.

Worked Example 3

Problem: An electron drops from a higher level to a lower level. What evidence may appear?

  1. A drop means the electron loses energy.
  2. Lost energy is emitted as a photon.
  3. The photon can appear as a spectral line.
  4. Conclusion: emission spectrum evidence may appear.
Common Mistake: Using the wrong Unit 2 tool for the stimulus, such as treating an isotope table like a periodic trend graph.
Incorrect example: The atom is chlorine because the spectrum has lines and chlorine is on the periodic table.
Correct example: Element identity needs evidence such as proton number or a matching spectral pattern. A spectrum line pattern can support identification only when compared to known spectral data.

Teachable Explanation

Each worked example shows the same learning pattern: identify the evidence, choose the correct atomic model or rule, apply it step by step, and interpret the result in context.
