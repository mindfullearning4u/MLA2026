P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: Find neutrons in chlorine-37.

  1. Step 1: Chlorine has atomic number 17, so protons = 17.
  2. Mass number is 37.
  3. Neutrons = 37 - 17 = 20.
  4. Answer: 20 neutrons.
  5. Interpretation: Explain what the chemistry evidence shows and why it supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Why is average atomic mass closer to 35 than 37 for chlorine?

  1. Chlorine-35 abundance is 75%.
  2. Chlorine-37 abundance is 25%.
  3. The more common isotope pulls the weighted average closer to 35.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Estimate chlorine average mass from 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37.

  1. Convert percent to decimals: 0.75 and 0.25.
  2. Multiply: 35 x 0.75 = 26.25.
  3. Multiply: 37 x 0.25 = 9.25.
  4. Add: 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 amu.
Common Mistake: Averaging isotope masses without using abundance, or thinking different neutrons create a different element.
Incorrect: Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 are different elements because they have different mass numbers.
Correct: Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 are isotopes of the same element because both have 17 protons; they differ in neutrons.

Teachable Explanation

Each worked example shows the same learning pattern: identify the evidence, choose the correct atomic model or rule, apply it step by step, and interpret the result in context.
