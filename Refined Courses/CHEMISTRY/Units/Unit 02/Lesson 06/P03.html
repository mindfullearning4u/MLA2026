Energy is a staircase, not a ramp

An electron may occupy an allowed energy level, but not an energy value between levels. The staircase is only a model: electrons do not literally stand on steps.

Four vital statements - copy these into your notebook

  1. An electron must absorb exactly the required energy to move to a higher allowed level.
  2. An electron moving to a lower allowed level emits a photon.
  3. The photon energy equals the difference between the two energy levels.
  4. Separate spectral lines show that only specific photon energies are produced.

How a spectrum becomes evidence

If electrons could have every possible energy, atoms could emit a continuous range of colors. Instead, a hydrogen sample produces separate bright lines. Each line represents a specific transition and photon energy; this supports the quantized-energy model.
