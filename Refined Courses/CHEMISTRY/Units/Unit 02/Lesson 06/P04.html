Worked Example 1

Problem: An electron absorbs energy and moves from level 1 to level 3. What happened?

  1. Step 1: The electron started lower.
  2. It gained energy.
  3. It moved to a higher allowed level.
  4. This is absorption.
  5. Interpretation: Explain what the chemistry evidence shows and why it supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 2

Problem: An electron drops from level 4 to level 2. What happens to energy?

  1. The electron moves to a lower level.
  2. It loses energy.
  3. The atom emits a photon.
  4. The photon energy equals the level difference.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Why is a line spectrum evidence of quantized levels?

  1. The spectrum has separate lines.
  2. Separate lines mean specific photon energies.
  3. Specific photon energies mean specific electron transitions.
  4. Conclusion: electron energies are quantized.
Common Mistake: Thinking electrons can have any energy value between levels inside an atom.
Incorrect: Atoms emit every color because electrons can drop by any amount of energy.
Correct: Atoms emit specific spectral lines because electrons drop between allowed energy levels and release specific photon energies.

Teachable Explanation

Each worked example shows the same learning pattern: identify the evidence, choose the correct atomic model or rule, apply it step by step, and interpret the result in context.
