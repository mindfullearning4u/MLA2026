Practice, Model, and Guided Explanation

Worked example 1 - absorption

Problem: An electron moves from n = 1 to n = 3.

  1. The final level is higher.
  2. The electron must gain energy.
  3. Energy enters the atom.
  4. Conclusion: this is absorption and the atom is excited.

Worked example 2 - emission

Problem: An electron moves from n = 4 to n = 2.

  1. The final level is lower.
  2. The electron loses the exact energy difference.
  3. That energy leaves as a photon.
  4. Conclusion: this is emission.

Worked example 3 - reading spectrum evidence

Observation: Hydrogen produces separate lines at about 410, 434, 486, and 656 nm.

  1. The lines are separate, not continuous.
  2. Each wavelength represents one photon energy.
  3. Each photon came from a specific electron transition.
  4. Conclusion: allowed electron energies are quantized.

Model-check rule: always identify the starting level, ending level, energy direction, and evidence before naming absorption or emission.
