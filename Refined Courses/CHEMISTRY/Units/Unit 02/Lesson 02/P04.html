Three Worked Examples

Study all three examples. The reasoning is shown for you; do not submit work from this page.

Example 1 - Neutral atom

Given: carbon-12, atomic number 6, charge 0.

  1. Protons = atomic number = 6.
  2. Neutrons = 12 - 6 = 6.
  3. Neutral means electrons = protons = 6.

Answer: 6 p+, 6 n0, 6 e-.

Example 2 - Positive ion

Given: magnesium-24, atomic number 12, charge +2.

  1. Protons = 12.
  2. Neutrons = 24 - 12 = 12.
  3. A +2 charge means two fewer electrons than protons: 12 - 2 = 10.

Answer: 12 p+, 12 n0, 10 e-.

Example 3 - Negative ion

Given: fluorine-19, atomic number 9, charge -1.

  1. Protons = 9.
  2. Neutrons = 19 - 9 = 10.
  3. A -1 charge means one more electron than protons: 9 + 1 = 10.

Answer: 9 p+, 10 n0, 10 e-.

Problem-solving check: Always verify that p+ + n0 = mass number and p+ - e- = charge.
