P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: Which is usually larger, lithium or fluorine?

  1. Both are in Period 2.
  2. Atomic radius generally decreases left to right.
  3. Lithium is farther left than fluorine.
  4. Answer: lithium is usually larger.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Why does atomic radius increase down a group?

  1. Each lower period adds an occupied energy level.
  2. Outer electrons are farther from the nucleus.
  3. Inner electrons shield outer electrons.
  4. Atoms are usually larger down a group.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Which usually has higher ionization energy, sodium or chlorine?

  1. Both are in Period 3.
  2. Ionization energy generally increases left to right.
  3. Chlorine is to the right of sodium.
  4. Answer: chlorine usually has higher ionization energy.
Common Mistake: Memorizing arrows without explaining the atomic-structure reason behind the trend.
Incorrect example: Fluorine is smaller than lithium because the chart says so, and no other reason is needed.
Correct example: Fluorine is smaller than lithium across the same period because increased nuclear charge pulls electrons closer in the same main energy level.

Teachable Explanation

Each worked example shows the same learning pattern: identify the evidence, choose the correct atomic model or rule, apply it step by step, and interpret the result in context.
