Three Worked Examples

Example 1 - Rank atomic radius

Question: Rank Na, Mg, and Cl from largest to smallest.

  1. All three are in Period 3.
  2. Radius generally decreases from left to right.
  3. Na is farthest left, then Mg, then Cl.
  4. Answer: Na > Mg > Cl.

Example 2 - Compare elements in one group

Question: Which is larger, Li or K?

  1. Both are in Group 1.
  2. K is below Li and has more occupied energy levels.
  3. Added distance and shielding outweigh the increased nuclear charge.
  4. Answer: K has the larger atomic radius and lower ionization energy.

Example 3 - Explain an exception

Data: N = 1402 kJ/mol; O = 1314 kJ/mol.

  1. The overall Period 2 ionization-energy trend increases.
  2. O is lower than N, so this pair is an exception.
  3. N has a relatively stable half-filled p sublevel; pairing in O increases repulsion.
  4. Conclusion: Use the actual data when the numerical pattern contains an exception.
