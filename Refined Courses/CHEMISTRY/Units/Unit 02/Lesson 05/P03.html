Why atomic radius changes

Across a period, proton count rises while electrons enter the same general energy level. Stronger nuclear attraction pulls the electron cloud closer, so radius usually decreases. Down a group, new occupied levels and more shielding place valence electrons farther from the nucleus, so radius increases.

Why ionization energy and electronegativity change

Smaller atoms generally hold valence electrons more tightly. This raises ionization energy and strengthens attraction for shared electrons. Larger atoms with more shielding generally hold outer electrons less tightly.

Important: Trends describe overall patterns, not a perfect numerical increase or decrease at every step. Sublevel stability creates exceptions such as Be-to-B and N-to-O.
