Use the current PhET screen names: choose Single. Do not look for “Make Molecules” or “Larger Molecules”; those are not choices in the current version. Do not select Multiple or Playground for this activity.

Before building

  1. Download the PDF. Enter your name, date, and “U3 L3.” Keep it open while using PhET.
  2. Open PhET. On the title screen, click the large picture labeled Single.
  3. Wait for an atom kit to appear at the bottom. Use this color key: white = hydrogen (H), red = oxygen (O), black or dark gray = carbon (C), and blue = nitrogen (N).
  4. The available atoms change from kit to kit. If the atoms required below are not in the current kit, use the left or right kit/collection arrow near the bottom of the simulation until the needed colors appear.
  5. To connect atoms, drag one atom from its tray into the open workspace. Drag the next atom close enough for the atoms to snap together. A molecule is connected only when the atoms move together as one group.

Build 1 — water, H2O

  1. Find a kit containing red oxygen and white hydrogen atoms.
  2. Drag 1 red O into the workspace.
  3. Drag 1 white H to the O until it snaps into place.
  4. Drag a second white H to the same O. The connected model must contain 2 H and 1 O.
  5. Record H2O, the atom counts, and a drawing of the connected model in the Water row of the PDF.

Build 2 — carbon dioxide, CO2

  1. Use the kit arrows until a kit contains black/dark-gray carbon and red oxygen atoms.
  2. Drag 1 C into the workspace.
  3. Attach 1 O to one side of C and a second O to the other side. The connected model must contain 1 C and 2 O.
  4. Record CO2, the atom counts, and the connected O-C-O model in the Carbon dioxide row.

Build 3 — ammonia, NH3

  1. Use the kit arrows until a kit contains blue nitrogen and white hydrogen atoms.
  2. Drag 1 blue N into the workspace.
  3. Attach 3 white H atoms to that N, one at a time. Confirm the connected model contains 1 N and 3 H.
  4. Record NH3, the atom counts, and a drawing of the connected model in the Ammonia row.
If an atom connects incorrectly: drag the unwanted atom away from the molecule to separate it. If the screen becomes confusing, click the orange circular Reset All button in the lower-right corner, confirm if asked, select Single again, and return to the needed kit.

Complete and save the PDF

  1. For every molecule, confirm that each subscript equals the number of that atom in the model.
  2. Answer all three Part B questions using shared electron pairs, covalent bond, molecule, and molecular formula.
  3. Save the same PDF. On the next page, compare and correct this work.
