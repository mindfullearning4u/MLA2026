Three Complete Property Explanations

Study how each answer moves from evidence to particles to bonding. Do not submit work from this page.

Example 1 — Why copper wire conducts

Observation: Current flows through copper wire.

e- → e- → e- →
Cu+   Cu+   Cu+
  1. Copper contains delocalized valence electrons.
  2. Voltage gives those electrons a net direction of motion.
  3. Moving electrons carry charge through the stationary lattice.

Conclusion: Copper conducts because its electrons are mobile.

Example 2 — Why aluminum foil bends

Observation: Aluminum rolls into thin sheets.

Al+   Al+   Al+
force → layer shifts →
  Al+   Al+   Al+
  1. Force shifts a layer of positive metal cores.
  2. The delocalized electron cloud redistributes.
  3. The cores remain attracted to the negative electron cloud.
  4. The structure changes shape without separating.

Conclusion: Aluminum is malleable because metallic attraction continues after layers shift.

Example 3 — Why an ionic crystal cracks but a metal bends

Before ionic layers shift After shifting
+   -   +   -
-   +   -   +		 +   -   +   -
+   -   +   -
  1. Opposite charges attract before the shift.
  2. The shift places like charges beside one another.
  3. Like charges repel, so the ionic crystal splits.
  4. In a metal, mobile electrons remain between positive cores, so attraction continues.

Conclusion: Ionic solids are often brittle; metals are often malleable and ductile.

Four-part reasoning pattern

  1. Name the observed property.
  2. Name the relevant particles.
  3. State what moves and what attraction remains or fails.
  4. Connect that particle behavior to the property.
