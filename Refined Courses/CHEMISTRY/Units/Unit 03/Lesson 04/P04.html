Example 1 — sodium metal compared with sodium chloride

Sodium metal: Na⁺ centers are surrounded by mobile shared electrons. Sodium chloride: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions alternate in fixed positions; it does not have a mobile electron sea.

Example 2 — why copper can become wire

  1. A pulling force shifts rows of copper cations.
  2. The mobile electrons rearrange around the shifted rows.
  3. The attraction between cations and electrons remains.
  4. The copper changes shape without the metallic bond breaking apart.

Example 4 — why metals work for cookware

Mobile electrons transfer thermal energy quickly, and strong metallic attractions help the solid remain intact at cooking temperatures. Therefore copper, aluminum, and iron are useful cookware materials.
