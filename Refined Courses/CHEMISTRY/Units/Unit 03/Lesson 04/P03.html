Lesson summary

  1. Metallic bonds: Positive metal cations are attracted to a shared “sea” of mobile, delocalized valence electrons.
  2. Extended structure: A metal is a repeating structure, not a collection of separate molecules.
  3. Conductivity: Mobile electrons can carry electric charge and transfer thermal energy through the metal.
  4. Malleability and ductility: Layers of cations can slide while the electron sea moves with them and keeps the structure bonded.
  5. Melting and boiling points: Stronger attractions require more energy to overcome. Magnesium contributes more delocalized electrons and forms Mg²⁺, so its attractions are stronger than sodium’s.
