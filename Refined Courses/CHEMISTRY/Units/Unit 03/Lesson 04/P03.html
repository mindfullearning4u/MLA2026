How Metallic Bonding Explains Metal Properties

This is the teaching page. Study the written explanations and particle models; no independent work is submitted here.

1. From metal atoms to a metallic bond

  1. A metal atom holds its outer, or valence, electrons relatively loosely.
  2. In a solid metal, many atoms pack closely together.
  3. The valence electrons become delocalized: they are shared by the entire metal instead of belonging to one atom.
  4. After contributing valence electrons, the remaining positively charged metal cores form an orderly lattice.
  5. Attraction between the positive cores and the mobile negative electron cloud is the metallic bond.
Particle model of a metal
M+ M+ M+ M+
e- ⇄ e- ⇄ e- ⇄ e- ⇄ e-
M+ M+ M+ M+

Red M+ = positive metal cores fixed in an orderly lattice. Blue e- = delocalized electrons moving throughout the metal.

2. Electrical and thermal conductivity

When a voltage is applied, delocalized electrons move through the lattice and carry charge. The same mobile electrons transfer kinetic energy rapidly, so many metals also conduct heat.

Electrical model: M+   M+   M+
e- → e- → e- →
Cause: mobile electrons  →  evidence: current flows.

3. Malleability and ductility

Malleability means a material can be hammered or rolled into sheets. Ductility means it can be drawn into wire. Metallic attraction is nondirectional: when force shifts one layer of positive cores, the mobile electron cloud redistributes and continues attracting the shifted cores.

M+   M+   M+
force → layer shifts →
M+   M+   M+
mobile electron cloud follows and attraction remains

4. Why ionic crystals are brittle but metals bend

When layers shift Metallic solid Ionic solid
Particles that become neighbors Positive cores remain surrounded by mobile electrons. Like charges can line up: + beside + and - beside -.
Result Attraction continues; the metal bends. Like charges repel; the crystal cracks.

5. Luster and alloys

Mobile electrons interact with incoming light and re-emit much of it, producing metallic luster. An alloy contains a metal plus one or more other elements. Different-sized atoms disrupt the regular layers, often making an alloy harder and less easy to bend than a pure metal while metallic bonding still permits conductivity.

Reasoning rule: observed property → particle that can move → attraction that remains or breaks → property conclusion.
