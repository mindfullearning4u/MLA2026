Lesson summary

Solution: a homogeneous mixture. The solute is dissolved in the solvent.

Concentration: compares the amount of solute with solution volume. This lesson’s PDF uses mass concentration: C = solute mass ÷ solution volume, reported in g/mL.

Molarity: Savvas also expresses concentration as moles of solute per liter of solution: M = mol/L.

Dilution: adding solvent increases volume while solute amount remains constant, so concentration decreases. Evaporating solvent decreases volume, so concentration increases.

Write this in your notebook: “Concentration is a ratio of solute amount to solution volume. I must identify the given units, substitute into the correct ratio, divide, and report units. Adding water lowers concentration when solute stays constant; evaporating water raises it.”
