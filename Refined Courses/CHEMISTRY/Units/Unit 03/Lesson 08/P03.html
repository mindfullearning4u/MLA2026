How Solutions Form and Change

This page teaches the foundation. Read and study; only copy the blue “Notebook notes” box.

1. Why water dissolves many substances

A water molecule is polar: oxygen has a partial negative charge and the hydrogen ends have partial positive charges. Water molecules attract ions and other polar particles. When those attractions are strong enough, water separates and surrounds solute particles so they spread through the solution.

2. Solute, solvent, and solution

In salt water, salt is the solute, water is the solvent, and the uniform mixture is the solution. Dissolving does not destroy particles; it separates and disperses them.

3. Concentration

Concentration = amount of solute ÷ volume of solution
If mass is measured in grams and volume in milliliters, the unit is g/mL.

4. Predicting changes

ActionConcentration changeReason
Add soluteIncreasesMore solute in about the same volume
Add waterDecreasesSame solute spread through more volume
