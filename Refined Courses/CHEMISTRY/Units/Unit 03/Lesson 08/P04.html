Three Worked Examples

Study how each answer is obtained. These are models; you are not being graded on this page.

Example 1 — Calculate concentration

12.0 g solute in 200 mL solution

12.0 g ÷ 200 mL = 0.060 g/mL

Reasoning: substitute the values, divide, then attach g/mL.

Example 2 — Compare two solutions

A: 12 g ÷ 200 mL = 0.060 g/mL
B: 8 g ÷ 100 mL = 0.080 g/mL

B is more concentrated because 0.080 g/mL is greater than 0.060 g/mL. The larger total mass does not automatically mean the larger concentration.

Example 3 — Predict a change

If water is added while solute amount stays constant, volume increases. The numerator stays the same and the denominator increases, so concentration decreases. If water evaporates, volume decreases, so concentration increases.

Notebook evidence: Copy each equation and one sentence explaining why the answer makes sense.

Visual Derivation: Concentration and Particle Models

Example 1 — Calculate concentration

C = solute ÷ solution volume
12.0 g ÷ 200 mL = 0.060 g/mL

  1. Write the formula.
  2. Substitute 12.0 g for solute and 200 mL for volume.
  3. Divide: 12.0 ÷ 200 = 0.060.
  4. Carry both units into the quotient: g/mL.

Example 2 — Compare equal-sized particle models

A: 12 ÷ 200 = 0.060 g/mL. B: 8 ÷ 100 = 0.080 g/mL. Since 0.080 > 0.060, B is more concentrated even though B contains less total solute.

Example 3 — Add water or evaporate water

Change Solute amount Volume Concentration
Add water same increases ↑ decreases ↓
Evaporate water same decreases ↓ increases ↑

The numerator stays constant. Increasing the denominator makes the quotient smaller; decreasing the denominator makes the quotient larger.

Reasoning sequence: identify solute amount and volume → calculate or compare the ratio → use a particle model to check whether the result makes sense.
