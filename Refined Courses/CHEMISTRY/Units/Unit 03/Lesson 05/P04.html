Four Worked Examples

Study each example. The reasoning is modeled for you; do not submit work from this page.

Example 1 — CH₄

H
|
H—C—H    ⋯    H—C—H
|
H
  1. CH₄ has no H bonded to N, O, or F.
  2. Its symmetric shape makes it nonpolar.
  3. Therefore its strongest intermolecular force is London dispersion.

Example 2 — HCl

Hδ+—Clδ−   ⋯   Hδ+—Clδ−
  1. H is not bonded to N, O, or F, so this is not hydrogen bonding.
  2. The H—Cl bond creates an uneven charge distribution; HCl is polar.
  3. The δ⁺ end of one molecule attracts the δ⁻ end of another.
  4. Its strongest force is dipole-dipole.

Example 3 — H₂O

Hδ+—Oδ−—Hδ+   ·····   Oδ−—Hδ+
dotted line = hydrogen bond between molecules; solid O—H line = covalent bond inside a molecule
  1. H is directly bonded to O.
  2. The δ⁺ H of one molecule is attracted to the δ⁻ O of another.
  3. The strongest intermolecular force is hydrogen bonding.
  4. Boiling separates molecules by overcoming the dotted attractions; it does not break the solid O—H bonds.
