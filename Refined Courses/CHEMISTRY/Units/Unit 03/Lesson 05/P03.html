Lesson summary

  1. Inside versus between: Covalent bonds hold atoms together inside a molecule; intermolecular forces attract one molecule to another.
  2. Dispersion: Every particle has London dispersion forces. Larger, more polarizable electron clouds usually produce stronger dispersion forces.
  3. Dipole-dipole: Polar molecules attract because opposite partial charges line up between neighboring molecules.
  4. Hydrogen bonding: Molecules with H directly bonded to N, O, or F can form especially strong intermolecular attractions.
  5. Properties: Stronger intermolecular forces require more energy to separate molecules, usually producing a higher boiling point and slower evaporation.

How to identify the strongest force

  1. Is H directly bonded to N, O, or F? Yes → hydrogen bonding.
  2. If not, is the molecule polar? Yes → dipole-dipole.
  3. If not, the strongest force is London dispersion.
  4. Remember: dispersion is present in all molecules, even when another force is stronger.
