How to Reason Through This Lesson

  1. First identify the covalent bonds inside each molecule.
  2. Then look for attractions between different molecules.
  3. Recognize that stronger attractions between molecules require more energy to separate particles.
  4. Connect stronger intermolecular forces to higher boiling points and lower evaporation rates.
  5. Do not confuse breaking intermolecular forces during boiling with breaking covalent bonds inside molecules.
Checkpoint: Cover the conclusion column. Use the case, key information, and reasoning columns to predict the conclusion. Then uncover the conclusion and compare.

Why This Matters

Chemical bonding is not memorizing labels. The goal is to connect particle structure, charge, formulas, and properties so a chemical explanation can be defended with evidence.
