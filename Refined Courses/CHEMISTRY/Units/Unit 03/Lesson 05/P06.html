Possible results

  • Trial 1: Equal electronegativities produce no bond dipole and no meaningful partial charges.
  • Trial 2: A moderate difference produces a dipole arrow toward the more electronegative atom and visible partial charges.
  • Trial 3: The largest difference produces the largest bond dipole and strongest partial-charge separation.
  • Trial 4: In a symmetric three-atom arrangement, equal bond dipoles point in opposing directions and can cancel, so the molecular dipole is zero.
  • Trial 5: In an asymmetric arrangement, bond dipoles do not fully cancel, so a molecular dipole remains.

Analysis answers

  1. A greater electronegativity difference produces a larger bond dipole.
  2. Equal bond dipoles cancel when their arrows have equal magnitude and opposite directions in a symmetric geometry.
  3. A polar molecule has persistent partial charges, allowing the δ⁺ end of one molecule to attract the δ⁻ end of another through dipole-dipole forces.
