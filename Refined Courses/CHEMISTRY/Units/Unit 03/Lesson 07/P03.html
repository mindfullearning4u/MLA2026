Lesson summary

Classify first: a metal with a nonmetal or polyatomic ion is generally ionic; two nonmetals form a molecular compound.

Ionic names: name the positive ion first, then the negative ion. A one-element anion ends in -ide. Do not use prefixes. Use a Roman numeral when the metal can have more than one charge.

Molecular names: use Greek prefixes to show atom counts. The second element ends in -ide; mono- is usually omitted from the first element.

Formulas: ionic formulas balance charge to zero; molecular formulas translate prefixes directly into subscripts.

Write this in your notebook: “I classify a compound before naming it. Ionic compounds use ion names, charge balance, and Roman numerals when needed—but no prefixes. Molecular compounds use prefixes to communicate atom counts. A precise name and formula must identify the same substance.”
