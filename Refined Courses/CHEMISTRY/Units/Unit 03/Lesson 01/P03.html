Why Atoms Bond

This page teaches the lesson. Do not complete an assignment here.

1. A bond is an energy-and-force relationship

Atoms contain positive nuclei and negative electrons. When atoms approach, each nucleus attracts the other atom’s electrons. At the same time, the two nuclei repel one another and the electrons repel one another. A bond can form at a distance where attraction produces a lower-energy arrangement without the particles collapsing together.

2. Valence electrons control most bonding

Valence electrons are farthest from the nucleus and interact with other atoms. Atoms may transfer them, share them, or allow them to move among many atoms. Those patterns lead to ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding.

3. The octet rule is a pattern, not the cause

Many main-group atoms become stable with eight valence electrons, but atoms do not bond merely because they “want eight.” The cause is electrostatic interaction and a lower-energy arrangement. The octet rule helps predict many outcomes; it does not replace the force-and-energy explanation.

4. Bonding previews

  • Ionic: electron transfer creates oppositely charged ions that attract.
  • Covalent: nuclei attract shared electrons.
  • Metallic: positive metal ions are attracted to mobile valence electrons.
