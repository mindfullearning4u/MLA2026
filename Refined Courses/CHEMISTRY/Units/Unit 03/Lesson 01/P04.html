Three Guided Bonding Examples

Study each example. The reasoning is shown for you; do not submit work from this page.

Example 1 — Hydrogen molecule (H₂)

  1. Each H atom has one proton and one valence electron.
  2. As the atoms approach, each proton attracts both electrons.
  3. The electrons are shared between the nuclei.
  4. At the stable distance, attraction lowers potential energy while repulsion prevents collapse.

Conclusion: H₂ forms a covalent bond because both nuclei are attracted to the shared electrons.

Example 2 — Sodium chloride (NaCl)

  1. Na has one valence electron; Cl has seven.
  2. Na transfers one electron to Cl, producing Na⁺ and Cl⁻.
  3. Opposite charges attract.
  4. The ion pair forms a lower-energy arrangement.

Conclusion: the ionic bond is the electrostatic attraction between Na⁺ and Cl⁻.

Example 3 — Magnesium oxide (MgO)

  1. Mg has two valence electrons; O has six.
  2. Mg transfers two electrons to O, producing Mg²⁺ and O²⁻.
  3. The 2+ and 2− ions strongly attract.
  4. A repeating lattice lets each ion attract several oppositely charged neighbors.

Conclusion: identify the electron transfer, then explain the resulting attraction. Do not stop at “both have eight electrons.”

Problem-solving pattern: (1) identify valence electrons, (2) track electron movement or sharing, (3) identify resulting charges, (4) name the attraction, and (5) connect it to lower energy and stability.
