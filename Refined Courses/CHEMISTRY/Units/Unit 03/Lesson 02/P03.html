How Ionic Compounds Form

This is the teaching page. Read and study; do not complete student work here.

1. Electrons create ions

An atom is neutral when protons equal electrons. Losing electrons leaves more protons than electrons and forms a positive cation. Gaining electrons creates more electrons than protons and forms a negative anion.

2. Opposite charges attract

Electron transfer produces ions. Electrostatic attraction holds oppositely charged ions together; this attraction is the ionic bond. Like charges repel, so a stable arrangement places positive and negative ions next to one another.

3. Ionic formulas must be neutral

The subscripts show the least whole-number ratio that makes total charge zero. For Mg2+ and Cl-, one Mg2+ requires two Cl-: (+2) + 2(-1) = 0, so the formula is MgCl2.

4. How to draw a repeating ionic lattice model

A lattice is a repeating arrangement, not one isolated molecule. The checkpoint uses CaF2. One formula unit contains one Ca2+ for every two F- ions. Two repeated formula units therefore require two Ca2+ and four F- ions.

Visual model — two repeating CaF2 units
F-—Ca2+—F-
|
F-—Ca2+—F-

Color key: red circles are Ca2+ cations; blue circles are F- anions. The lines show nearest-neighbor attraction, not covalent bonds.

Draw it step by step:
  1. Write and circle one Ca2+ near the center.
  2. Place one circled F- on each side so opposite charges are neighbors: F- — Ca2+ — F-.
  3. Repeat the pattern below it with a second Ca2+ and two more F- ions.
  4. Label every ion with its symbol and charge. Do not draw neutral Ca or F atoms.
  5. Count the model: 2 Ca2+ gives +4 and 4 F- gives -4. The total is zero.
  6. Show repetition by continuing the alternating arrangement or adding small arrows at the edges.

Checkpoint connection: when asked to draw the lattice, copy this construction method, but produce your own neat labeled model on the checkpoint answer sheet.
