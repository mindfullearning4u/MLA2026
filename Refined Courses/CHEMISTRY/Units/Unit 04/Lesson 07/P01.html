P01 Lesson Overview

P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: Quantitative Reaction Data
Lesson Purpose: Reaction data reasonableness and limiting information analysis using data tables and calculation organizers. No reaction performance is required.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.CHEM.REA.02; MLA.CHEM.LAB.03
Support Standards: MA.K12.MTR.6.1

What You Will Learn

You will learn the chemistry concept in this lesson by reading evidence, using models or data, and explaining the reasoning that connects the evidence to the mapped standard.

What You Will Do

  1. Read the vocabulary and teaching sequence carefully.
  2. Use the included tables, models, diagrams, scenarios, simulations, or data displays to make sense of the chemistry idea.
  3. Complete notebook evidence that shows your reasoning, not just your answer.
  4. Use the checkpoint to show independent mastery.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by explaining the lesson concept with accurate chemistry vocabulary, using evidence from the required stimulus, and writing reasoning that connects the evidence to the standard.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you work like a chemistry student: you will read evidence, use models or data, and explain how the evidence supports a scientific answer.

Lesson Content and Resource Directions

Standard(s): MLA.CHEM.REA.02; MLA.CHEM.LAB.03

Florida alignment: MA.K12.MTR.6.1

Learning target: I can analyze reaction data for reasonableness, evidence, and calculation accuracy.

Start Here

This lesson teaches the process step by step. Read the vocabulary, model, and examples in order before answering practice questions.

Vocabulary Before Use

TermMeaning
quantitative datanumerical evidence from measurement or calculation
percent yieldactual yield divided by theoretical yield, multiplied by 100 percent
reasonablenesswhether an answer makes sense based on units, size, and chemistry context
source of errora factor that can make measured data differ from the expected value
Science Safety and Resource Note: Reaction data reasonableness and limiting information analysis using data tables and calculation organizers. No reaction performance is required. Physical reactions, heating, mixing, chemicals, pressure, or nuclear/radiation materials are not part of this independent lesson and require separate Teacher of Record assignment and safety controls.

  1. After the simulation opens, use the reaction or molecules screen rather than the sandwich analogy if that screen is available.

  3. Observe the products formed and any leftover reactants after the model reaction.
