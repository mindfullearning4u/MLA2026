P01 Lesson Overview

P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: Evidence of Chemical Reactions
Lesson Purpose: Reaction evidence scenario/video/data analysis using observation tables. No unsupervised reactions are required.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.CHEM.REA.01; MLA.CHEM.MAT.02
Support Standards: ELA.K12.EE.3.1

What You Will Learn

You will learn the chemistry concept in this lesson by reading evidence, using models or data, and explaining the reasoning that connects the evidence to the mapped standard.

What You Will Do

  1. Read the vocabulary and teaching sequence carefully.
  2. Use the included tables, models, diagrams, scenarios, simulations, or data displays to make sense of the chemistry idea.
  3. Complete notebook evidence that shows your reasoning, not just your answer.
  4. Use the checkpoint to show independent mastery.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by explaining the lesson concept with accurate chemistry vocabulary, using evidence from the required stimulus, and writing reasoning that connects the evidence to the standard.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you work like a chemistry student: you will read evidence, use models or data, and explain how the evidence supports a scientific answer.

Lesson Content and Resource Directions

Standard(s): MLA.CHEM.REA.01; MLA.CHEM.MAT.02

Florida alignment: ELA.K12.EE.3.1

Learning target: I can identify and characterize evidence that a chemical reaction has occurred.

Start Here

Physical reactions, heating, mixing, chemicals, pressure, or nuclear/radiation materials are not part of this independent lesson and require separate Teacher of Record assignment and safety controls.

Required Direct Simulation Link

Open this exact simulation/resource: PhET Reactants, Products and Leftovers

Direct URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/reactants-products-and-leftovers/latest/reactants-products-and-leftovers_all.html

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. After the simulation opens, use the reaction or molecules screen rather than the sandwich analogy if that screen is available.
  2. Set starting reactant amounts, then change only one reactant amount at a time.
  3. Observe the products formed and any leftover reactants after the model reaction.
  4. Record reactant amounts, product amounts, leftovers, and the ratio or conservation evidence used for Evidence of Chemical Reactions.

Evidence check: Observe the model, table, data, image, or source section carefully. Your notes must include the requested observations, data, or model evidence before you answer the lesson questions.
