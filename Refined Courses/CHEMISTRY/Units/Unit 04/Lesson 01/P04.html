Worked example 1 — moles to molecules

Question: How many H2O molecules are in 2.00 mol?

  1. Start with 2.00 mol H2O.
  2. Choose 6.022 × 1023 molecules / 1 mol so mol cancels.
  3. 2.00 × 6.022 × 1023 = 1.20 × 1024 molecules.

Worked example 2 — grams to moles

Question: How many moles are in 36.0 g H2O?

  1. Use the molar mass 18.02 g/mol.
  2. 36.0 g × (1 mol / 18.02 g); grams cancel.
  3. The result is 2.00 mol H2O.

Worked example 3 — particles to mass

Question: Find the mass of 3.011 × 1023 CO2 molecules.

  1. Particles → moles: divide by 6.022 × 1023 = 0.5000 mol.
  2. Molar mass CO2 = 12.01 + 2(16.00) = 44.01 g/mol.
  3. 0.5000 mol × 44.01 g/mol = 22.0 g CO2.
Reasonableness check: half a mole contains half Avogadro’s number and has half the molar mass in grams.
