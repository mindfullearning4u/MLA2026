Unit 4 Lesson 08: Putting It All Together

Standard(s): MLA.CHEM.REA.01; MLA.CHEM.REA.02; MLA.CHEM.REA.03; MLA.CHEM.LAB.02; MLA.CHEM.LAB.03

Florida alignment: ELA.K12.EE.1.1; MA.K12.MTR.6.1

Learning target: I can synthesize reaction evidence, reaction types, conservation, mole reasoning, stoichiometry, nuclear comparisons, and reaction data.

Start Here

This lesson teaches the process step by step. Read the vocabulary, model, and examples in order before answering practice questions.

Vocabulary Before Use

TermMeaning
reaction evidenceobservations or data that support a reaction claim
balanced equation evidenceatom-count evidence showing conservation
quantity evidencemole, mass, or yield data used to support a calculation
synthesis explanationa complete explanation using multiple types of evidence
Science Safety and Resource Note: Unit synthesis across reaction evidence, equations, mole tables, stoichiometry data, and nuclear/chemical comparisons. No chemical or nuclear materials are required. Physical reactions, heating, mixing, chemicals, pressure, or nuclear/radiation materials are not part of this independent lesson and require separate Teacher of Record assignment and safety controls.

Required Direct Simulation Link

Open this exact simulation/resource: PhET Balancing Chemical Equations

Direct URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/balancing-chemical-equations/latest/balancing-chemical-equations_all.html

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. After the simulation opens, start with the introduction or balance-equation screen.
  2. Select one equation from the lesson activity and change coefficients only, not subscripts.
  3. Turn on atom counts, bar charts, or balance indicators if those tools are available.
  4. Record the unbalanced equation, final coefficients, atom counts on both sides, and the conservation rule shown by the model.

Evidence check: Observe the model, table, data, image, or source section carefully. Your notes must include the requested observations, data, or model evidence before you answer the lesson questions.

Do not leave this linked simulation/resource or use another one. Use this exact link so the model or source matches the lesson requirement.

Success Criteria

  • Use evidence from the page before making a claim.
  • Show units and equation reasoning when quantities are involved.
  • Explain the chemistry without relying on a live presentation.
