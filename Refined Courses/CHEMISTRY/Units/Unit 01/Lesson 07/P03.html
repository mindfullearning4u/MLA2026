P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue the Teaching

The phrase 'just a theory' is not accurate scientific language. In science, a theory is a strong explanation supported by many lines of evidence. It is different from a guess.

Models are useful because Chemistry often studies particles too small to see directly. But every model leaves something out. A ball-and-stick model can show atoms connected, but it does not show actual atom size, constant motion, or electron behavior fully.

Reasoning Steps for This Lesson

  1. Ask what the item does: represent, describe, explain, or support a decision.
  2. Classify it as model, law, theory, or decision evidence.
  3. State one strength of the item.
  4. State one limitation or caution.
  5. Use evidence, not slogans, when making decisions.
Common Mistake: Treating a scientific theory as a guess or treating a model as a perfect copy of reality.
Incorrect example: Atomic theory is only a guess because it has the word theory.
Correct example: Atomic theory is a well-supported explanation for matter based on many lines of evidence; it is not a random guess.

Teachable Explanation

Scientific words have precise meanings. Confusing everyday meanings with scientific meanings leads to weak reasoning.
