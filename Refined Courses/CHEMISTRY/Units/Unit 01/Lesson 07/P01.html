P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: Models, Laws, Theories, and Chemistry Decisions
Lesson Purpose: Distinguish models, laws, theories, and chemistry-based decisions.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.CHEM.SCI.03
Support Standards: SC.912.N.3.3; SC.912.N.3.5; SC.912.N.4.1

What You Will Learn

  • distinguish scientific models, laws, and theories
  • explain how models help chemistry thinking while remaining limited
  • use chemistry evidence to support a decision

What You Will Do

  1. Read the vocabulary and teaching sequence carefully.
  2. Use the included tables, models, diagrams, scenarios, or data displays to make sense of the chemistry idea.
  3. Complete notebook evidence that shows your reasoning, not just your answer.
  4. Use the checkpoint to show independent mastery.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by explaining the lesson concept with accurate chemistry vocabulary, using evidence from the required stimulus, and writing reasoning that connects the evidence to the standard.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you work like a chemistry student: you will read evidence, use models or data, and explain how the evidence supports a scientific answer.

Science Safety and Resource Note: Lab/Data/Safety Connection: Model/law/theory comparison and chemistry decision evidence using case scenarios.
Safety: Use written case evidence only. Do not test consumer products or chemicals without assignment.

Required Direct Simulation Link

Direct URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/rutherford-scattering/latest/rutherford-scattering_all.html

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. After the simulation opens, start with the atom model shown on the screen and turn on alpha-particle firing if it is not already moving.
  2. Observe several alpha particles and separate their paths into mostly straight, slightly deflected, or strongly deflected.
  3. Switch or compare available atom-model views if the simulation provides more than one model.
  4. Record which observation supports a small dense nucleus and one limitation of the model for the lesson focus: Models, Laws, Theories, and Chemistry Decisions.
