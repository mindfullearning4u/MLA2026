P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: A ball-and-stick model shows water as two hydrogen atoms connected to one oxygen atom. What is useful and what is limited?

  1. Classify: it is a model because it represents a molecule.
  2. Useful feature: it shows composition and connection pattern.
  3. Limitation: it does not show actual atom size, scale, or constant motion.
  4. Conclusion: use the model for structure, but do not treat it as a perfect picture.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Explain why 'theory means guess' is wrong.

  1. Identify the word: theory.
  2. Use scientific meaning: a theory is a well-supported explanation.
  3. Connect evidence: theories are built from repeated observations and investigations.
  4. Conclusion: scientific theory is not a random guess.

Worked Example 3

Problem: A product says 'natural and safe.' What evidence should guide a Chemistry decision?

  1. Identify the claim: the product is safe.
  2. Check support: 'natural' is not enough evidence.
  3. Ask for chemical identity, concentration, exposure route, intended use, and reliable safety data.
  4. Conclusion: no safety decision should rely on the label alone.
Common Mistake: Treating a scientific theory as a guess or treating a model as a perfect copy of reality.
Incorrect example: Atomic theory is only a guess because it has the word theory.
Correct example: Atomic theory is a well-supported explanation for matter based on many lines of evidence; it is not a random guess.

Teachable Explanation

The examples show that scientific reasoning depends on precise meanings and evidence. A model, law, theory, or decision claim must be used for the correct purpose.
