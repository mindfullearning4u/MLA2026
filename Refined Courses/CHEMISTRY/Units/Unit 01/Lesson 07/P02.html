P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Unit 1 Lesson 07 - Models, Laws, Theories, and Chemistry Decisions

Vocabulary

  • Model: a representation used to explain or predict a system.
  • Law: a description of a consistent natural pattern.
  • Theory: a well-supported explanation for a broad body of evidence.
  • Limitation: something a model does not show or cannot explain.
  • Evidence-based decision: a choice supported by data, risks, benefits, and reliable sources.

Start With the Big Idea

Chemistry uses models, laws, and theories differently. A model represents, a law describes a pattern, and a theory explains why a broad pattern happens. Chemistry decisions should use evidence, not labels or opinions alone.

Step-by-Step Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1: Identify the scientific tool. Ask whether the item represents, describes, or explains.
  2. Step 2: A model represents. A particle diagram represents spacing and motion, but it is not a perfect copy of reality.
  3. Step 3: A law describes. A law tells what consistently happens, such as mass conservation in a closed system.
  4. Step 4: A theory explains. Atomic theory explains matter in terms of atoms and evidence from many investigations.
  5. Step 5: Use evidence for decisions. A chemical decision should consider identity, concentration, exposure, benefits, risks, and source reliability.
