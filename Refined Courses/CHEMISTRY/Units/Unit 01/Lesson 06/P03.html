P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue the Teaching

Some evidence can be misleading if it is used alone. Bubbles do not automatically mean chemical change. Boiling water bubbles because liquid water becomes water vapor, which is physical. A reaction that produces a new gas can also form bubbles, which may be chemical. Context decides.

Color change also needs context. Food coloring spreading through water changes color but does not prove a new substance formed. Rusting changes color because iron oxide forms, which is chemical.

Reasoning Steps for This Lesson

  1. Name the observed change.
  2. Ask whether the substance identity stayed the same.
  3. Look for evidence of a new substance.
  4. Check whether the same evidence could also occur in a physical change.
  5. Write the classification and justify it with evidence.
Common Mistake: Assuming every color change, bubble, or temperature change proves a chemical change without checking whether a new substance formed.
Incorrect example: The water boiling is chemical because bubbles appear.
Correct example: Boiling water is physical because liquid water changes to water vapor, but the substance is still water.

Teachable Explanation

The same observation can have different meanings in different contexts. Chemistry classification requires evidence plus reasoning.
