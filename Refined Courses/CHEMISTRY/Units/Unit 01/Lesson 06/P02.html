Notebook Title: Unit 1 Lesson 06 - Physical and Chemical Properties and Changes

Vocabulary

  • Physical property: a characteristic observed without changing substance identity.
  • Chemical property: a characteristic describing how a substance can form new substances.
  • Physical change: a change in form, size, or state without forming a new substance.
  • Chemical change: a change that produces one or more new substances.
  • Evidence of chemical change: observations such as gas, precipitate, energy change, odor, or color change when context supports new substance formation.

Start With the Big Idea

The key question is not 'Did it look different?' The key question is 'Did a new substance form?' Chemistry classification depends on evidence of identity change.

Step-by-Step Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1: Identify the observation. What changed: color, state, shape, temperature, gas, odor, or solid formation?
  2. Step 2: Ask whether identity changed. If the same substance remains, the change is physical.
  3. Step 3: Look for new-substance evidence. Rust, ash, gas from reaction, precipitate, or new odor may support chemical change.
  4. Step 4: Use context. Bubbles from boiling are physical. Bubbles from a reaction may be chemical.
  5. Step 5: Explain the classification. Name the evidence and connect it to physical or chemical change.
