P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: Classify ice melting.

  1. Observation: solid ice becomes liquid water.
  2. Identity check: the substance is still water.
  3. New substance evidence: none.
  4. Classification: physical change.
  5. Reasoning: only state changed; identity did not.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Classify iron rusting.

  1. Observation: iron develops reddish-brown rust.
  2. Identity check: rust is iron oxide, not pure iron.
  3. New substance evidence: new material forms on the surface.
  4. Classification: chemical change.
  5. Reasoning: new substance formation is the key evidence.

Worked Example 3

Problem: A colorless liquid turns blue after food coloring is added. Is that chemical?

  1. Observation: color changes.
  2. Context: food coloring spreads through the liquid.
  3. New substance evidence: none given.
  4. Classification: physical mixing unless reaction evidence is provided.
  5. Reasoning: color change alone does not prove new substance formation.
Common Mistake: Assuming every color change, bubble, or temperature change proves a chemical change without checking whether a new substance formed.
Incorrect example: The water boiling is chemical because bubbles appear.
Correct example: Boiling water is physical because liquid water changes to water vapor, but the substance is still water.

Teachable Explanation

The examples use the same decision test every time: observation, identity check, new-substance evidence, classification, and reasoning.
