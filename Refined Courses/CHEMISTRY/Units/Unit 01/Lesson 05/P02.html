P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Unit 1 Lesson 05 - States of Matter and Particle Models

Vocabulary

  • Solid: matter with particles close together in fixed positions that vibrate.
  • Liquid: matter with particles close together that can slide past one another.
  • Gas: matter with particles far apart moving freely.
  • Phase change: a change from one state of matter to another.
  • Particle model: a representation showing particle spacing, arrangement, and motion.

Start With the Big Idea

States of matter are not just names. They are explained by particle spacing, particle motion, and energy. A phase change changes particle arrangement and motion, but it does not automatically create a new substance.

Step-by-Step Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1: Look at particle spacing. Close and organized often means solid. Close but disordered often means liquid. Far apart often means gas.
  2. Step 2: Look at particle motion. Solid particles vibrate in place. Liquid particles slide past each other. Gas particles move freely.
  3. Step 3: Connect energy to motion. Adding energy usually increases motion. Removing energy usually decreases motion.
  4. Step 4: Identify the phase change. Melting is solid to liquid. Freezing is liquid to solid. Vaporization is liquid to gas. Condensation is gas to liquid.
  5. Step 5: Check identity. If water freezes, it is still water. The state changed, not the substance identity.

Required Visual, Model, or Data Display

SolidVery closeVibrate in placeDots packed in an orderly pattern.

GasFar apartMove freelyDots spread throughout the container.

How to Read the Visual

  1. Ignore color first; focus on spacing.
  2. Ask whether the particles are fixed, sliding, or free-moving.
  3. Use spacing and motion together. Spacing alone is not enough.
  4. Connect the model to shape and volume: fixed shape for solid, container shape for liquid, fills container for gas.

Notebook Evidence

Draw three particle models: solid, liquid, and gas. Under each model, write one sentence explaining spacing and one sentence explaining motion.
