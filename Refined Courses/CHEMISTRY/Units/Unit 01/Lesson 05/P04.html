P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Problem: A diagram shows particles packed tightly in a fixed pattern with small vibration arrows. What state is shown?

  1. Step 1: Read spacing: particles are very close.
  2. Read arrangement: fixed pattern.
  3. Read motion: vibration in place.
  4. Connect to definition: close, fixed, vibrating particles describe a solid.
  5. Answer: solid.
  6. Interpretation: Explain what the chemistry evidence shows and why it supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Explain melting using particle motion.

  1. Starting state: solid.
  2. Ending state: liquid.
  3. Energy change: energy is added.
  4. Particle effect: particles move more and can slide past one another.
  5. Identity check: the substance is the same, so melting is a physical phase change.

Worked Example 3

Problem: A container is filled by particles that are far apart and moving in all directions. What state is shown and why?

  1. Far spacing suggests gas.
  2. Free motion in all directions supports gas.
  3. Filling the container also supports gas behavior.
  4. Answer: gas, because particles are far apart and move freely.
Common Mistake: Thinking a phase change creates a new substance because the material looks different.
Incorrect: When ice melts, it becomes a new substance because it changes from hard solid to liquid.
Correct: When ice melts, it becomes liquid water. The particles move differently, but the substance is still water, so the change is physical.

Teachable Explanation

The examples show how to read particle models instead of guessing from the word solid, liquid, or gas. Always use spacing, motion, and identity.
