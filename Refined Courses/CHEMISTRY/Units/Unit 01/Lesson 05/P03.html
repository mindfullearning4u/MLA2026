Continue the Teaching

A phase change is physical when the substance identity stays the same. Ice, liquid water, and water vapor are all water. The particles have different motion and spacing, but the molecules are still water molecules.

Energy explains why phase changes happen. When energy is added during melting, particles move enough to leave fixed positions. When energy is removed during freezing, particles slow down and settle into fixed positions.

Reasoning Steps for This Lesson

  1. Identify the starting state.
  2. Identify the ending state.
  3. Describe how particle spacing changes.
  4. Describe how particle motion changes.
  5. State whether the substance identity changed.
Common Mistake: Thinking a phase change creates a new substance because the material looks different.
Incorrect: When ice melts, it becomes a new substance because it changes from hard solid to liquid.
Correct: When ice melts, it becomes liquid water. The particles move differently, but the substance is still water, so the change is physical.

Teachable Explanation

Appearance can change dramatically during a phase change. Chemistry classification depends on particle identity, not just appearance.
