Worked Example 1

Problem: A procedure says, 'Heat an unknown liquid until it changes.' What Unit 1 issues appear?

  1. Step 1: Stimulus type: safety/procedure.
  2. Safety issue: unknown liquid plus heat is not assigned without controls.
  3. Replication issue: no amount, tool, temperature, or time is provided.
  4. Correct response: do not perform the procedure; use assigned data or rewrite with safety controls.
  5. Reasoning: unsafe and vague procedures weaken both safety and evidence.
  6. Interpretation: Explain what the chemistry evidence shows and why it supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 2

Problem: A graph shows temperature increasing from 22.0 deg C to 35.5 deg C over 6 minutes.

  1. Stimulus type: graph/data.
  2. Read axes: time is on the x-axis; temperature is on the y-axis.
  3. Read trend: temperature increases over time.
  4. Conclusion: the sample warmed during the recorded interval.
  5. Do not overclaim: this trend alone does not prove a new substance formed.

Worked Example 3

Problem: A particle diagram shows far-apart particles moving freely, and a claim says the sample became a new substance.

  1. Stimulus type: particle model plus claim.
  2. Particle evidence: far-apart, freely moving particles indicate gas.
  3. Identity check: the model shows state, not necessarily a new substance.
  4. Conclusion: the diagram supports gas state, but more evidence is needed to prove a chemical change.
Common Mistake: Answering from memory without first identifying which Unit 1 skill the stimulus requires.
Incorrect: The answer is chemical because the scenario looks like a lab.
Correct: The classification depends on evidence. If a new substance forms, the change is chemical; if only state, shape, or size changes, it is physical.

Teachable Explanation

Synthesis examples must avoid overclaiming. Use exactly what the stimulus supports, then state what cannot be concluded without more evidence.
