P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: Putting It All Together
Lesson Purpose: Synthesize inquiry, safety, measurement, data, matter, and scientific models.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.CHEM.SCI.01; MLA.CHEM.SCI.02; MLA.CHEM.SCI.03; MLA.CHEM.LAB.01; MLA.CHEM.LAB.02; MLA.CHEM.LAB.03; MLA.CHEM.MAT.01; MLA.CHEM.MAT.02; MLA.CHEM.MAT.03
Support Standards: ELA.K12.EE.1.1; MA.K12.MTR.6.1

What You Will Learn

  • combine Unit 1 evidence, safety, measurement, data, and matter concepts
  • interpret mixed chemistry stimuli such as procedures, tables, graphs, and particle models
  • prepare for the Unit 1 assessment by explaining reasoning step by step

What You Will Do

  1. Read the vocabulary and teaching sequence carefully.
  2. Use the included tables, models, diagrams, scenarios, or data displays to make sense of the chemistry idea.
  3. Complete notebook evidence that shows your reasoning, not just your answer.
  4. Use the checkpoint to show independent mastery.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by explaining the lesson concept with accurate chemistry vocabulary, using evidence from the required stimulus, and writing reasoning that connects the evidence to the standard.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you work like a chemistry student: you will read evidence, use models or data, and explain how the evidence supports a scientific answer.

Science Safety and Resource Note: Lab/Data/Safety Connection: Unit synthesis using only assigned lesson scenarios, tables, graphs, particle diagrams, and matter classification evidence.
Safety: No physical investigation is required for this synthesis lesson. Use provided stimuli only.

Required Direct Simulation Link

Open this exact simulation/resource: PhET States of Matter

Direct URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/states-of-matter/latest/states-of-matter_all.html

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. After the simulation opens, use the screen that shows particles for solid, liquid, and gas behavior.
  2. Select or display one state of matter at a time: solid, liquid, then gas.
  3. Use the temperature or heating/cooling control only after recording the starting particle spacing and motion.
  4. Record a three-row table with state, particle spacing, particle motion, temperature or energy change, and the phase-change conclusion.

Evidence check: Observe the model, table, data, image, or source section carefully. Your notes must include the requested observations, data, or model evidence before you answer the lesson questions.

Do not leave this linked simulation/resource or use another one. Use this exact link so the model or source matches the lesson requirement.
