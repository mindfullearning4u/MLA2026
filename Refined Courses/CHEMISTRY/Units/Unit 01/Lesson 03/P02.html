Notebook Title: Unit 1 Lesson 03 - Measurement, Units, and Significant Figures

Vocabulary

  • Measurement: a number and a unit from a tool or calculation.
  • Unit: the label that tells what quantity is measured, such as g, mL, or deg C.
  • Precision: how detailed a tool reading is or how close repeated readings are.
  • Significant figures: digits that communicate measurement precision.
  • Scientific notation: writing a number as a value from 1 to less than 10 multiplied by a power of 10.

Start With the Big Idea

Chemistry measurements must communicate both size and precision. A number without a unit is incomplete, and extra digits that a tool did not measure can make data look more precise than it really is.

Step-by-Step Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1: Read the unit. The unit tells whether the value is mass, volume, temperature, time, or another quantity.
  2. Step 2: Read the tool precision. A balance reading 12.36 g supports hundredths of a gram. A beaker marking every 50 mL does not support 12.36 mL.
  3. Step 3: Keep significant figures tied to the tool. Do not drop measured digits without reason, and do not invent extra digits.
  4. Step 4: Use scientific notation for very large or small values. Move the decimal to make a number from 1 to less than 10, then count decimal moves for the exponent.
  5. Step 5: Check the direction of the exponent. Large original numbers get positive exponents. Small decimal numbers get negative exponents.

How to Read the Visual

  1. Start with the unit because it identifies the quantity.
  2. Then look at decimal places and zeros to decide precision.
  3. For scientific notation, move the decimal until one nonzero digit is left before the decimal.
  4. Check whether the original number was large or small to choose a positive or negative exponent.

Notebook Evidence

Write four measurement examples: one mass, one volume, one temperature, and one value in scientific notation. For each, explain what the unit and digits tell the reader.
