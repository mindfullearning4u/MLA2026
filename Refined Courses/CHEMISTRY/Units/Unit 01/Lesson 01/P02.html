Notebook Title: Unit 1 Lesson 01 - What Counts as Chemistry Evidence?

Vocabulary

  • Claim: the answer or conclusion you want to support.
  • Evidence: observations, measurements, data, or reliable source details that can support a claim.
  • Reasoning: the Chemistry idea that explains why the evidence supports the claim.
  • Reliable source: a source that identifies evidence, methods, author or organization, and can be checked against other evidence.
  • Inference: a logical explanation based on evidence, not a random guess.

Start With the Big Idea

Chemistry does not accept a statement just because it sounds scientific. A Chemistry answer needs a claim, evidence, and reasoning. First, decide what the claim says. Next, identify the evidence that can be checked. Then explain why that evidence supports or weakens the claim.

Step-by-Step Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1: Identify the claim. A claim is the statement being argued. For example, 'The unknown liquid is water' is a claim because it can be supported or challenged.
  2. Step 2: Identify the evidence. Evidence might be a boiling point, density, mass, observation, safety label, or repeated data table. A statement such as 'it looks like water' is weak because appearance alone is not enough.
  3. Step 3: Separate observation from inference. 'Bubbles appeared' is an observation. 'A gas formed' is an inference that may be reasonable, but it still needs context.
  4. Step 4: Check source reliability. A lab data table, safety data sheet, textbook, or official science source is stronger than an advertisement with no method or data.
  5. Step 5: Write reasoning. Reasoning connects the evidence to the concept. If density and boiling point match water, that evidence supports the claim more strongly than appearance alone.

How to Read the Visual

  1. Read the information column first and underline numbers, units, or source words.
  2. Decide whether the row is a claim, observation, measurement, or repeated data.
  3. Ask whether the row can actually support the claim.
  4. Use the strongest evidence first in a CER response.

Notebook Evidence

Create a CER organizer with three rows. In row 1, write a weak claim. In row 2, list the strongest evidence from the table. In row 3, explain why that evidence supports or does not support the claim.
