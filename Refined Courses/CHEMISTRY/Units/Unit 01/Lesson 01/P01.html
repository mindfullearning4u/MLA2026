P01 Lesson Overview

P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: What Counts as Chemistry Evidence?
Lesson Purpose: Establish scientific inquiry, chemical evidence, and reliable sources.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.CHEM.SCI.01; MLA.CHEM.SCI.02
Support Standards: ELA.K12.EE.1.1; ELA.K12.EE.2.1

What You Will Learn

  • identify observations, measurements, inferences, claims, and evidence
  • separate reliable chemistry evidence from unsupported statements
  • use a claim-evidence-reasoning organizer to explain a chemistry conclusion

What You Will Do

  1. Read the vocabulary and teaching sequence carefully.
  2. Use the included tables, models, diagrams, scenarios, or data displays to make sense of the chemistry idea.
  3. Complete notebook evidence that shows your reasoning, not just your answer.
  4. Use the checkpoint to show independent mastery.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by explaining the lesson concept with accurate chemistry vocabulary, using evidence from the required stimulus, and writing reasoning that connects the evidence to the standard.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you work like a chemistry student: you will read evidence, use models or data, and explain how the evidence supports a scientific answer.

Science Safety and Resource Note: Lab/Data/Safety Connection: Use a paper-based evidence investigation. No chemicals are handled. Students compare claim cards, observation notes, and data snippets.
Safety: This lesson uses written scenarios only. Do not taste, smell, mix, or handle unknown substances to create evidence.

Required Direct Simulation Link

Open this exact simulation/resource: PhET Reactants, Products and Leftovers

Direct URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/reactants-products-and-leftovers/latest/reactants-products-and-leftovers_all.html

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. After the simulation opens, use the Sandwich Shop screen as the evidence model for claims and data.
  2. Change the starting amounts of the ingredients one at a time.
  3. Observe what product is made and which ingredient is left over.
  4. Record the starting amounts, product amount, leftover amount, and one claim that is supported by the data.

Evidence check: Observe the model, table, data, image, or source section carefully. Your notes must include the requested observations, data, or model evidence before you answer the lesson questions.
