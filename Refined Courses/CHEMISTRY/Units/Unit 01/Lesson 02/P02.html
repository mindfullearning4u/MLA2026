Notebook Title: Unit 1 Lesson 02 - Lab Safety, Tools, and Procedures

Vocabulary

  • Procedure: an ordered set of steps for completing an investigation.
  • Apparatus: the tools or equipment used in a lab.
  • Hazard: a possible source of harm.
  • Replicable: detailed enough that someone else can repeat the process the same way.
  • Control: a safety or procedure condition kept consistent to reduce risk or error.

Start With the Big Idea

A safe Chemistry procedure protects people and protects the quality of the data. If the procedure is vague, unsafe, or missing tools and units, another person cannot repeat it and the evidence is weaker.

Step-by-Step Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1: Read for hazards. Look for heat, glassware, unknown substances, fumes, acids, bases, spills, or broken equipment.
  2. Step 2: Identify the required tool. A balance measures mass. A graduated cylinder measures liquid volume. A thermometer measures temperature.
  3. Step 3: Check quantities and units. A replicable procedure says 25.0 mL, 10.2 g, or 5 minutes, not 'some,' 'a little,' or 'for a while.'
  4. Step 4: Check order. The order matters because doing steps out of order can create hazards or change results.
  5. Step 5: Rewrite unsafe or vague steps. Replace vague wording with exact tool, amount, unit, safety control, and action.

How to Read the Visual

  1. Read the original procedure detail.
  2. Name the exact problem: missing quantity, missing tool, unsafe action, or unclear order.
  3. Use the better version to see how the problem is corrected.
  4. When writing your own procedure, include tool, unit, amount, order, and safety boundary.

Notebook Evidence

Rewrite two weak procedure steps from the table. For each rewrite, underline the tool, circle the quantity, and label the safety control.
