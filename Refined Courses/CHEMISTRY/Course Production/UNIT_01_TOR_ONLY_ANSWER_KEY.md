## Lesson 1 — Laboratory Safety, Tools, and Procedures

**Notebook Evidence:** Responses must select the safest action and explain the hazard prevented. Accept goggles/PPE, tied hair, clear aisles, no tasting, wafting only when directed, heated containers pointed away from people, immediate reporting, heat protection, directed disposal, and handwashing.

**Checkpoint exemplar:** “The unlabeled liquid is a hazard. Do not touch or smell it; notify the TOR. This prevents unknown chemical exposure and incorrect disposal.”

## Lesson 2 — Measurement, Units, Significant Figures, and Percent Error

Each calculation must show the formula, substituted values, arithmetic, correct unit, and final rounding. Percent error is `|experimental − accepted| ÷ accepted × 100%`. Use the correct precision rule for the operation. Grade numerical values against the student answer sheet; a final number without units or required work is incomplete.

## Lesson 3 — Data, Graphing, Accuracy, and Precision

Graphs need a descriptive title, independent variable on x, dependent variable on y, labeled units, even scale, accurate plotting, and an appropriate display type. Accuracy means closeness to an accepted value; precision means closeness of repeated measurements to one another. Conclusions must cite a visible pattern or specific data.

## Lesson 4 — Matter, States, and Particle Models

- Solid: close, ordered particles vibrating in place.
- Liquid: close, disordered particles moving past one another.
- Gas: widely spaced particles moving freely and filling the container.

Phase changes alter energy, motion, spacing, or arrangement—not particle identity. Checkpoint diagrams must show correct spacing/motion, labels, and justification.

## Lesson 5 — Physical and Chemical Properties and Changes

Physical properties can be observed without changing identity; chemical properties describe the ability to form new substances. Physical changes alter form/state without changing identity. Chemical-change evidence may include gas, precipitate, unexpected energy change, or lasting color/odor change and should use multiple observations when possible.

## Lesson 6 — Designing Investigations and Analyzing Evidence

Independent variable = deliberately changed; dependent variable = measured; controls = held constant. A CER needs a direct claim, specific data, and reasoning connecting evidence to the scientific idea. Reject unsupported causal claims when the design shows only correlation or lacks controls.

## Lesson 7 — Models, Laws, Theories, and Evidence-Based Decisions

**Notebook exemplar:** The sealed sample undergoes a physical phase change near 63°C. Particle identity remains the same while arrangement and motion change. Conservation of mass is supported by the unchanged 4.50 g mass. Reheating should melt the sample again near 63°C while mass remains unchanged if sealed.

**Checkpoint exemplar:** The permanent-corrosion-prevention claim is not fully supported. Coating reduces corrosion, but the coated sample gains 0.04 g and develops rust at scratches; the uncoated sample gains 0.42 g with a thick reddish-brown coating. Oxygen and water can reach exposed iron where coating is damaged. A defensible decision is to use coating with inspection and repair.
