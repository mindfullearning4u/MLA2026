# Chemistry: Savvas Source Scope and Delivery Decision

## Purpose

This document records the first source-grounded decision for the Chemistry rebuild. It supersedes only the earlier **six-unit/eight-lesson architecture assumption**. It does not authorize the creation of substitute Chemistry instructional content.

## Source reviewed

- **Savvas Realize program:** Florida Experience Chemistry
- **Course:** Florida Chemistry 1, CPALMS course number `2003340`
- **Savvas course root:** `https://www.savvasrealize.com/dashboard/program/25700d59-2c62-3ea9-abe9-d19afceb1304/43/enhanced-toc`
- **CPALMS course source:** `https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewCourse/Preview/23853`

The course has four Savvas storylines, fifteen investigations, fifty-six named Experiences, an investigative phenomenon and investigation evaluation for each investigation, and additional Nature of Science, laboratory-safety, and program resources.

## Delivery rule

MLA course pages will be **student-navigation and accountability pages**, not replacement textbooks. Each page must contain:

1. A plain-language purpose and exact completion sequence.
2. A direct, authenticated Savvas URL for every required Student eText, activity, lab, video, simulation, or quiz.
3. The exact Savvas resource title beside the link.
4. Explicit submission or evidence directions and the Tor-support route.
5. Only the minimum MLA explanation necessary to tell a student what to open, what to do, and what evidence to submit.
6. A visible **MLA Standards** section listing only the lesson's primary and supporting MLA Chemistry standard code(s). Do not display Florida, B.E.S.T., Common Core, SAT, ACT, ELD, or other external-framework codes in student-facing Chemistry lessons.

Teacher-only Savvas resources must never be presented as a required student action. When a Savvas item is initially hidden, it must be assigned and made visible before its MLA page is published.

## Required resource-link and evidence protocol

Each assigned Savvas resource must be presented in Moodle as one numbered, complete action. Do not use a generic course-home link when a direct resource URL is available.

Each action must state, in this order:

1. **Click:** a descriptive link whose visible text is the exact Savvas resource title.
2. **Complete:** the precise student action in Savvas (read, watch, interact, answer, calculate, annotate, or take the quiz).
3. **Record:** whether the student must write answers in the chemistry notebook, answer directly in Savvas, download and complete a worksheet, or submit no separate evidence.
4. **Submit:** the exact Moodle submission method. For a completed worksheet, students download it when Savvas permits, complete it, and upload the completed file; if download/upload is unavailable, they submit a clear photograph or scan of every completed page.
5. **Check:** the required completion evidence, feedback action, score, or revision step.

Every Moodle direction must make the evidence location unambiguous. For example: “Answer the Savvas questions in the activity and copy responses 1–4 into your chemistry notebook,” or “Download the worksheet, complete all pages, then upload the file or clear photographs of all completed pages to this Moodle assignment.”

The Moodle page must never imply that the student should independently search Savvas, infer which resource to open, decide whether to record work, or guess where to submit it.

| Page | Required function |
|---|---|
| P01 | Lesson overview, MLA Chemistry standards only, objectives, readiness/materials, and the complete ordered resource path. |
| P02 | Vocabulary definitions plus exact notebook-writing directions. |
| P03 | Direct Savvas instructional link(s) and step-by-step read/watch/annotate directions. |
| P04 | Direct Savvas model, animation, example, or data link and a guided “follow this model” procedure. |
| P05 | Direct Savvas guided-practice link with explicit response, notebook, feedback, and correction directions. |
| P06 | Direct Savvas lab, simulation, worksheet, data activity, or application link with explicit evidence-capture and Moodle-submission directions. |
| P07 | Checkpoint, final evidence checklist, direct Savvas quiz or Investigation Evaluation link, score/revision procedure, and Tor-support route. |

## Recommended unit structure

The appropriate instructional units are the four Savvas storylines. A short ungraded **Course Start: Savvas Navigation, Laboratory Safety, and Nature of Science** module precedes Unit 1; it is not a content unit and does not alter the unit count.

| MLA Unit | Savvas storyline | Investigations | Savvas Experiences | Rationale |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Unit 1 | Atoms, Elements, and Molecules | 1-3 | 13 | Establishes particulate matter, atomic structure, periodicity, bonding, and compounds before quantitative reactions. |
| Unit 2 | Understanding Chemical Reactions | 4-8 | 19 | Groups properties, quantities, reactions, stoichiometry, and thermochemistry as one connected reaction-and-energy system. |
| Unit 3 | Equilibrium | 9-11 | 12 | Groups gas behavior, reaction rates/equilibrium, and acid-base equilibria. |
| Unit 4 | Industrial Applications | 12-15 | 12 | Applies prior chemistry to redox/electrochemistry, organic chemistry, nuclear processes, and green chemistry. |

**Total:** 4 content units, 15 Savvas-investigation modules, and 56 Savvas Experience lessons.

## Lesson granularity decision

One MLA lesson maps to one named **Savvas Experience**, not to a newly written content lesson. Each Savvas Investigation is an MLA lesson group containing its phenomenon, its sequence of Experience lessons, a selected Savvas lab/data/CER activity, and the Savvas Investigation Evaluation.

This creates 56 content lessons distributed as 13 / 19 / 12 / 12. This is intentionally different from Biology's 48 lessons: the count follows the adopted Chemistry program rather than a reusable numerical pattern.

## Source evidence for the student-resource model

Savvas Experience 1, *The Particle Nature of Matter*, exposes student-ready resources including the Student eText, eText, inquiry-lab variants, data-analysis activities, a CER activity, and a standards-progress quiz. This resource pattern confirms that Savvas—not MLA-authored pages—will supply the primary instruction, practice, laboratory experience, and assessment source.

## Required next controls

- Capture and validate each required item’s direct Savvas URL while signed in.
- Choose one safe, student-visible lab/data/CER resource per investigation; do not require duplicate PDF and online versions of the same activity.
- Record the exact Savvas standards tag(s) for every Experience and investigation evaluation.
- Revise the MLA standard inventory and crosswalk so redox/electrochemistry, organic chemistry, nuclear processes, and green chemistry are explicitly represented, while identifying standards-bearing versus Savvas-enrichment content.
- Build an internal lesson-level standards ledger for all 57 lessons. Each row must identify the unit, lesson, Savvas Experience/resource, primary MLA standard(s), supporting MLA standard(s), CPALMS/Florida alignment, B.E.S.T. literacy and MTR support, Common Core literacy support, SAT readiness skill, ACT Science category, and required assessment evidence. Only the MLA Chemistry codes transfer to student-facing lesson and assessment displays.
- Attach one or more MLA standard codes to every assessment question. An assessment item's metadata and feedback must preserve the same code(s), with a clear distinction between the primary assessed standard and supporting/prerequisite standards.
- Do not publish an MLA link until the target item has been assigned, visible to students, and opened successfully from a student-facing test account or equivalent preview.
