P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Asking Strong Global Questions: Evidence, Perspective, and Explanation

Mapped Standard: MLA.GP.FND.1

Vocabulary

TermStudent-Friendly MeaningHow To Use It
Global issueA problem or question connected to more than one place, group, system, or country.Name the issue and identify the scale.
StakeholderA person, group, institution, or community affected by the issue or involved in decisions.Ask who benefits, who is harmed, and who has power.
PerspectiveA point of view shaped by experience, role, values, information, and interest.Compare what different stakeholders notice or prioritize.
EvidenceSpecific information from a source, map, table, graph, image, or scenario.Quote, paraphrase, or describe the exact detail that proves the claim.
LimitationWhat the evidence cannot prove by itself.Use limits to avoid exaggerating or overclaiming.

Teaching Sequence

  1. Step 1 - Name the issue exactly. Do not start with a vague topic. Write the specific issue connected to this lesson: Asking Strong Global Questions.
  2. Step 2 - Identify the scale. Decide whether the evidence is local, national, regional, or global. Scale matters because a claim about one place cannot automatically prove a claim about the whole world.
  3. Step 3 - Read the visual/source support. Use the question-quality checklist before writing. Notice labels, categories, patterns, missing information, and stakeholders.
  4. Step 4 - Connect evidence to the standard. Your explanation must show the thinking required by MLA.GP.FND.1.
  5. Step 5 - Check the limit. Add one sentence that says what the evidence does not prove yet.
