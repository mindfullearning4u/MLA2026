# Social Studies Visual and Source Standard

## Purpose

Social studies and social science courses do not require external simulations by default. They do require embedded visuals, source supports, and evidence organizers whenever those items help a student understand the mapped standard, remove ambiguity, or provide the source stimulus needed to answer a lesson task or assessment question.

This standard applies to history, government, economics, geography, psychology, sociology, global perspectives, cultural studies, and other social studies or social science courses.

## Simulation Rule

Do not add simulations as a standard requirement for social studies or social science courses.

External simulations, interactives, or resource links are optional enrichment only unless:

- the user explicitly requests them for a course, unit, or lesson
- the approved mapping requires an interactive resource
- the standard cannot be taught or assessed defensibly without the external activity

When an external resource is used, follow `.codex/standards/11-external-learning-resources-and-simulations-standard.md`.

## Required Embedded Supports

Use embedded supports directly inside lesson HTML when they strengthen mastery or are needed for the task:

- maps
- timelines
- charts
- tables
- graphs and data displays
- source excerpts
- primary-source or secondary-source organizers
- cause-and-effect organizers
- compare-and-contrast organizers
- chronology organizers
- civic process diagrams
- constitutional principle charts
- court-case decision charts
- policy impact charts
- political cartoons, photographs, paintings, posters, or public-domain images when relevant
- demographic, economic, migration, election, or conflict data displays

The visual or source support must appear near the explanation, example, task, or question it supports. Students must not have to search elsewhere, infer which source to use, or rely on a teacher to provide the missing representation.

## Assessment XML Requirement

For Moodle XML assessments, embed the required stimulus directly inside the question whenever the question depends on it.

Examples that normally require embedded stimulus:

- "Use the map..."
- "Based on the timeline..."
- "According to the excerpt..."
- "Use the chart/table/data..."
- "Which source best supports..."
- "Which civic process is shown..."
- "What conclusion is supported by this political cartoon/image..."
- questions requiring geographic, chronological, source-analysis, data-analysis, or visual-evidence reasoning

Do not reference "the map," "the timeline," "the source," "the chart," "the table," or "the image" unless that object is included in the question.

## Mapping Alignment

Every embedded support must trace to the approved crosswalk, unit mapping, and lesson mapping. Do not add visuals or sources that pull the lesson outside the assigned standard or create future-unit/future-lesson content.

For every social studies/social science unit, the audit must verify:

- mapped standards are taught and assessed
- lesson visuals and sources support the assigned standard
- assessment stimuli match the specific lesson or unit being assessed
- no assessment question requires outside knowledge, a missing source, or an unstated visual
- no simulation requirement was introduced unless explicitly required

## Certification Gate

A social studies or social science unit cannot pass if:

- a required map, source excerpt, timeline, chart, data display, or civic/process visual is missing
- an assessment question references a visual or source that is not embedded
- a student must search elsewhere to answer a question
- a visual or source is decorative instead of instructional
- a visual or source introduces content outside the approved mapping
- a simulation/resource requirement is added without user direction or mapping evidence
