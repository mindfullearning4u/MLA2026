# MLA Math for College Readiness Unit Mapping v1

## Purpose

This document establishes the official MLA Math for College Readiness instructional blueprint. The approved standards crosswalk defines what must be taught; this mapping defines when concepts are taught and in what instructional order.

Instruction is sequenced by quantitative reasoning development, College Algebra readiness, SAT readiness, ACT readiness, financial literacy readiness, data literacy readiness, probability and risk reasoning, applied geometry and measurement, workforce readiness, and real-world modeling.

This course uses the approved Hybrid Model. It is not Algebra I remediation, Algebra II remediation, Precalculus preparation, consumer math only, or SAT/ACT test preparation only.

## Course Structure

- 6 units
- 8 lessons per unit
- Lesson 8 in every unit: Putting It All Together
- Every approved MLA MCR standard is mapped once as a primary instructional target.
- Support-only review, modeling, reasoning, or synthesis lessons do not create duplicate primary benchmark ownership.

## Unit 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving

**Unit Purpose:** Build foundational readiness through quantities, units, percent reasoning, ratios, weighted averages, formulas, function notation, estimation, and reasonableness.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.MCR.QR.1, MLA.MCR.QR.2, MLA.MCR.QR.3, MLA.MCR.QR.4, MLA.MCR.QR.5, MLA.MCR.QR.6

**Unit Justification:** Quantitative reasoning comes first because every later domain depends on interpreting quantities, tracking units, using rates and percents, checking reasonableness, and communicating what results mean in context.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Rational number operations, percent language, ratio/rate language, evaluating expressions, reading tables and graphs, identifying quantities and units, and basic calculator or spreadsheet use.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports applied algebra, data interpretation, financial decisions, probability, measurement, modeling, SAT/ACT readiness, workforce readiness, and College Algebra readiness.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** SAT-style quantitative comparison, ACT-style mixed-skill items, spreadsheet weighted-score investigations, error analysis, open-ended estimation tasks, and multi-step reasonableness challenges.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Unit 1 quantitative reasoning, formulas, function notation, average rate of change, coordinate plane, graph interpretation, solving linear equations, and proportional reasoning.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** SAT-style algebra models, ACT-style mixed algebra/function problems, constraints optimization tasks, spreadsheet scenario modeling, piecewise graph interpretation, and error analysis.

# Appendix 3: Instructional Sequencing Validation

The sequence begins with quantitative reasoning because students must be able to identify quantities, track units, estimate, check reasonableness, and interpret results before applying algebra, data, finance, probability, or measurement.

Applied algebra comes next because linear models, inequalities, systems, exponential models, piecewise relationships, and constraints provide the modeling language needed across the rest of the course. This placement supports College Algebra readiness without turning the course into Algebra I or Algebra II remediation.

Data literacy follows algebra because students need graph interpretation, slope, function models, and quantitative comparison before evaluating distributions, bivariate data, correlation, causation, sampling, and reports.

Financial literacy follows quantitative, algebraic, and data literacy because budgeting, interest, debt, insurance, retirement, and spreadsheet-supported decisions require percent reasoning, formulas, exponential growth, constraints, and data interpretation.

Probability and risk follow data and finance because students need two-way tables, relative frequency, financial risk contexts, and decision reasoning before conditional probability, expected value, and fair decisions.

Measurement and applied geometry conclude the course because they synthesize proportional reasoning, algebra, units, geometry, trigonometry, design constraints, and workforce modeling.
