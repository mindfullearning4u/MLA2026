UnemploymentCondition in which people who are able and seeking to work cannot find employment.
ReliefAssistance provided to people experiencing economic hardship.
BreadlineLine of people waiting to receive free or low-cost food.
Soup kitchenOrganization or location providing meals to people in need.
HoovervilleInformal settlement of makeshift housing during the Great Depression, named critically after President Herbert Hoover.
ForeclosureLegal process by which a lender takes property after loan obligations are not met.
Dust BowlSevere environmental and agricultural disaster affecting parts of the Great Plains during the 1930s through drought, soil erosion, wind, and farming practices.
DroughtExtended period of unusually low precipitation causing water shortages and agricultural stress.
Soil erosionRemoval or loss of topsoil through wind, water, or other processes.
MigrationMovement of people from one place to another.
Push factorCondition encouraging people to leave a place.
Pull factorCondition attracting people to another place.
Migrant workerWorker who moves from one location to another seeking employment, often in seasonal industries.
RepatriationReturn or removal of people to a country considered their country of origin; in Depression-era campaigns this affected Mexican nationals and some U.S. citizens of Mexican ancestry.
Reconstruction Finance CorporationFederal agency created under President Hoover to provide financial support to banks, railroads, and other institutions during the Depression.
Bonus ArmyGroup of World War I veterans who gathered in Washington, D.C., in 1932 seeking early payment of a promised veterans' bonus.
Documentary photographyPhotography intended to record people, places, conditions, or events for informational, social, or historical purposes.
Geographic reasoningUse of place, region, environment, migration, and spatial patterns to explain historical developments.
