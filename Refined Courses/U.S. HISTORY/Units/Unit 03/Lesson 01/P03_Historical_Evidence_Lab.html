P03 — Historical Evidence Lab

Evidence Question

What does each source reveal about growth, and what prevents us from treating it as the whole story?

Worked Model — Automobile Data

Source: U.S. Census historical table reports about 9.2 million registered motor vehicles in 1920 and about 20.1 million in 1925.

Actor/type Growth evidence Limitation Inference
Census quantitative data Registrations more than doubled. National totals do not show who owned vehicles or regional inequality. Automobile use expanded rapidly, but the statistic cannot prove equal access.

How the reasoning works: describe the trend first, infer cautiously, then name what the evidence cannot establish.

Evidence Set
  1. Business/production: Ford and manufacturing records reveal efficiency claims; business sources may emphasize success.
  2. Advertisement: a 1920s automobile advertisement reveals audience, aspiration, and persuasion—not average ownership.
  3. Credit source: installment promotions emphasize affordability but may omit total cost or debt risk.
  4. Agricultural evidence: farm testimony or price data exposes distress but varies by crop and region.
  5. Worker/household account: reveals lived experience but cannot represent all households.
  6. Secondary interpretation: explains boom and inequality but reflects a historian’s argument.

Authoritative starting points: Library of Congress: Merchandising and Advertising; U.S. Census historical statistics.
