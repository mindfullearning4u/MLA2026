P05 — Guided Investigation

Guided Question

Which interacting causes best explain rapid 1920s growth, and how should a historian test whether the prosperity was broadly shared?

Step 1 — Define the Outcome

We are explaining rapid growth during much of the 1920s, not the entire decade and not equal prosperity. A precise outcome prevents an overbroad argument.

Step 2 — Sort the Causes
Category Worked examples Why it matters
Production Productivity, standardization, mass production More goods could be produced at lower unit cost in some industries.
Demand Advertising, national brands, consumer aspirations Businesses encouraged purchases and expanded markets.
Access Credit and installment plans More households could buy costly goods, but assumed debt.
Network effects Automobiles connected steel, rubber, glass, oil, roads, and tourism Growth in one sector stimulated others.
Step 3 — Explain Interaction

Worked reasoning: Mass production alone creates supply, not guaranteed sales. Advertising encouraged demand; credit changed when payment occurred; automobiles stimulated connected industries. Together these factors reinforced growth.

Why this is stronger: it explains the mechanism instead of merely naming several causes.

Step 4 — Test the Claim
Test Worked example
Quantitative trend Vehicle registrations increased sharply from 1920 to 1925.
Corroboration Production data and advertising support expansion from different perspectives.
Counterevidence Farm prices, debt, rural electrification, and discriminatory employment reveal limits.
Qualification The boom was substantial but uneven across sectors, groups, and regions.
Step 5 — Model CER

Claim: Productivity and mass production were foundational, but their effect depended on credit, advertising, and connected markets.

Evidence: rapidly rising vehicle registrations show expanding demand, while automobile production supported steel, rubber, oil, roads, and services.

Reasoning: production efficiency increased supply; financing and persuasion increased purchasing; linked industries multiplied the effect. Farm distress and unequal access limit the claim.

Pause and Review — Notebook

Write a four-part summary: strongest cause; interaction; evidence; limitation. Use the model above, but explain the ideas in your own words.

Page Summary — Copy into Notebook

The guided process defined a precise outcome, categorized causes, explained interaction, tested the claim with corroborating and counterevidence, and formed a qualified CER. The key historical habit is to explain how causes worked together and then test whether national growth reached different groups equally.
