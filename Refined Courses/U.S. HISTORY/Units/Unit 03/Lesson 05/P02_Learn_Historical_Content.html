Vocabulary â€” Copy Every Term and Definition
Great DepressionSevere and prolonged economic downturn beginning in 1929 and affecting the United States and much of the world during the 1930s.
SpeculationPurchase of an asset substantially because of an expectation that its price will rise.
Buying on marginPurchasing securities by paying part of the price and borrowing the remainder.
Stock-market crashRapid and severe decline in stock prices.
Black TuesdayOctober 29, 1929, one of the most severe days of stock-market selling during the 1929 crash.
Bank runSituation in which many depositors attempt to withdraw funds from a bank at the same time because they fear losing their money.
Bank failureClosure of a bank because it cannot meet financial obligations or depositor withdrawals.
Credit contractionReduction in the availability of loans and borrowing.
OverproductionProduction of goods beyond what can be sold profitably at prevailing levels of demand.
UnderconsumptionInsufficient consumer purchasing relative to the amount of goods and services available for sale.
DeflationGeneral decline in prices across an economy.
UnemploymentCondition in which people who are able and seeking to work cannot find employment.
ForeclosureLegal process through which a lender takes property after required loan payments are not made.
TariffTax placed on imported goods.
Smoot-Hawley Tariff1930 federal tariff law that raised duties on many imports and contributed to worsening international trade tensions during the Depression.
Economic contractionDecline in overall economic activity such as production, employment, spending, and investment.
