Worked Example 1: Reading the standard

Problem: A student is studying World War II Causes, U.S. Response, and Global Consequences and needs to explain this evidence detail: Axis aggression included Japanese expansion in Asia, Italian expansion in Africa, and Nazi expansion in Europe

Worked Model: Begin by naming the historical setting, because the same word can mean different things in different eras. In this lesson, the important detail is Axis aggression included Japanese expansion in Asia, Italian expansion in Africa, and Nazi expansion in Europe. Do not stop at naming it; ask what problem, decision, or conflict this detail helps explain.

Student Think-Aloud: The detail points toward this relationship: The United States began with neutrality and isolationist pressure but increasingly supported Allies through policies such as Lend-Lease. That tells you the answer needs cause-and-effect reasoning, not just a definition.

Model Answer: Axis aggression included Japanese expansion in Asia, Italian expansion in Africa, and Nazi expansion in Europe. This matters because it shows this relationship: The United States began with neutrality and isolationist pressure but increasingly supported Allies through policies such as Lend-Lease. The answer is strong because it uses a specific detail, explains the historical relationship, and stays within World War II Causes, U.S. Response, and Global Consequences instead of drifting into another lesson.

Incorrect shortcut: "Axis aggression included Japanese is enough because I remember seeing it in the lesson."
Correct thinking: The evidence must be connected to the historical relationship it proves: The United States began with neutrality and isolationist pressure but increasingly supported Allies through policies such as Lend-Lease.
Teachable correction: Do not treat U.S. entry as sudden with no background. Students need the sequence from global aggression to U.S. response. The fix is to pair the evidence with a cause, consequence, comparison, or policy impact from this exact lesson.
