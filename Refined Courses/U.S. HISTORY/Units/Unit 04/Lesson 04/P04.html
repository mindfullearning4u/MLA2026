Worked Example 1: Reading the standard

Problem: A student is studying Red Scare, Early Cold War, and Nuclear Proliferation and needs to explain this evidence detail: The early Cold War grew from conflict between the United States and Soviet Union over ideology, security, influence, and the future of Europe and Asia

Worked Model: Begin by naming the historical setting, because the same word can mean different things in different eras. In this lesson, the important detail is The early Cold War grew from conflict between the United States and Soviet Union over ideology, security, influence, and the future of Europe and Asia. Do not stop at naming it; ask what problem, decision, or conflict this detail helps explain.

Student Think-Aloud: The detail points toward this relationship: Containment shaped policies such as the Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO, and support for allies against communist expansion. That tells you the answer needs cause-and-effect reasoning, not just a definition.

Model Answer: The early Cold War grew from conflict between the United States and Soviet Union over ideology, security, influence, and the future of Europe and Asia. This matters because it shows this relationship: Containment shaped policies such as the Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO, and support for allies against communist expansion. The answer is strong because it uses a specific detail, explains the historical relationship, and stays within Red Scare, Early Cold War, and Nuclear Proliferation instead of drifting into another lesson.

Incorrect shortcut: "early Cold War grew is enough because I remember seeing it in the lesson."
Correct thinking: The evidence must be connected to the historical relationship it proves: Containment shaped policies such as the Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO, and support for allies against communist expansion.
Teachable correction: Do not confuse the post-World War I Red Scare with the early Cold War Red Scare.
