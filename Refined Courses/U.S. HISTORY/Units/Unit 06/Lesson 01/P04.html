Unit 06: Contemporary U.S. History and Evidence-Based Citizenship

Worked Example 1: Reading the standard

Problem: A student is studying U.S. Foreign Policy in Africa, Asia, the Caribbean, Latin America, and the Middle East and needs to explain this evidence detail: In Latin America and the Caribbean, U.S. policy involved Cuba, the Cold War, migration, trade, security, and debates over intervention

Worked Model: Begin by naming the historical setting, because the same word can mean different things in different eras. In this lesson, the important detail is In Latin America and the Caribbean, U.S. policy involved Cuba, the Cold War, migration, trade, security, and debates over intervention. Do not stop at naming it; ask what problem, decision, or conflict this detail helps explain.

Student Think-Aloud: The detail points toward this relationship: In the Middle East, U.S. policy involved oil, Israel and Arab states, the Persian Gulf, terrorism, military action, and diplomacy. That tells you the answer needs cause-and-effect reasoning, not just a definition.

Model Answer: In Latin America and the Caribbean, U.S. policy involved Cuba, the Cold War, migration, trade, security, and debates over intervention. This matters because it shows this relationship: In the Middle East, U.S. policy involved oil, Israel and Arab states, the Persian Gulf, terrorism, military action, and diplomacy. The answer is strong because it uses a specific detail, explains the historical relationship, and stays within U.S. Foreign Policy in Africa, Asia, the Caribbean, Latin America, and the Middle East instead of drifting into another lesson.

Incorrect shortcut: "In Latin America and is enough because I remember seeing it in the lesson."
Correct thinking: The evidence must be connected to the historical relationship it proves: In the Middle East, U.S. policy involved oil, Israel and Arab states, the Persian Gulf, terrorism, military action, and diplomacy.
Teachable correction: Do not treat all regions as the same. The same policy tool, such as aid or military action, can have different causes and consequences depending on the region. The fix is to pair the evidence with a cause, consequence, comparison, or policy impact from this exact lesson.
