Worked Example 1: Reading the standard

Problem: A student is studying Immigration Policy and Migration Since 1950 and needs to explain this evidence detail: Push factors include violence, poverty, political instability, disaster, and limited opportunity

Worked Model: Begin by naming the historical setting, because the same word can mean different things in different eras. In this lesson, the important detail is Push factors include violence, poverty, political instability, disaster, and limited opportunity. Do not stop at naming it; ask what problem, decision, or conflict this detail helps explain.

Student Think-Aloud: The detail points toward this relationship: Pull factors include jobs, family networks, safety, education, and political or religious freedom. That tells you the answer needs cause-and-effect reasoning, not just a definition.

Model Answer: Push factors include violence, poverty, political instability, disaster, and limited opportunity. This matters because it shows this relationship: Pull factors include jobs, family networks, safety, education, and political or religious freedom. The answer is strong because it uses a specific detail, explains the historical relationship, and stays within Immigration Policy and Migration Since 1950 instead of drifting into another lesson.

Incorrect shortcut: "Push factors include violence, is enough because I remember seeing it in the lesson."
Correct thinking: The evidence must be connected to the historical relationship it proves: Pull factors include jobs, family networks, safety, education, and political or religious freedom.
Teachable correction: Do not confuse migration patterns with immigration policy. Patterns describe movement; policy describes rules and government decisions. The fix is to pair the evidence with a cause, consequence, comparison, or policy impact from this exact lesson.
