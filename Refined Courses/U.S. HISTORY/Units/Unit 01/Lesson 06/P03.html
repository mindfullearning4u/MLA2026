Evidence Method

For every source identify actor/institution | geographic issue | goal or claim | consequence | limitation.

Required Source Set

  1. Indigenous speech, petition, testimony, oral history, or tribal document.
  2. Dawes Act or federal report.
  3. Homestead, settler, or railroad-promotion source.
  4. Railroad/migration map.
  5. Indigenous land/reservation map.
  6. Authoritative secondary interpretation.

Remember: one leader cannot represent every nation; promotions may exaggerate; federal reports reflect policy goals; maps simplify; historians may differ in emphasis.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 1

Complete the six remaining evidence rows using the worked example. Include a limitation in every row.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 2

Compare one Indigenous source with one federal source: perspective, goal, geographic issue, and point of conflict.

Notebook Summary — Evidence

Write this supplied summary: Treaties and federal documents show formal policy; Indigenous sources show sovereignty and perspective; settler sources show migration motives; maps reveal spatial patterns and territorial change. Corroboration is required because agreements do not prove compliance, maps simplify, and individual sources cannot represent every nation.
