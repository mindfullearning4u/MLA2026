U.S. HISTORY • UNIT 1 • SYNTHESIS

P05 • Guided Synthesis Investigation

Guided Question

How did federal power produce both expanded opportunities and major conflicts during Unit 1?

This page models the complete process. Copy only the Pause and Review and final summary.

CLAIM

Federal power transformed post–Civil War life by expanding constitutional rights and national economic networks while also producing conflict over enforcement, land, sovereignty, and corporate growth.

EVIDENCE 1 — Reconstruction / Constitution

The 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments ended slavery, defined citizenship/equal protection, and prohibited racial voting discrimination. African American political participation shows agency, while violence and disenfranchisement show contested implementation.

EVIDENCE 2 — Western policy

Federal land, treaty, reservation, railroad, and allotment policies accelerated settlement and markets. Indigenous-produced evidence demonstrates resistance, sovereignty claims, and cultural continuity amid dispossession.

EVIDENCE 3 — Industrial economy

Federal support for transportation and national markets helped industrial growth. Corporations created jobs and production, while worker and immigrant evidence reveals conflict over hours, safety, power, discrimination, and urban conditions.

REASONING + COUNTERPOINT

The same national authority could protect constitutional rights, organize land, and promote infrastructure; consequences depended on enforcement, policy design, and who held power. Counterpoint: state governments, corporations, communities, and individuals also shaped outcomes, so federal action was not the only cause.

Pause and Review — notebook task

Copy and answer: How does the counterpoint improve the claim?

Model: It limits an overstatement. Federal power was central, but outcomes resulted from interaction with state authority, business, workers, African American agency, Indigenous resistance, migration, and local institutions.

Notebook Summary — copy this

The guided synthesis used evidence from Reconstruction, western expansion, and industrialization to trace one cross-unit theme. A strong synthesis claim explains both opportunity and conflict, treats affected groups as actors, connects evidence through reasoning, and includes a counterpoint that sets accurate limits.
