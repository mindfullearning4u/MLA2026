U.S. HISTORY • UNIT 1 • SYNTHESIS

P04 • Integrated Historical Thinking

Eight-step process

  1. Identify a significant development.
  2. Establish context.
  3. Explain causes.
  4. Explain consequences.
  5. Identify continuity and change.
  6. Compare with another development.
  7. Determine significance using scope, depth, duration, and legacy.
  8. Construct Claim-Evidence-Reasoning.

Worked synthesis: westward expansion + industrialization

MoveModel reasoning
ContextAfter the Civil War, federal policy encouraged western settlement while rail and corporate networks expanded.
Cause/effectLand policy and rail construction increased migration and markets; settlement and industry then increased demand for transportation and resources.
Change/continuityProduction, movement, and land use changed rapidly; Indigenous sovereignty, regional difference, and unequal opportunity remained contested.
ComparisonBoth developments expanded economic opportunity for some, but industrialization reorganized labor and cities while western policy transformed land and sovereignty.
SignificanceTheir wide geographic scope, long duration, and continuing effects make their interaction historically significant.
LimitationA railroad map shows networks but not motives, working conditions, or Indigenous experience.

Pause and Review — notebook task

Copy and answer: What turns an important event into a historical-significance argument?

Model: The historian applies criteria—how many people and places were affected, how deeply, for how long, and with what legacy—then compares alternatives using evidence.

Notebook Summary — copy this

Integrated synthesis combines context, causation, consequence, continuity/change, comparison, significance, and CER. Each reasoning move answers a different question; together they defend why a development mattered more than an alternative without ignoring affected groups or evidence limits.
