Guided question

How did corporate growth, labor, immigration, and urbanization interact?

This page models every thinking step. You do not create a separate response; copy only the Pause and Review and final summary.

Step 1 — Build the causal chain

Technology + capital + resources + transportation + communication → larger production and national markets → corporate growth → labor demand → migration and immigration → urban growth → workplace and social conflict.

Why: Arrows explain mechanisms, not just chronology.

Step 4 — Use quantitative evidence

Trend: Urban population rises during industrial expansion. Inference: Industrial jobs, immigration, internal migration, and transportation contributed to city growth. Limitation: A population table does not isolate each cause or describe daily life.

Step 5 — Construct CER

Claim: Industrialization transformed society most through an interacting system rather than one isolated consequence.

Evidence: Rail networks linked production and markets; corporations organized capital; worker sources reveal conflicts; immigrant comparisons show regional opportunity and discrimination; urban data show city growth.

Reasoning: Business expansion created labor demand, people moved toward jobs, and growing cities generated both community institutions and housing/infrastructure pressures.
