Worked Example 1: Reading the standard

Problem: A student is studying Native American Experience and Western Regions and needs to explain this evidence detail: Great Plains pressure increased as railroads, mining, ranching, and farming expanded into Native homelands

Worked Model: Begin by naming the historical setting, because the same word can mean different things in different eras. In this lesson, the important detail is Great Plains pressure increased as railroads, mining, ranching, and farming expanded into Native homelands. Do not stop at naming it; ask what problem, decision, or conflict this detail helps explain.

Student Think-Aloud: The detail points toward this relationship: Federal policy used treaties, reservations, military force, allotment, and assimilation programs such as boarding schools. That tells you the answer needs cause-and-effect reasoning, not just a definition.

This matters because it shows this relationship: Federal policy used treaties, reservations, military force, allotment, and assimilation programs such as boarding schools. The answer is strong because it uses a specific detail, explains the historical relationship, and stays within Native American Experience and Western Regions instead of drifting into another lesson.

Incorrect shortcut: "Great Plains pressure increased is enough because I remember seeing it in the lesson."
Correct thinking: The evidence must be connected to the historical relationship it proves: Federal policy used treaties, reservations, military force, allotment, and assimilation programs such as boarding schools.
Teachable correction: Do not describe Native Americans as one identical group. Different nations had different locations, cultures, leaders, and responses. The fix is to pair the evidence with a cause, consequence, comparison, or policy impact from this exact lesson.
