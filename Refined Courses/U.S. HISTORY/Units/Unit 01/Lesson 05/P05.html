P05 — Guided Investigation

Guided Question

Which combination best explains why Reconstruction-era rights became difficult to enforce? Watch the full reasoning process. Do not complete a separate task on this page.

Step 1 — Define the Outcome

Outcome: constitutional rights remained, but voting, safety, equality, and political participation became increasingly restricted in practice.

Step 2 — Evaluate Three Causes

Cause Evidence Right/Consequence Significance
Political violence Intimidation targeted voters and officeholders Voting and representation declined High: changed behavior and political power
Reduced federal enforcement Commitment weakened after earlier Enforcement Acts Protection became less reliable High: allowed other restrictions to operate
Voting restrictions Poll taxes, literacy tests, registration barriers Fifteenth Amendment implementation weakened High: institutionalized exclusion

Step 3 — Explain Interaction

Violence discouraged participation; weakened enforcement reduced protection; state barriers then made exclusion routine. Each factor increased the power of the others.

Step 4 — Consider an Alternative

Alternative: economic dependency was most important because debt restricted independence. Response: economic dependency mattered, but it became more politically damaging when violence, weak protection, and legal barriers operated together.

Step 5 — Model CER

Claim: Violence, reduced enforcement, and voting restrictions formed the strongest combination. Evidence: intimidation targeted political participation, enforcement weakened, and formal barriers restricted registration and voting. Reasoning: the causes reinforced one another, turning constitutional promises into rights that were difficult to exercise.
