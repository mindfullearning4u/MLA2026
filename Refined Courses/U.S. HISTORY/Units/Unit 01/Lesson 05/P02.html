Term Definition
Jim Crow Laws, policies, practices, and customs enforcing racial segregation and inequality.
Disenfranchisement Removal or restriction of voting rights.
Segregation Separation by race in institutions, facilities, or public life.
Ku Klux Klan White supremacist organization using violence and intimidation against Reconstruction rights.
Enforcement Acts Federal laws protecting voting and suppressing organized political violence.
Compromise of 1877 Political settlement associated with resolving the disputed 1876 election and ending remaining federal intervention in Southern Reconstruction governments.
Sharecropping Farming another person's land and paying rent with a crop share.
Debt peonage Debt used to bind workers to labor or restrict economic freedom.
Poll tax Tax required as a condition for voting.
Literacy test Reading or interpretation requirement used as a voting barrier.
Grandfather clause Exemption designed to protect some voters while excluding many Black voters.

Nadir Period after Reconstruction when Black civil and political protections deteriorated severely.
Political intimidation Threats, coercion, or violence used to influence political behavior.

Part 1 — Political Resistance and Violence

The Klan and related groups used terror to reduce Black voting, attack officeholders and white Reconstruction supporters, and destroy Republican organization. The Enforcement Acts show a federal response, but enforcement varied and commitment later weakened.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 2

Explain the political purpose of intimidation in two substantive sentences.

Part 2 — 1876–1877 Transition

The disputed Hayes–Tilden election produced a political settlement associated with withdrawal of remaining troops from Southern statehouses. It was not one signed treaty and not the only cause of Reconstruction’s decline.

Part 3 — Economic Dependency

Sharecropping offered legal freedom and sometimes family autonomy, but lack of land, credit systems, crop liens, and debt could create dependency. Debt peonage used debt and coercion to restrict mobility. Neither system was identical to chattel slavery.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 3

Create the cycle: no land → borrow supplies → grow crop → crop sold → debt calculated → debt remains → dependence continues. Then write one sentence explaining why outcomes varied.

Part 4 — Black Codes, Jim Crow, and Voting

Black Codes were early postwar limits on freedpeople’s autonomy. Jim Crow was a later, broader legal and social system of segregation and inequality. Poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses, registration barriers, intimidation, and violence restricted voting while the Fifteenth Amendment remained in force.

Part 5 — Plessy and the Nadir

Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) did not create segregation; it gave major Supreme Court support to state-mandated segregation. The Nadir describes severe deterioration in rights, not the disappearance of African American agency. Organizing, journalism, churches, education, litigation, institutions, and migration continued.

Notebook Summary — Historical Content

Write this supplied summary: Reconstruction rights were weakened through interacting political violence, declining federal enforcement, economic dependency, voting barriers, segregation laws, and court support. Black Codes and Jim Crow were related but distinct. The Reconstruction Amendments remained constitutional law even when implementation was restricted, and African Americans continued resistance and institution-building.
