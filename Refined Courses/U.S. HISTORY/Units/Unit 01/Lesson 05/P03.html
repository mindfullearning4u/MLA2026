P03 — Historical Evidence Lab

Evidence Process

For every source identify: actor/institution | action or policy | right affected | cause or consequence | source limitation.

Worked Example — Violence Testimony

Actor Action Right Cause/Consequence Limitation
Black voter giving testimony Reports intimidation Voting and safety Violence discourages participation and weakens political organization One experience does not represent every community

Source Set

  1. Enforcement Act: federal purpose, protected right, enforcement authority; law shows intent, not success.
  2. Sharecropping contract: responsibilities, payment, credit/debt; formal terms may hide unequal power.
  3. Voting restriction: compare poll tax, literacy, or registration wording with the Fifteenth Amendment.
  4. Plessy: compare majority reasoning with Harlan’s dissent; opinions show legal reasoning, not lived experience.
  5. African American resistance: newspaper, petition, speech, or legal argument; advocacy has a purpose.
  6. Secondary interpretation: historian’s explanation; scholars may debate timing and relative causes.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 1

Complete the evidence table for the six remaining sources. Use the worked example as your model and include a limitation in every row.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 2

Write three sentences comparing what a federal law can prove, what a labor contract can prove, and what testimony can prove.

Notebook Summary — Evidence Lab

Write this supplied summary: Different sources answer different questions. Testimony reveals experience, laws and court opinions reveal formal policy and legal reasoning, contracts show stated economic terms, resistance sources reveal agency, and historians synthesize patterns. Corroboration is necessary because no source alone proves implementation or represents every community.
