Unit 1 • Lesson 5

P04 — Historical Thinking: Cause and Effect

Six-Step Process

  1. Name the outcome: Reconstruction-era rights became difficult to enforce.
  2. Sort causes into political, legal, economic, violent/social, and institutional categories.
  3. Attach evidence to each cause.
  4. Explain interactions—how one cause strengthened another.
  5. Separate causes from consequences.
  6. Evaluate relative significance.

Worked Multi-Cause Model

Cause Evidence Interaction
Violence Intimidation of voters and officeholders Reduced participation helped opponents gain power.
Federal enforcement Enforcement Acts followed by declining commitment Weak enforcement made intimidation more effective.
Economic Debt and landlessness Dependency made political and labor choices riskier.
Legal Voting barriers and Plessy Law institutionalized restrictions and segregation.

Model Reasoning

The decline cannot be explained by one election or law. Violence reduced participation while dependency limited independence. Declining federal commitment allowed restrictions to spread, and state laws and court decisions supported exclusion. These forces reinforced one another.

Pause and Review — Notebook Task 1

Complete a five-category cause organizer. For each category write one cause, one evidence detail, and one interaction.

Correct the Misconceptions

  • 1877 was part of a longer multi-cause process.
  • Sharecropping was legally distinct from slavery but could be coercive.
  • The Fifteenth Amendment remained in force.
  • Plessy supported existing segregation; it did not originate all segregation.
  • African American resistance continued.

Notebook Summary — Cause and Effect

Write this supplied summary: Historians explain major outcomes through multiple interacting causes. The process is to define the outcome, categorize causes, attach evidence, explain interactions, separate causes from consequences, and rank significance. This avoids an inaccurate one-cause explanation.
