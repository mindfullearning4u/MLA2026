Worked Example 1: Reading the standard

Problem: A student is studying Vietnam, Watergate, and Public Trust and needs to explain this evidence detail: Vietnam divided Americans over containment, escalation, the draft, casualties, protest, and whether the war was justified

Worked Model: Begin by naming the historical setting, because the same word can mean different things in different eras. In this lesson, the important detail is Vietnam divided Americans over containment, escalation, the draft, casualties, protest, and whether the war was justified. Do not stop at naming it; ask what problem, decision, or conflict this detail helps explain.

Student Think-Aloud: The detail points toward this relationship: Television and journalism brought war images, official claims, and public criticism into American homes. That tells you the answer needs cause-and-effect reasoning, not just a definition.

Model Answer: Vietnam divided Americans over containment, escalation, the draft, casualties, protest, and whether the war was justified. This matters because it shows this relationship: Television and journalism brought war images, official claims, and public criticism into American homes. The answer is strong because it uses a specific detail, explains the historical relationship, and stays within Vietnam, Watergate, and Public Trust instead of drifting into another lesson.

Incorrect shortcut: "Vietnam divided Americans over is enough because I remember seeing it in the lesson."
Correct thinking: The evidence must be connected to the historical relationship it proves: Television and journalism brought war images, official claims, and public criticism into American homes.
Teachable correction: Do not combine Vietnam and Watergate as the same issue. They affected trust in different but connected ways. The fix is to pair the evidence with a cause, consequence, comparison, or policy impact from this exact lesson.
