P04 • Historical Thinking: Significance

Progressive Reform and the Expansion of Democracy

Eight-step historical-significance process

  1. Identify the reform.
  2. Determine its problem.
  3. Measure scale.
  4. Measure degree of change.
  5. Evaluate duration.
  6. Evaluate institutional or constitutional impact.
  7. Identify included and excluded groups.
  8. Reach an evidence-based significance judgment.
Criterion Worked Nineteenth Amendment analysis
Scale Changed formal voting-rights protection nationwide.
Degree/institution Constitutional amendment prohibited denial or abridgment on account of sex.
Duration/precedent Enduring constitutional rule and foundation for later political participation.
Included/excluded Expanded the formal electorate, but racial discrimination and other barriers still blocked many women.
Judgment Highly significant constitutional change whose implementation and benefits were unequal.
Correct misconceptions: Progressivism was not unified; journalists did not personally pass laws; the Nineteenth Amendment did not create equal practical access for every woman; Progressives did not uniformly support racial equality; regulation did not eliminate large business.

Pause and Review — notebook

Why must “who remained excluded?” be a significance criterion? Model: Scale and law can exaggerate change if access was unequal. Inclusion evidence reveals the actual reach and limits of reform.

Historical Thinking Summary — copy

Historical significance is a criteria-based judgment, not a synonym for “important.” It measures scale, change, duration, institutional impact, precedent, and the distribution of benefits and exclusions.
