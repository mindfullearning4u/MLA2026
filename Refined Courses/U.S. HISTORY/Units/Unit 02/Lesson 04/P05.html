P05 • Guided Investigation

U.S. Imperialism and the Spanish-American War

Guided Question

How do historians decide which cause mattered most when factors interacted?

Copy only the Pause and Review and summary.

Factor Evidence of contribution Limitation
Economic interests Trade/market links encouraged overseas attention. Cannot explain humanitarian rhetoric or exact timing alone.
Naval strategy Mahan, bases, routes, two-ocean geography. Strategy is an argument, not automatic policy.
Expansionist ideology Mission/prestige/racial rhetoric justified power. Belief was contested and historically biased.
Cuban crisis Rebellion, colonial violence, U.S. interests/concern. Cuban agency must not be reduced to U.S. intervention.
Journalism/public opinion Sensational coverage intensified attention. Opinion was varied; newspapers did not make the final decision.
National prestige Power competition encouraged global role. Anti-imperialists challenged costs and constitutional conflict.

Model reasoning

Define “strongest” as explanatory reach, evidence, timing, and interaction. A top factor should explain several decisions and connect to other pressures. Then test an alternative interpretation rather than dismissing it.

Pause and Review — notebook

How could naval strategy and economic interests reinforce each other? Model: Markets increased interest in routes; bases and naval power protected access. Each made the other more politically persuasive.

Guided Investigation Summary — copy

A defensible causal judgment applies the same criteria to every factor, explains interaction, distinguishes evidence from rhetoric, and answers a plausible alternative.
