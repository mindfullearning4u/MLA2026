P03 • Historical Evidence Lab

Evidence-reading process

  1. Identify actor and source type.
  2. Extract a detail about an urban or immigration issue.
  3. Explain what changed and what continued.
  4. Identify perspective.
  5. State a limitation before making a claim.

Worked examples

Evidence Issue Changed Continued Perspective Limitation
Immigrant letter describing work and community aid Migration and settlement New city work and institutions Family/community reliance Individual immigrant One account cannot represent every immigrant.
Tenement photograph Housing Dense urban living became more visible Class inequality Photographer’s selected frame One location and moment; conditions varied.
Political cartoon criticizing a machine Urban politics Machines adapted to growing electorates Political competition and corruption Persuasive critic Cartoons exaggerate and do not neutrally measure services.
Urban census table Population Rapid city growth Regional differences Government categories Trend does not identify every cause or lived experience.

Pause and Review — notebook task

Copy and answer: Why should a historian pair immigrant testimony with quantitative data?

Model: Testimony explains lived experience and perspective; data establishes scale and trend. Neither alone proves the entire urban story.

Notebook Summary — copy this

A strong evidence lab compares immigrant accounts, photographs, machine-politics evidence, nativist arguments, census data, a second regional immigrant perspective, and a secondary interpretation. Source, detail, change, continuity, perspective, and limitation keep conclusions specific and prevent stereotypes.
