U.S. HISTORY • UNIT 2 • LESSON 1

Immigration, Urban Life, and the Gilded Age

Investigation Question

How did immigration, urbanization, political machines, and social inequality reshape American cities during the Gilded Age?

What this lesson does

Unit 1 explained industrial growth. This lesson shifts to people, cities, community, politics, and social change. You will study rapid change alongside continuities in inequality, prejudice, community reliance, and political competition.

Learn Think like a historian
Gilded Age label, urban growth, immigration and city geography Identify earlier and later conditions
Ethnic communities, tenements, infrastructure, social class Use maps, photographs, and data with limitations
Political machines, Tammany Hall, patronage, corruption Evaluate both services and political control
European-East and Asian-West experiences; nativism Compare without treating either group as uniform

Notebook on this page

Copy the investigation question and this overview: Industrial jobs, migration, transportation, and population growth reshaped cities. Immigrant communities built institutions, city governments faced infrastructure pressure, and political machines mixed services with patronage and corruption. Historians measure change while identifying inequality and prejudice that continued.
