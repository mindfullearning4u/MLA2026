Labor Movements, Farmers, Populism, and Economic Reform

How historians test a source

  1. Identify the group and problem.
  2. State the proposed solution or claim.
  3. Quote or paraphrase one exact detail.
  4. Explain what the detail proves.
  5. Name what the source cannot prove alone.
Evidence Group / problem Claim, evidence, limitation — worked guidance
A. Union statement Workers; wages/hours/conditions Claim: collective action improves leverage. Evidence: stated bargaining or eight-hour goal. Limit: advocates worker interests.
B. Worker/strike account Participant; conflict Reveals experience and grievance; compare employer, government, and newspaper accounts because one participant cannot represent all actors.
C. Employer argument Business; property, contract, production, order Explains resistance to strikes; it defends business priorities and cannot alone establish workers’ conditions.
D. Farmer/Alliance source Farmers; debt, rates, credit Connects economic pressure to cooperation/reform; does not represent every farmer or region.
E. Omaha Platform People’s Party; concentrated power Match grievance to proposal and reasoning; a platform states goals, not implementation.
F. Farm data Economic trend 3–2–1: record 3 observations, 2 defensible inferences, 1 limitation. Statistics show trend but not cause alone.
G. Cross of Gold rhetoric Bryan/free-silver supporters Analyze audience, imagery, and persuasive purpose; campaign rhetoric is not neutral description.
H. Secondary interpretation Historian Compare its labor/Populist judgment with primary evidence; later historians may disagree.

Pause and Review — notebook

Choose row E and explain why a platform requires corroboration. Example: The Omaha Platform proves what Populists demanded, but election returns, laws, and implementation records are needed to determine public support and results.

Evidence Lab Summary — copy

Perspective shapes what a source emphasizes. Corroboration combines advocates, opponents, participants, public records, data, rhetoric, and later interpretation so a claim is not controlled by one viewpoint. Quantitative evidence identifies patterns but must be joined to contextual evidence before explaining cause.
