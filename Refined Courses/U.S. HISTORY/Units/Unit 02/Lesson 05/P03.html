P03 • Historical Evidence Lab

Panama, Roosevelt, and an Expanding U.S. Global Role

Evidence routine

  1. Source the actor, date, audience, and purpose.
  2. Identify the geographic or diplomatic goal.
  3. Connect the claim to U.S. interests.
  4. Ask how regional sovereignty was affected.
  5. State an inference and limitation.
Evidence Worked use Limitation
A Roosevelt/Panama source Reveals presidential strategic reasoning and justification. Advocacy does not represent every motive or Panamanian view.
B Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty Establishes extensive U.S. canal rights and legal terms. Terms do not represent all affected people.
C Panamanian/Latin American source Centers a regional view of separation, intervention, or sovereignty. One source is not an entire region.
D Roosevelt Corollary Shows claimed intervention authority beyond the Monroe Doctrine. States policy justification, not every implementation.
E Canal map Shows isthmus, oceans, routes, and strategic movement. Maps simplify political influence.
F Canal labor source Restores Caribbean worker experience and unequal status. One image or testimony cannot represent all workers.
G Taft/Wilson source Tests continuity and change after Roosevelt. Policy labels simplify complex practice.
H Historian Interprets strategic necessity and imperial controversy. Historians weigh causes differently.

Pause and Review — notebook

Why pair the treaty with a Panamanian source? Model: The treaty establishes formal rights; the regional source reveals whose sovereignty and consent were affected.

Evidence Lab Summary — copy

Strong analysis combines law, presidential rhetoric, maps, labor experience, regional perspectives, policy comparison, and later interpretation instead of treating U.S. policy as the only viewpoint.
