Panama, Roosevelt, and an Expanding U.S. Global Role

Guided Question

How do historians evaluate why Panama became central to U.S. policy?

Copy only the Pause and Review and summary.

Factor Evidence and geographic link Consequence/limit
Trade Shorter connection improved shipping and access. Commerce alone cannot explain military control.
Naval mobility Warships could transfer between oceans. Required construction and secure access.
Security Caribbean-Pacific linkage protected expanding interests. Security claims could justify intervention.
Regional influence Canal Zone and Corollary expanded U.S. leverage. Reduced or threatened local sovereignty.
Global prestige Completion displayed technological and geopolitical power. Prestige is difficult to measure alone.
Geographic location The isthmus made all other goals possible. Location alone could not build or govern the canal.

Pause and Review — notebook

How did geography and diplomacy interact? Model: Geography made the route valuable; diplomacy and intervention secured political control needed to build and use it.

Guided Investigation Summary — copy

A strong judgment applies the same criteria to all six factors, connects each to spatial evidence, evaluates consequences, and explains interaction rather than choosing a factor by preference.
