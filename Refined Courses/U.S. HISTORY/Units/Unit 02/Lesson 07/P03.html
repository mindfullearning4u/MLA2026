P03 • Historical Evidence Lab

World War I at Home and the Postwar Settlement

Evidence routine

  1. Source creator, context, audience, and purpose.
  2. Identify wartime or treaty position.
  3. Find evidence of continuity/change or competing experience.
  4. Corroborate with a different source type.
  5. State usefulness and limitation.
Evidence Worked use Limitation
A Mobilization record/data Measures military, production, or finance growth. Numbers require context.
B CPI poster Reveals persuasion, audience, and emotional technique. Propaganda is not neutral public opinion.
C Espionage/Sedition text Establishes legal restriction. Text alone does not show enforcement.
D Dissent source Explains antiwar or civil-liberties reasoning. One constituency is not all dissent.
E African American/migration source Connects labor demand, mobility, service, and discrimination. One person/place is not universal.
F Women/diverse-group source Tests opportunity and restriction across groups. Experiences differed by race, class, region, and role.
G Wilson/Fourteen Points Shows stated peace and League goals. Advocacy differs from negotiated treaty terms.
H Lodge/Senate source Shows reservation, sovereignty, and constitutional objections. Does not represent every opponent.

Pause and Review — notebook

Why pair a law with dissent testimony? Model: The law establishes authority; testimony shows how restriction was experienced and contested.

Evidence Lab Summary — copy

Corroboration combines law, data, propaganda, affected groups, presidential advocacy, and Senate opposition to explain both change and continuing conflict.
