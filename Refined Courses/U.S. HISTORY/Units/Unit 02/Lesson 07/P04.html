P04 • Historical Thinking: Continuity and Change

World War I at Home and the Postwar Settlement

Process

  1. Choose a baseline before wartime mobilization.
  2. Identify the wartime development.
  3. Measure the degree and pace of change.
  4. Identify what continued.
  5. Compare effects across groups.
  6. Trace an immediate and longer-term consequence.
  7. Support the judgment with qualitative and quantitative evidence.
Area Change Continuity
Federal power More coordination of economy and information Constitutional conflicts persisted.
African American life Migration and new work/community patterns Racism and discrimination persisted.
Women's roles Expanded wartime participation Unequal status persisted.
Civil liberties Wartime restrictions intensified Dissent and rights debate continued.
Foreign policy Expanded involvement and peace leadership Domestic limits constrained commitments.
Correct misconceptions: propaganda was not objective information; mobilization affected groups unevenly; armistice and treaty differ; the treaty did not duplicate the Fourteen Points; the U.S. did not join the League.

Pause and Review — notebook

How can change and continuity coexist? Model: African American migration changed location and opportunity while segregation and racial violence continued.

Historical Thinking Summary — copy

Continuity/change analysis defines a baseline, measures degree and duration, compares groups, and explains why a major transformation can coexist with persistent institutions and inequality.
