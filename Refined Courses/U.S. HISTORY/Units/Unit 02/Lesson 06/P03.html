P03 • Historical Evidence Lab

World War I: Causes, Neutrality, and U.S. Entry

Evidence routine

  1. Source actor, date, audience, and purpose.
  2. Identify the position or development.
  3. Classify its cause category.
  4. Explain its effect on neutrality.
  5. State an inference and limitation.
Evidence Worked use Limitation
A Wilson neutrality source Explains official 1914 policy and rhetoric. Presidential advocacy is not all public opinion.
B Lusitania/submarine source Shows shipping danger and diplomatic tension. One event did not produce entry in 1915.
C Trade/finance data Shows growing economic connections. Trend cannot alone prove motive.
D Zimmermann Telegram Shows a German proposal affecting security perceptions. Mexico did not accept or implement it.
E Preparedness source Advocates readiness/intervention. Represents a constituency, not consensus.
F Neutrality/antiwar source Shows risks and principles supporting nonentry. Does not represent every opponent.
G Wilson war message States submarine, rights, order, and democracy reasoning. Justifies policy and may omit other interests.
H Historian Weighs the multi-causal path to war. Interpretations rank factors differently.

Pause and Review — notebook

Why pair trade data with competing speeches? Model: Data shows material connections; speeches reveal how actors interpreted interests and risks.

Evidence Lab Summary — copy

Chronological source sets prevent one-event explanations and require evidence from policy, war developments, economics, diplomacy, and competing perspectives.
