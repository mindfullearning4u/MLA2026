Core Content Mini-Lesson

Immigration and internal migration changed cities, labor markets, neighborhoods, politics, culture, and reform needs. Push factors drove people away; pull factors attracted them to new places.

Teaching moveWhat you should do
Move 1Southern and Eastern European immigrants often entered through Ellis Island, while many Asian immigrants encountered Angel Island and exclusion laws on the West Coast.
Move 2Push factors included poverty, persecution, famine, political unrest, and limited opportunity; pull factors included jobs, family networks, religious freedom, and political opportunity.
Move 3Urban settlement patterns included ethnic neighborhoods, tenements, settlement houses such as Hull House, and political machines that traded services for votes.
Move 4Nativism and restriction efforts grew as some Americans blamed immigrants for job competition, cultural change, religious difference, or urban problems.
