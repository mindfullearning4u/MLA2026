# U.S. History Course Overview

Course: United States History
Course number: 2100310
Credits: 1.0
Structure: 6 units, 8 lessons per unit
Production format: Moodle HTML lessons and Moodle XML assessments
Official standards source: CPALMS United States History (#2100310), 2024-2026 version, retrieved 2026-07-10
Official source URL: https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewCourse/Preview/22636

## Course Purpose

U.S. History develops students' ability to explain United States historical change from Reconstruction to the present using evidence, chronology, geography, economics, political decision-making, primary and secondary sources, maps, timelines, graphs, charts, photographs, political cartoons, and civic/historical reasoning. Students review essential pre-Reconstruction foundations only when needed to understand Reconstruction and later developments.

## Mastery Model

Each lesson is self-contained and written for asynchronous learning. The lesson pages provide the teaching. Students use the Teacher of Record for support, checkpoint feedback, intervention, retake workflow, and clarification when needed.

Lessons must not assume a live teacher is teaching. Every page must anticipate confusion, define terms in context, model evidence use, and show students exactly how to move from source/stimulus to historical claim.

## Unit Sequence

1. Historical Thinking, Civil War Review, Reconstruction, and the Nadir - Build source analysis, chronology, historiography, Reconstruction foundations, constitutional amendments, Jim Crow, Black Codes, sharecropping, and Native American experience.
2. Industrialization, Immigration, Urbanization, Labor, and Progressive Reform - Study economic change, farmers, industrialization, immigration, urbanization, labor, political machines, reform, and Florida connections.
3. Imperialism, World War I, the 1920s, Civil Rights Currents, and the Great Depression - Analyze expansion, territorial acquisition, war preparation, World War I, treaty debates, demobilization, the Red Scare, Roaring Twenties, social tensions, and the Great Depression/New Deal.
4. World War II, Rights, International Justice, and Early Cold War - Examine World War II, the Holocaust, wartime rights, Japanese American internment, atomic weapons, Nuremberg, the United Nations, Red Scare, early Cold War, Korea, Vietnam, and Florida connections.
5. Postwar America, Civil Rights, Great Society, Vietnam, and Watergate - Study prosperity, inequality, women, 1960s presidencies, civil rights approaches, organizations, Supreme Court decisions, Great Society, Vietnam, Watergate, and social movements.
6. Contemporary United States, Global Economy, Terrorism, Immigration, Florida, and Historical Synthesis - Analyze late 20th and 21st century concerns, foreign policy, global economy, terrorism, immigration, Florida connections, public policy, and course synthesis.

## Assessment Model

Each unit includes a pretest, guided practice for every lesson, lesson quizzes for Lessons 1-7, and a unit assessment in Lesson 8. Moodle XML is the only production assessment format. Lesson 8 is synthesis and does not have a lesson quiz.

## Visual and Source Model

U.S. History requires direct visual/source support. Lessons and assessments must embed required maps, timelines, political cartoons, photographs, charts, graphs, excerpts, tables, constitutional text, court-case summaries, policy comparisons, or data displays when needed. Students must not be sent elsewhere to find a stimulus required for a lesson or question.

## Production Boundary

CPALMS U.S. History Social Studies standards define the content. Florida B.E.S.T. ELA expectations, ELD support, Common Core history/social studies literacy skills, SAT readiness, and ACT readiness support the evidence-reading and reasoning demands. Support alignments must not add off-standard lesson topics.
