# MLA Algebra II Unit Mapping Audit

## Executive Summary

This audit evaluates `mla_algebra_2_unit_mapping_v1.md` from the perspective of instructional sequencing only. Standards coverage, benchmark coverage, and crosswalk accuracy were not audited here.

The Algebra II sequence is fundamentally sound. It follows a defensible progression from advanced expressions and equations to polynomial functions, radical/rational relationships, exponential/logarithmic functions, systems and advanced function analysis, and finally data/modeling. The course does not treat Algebra II as Algebra I remediation, which is appropriate.

The mapping should pass with revisions. The main recommended improvements are to reduce cognitive load in Unit 3, adjust the quadratic-to-polynomial function progression in Unit 2, add more explicit prerequisite callouts, and reduce the instructional density of Unit 5.

**Final Determination:** PASS WITH REVISIONS

---

## Strengths

- The overall six-unit sequence reflects strong Algebra II instructional logic.
- Unit 1 appropriately functions as a readiness bridge rather than a full remediation unit.
- Complex numbers are placed after factoring and before higher-degree equations, which is instructionally sound.
- Polynomial division appears before higher-degree equation solving, supporting later zeros and roots work.
- Exponential functions are introduced before logarithms, which supports conceptual understanding.
- Composition appears before inverse functions, which is appropriate because inverse verification depends on composition.
- Lesson 8 in every unit is reserved for synthesis rather than new primary content.
- Advanced learner extensions are included in every unit.
- SAT and ACT readiness are addressed throughout the course through function analysis, equation solving, modeling, and data interpretation.

---

## Findings

## 1. Unit 1: Advanced Expressions and Equations

**Readiness Review:** Mostly sufficient, but the readiness lesson should explicitly call out graph interpretation, domain/range language, factoring review, and calculator/technology expectations. Algebra II students often fail later not because they cannot perform one isolated procedure, but because they cannot connect notation, graph behavior, and solution meaning.

**Complex Numbers Placement:** Appropriate. Complex numbers belong after factoring and before higher-degree equations because students need them to interpret non-real quadratic solutions and later polynomial roots.

**Higher-Degree Equations Placement:** Appropriate. Higher-degree equations come after operations, factoring, complex numbers, and polynomial division. This is a strong placement.

**Potential Issue:** Unit 1 moves quickly from readiness to polynomial operations, factoring, complex numbers, division, and higher-degree equations. This is acceptable for Algebra II, but struggling students will need clearly embedded review supports.

---

## 2. Unit 2: Polynomial Functions

**Zeros, Roots, Multiplicity, and Graphing:** The internal sequence is mostly logical: end behavior, zeros/roots, graphing. However, quadratic function modeling currently appears after general polynomial graphing. For many students, quadratics are the most concrete nonlinear function family and should be used as the bridge into broader polynomial behavior.

**Polynomial Modeling Placement:** Modeling is present through quadratic models and applications, but a distinct polynomial modeling lesson would improve clarity. Current Lesson 5 and Lesson 6 are quadratic-focused, while the unit title and purpose are broader polynomial functions.

**Potential Issue:** Students may encounter higher-degree polynomial graphing before revisiting quadratic key features in enough depth. This could increase cognitive load for struggling learners.

---

## 3. Unit 3: Radical and Rational Relationships

**Progression:** The progression from radical expressions to radical functions/equations, then rational expressions/equations/functions, is logical.

**Overloaded Lessons:** Lesson 4, Rational Expressions, is likely overloaded because it includes simplifying, multiplying, dividing, adding, and subtracting rational expressions in one lesson. Adding and subtracting rational expressions requires factoring, restrictions, common denominators, and careful symbolic manipulation. That is a high load for one lesson.

**Potential Issue:** Lesson 7 combines radical applications and rational applications. This is instructionally reasonable as a synthesis lesson, but struggling students may need separate checkpoints or scaffolded contexts because radical and rational modeling have different domain and interpretation issues.

---

## 4. Unit 4: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

**Logarithm Placement:** Appropriate. Logarithms appear after exponential growth, decay, and financial growth models. This supports the idea that logarithms answer exponential questions.

**Applications Placement:** Appropriate. Applications appear after exponential and logarithmic procedures are developed. Financial growth models in Lesson 3 are also well placed because they rely directly on exponential growth.

**Potential Issue:** Lesson 3 contains multiple financial standards and may become dense if it includes interest, savings, loans, amortization, and comparison of financial options at once. The lesson is appropriately placed, but pacing guidance should prevent overload.

---

## 5. Unit 5: Systems and Advanced Function Analysis

**Systems Placement:** Systems can remain in Unit 5. Moving systems earlier would support equation-solving continuity, but the current placement allows students to solve systems involving multiple function families, which is a valid Algebra II approach.

**Composition Before Inverses:** Appropriate. Students should understand function operations and composition before verifying or constructing inverse functions.

**Advanced Function Analysis Placement:** Appropriate as a synthesis lesson before Unit 6, but Unit 5 is the densest unit in the course.

**Potential Issue:** Unit 5 contains systems, systems of inequalities, nonlinear systems, transformations, function operations, composition, inverse functions, and advanced function analysis. This is a heavy conceptual load. The unit is instructionally defensible, but it needs stronger prerequisite supports and optional pacing flexibility.

---

## 6. Unit 6: Financial Models, Data, and Mathematical Modeling

**Financial Literacy Placement:** Financial literacy appears in Unit 4 as primary exponential modeling and again in Unit 6 as applied review. This is acceptable. Unit 6 Lesson 1 should remain clearly marked as application/review so it does not feel like a disconnected restart.

**Data Modeling Sequence:** Logical. Data modeling precedes regression, and regression precedes model comparison.

**Model Comparison Placement:** Appropriate. Model comparison belongs after students have learned polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, and regression models.

**Potential Issue:** Sequences and series appear as support-only readiness in Unit 6 Lesson 2. This is useful for precalculus readiness, but it may feel underdeveloped or disconnected unless framed as a modeling bridge rather than a full Algebra II strand.

## Prerequisite Skill Recommendations

The mapping already includes prerequisites, but several should be made more explicit in the lesson rows or instructional notes.

Recommended prerequisite callouts:

- **Before Unit 1 Lesson 2:** Distributive property, exponent rules, combining like terms, polynomial vocabulary.
- **Before Unit 1 Lesson 3:** Factoring review, greatest common factor, difference of squares, trinomial factoring, zero-product property.
- **Before Unit 1 Lesson 4:** Square roots, perfect squares, discriminant meaning, real versus non-real solutions.
- **Before Unit 2 Lesson 2:** Degree, leading coefficient, parent function behavior, graphing technology expectations.
- **Before Unit 2 Lesson 3:** x-intercepts, factors, roots, zeros, multiplicity language.
- **Before Unit 3 Lesson 1:** Rational exponent notation, square root simplification, exponent properties.
- **Before Unit 3 Lesson 4:** Factoring, restrictions, least common denominator, excluded values.
- **Before Unit 4 Lesson 1:** Percent change, growth factor, decay factor, repeated multiplication.
- **Before Unit 4 Lesson 4:** Inverse operations, exponential notation, graph reflection over `y = x`.
- **Before Unit 5 Lesson 1:** Solving systems from Algebra I, graphing intersections, substitution and elimination.
- **Before Unit 5 Lesson 4:** Parent functions, domain and range, transformations from Algebra I and Geometry coordinate reasoning.
- **Before Unit 5 Lesson 6:** Composition, one-to-one functions, domain restrictions.
- **Before Unit 6 Lesson 3:** Reading tables, scatterplots, residual thinking, technology tools for regression.

## Advanced Learner Recommendations

The mapping includes appropriate advanced learner extensions, but several could be sharpened so advanced students are not under-challenged.

Recommended extension additions:

- Add proof-style justification tasks for polynomial identities and complex roots.
- Add SAT non-calculator style polynomial and exponential questions.
- Add ACT timed mixed-practice sets for functions, systems, and rational expressions.
- Add Desmos or graphing-technology investigations for transformations, asymptotes, regression, and model comparison.
- Add error-analysis tasks involving extraneous radical/rational solutions.
- Add open-ended financial modeling comparing savings, loans, interest, and investment scenarios.
- Add inverse/composition challenge tasks involving restricted domains.
- Add model-selection tasks requiring students to defend why one function family fits a data set better than another.

Advanced students should not need different mastery expectations, but they should have opportunities for deeper abstraction, justification, and mixed-model reasoning.

## SAT Readiness Review

The sequence supports SAT readiness well overall.

Strengths:

- Advanced Math is strongly represented through polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic, systems, and function analysis work.
- Problem-Solving and Data Analysis is addressed through financial modeling, regression, and model comparison.
- Algebra readiness is reinforced through expressions, equations, and systems.

Recommended SAT improvements:

- Add SAT-style mixed function comparison tasks in Units 2, 4, 5, and 6.
- Include non-calculator-style manipulation practice in Units 1 and 3.
- Include data interpretation and model-selection practice earlier as short spirals before Unit 6.

---

## ACT Readiness Review

The sequence supports ACT readiness well overall.

Strengths:

- Intermediate Algebra and Functions are strongly represented.
- Coordinate Geometry is supported through graphing, systems, and intersections.
- Statistics and Probability readiness is addressed in Unit 6.

Recommended ACT improvements:

- Add timed mixed-practice opportunities for rational expressions, systems, and function transformations.
- Include ACT-style distractor analysis where students identify common algebraic errors.
- Spiral graph interpretation and domain/range questions throughout Units 2-5.

---

## College Readiness Review

The mapping supports College Algebra readiness. Students encounter the major Algebra II function families, equation types, function operations, inverses, regression, and modeling.

The mapping also supports Precalculus readiness, especially through polynomial behavior, rational functions, exponential/logarithmic functions, composition, inverses, and sequences as a support topic.

Recommended college-readiness improvements:

- Make domain/range and restrictions more explicit across Units 2-5.
- Add function-family comparison tasks before Unit 6 so model comparison does not appear only at the end.
- Include technology expectations for graphing, regression, and interpretation.
- Preserve sequences and series as an optional bridge to Precalculus, but do not allow it to overload Unit 6.

---

## Final Determination

**PASS WITH REVISIONS**

The Algebra II unit mapping is instructionally sound and appropriate for a mastery-based high school Algebra II course. It does not contain major sequencing flaws. Revisions are recommended to reduce cognitive load, strengthen prerequisite scaffolding, improve polynomial function progression, and provide clearer supports for both struggling and advanced learners.
