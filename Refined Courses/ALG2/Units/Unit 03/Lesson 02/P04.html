P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Study each example carefully. Each one shows a different way the lesson idea appears in Algebra II work.

Worked Example 1: Identify Domain

Problem: Find the domain of f(x) = √x - 4.

Step 1: The expression under an even root must be nonnegative.

Step 2: Set x - 4 ≥ 0.

Step 3: Solve the inequality.

Domain: x ≥ 4

Reasoning: The radicand controls the domain of a square-root function.

Worked Example 2: Find an Output

Problem: For f(x) = √x + 1, find f(8).

Step 1: Replace x with 8.

Step 2: Compute 8 + 1.

Step 3: Take the square root.

f(8) = 3

Reasoning: Function notation still means input-output evaluation.

Worked Example 3: Interpret a Starting Point

Problem: A radical graph starts at (2, 0). What does this tell you?

Step 1: The first x-value is 2.

Step 2: The first output is 0.

Step 3: Use the point to describe domain and range start.

Domain starts at x = 2 and range starts at y = 0.

Reasoning: Radical functions often have a starting point.

Visual support: A square root graph starts at its endpoint and then moves right.

Common Mistake

Problem: Find the domain of √x - 5.

Incorrect:

All real numbers

Why it is wrong: Inputs below 5 make the radicand negative.

Correct:

x ≥ 5

Fix: Set the radicand greater than or equal to 0.
