P04 Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Each one shows a different way the lesson idea appears in Algebra II work.

Worked Example 1: Solve a Radical Equation

Problem: Solve √x + 5 = 4.

Step 1: Square both sides to remove the square root.

Step 2: Solve x + 5 = 16.

Step 3: Check the solution in the original equation.

x = 11

Reasoning: Checking helps confirm the solution works.

Worked Example 2: Check for Extraneous Solutions

Problem: Solve √x - 1 = x - 3.

Step 1: Square both sides.

Step 2: Solve the resulting quadratic.

Step 3: Check each candidate in the original equation.

Only checked solutions are valid.

Reasoning: Squaring can create extraneous solutions.

Worked Example 3: Interpret a Radical Model

Problem: A model h = √t gives height units. What does t = 25 produce?

Step 1: Substitute t = 25.

Step 2: Compute √25.

Step 3: Interpret the output in context.

h = 5 units

Reasoning: The solution must be connected back to the situation.

Common Mistake

Problem: Solve √x = -3.

Incorrect:

x = 9

Why it is wrong: The principal square root cannot equal a negative number.

Correct:

No solution

Fix: Check whether the original equation is possible before accepting squared results.
