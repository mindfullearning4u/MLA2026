P04 Worked Examples

Work through each example slowly. The goal is not only to get an answer, but to understand why each step is valid and how the idea connects to the lesson standard.

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Worked Example 1: Factor a Greatest Common Factor

Problem: Factor 6x2 + 9x.

Step 1: Find the greatest common factor of 6x2 and 9x.

Step 2: Factor 3x out of both terms.

Step 3: Check by distributing.

3x(2x + 3)

Reasoning: Factoring rewrites a sum as a product without changing its value.

Worked Example 2: Factor a Trinomial

Problem: Factor x2 + 7x + 12.

Step 1: Find two numbers that multiply to 12.

Step 2: The same two numbers must add to 7.

Step 3: Use 3 and 4 as binomial constants.

(x + 3)(x + 4)

Reasoning: The factors show the structure that creates the middle term.

Worked Example 3: Use Difference of Squares

Problem: Factor x2 - 25.

Step 1: Recognize two square terms.

Step 2: Use a2 - b2 = (a - b)(a + b).

Step 3: Write the matching binomials.

(x - 5)(x + 5)

Reasoning: Difference of squares works only for subtraction between square terms.

Common Mistake

Problem: Factor x2 + 9.

Incorrect:

(x + 3)(x - 3)

Why it is wrong: That product equals x2 - 9, not x2 + 9.

Correct:

x2 + 9 is not factorable over the real numbers.

Fix: Check the sign and verify by multiplying the factors back together.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it uses the lesson process accurately and connects the answer back to the mathematical structure or context.
