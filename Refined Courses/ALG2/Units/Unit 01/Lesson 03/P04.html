P04 Concept Development and Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Factor by GCF, Then Pattern

Problem: Factor 3x3 - 12x.

Step 1: Factor out the GCF.

3x3 - 12x = 3x(x2 - 4)

Step 2: Factor the difference of squares.

3x(x2 - 4) = 3x(x - 2)(x + 2)

Answer: 3x(x - 2)(x + 2)

Example 2: Factor a Trinomial with Negative Signs

Problem: Factor x2 - 9x + 20.

Step 1: Find two numbers that multiply to 20 and add to -9.

-4 and -5 multiply to 20 and add to -9.

Step 2: Write the factors.

x2 - 9x + 20 = (x - 4)(x - 5)

Example 3: Verify Equivalent Forms

Problem: Verify that (x + 2)(x + 6) is equivalent to x2 + 8x + 12.

Multiply the factors:

(x + 2)(x + 6) = x2 + 6x + 2x + 12 = x2 + 8x + 12

Conclusion: The forms are equivalent because multiplying the factors gives the original polynomial.

Common Mistake

A common factoring mistake is choosing factor pairs that multiply correctly but do not add to the middle coefficient.

Problem: Factor x2 + 11x + 24.

Incorrect:

x2 + 11x + 24 = (x + 4)(x + 6)

Correct:

x2 + 11x + 24 = (x + 3)(x + 8)

Why: 3 and 8 multiply to 24 and add to 11.
