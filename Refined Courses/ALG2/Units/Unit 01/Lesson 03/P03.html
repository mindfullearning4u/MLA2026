Difference of Squares

Pattern: a2 - b2 = (a - b)(a + b)

Example: Factor x2 - 49.

x2 - 49 = x2 - 72

x2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)

Answer: (x - 7)(x + 7)

Factoring Trinomials

Example: Factor x2 + 7x + 12.

Step 1: Find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7.

3 and 4 multiply to 12 and add to 7.

Step 2: Write the factors.

x2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)

Sign Patterns in Trinomials

  • If the constant is positive and the middle term is positive, both binomial signs are positive.
  • If the constant is positive and the middle term is negative, both binomial signs are negative.
  • If the constant is negative, the binomial signs are opposite.

Problem: Factor x2 - x - 20.

Incorrect:

x2 - x - 20 = (x - 4)(x + 5)

Correct:

x2 - x - 20 = (x + 4)(x - 5)

Why: 4 and -5 multiply to -20 and add to -1.

Real and Complex Factored Forms

Some polynomials do not factor over the real numbers, but they can factor over the complex numbers.

Example: x2 + 25 is a sum of squares, so it does not factor over the real numbers.

Using i2 = -1, the expression can be written as a product over the complex numbers.

x2 + 25 = (x - 5i)(x + 5i)

Why: The middle terms cancel and (-5i)(5i) = -25i2 = 25.

Why This Matters Mathematically

Factored form reveals multiplication structure. In later lessons, that structure helps solve equations, identify roots, and connect factors to zeros of polynomial functions.
