Lesson 3: Factoring and Algebraic Structure

Why Factoring Follows Polynomial Operations

Lesson 2 focused on adding, subtracting, and multiplying polynomial expressions. Factoring reverses multiplication by rewriting a sum as a product.

Factoring is structure work. You look for common factors, patterns, and relationships between terms.

Factored form is especially useful when solving polynomial equations and identifying zeros later in Algebra II.

Factoring Vocabulary

  • Factor: An expression that is multiplied by another expression to form a product.
  • Factored form: A product form of an expression.
  • Greatest common factor: The largest factor shared by all terms in an expression.
  • Difference of squares: A pattern in the form a2 - b2 = (a - b)(a + b).
  • Equivalent expressions: Expressions that have the same value for every allowed input.

Factoring Strategy Order

  • Step 1: Look for a greatest common factor.
  • Step 2: Look for a special pattern, such as difference of squares.
  • Step 3: For trinomials, find factor pairs that multiply to the constant and add to the middle coefficient.
  • Step 4: Check by multiplying the factors back together.

Example 1: Factor Out the GCF

Problem: Factor 6x3 + 9x2.

Think: The greatest common factor is 3x2.

6x3 + 9x2 = 3x2(2x + 3)

Check: 3x2(2x + 3) = 6x3 + 9x2.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not stop factoring before the expression is fully factored.

Expression: 2x2 - 18

Incorrect:

2x2 - 18 = 2(x2 - 9)

Correct:

2x2 - 18 = 2(x - 3)(x + 3)

Why: After factoring out 2, x2 - 9 is still a difference of squares.
