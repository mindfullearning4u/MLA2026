Worked Example 1: Set Up Polynomial Long Division

Problem: Divide x2 + 5x + 6 by x + 2.

Step 1: Write the dividend under the division bar.

Step 2: Divide the leading term x2 by x to get x.

Step 3: Multiply, subtract, and bring down the next term.

x + 3

Reasoning: The quotient shows that x + 2 is a factor when the remainder is 0.

Worked Example 2: Interpret a Remainder

Problem: Divide x2 + 4x + 1 by x + 1.

Step 1: Divide x2 by x to get x.

Step 2: Continue the long division process.

Step 3: Read the leftover constant as the remainder.

Quotient: x + 3, Remainder: -2

Reasoning: A nonzero remainder means the divisor is not a factor.

Worked Example 3: Use Division to Rewrite

Problem: Rewrite (x2 - 1) ÷ (x - 1).

Step 1: Recognize x2 - 1 as a difference of squares.

Step 2: Factor as (x - 1)(x + 1).

Step 3: Cancel the common factor when x ≠ 1.

x + 1, with x ≠ 1

Reasoning: Polynomial division can reveal an equivalent expression and any needed restriction.

Common Mistake

Problem: Divide x2 + 3x + 2 by x + 1.

Incorrect:

Remainder is ignored.

Why it is wrong: Ignoring a remainder changes the value of the expression when the remainder is not zero.

Correct:

Check the full quotient and remainder.

Fix: Complete the division and state the remainder so the answer is mathematically accurate.
