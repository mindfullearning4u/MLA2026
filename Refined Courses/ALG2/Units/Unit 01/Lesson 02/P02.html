Lesson 2: Polynomial Operations

Why Polynomial Operations Come Next

Lesson 1 reviewed functions and notation. Algebra II functions are often built from polynomial expressions, so you need to operate with those expressions accurately.

Polynomial operations are structure work. You are not just moving symbols. You are using terms, powers, coefficients, and like terms to create equivalent expressions.

These skills support factoring, polynomial division, polynomial equations, rational expressions, and graph interpretation later in the course.

Polynomial Vocabulary

  • Polynomial: An expression made of terms with variables raised to whole-number exponents.
  • Term: A number, variable, or product of numbers and variables in an expression.
  • Coefficient: The numerical factor of a variable term.
  • Constant: A term with no variable.
  • Like terms: Terms with the same variable raised to the same power.

Rules to Remember

  • Add polynomials: Combine like terms.
  • Subtract polynomials: Distribute the subtraction sign to every term in the second polynomial, then combine like terms.
  • Multiply polynomials: Distribute each term and use exponent rules.
  • Standard form: Write terms in descending order by exponent.

Example 1: Add Polynomials

Problem: Add (3x2 + 2x - 5) + (x2 - 7x + 9).

Think: Combine terms with the same variable and exponent.

3x2 + x2 = 4x2

2x - 7x = -5x

-5 + 9 = 4

Answer: 4x2 - 5x + 4

Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not combine unlike terms.

Expression: 5x2 + 3x

Incorrect:

5x2 + 3x = 8x2

Correct:

5x2 + 3x cannot be combined.

Why: The terms do not have the same variable raised to the same power.
