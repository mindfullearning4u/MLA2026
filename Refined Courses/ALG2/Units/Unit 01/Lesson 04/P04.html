Worked Example 1: Simplify a Square Root of a Negative

Problem: Simplify √-36.

Step 1: Separate the negative factor from the perfect square.

Step 2: Use √-1 = i.

Step 3: Use √36 = 6.

√-36 = 6i

Reasoning: The imaginary unit i represents √-1.

Worked Example 2: Write a Complex Number

Problem: Simplify 4 + √-25.

Step 1: Rewrite √-25 as √25 · √-1.

Step 2: Simplify √25 as 5.

Step 3: Write the result in a + bi form.

4 + 5i

Reasoning: A complex number has a real part and an imaginary part.

Worked Example 3: Use the Discriminant

Problem: For x2 + 4x + 13 = 0, decide whether solutions are real or non-real.

Step 1: Identify a = 1, b = 4, and c = 13.

Step 2: Compute b2 - 4ac.

Step 3: A negative discriminant means non-real complex solutions.

42 - 4(1)(13) = -36, so the solutions are non-real complex.

Reasoning: The discriminant describes the type of quadratic solutions before solving completely.

Common Mistake

Problem: Simplify √-49.

Incorrect:

-7

Why it is wrong: Squaring -7 gives 49, not -49.

Correct:

7i

Fix: Use i for √-1, so √-49 = √49 · √-1 = 7i.
