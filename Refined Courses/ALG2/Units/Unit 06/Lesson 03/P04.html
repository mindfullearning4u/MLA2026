P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Study each example carefully. Each step shows the reasoning students should be able to reproduce independently.

Worked Example 1: Choose a Model Type

Problem: A scatterplot rises by about the same amount each step.

Step 1: Look for a constant rate of change.

Step 2: Constant additive change suggests linear.

Step 3: Name the model type.

Linear model

Reasoning: Model choice depends on the data pattern.

Visual support: Points rising at a nearly constant rate suggest a linear model first.

Worked Example 2: Interpret Scatterplot Trend

Problem: Points rise from left to right.

Step 1: Read the direction of the pattern.

Step 2: Connect increasing x to increasing y.

Step 3: Describe association.

Positive association.

Reasoning: Scatterplots show relationships between variables.

Worked Example 3: Use Technology Output

Problem: A regression gives y=2.5x+4.

Step 1: Identify slope 2.5.

Step 2: Identify intercept 4.

Step 3: Interpret the rule as a prediction model.

Predicted y starts at 4 and increases 2.5 per x.

Reasoning: Regression equations summarize data trends.

Common Mistake

Problem: A curved increasing scatterplot is always linear.

Incorrect:

Use a line because it increases.

Why it is wrong: Increasing does not guarantee a constant rate.

Correct:

Choose a model based on shape and residuals.

Fix: Look at pattern shape, not only direction.
