P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Study each example carefully. Each step shows the reasoning students should be able to reproduce independently.

Worked Example 1: Graph a Boundary

Problem: Graph y ≤ 2x + 1.

Step 1: Use y = 2x + 1 as the boundary.

Step 2: Use a solid line because equality is included.

Step 3: Shade below the line.

Solid boundary, shade below.

Reasoning: The inequality symbol tells how to draw and shade.

Visual support: Because y is less than or equal to the boundary, the solution includes the line and the half-plane below it.

Worked Example 2: Find a Feasible Point

Problem: Check whether (1, 2) satisfies y < 3x.

Step 1: Substitute x = 1 and y = 2.

Step 2: Compare 2 < 3(1).

Step 3: Decide whether the statement is true.

Yes, (1, 2) is feasible.

Reasoning: A feasible point satisfies the inequality.

Worked Example 3: Interpret a Feasible Region

Problem: A shaded region shows all budget choices that fit two limits.

Step 1: Each inequality represents one condition.

Step 2: The overlap satisfies both conditions.

Step 3: Interpret points in the overlap as possible choices.

The overlap is the feasible region.

Reasoning: Systems of inequalities model constraints.

Common Mistake

Problem: Graph y > x - 2.

Incorrect:

Use a solid line.

Why it is wrong: Greater than does not include equality.

Correct:

Use a dashed line and shade above.

Fix: Use solid lines only for ≤ or ≥.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it uses the lesson process accurately and connects the answer back to the mathematical structure or context.
