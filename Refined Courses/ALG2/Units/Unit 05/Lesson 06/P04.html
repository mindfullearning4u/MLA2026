P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Study each example carefully. Each step shows the reasoning students should be able to reproduce independently.

Worked Example 1: Find an Inverse

Problem: Find the inverse of f(x)=x+4.

Step 1: Write y=x+4.

Step 2: Switch x and y.

Step 3: Solve for y.

f-1(x)=x-4

Reasoning: An inverse undoes the original function.

Worked Example 2: Verify an Inverse

Problem: Check f(x)=2x and g(x)=x/2.

Step 1: Compute f(g(x)).

Step 2: Compute g(f(x)).

Step 3: Both should equal x.

They are inverses.

Reasoning: Compositions verify inverse relationships.

Worked Example 3: Interpret an Inverse

Problem: A function converts hours to pay. What does the inverse do?

Step 1: Original input is hours.

Step 2: Original output is pay.

Step 3: The inverse reverses input and output roles.

The inverse converts pay back to hours.

Reasoning: Inverses reverse a process.

Common Mistake

Problem: Find inverse of f(x)=x-7.

Incorrect:

f-1(x)=x-7

Why it is wrong: The inverse must undo subtraction by 7.

Correct:

f-1(x)=x+7

Fix: Use opposite operations after switching x and y.
