Worked Example 1: Interpret Doubling

Problem: A population doubles every year from 100.

Step 1: Use initial value 100.

Step 2: Use factor 2.

Step 3: Write the model.

P=100(2t)

Reasoning: Doubling is exponential growth with factor 2.

Worked Example 2: Use a Log to Find Time

Problem: Solve 2t = 32.

Step 1: Recognize the unknown is in the exponent.

Step 2: Rewrite using powers of 2.

Step 3: Find the exponent.

t = 5

Reasoning: Logarithmic thinking helps solve for time in exponential models.

Worked Example 3: Interpret a Log Scale

Problem: Why might a log scale be used?

Step 1: Log scales handle very large ranges.

Step 2: They compare multiplicative changes.

Step 3: They make exponential patterns easier to read.

A log scale compresses large values.

Reasoning: Logarithms support interpretation of growth across large ranges.

Common Mistake

Problem: A quantity doubles from 50.

Incorrect:

P=50+2t

Why it is wrong: Adding 2 repeatedly is linear, not doubling.

Correct:

P=50(2t)

Fix: Doubling means multiplying by 2 each time period.
