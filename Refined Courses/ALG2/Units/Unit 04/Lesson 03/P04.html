P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Study each example carefully. Each one shows a different way the lesson idea appears in Algebra II work.

Worked Example 1: Simple Annual Growth

Problem: A savings account has $800 and grows by 5% annually.

Step 1: Use initial amount 800.

Step 2: Use growth factor 1.05.

Step 3: Write the exponential model.

A=800(1.05)t

Reasoning: Savings growth can be modeled exponentially when interest compounds annually.

Worked Example 2: Compute Future Value

Problem: Find the value of $1000 after 2 years at 4% annual growth.

Step 1: Use A=1000(1.04)2.

Step 2: Compute the power.

Step 3: Round to cents.

A=$1081.60

Reasoning: Financial answers usually need appropriate rounding.

Worked Example 3: Compare Two Options

Problem: Which grows faster: 1.03 or 1.07?

Step 1: Compare the growth factors.

Step 2: The larger factor creates faster growth.

Step 3: Connect to percent rates.

1.07 grows faster.

Reasoning: A higher growth factor means a higher percent increase each period.

Common Mistake

Problem: Model $500 growing at 6%.

Incorrect:

A=500(6)t

Why it is wrong: The percent must be converted to a growth factor.

Correct:

A=500(1.06)t

Fix: Use 1 + rate for growth.
