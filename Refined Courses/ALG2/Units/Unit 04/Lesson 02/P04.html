P04 Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Each one shows a different way the lesson idea appears in Algebra II work.

Worked Example 1: Identify Growth

Problem: Determine whether A=200(1.06)^t is growth or decay.

Step 1: Look at the factor 1.06.

Step 2: Compare it to 1.

Step 3: A factor greater than 1 means growth.

Growth at 6% per time period.

Reasoning: Growth factors are greater than 1.

Worked Example 2: Identify Decay

Problem: Determine whether A=500(0.92)^t is growth or decay.

Step 1: Look at the factor 0.92.

Step 2: Compare it to 1.

Step 3: A factor between 0 and 1 means decay.

Decay at 8% per time period.

Reasoning: Decay rate is 1 - 0.92 = 0.08.

Worked Example 3: Use a Model

Problem: Find A after 2 years for A=1000(1.05)^t.

Step 1: Substitute t = 2.

Step 2: Compute 1.05^2.

Step 3: Multiply by 1000.

A = 1102.50

Reasoning: Growth models estimate values after repeated percent increases.

Common Mistake

Problem: Interpret A=300(0.75)^t.

Incorrect:

Growth by 75%

Why it is wrong: A factor less than 1 represents decay, not growth.

Correct:

Decay by 25%

Fix: Subtract 0.75 from 1 to get the decay rate.
