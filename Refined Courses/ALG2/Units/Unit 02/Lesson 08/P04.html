P04 Worked Examples

Work through each example slowly. The goal is not only to get an answer, but to understand why each step is valid and how the idea connects to the lesson standard.

Worked Example 1: Analyze a Polynomial Quickly

Problem: For f(x) = -2x3 + x - 1, describe end behavior.

Step 1: Identify degree 3.

Step 2: Identify leading coefficient -2.

Step 3: Use odd degree with negative leading coefficient.

Left end up, right end down.

Reasoning: End behavior is controlled by the leading term.

Worked Example 2: Use Zeros and Multiplicity

Problem: For f(x) = (x - 2)2(x + 1), describe graph behavior at each zero.

Step 1: Identify zeros 2 and -1.

Step 2: Use multiplicity 2 at x = 2.

Step 3: Use multiplicity 1 at x = -1.

Bounce at x = 2; cross at x = -1.

Reasoning: Multiplicity controls whether the graph touches or crosses the x-axis.

Worked Example 3: Solve a Quadratic Inequality

Problem: An upward parabola has zeros -4 and 6. Solve f(x) ≤ 0.

Step 1: The graph is below or on the x-axis between the zeros.

Step 2: Include endpoints because the inequality allows equality.

Step 3: Write the closed interval.

-4 ≤ x ≤ 6

Reasoning: The inequality asks for all inputs where the output is not positive.

Common Mistake

Problem: An upward parabola has zeros 1 and 7. Solve f(x) > 0.

Incorrect:

1 < x < 7

Why it is wrong: Between the zeros an upward parabola is below the x-axis.

Correct:

x < 1 or x > 7

Fix: Use a quick sketch or sign check before writing the interval.
