P04 Worked Examples

Work through each example slowly. The goal is not only to get an answer, but to understand why each step is valid and how the idea connects to the lesson standard.

Worked Example 1: Interpret a Maximum

Problem: A polynomial model shows maximum profit of $500 at 20 items.

Step 1: Identify the input as items.

Step 2: Identify the output as profit.

Step 3: Interpret the coordinate in context.

Selling 20 items gives a maximum profit of $500.

Reasoning: Graph features become meaningful only when tied to context.

Worked Example 2: Interpret a Zero

Problem: A height model has a zero at t = 6.

Step 1: A zero means output is 0.

Step 2: Height 0 means the object is on the ground.

Step 3: Use the input as time.

The object reaches the ground at 6 seconds.

Reasoning: Zeros often represent break-even points, ground contact, or other output-zero events.

Worked Example 3: State a Model Limitation

Problem: A model predicts negative distance after 12 seconds.

Step 1: Recognize negative distance is not realistic in this context.

Step 2: Identify where the model stops making sense.

Step 3: State the limitation clearly.

The model should not be used after it predicts impossible distances.

Reasoning: Mathematical models have domains where they are meaningful.

Common Mistake

Problem: A revenue model has x-intercept 30.

Incorrect:

Revenue is highest at 30 items.

Why it is wrong: An x-intercept means revenue is 0, not maximum.

Correct:

At 30 items, revenue is 0.

Fix: Match each graph feature to its correct meaning.
