P04 Worked Examples

Work through each example slowly. The goal is not only to get an answer, but to understand why each step is valid and how the idea connects to the lesson standard.

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Worked Example 1: Solve a Quadratic Equation

Problem: Solve x2 - 5x + 6 = 0.

Step 1: Factor the quadratic.

Step 2: Set each factor equal to 0.

Step 3: Solve each equation.

x = 2 or x = 3

Reasoning: The zeros are the solutions to the equation.

Worked Example 2: Use a Graph for an Inequality

Problem: If a parabola crosses the x-axis at 1 and 5 and opens upward, solve f(x) > 0.

Step 1: An upward parabola is above the x-axis outside the zeros.

Step 2: Use open intervals because the inequality is greater than, not greater than or equal to.

Step 3: Write both intervals.

x < 1 or x > 5

Reasoning: Inequality solutions are intervals, not just points.

Visual support: The graph is above the x-axis outside the intercepts, so f(x) > 0 on x < 1 or x > 5.

Worked Example 3: Interpret an Interval

Problem: A profit model is positive for 2 < x < 10. What does that mean?

Step 1: Positive profit means profit is above 0.

Step 2: The interval gives input values that make that true.

Step 3: Use context units.

Profit is positive when the input is between 2 and 10 units.

Reasoning: Intervals describe all inputs that meet the condition.

Common Mistake

Problem: Solve f(x) > 0 when zeros are -2 and 4 and the graph opens upward.

Incorrect:

x = -2 or x = 4

Why it is wrong: Those are boundary points, not the full inequality solution.

Correct:

x < -2 or x > 4

Fix: Use intervals to describe where the graph is above the x-axis.
