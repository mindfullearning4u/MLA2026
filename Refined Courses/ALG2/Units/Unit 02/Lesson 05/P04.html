Graphing Polynomial Functions

A polynomial sketch combines end behavior, zeros, multiplicity, y-intercept, and possible turning points.

Worked Example 1

For f(x) = (x + 3)(x - 2)2, the graph crosses at -3, touches at 2, and has positive cubic end behavior.

Worked Example 2

For g(x) = -x(x - 4)(x + 1), the graph has zeros -1, 0, and 4 and falls to the right because the leading coefficient is negative.

Correct Reasoning Check

Correct: The worked examples identify the relevant polynomial feature first, then connect that feature to the equation, graph, or context.

Common Mistake

Do not draw a graph before checking whether each zero crosses or only touches the x-axis.
