P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Worked Example 1: Start with End Behavior

Problem: Sketch behavior for f(x) = x3 - x.

Step 1: Use the leading term x3.

Step 2: Odd degree and positive leading coefficient means left down, right up.

Step 3: Use this as the graph framework.

Left end down, right end up.

Reasoning: End behavior gives the overall direction before plotting points.

Worked Example 2: Use Zeros

Problem: For f(x) = (x + 1)(x - 2), identify x-intercepts.

Step 1: Set each factor equal to 0.

Step 2: Solve for x.

Step 3: Write intercepts as ordered pairs.

(-1, 0) and (2, 0)

Reasoning: Zeros give anchor points for the graph.

Worked Example 3: Combine Features

Problem: Sketch f(x) = (x + 2)2(x - 1).

Step 1: Use degree 3 with positive leading coefficient for end behavior.

Step 2: Identify zeros -2 and 1.

Step 3: Use multiplicity 2 at -2 to bounce and multiplicity 1 at 1 to cross.

Left down, bounce at -2, cross at 1, right up.

Reasoning: A polynomial sketch combines end behavior, zeros, and multiplicity.

Common Mistake

Problem: Sketch a graph with zero x = 3 of multiplicity 2.

Incorrect:

The graph crosses the x-axis at 3.

Why it is wrong: Even multiplicity usually touches and turns.

Correct:

The graph bounces at x = 3.

Fix: Use multiplicity to decide whether the graph crosses or bounces.
