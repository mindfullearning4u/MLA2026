Worked Example 1: Recognize a Quadratic as a Polynomial

Problem: Explain why f(x) = x2 - 4x + 3 is a polynomial function.

Step 1: Identify each term.

Step 2: Check that exponents are whole numbers.

Step 3: Identify the degree.

It is a degree 2 polynomial function.

Reasoning: Quadratics are one type of polynomial function.

Worked Example 2: Connect Intercepts to Factors

Problem: If f(x) = (x - 1)(x - 5), identify the zeros.

Step 1: Set each factor equal to 0.

Step 2: Solve x - 1 = 0 and x - 5 = 0.

Step 3: Connect zeros to x-intercepts.

Zeros: 1 and 5

Reasoning: Factored form makes x-intercepts visible.

Worked Example 3: Compare to a Higher-Degree Polynomial

Problem: Compare f(x) = x2 and g(x) = x3 near the ends of the graph.

Step 1: Identify the degree of each function.

Step 2: Recall that even-degree graphs have matching end directions when leading coefficient is positive.

Step 3: Recall that odd-degree graphs have opposite end directions when leading coefficient is positive.

x2 rises on both ends; x3 falls left and rises right.

Reasoning: Degree changes the overall graph behavior.

Common Mistake

Problem: Identify the degree of f(x) = 3x2 + 5x4 - 1.

Incorrect:

Degree 2

Why it is wrong: The first term is not always the highest-degree term.

Correct:

Degree 4

Fix: Find the greatest exponent after considering all terms.
