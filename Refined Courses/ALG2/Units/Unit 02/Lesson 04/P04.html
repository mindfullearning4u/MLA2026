P04 Worked Examples

Work through each example slowly. The goal is not only to get an answer, but to understand why each step is valid and how the idea connects to the lesson standard.

Step-by-step explanation: Each worked example below models the lesson process one step at a time and explains the reasoning before the final answer.

Worked Example 1: Connect Factor to Zero

Problem: Find the zero from the factor x - 6.

Step 1: Set the factor equal to 0.

Step 2: Solve x - 6 = 0.

Step 3: State the zero.

x = 6

Reasoning: A factor creates a zero when it equals 0.

Worked Example 2: Identify Multiplicity

Problem: For f(x) = (x + 2)2(x - 3), identify zeros and multiplicities.

Step 1: Set x + 2 = 0 and x - 3 = 0.

Step 2: Use the exponent on each factor as multiplicity.

Step 3: List zeros with multiplicity.

x = -2 has multiplicity 2; x = 3 has multiplicity 1.

Reasoning: Repeated factors create multiplicity.

Worked Example 3: Predict Bounce or Cross

Problem: At x = -2, a zero has multiplicity 2. What happens at the graph?

Step 1: Recognize multiplicity 2 is even.

Step 2: Even multiplicity means the graph touches and turns.

Step 3: Describe the behavior.

The graph bounces at x = -2.

Reasoning: Even multiplicity usually creates a bounce; odd multiplicity usually crosses.

Visual support: Odd multiplicity crosses the x-axis; even multiplicity touches and turns around.

Common Mistake

Problem: For (x - 4)2, identify the zero.

Incorrect:

Zero is -4

Why it is wrong: The sign is reversed when solving x - 4 = 0.

Correct:

Zero is 4

Fix: Set the factor equal to 0 and solve instead of copying the sign.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it uses the lesson process accurately and connects the answer back to the mathematical structure or context.
