# MLA Algebra II Unit Mapping v2

## Purpose

This document establishes the official MLA Algebra II instructional blueprint. The approved standards crosswalk defines what must be taught; this mapping defines when concepts are taught and in what instructional order.

Instruction is sequenced by mathematical dependency, conceptual development, student readiness, college readiness, SAT readiness, ACT readiness, and best instructional practice. Standards are not sequenced according to the order of Florida B.E.S.T., Common Core, SAT, or ACT documentation.

## Course Structure

- 6 units
- 8 lessons per unit
- Lesson 8 in every unit: Putting It All Together
- Every approved MLA Algebra II standard is mapped once as a primary instructional target.
- Support-only review lessons may reinforce prior standards but do not create duplicate primary ownership.

## Unit 1: Advanced Expressions and Equations

**Unit Purpose:** Develop Algebra II readiness through algebraic structure, polynomial operations, complex numbers, polynomial division, and higher-order equation solving.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A2.FN.1; MLA.A2.AE.1; MLA.A2.AE.2; MLA.A2.AE.3; MLA.A2.AE.4; MLA.A2.AE.5; MLA.A2.PF.1; MLA.A2.PF.7

**Unit Justification:** Algebra II begins by extending Algebra I fluency into advanced expression structure and equation solving. Students revisit function language briefly, then move into polynomial expressions, factoring, complex solutions, and higher-degree equations before analyzing full polynomial functions in Unit 2.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Algebra I equation solving, function notation, domain and range language, graph interpretation, operations with integer exponents, factoring basic quadratics, zero-product reasoning, graphing linear and quadratic functions, interpreting solutions, and appropriate use of graphing/calculator technology.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports polynomial functions, radical and rational expressions, systems, function analysis, and modeling throughout the course.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** SAT-style nonroutine factoring, ACT equation-structure items, error analysis of polynomial operations, multiple-method solution comparisons, and justification tasks involving complex roots.

## Unit 3: Radical and Rational Relationships

**Unit Purpose:** Develop understanding of radical expressions, rational exponents, radical functions, rational expressions, rational functions, equations, and modeling.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A2.AE.6; MLA.A2.AE.7; MLA.A2.AE.8; MLA.A2.RR.1; MLA.A2.RR.2; MLA.A2.RR.3; MLA.A2.RR.4; MLA.A2.RR.5; MLA.A2.RR.6

**Unit Justification:** Radical and rational relationships depend on polynomial structure and function analysis. Placing them after polynomial functions allows students to extend prior work into more complex algebraic and graphical forms.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Exponent rules, square roots, rational exponents, factoring, polynomial division, least common denominators, excluded values, function notation, domain/range language, graph interpretation, and solving equations.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** Domain and asymptote investigations, extraneous solution error analysis, ACT rational expression challenges, SAT-style radical equations, and technology-assisted graph comparisons.

## Unit 4: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

**Unit Purpose:** Develop understanding of exponential growth, decay, financial models, logarithmic functions, logarithmic properties, and inverse relationships.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A2.EX.1; MLA.A2.EX.2; MLA.A2.EX.3; MLA.A2.LOG.1; MLA.A2.LOG.2; MLA.A2.LOG.3; MLA.A2.LOG.4; MLA.A2.FM.2; MLA.A2.FM.3; MLA.A2.FM.4

**Unit Justification:** Exponential and logarithmic functions belong after students have worked with multiple nonlinear families. Financial models are placed here because compound growth and decay rely directly on exponential reasoning.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Function notation, percent increase/decrease, exponent rules, rational exponents, inverse operations, graph interpretation, modeling with parameters.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports inverse functions, model comparison, data regression, financial literacy, and multi-step applications.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** Compound-interest comparisons, logarithmic scale investigations, SAT-style exponential model interpretation, ACT function growth items, and inverse-function justification tasks.

**Pacing / Support Note:** Lesson 3 should focus on exponential financial reasoning and interpretation rather than attempting to exhaust every financial product type in one lesson. Additional financial comparison work is revisited in Unit 6.

## Unit 5: Systems and Advanced Function Analysis

**Unit Purpose:** Develop advanced function reasoning through systems, inequalities, nonlinear analysis, transformations, composition, inverses, and comparative function behavior.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A2.SYS.1; MLA.A2.SYS.2; MLA.A2.SYS.3; MLA.A2.SYS.4; MLA.A2.FN.3; MLA.A2.FN.4; MLA.A2.FN.5; MLA.A2.FN.6; MLA.A2.FN.7; MLA.A2.FN.8; MLA.A2.FN.9; MLA.A2.FN.10; MLA.A2.FN.11; MLA.A2.FN.12

**Unit Justification:** Systems and advanced function analysis require fluency with several function families. This unit deliberately follows polynomial, radical, rational, exponential, and logarithmic instruction so students can compare and operate on functions with meaning.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Linear and nonlinear equations, graphing, inequality notation, function notation, transformations, domain/range, inverse relationships, and solving equations.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports regression, comparing models, multi-step modeling, and college-level function analysis.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** Nonlinear system investigations, inverse verification proofs, composition domain challenges, SAT-style function transformations, ACT systems items, and multiple-representation tasks.

**Pacing / Support Note:** Unit 5 is the most conceptually dense unit. Add support checkpoints after Lesson 3 and Lesson 5 to verify systems readiness before transformations/composition and composition readiness before inverse functions.

## Unit 6: Financial Models, Data, and Mathematical Modeling

**Unit Purpose:** Apply Algebra II concepts to financial literacy, sequences, data modeling, regression, comparing models, and multi-step real-world applications.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A2.FM.1; MLA.A2.EX.4; MLA.A2.FN.2

**Unit Justification:** The final unit places students in application-rich contexts after they have studied major function families and systems. Data, regression, financial interpretation, and model comparison support college, SAT, and ACT readiness.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Exponential and logarithmic models, function analysis, systems, graph interpretation, reading tables, interpreting data displays, technology tools for regression, and contextual reasoning.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports precalculus, statistics, personal finance, college algebra, and career-based quantitative reasoning.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** Regression technology investigations, SAT data-analysis challenges, ACT statistics and functions items, financial comparison projects, model-selection justifications, and open-ended real-world applications.

# Appendix 3: Instructional Sequencing Validation

The Algebra II sequence begins with advanced expressions and equations because students must be able to manipulate algebraic structures before analyzing advanced function behavior. Unit 1 is a readiness bridge, not a full Algebra I remediation unit. It assumes prior Algebra I and Geometry completion while briefly reestablishing function language and algebraic fluency needed for Algebra II.

Polynomial functions follow expression and equation work because graphing, zeros, end behavior, and polynomial models depend on operations, factoring, division, and root interpretation. Radical and rational relationships are placed after polynomial functions because both rely on expression structure, domain restrictions, and nonlinear function interpretation.

Exponential and logarithmic functions are placed after students have built experience with several nonlinear families. Financial growth models appear within the exponential/logarithmic unit because they rely directly on growth, decay, percent change, and parameter interpretation.

Systems and advanced function analysis are placed after major function families so students can solve, compare, transform, compose, and invert functions using a broad enough toolkit. The final unit emphasizes data, regression, financial interpretation, and multi-step modeling so students apply Algebra II concepts in college-, SAT-, ACT-, and career-aligned contexts.

This sequence supports mastery-based learning because each unit builds prerequisite understanding before increasing abstraction, uses Lesson 8 for synthesis instead of new primary content, and allows students to demonstrate mastery through Notebook Evidence, Checkpoint Submission, and Lesson Quiz evidence before advancing.

## SAT Readiness

The sequence supports SAT readiness through recurring Algebra, Advanced Math, and Problem-Solving and Data Analysis connections. Geometry and Trigonometry domains are not primary emphasis areas within the Algebra II standards architecture and therefore have limited representation.

## ACT Readiness

The sequence supports ACT readiness through Intermediate Algebra, Functions, Coordinate Geometry where systems and graphs are involved, and Statistics and Probability in the data and modeling unit. Foundational ACT categories are reinforced as prerequisites rather than treated as Algebra II primary standards.
