## Course Architecture Purpose

Anatomy and Physiology is organized as a laboratory science course focused on human body structure and function. The architecture emphasizes body organization, homeostasis, cells, tissues, integumentary, skeletal, muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive, urinary, immune, reproductive, and whole-body system integration.

The course must remain Anatomy and Physiology. Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Health concepts are included only where they support human body structure, human physiology, homeostasis, disease processes, or medical applications.

## Major Architecture Controls

| Control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Standards fidelity | Every CPALMS-listed Anatomy and Physiology standard is represented in the MLA inventory, crosswalk, unit map, or support alignment. |
| Unit coherence | Each unit develops a clear human anatomy and physiology progression and avoids drift into general Biology, Health, Chemistry, or Physics. |
| Lesson coherence | Each unit has eight mapped lessons, and every primary unit standard is taught in Lessons 1-7 before Lesson 8 synthesis. |
| Lab science | Lab safety, measurement, variables, controls, medical data, models, simulations, case evidence, error, interpretation, and communication are embedded across the architecture. |
| Virtual lab/data | Virtual investigation, body-system simulations, anatomical models, physiological data, graph interpretation, and case analysis are explicitly represented. |
| Readiness | Common Core literacy, ACT Science, SAT science reading/data, college readiness, workforce readiness, and scientific literacy are present. |

## Anatomy and Physiology Laboratory Architecture Controls

Anatomy and Physiology is a high-school laboratory science course. The architecture must preserve laboratory science expectations across physical investigation, virtual investigation, data-based investigation, model use, medical case analysis, graph interpretation, and evidence-based scientific communication.

Laboratory architecture includes:

- lab safety
- experimental design
- measurement
- variables and controls
- anatomical models and diagrams
- histology image analysis
- physiological data collection or interpretation
- medical case investigation
- simulation-based investigation
- uncertainty and error reasoning
- evidence-based explanation
- scientific communication

Every unit must include at least one identifiable laboratory-style investigation experience in future lesson production.

## Anatomy and Physiology Virtual Laboratory Controls

Virtual laboratory architecture may include anatomical models, histology images, body-system simulations, physiological process simulations, case-study data, diagnostic data tables, graph-based investigations, and interactive medical models.

Virtual laboratory experiences must require students to observe a structure or process, identify relevant variables, interpret evidence, use models or data, explain structure/function relationships, make evidence-based claims, and reflect on uncertainty, error, or model limitations where appropriate.

## Unit Architecture

| Unit | Title | Primary Role |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Human Body Organization, Homeostasis, and Scientific Inquiry | Establish inquiry, lab/data practices, body organization, homeostasis, macromolecules, enzymes, and health evidence. |
| 2 | Cells, Tissues, and Integumentary System | Develop cell processes, cell cycle/cancer, tissue classification, histology, and integumentary system function. |
| 3 | Skeletal and Muscular Systems | Develop bone tissue, skeletal organization, major bones, muscle tissue, major muscles, muscle contraction, and myoneural transmission. |
| 4 | Nervous and Endocrine Systems | Develop nervous system anatomy/physiology, reflexes, spinal cord, brain, synapses, sensory systems, autonomic divisions, and endocrine regulation. |
| 5 | Cardiovascular, Respiratory, and Immune Systems | Develop blood, hemostasis, blood flow, heart sounds, hypertension, fetal circulation, lymphatic system, immunity, and respiratory physiology. |
| 6 | Digestive, Urinary, Reproductive Systems and Whole-Body Integration | Develop digestion, urine formation, reproduction, disease prevention, heredity/health relationships, and whole-body integration. |

## Architecture Decision

Anatomy and Physiology uses 28 MLA Anatomy and Physiology standards across nine domains. This count gives enough granularity for 48 lessons while keeping each standard teachable, mappable, and assessable at architecture level.

The course places foundational inquiry and homeostasis in Unit 1, microscopic and tissue-level organization in Unit 2, movement/support systems in Unit 3, regulation/control systems in Unit 4, transport/defense/exchange systems in Unit 5, and processing/elimination/reproduction/whole-body integration in Unit 6.

No lesson production begins in this phase.
