Teacher-Style Explanation

This lesson is focused on Blood Flow and Cardiovascular Factors. Start with the standard, not with memorization. The standard asks you to understand the body structure, function, model, data, or case evidence named in the lesson map: MLA.ANP.CRI.02.

  1. Name the structure or process. Say exactly what body part, body system, physiological process, data display, or model is being studied.
  2. Identify the evidence. Use the required model, diagram, table, graph, case, or data source: Blood flow diagram; pressure/flow graph.
  3. Connect structure to function. Explain what the structure does, how it does it, and why the body needs that function for homeostasis or health.
  4. Check the boundary of the lesson. Stay inside this standard. Do not add unrelated medical diagnosis, personal health disclosure, or body-system content from a later lesson.
  5. Make a claim from evidence. Your answer should include the claim, the evidence from the visual/model/data, and the reasoning that connects the evidence to the claim.

Visual or Data Model Required

Required model/data: Blood flow diagram; pressure/flow graph

Use the model the same way a careful anatomy teacher would use it: point to the part, name the part, describe the job of the part, then explain how the part supports the whole body system.

Resource: OpenStax: Blood Flow and Pressure

  1. Open the link in a new tab.

When to Contact the Teacher of Record

Contact the Teacher of Record when you can name terms but cannot explain how the structure, function, diagram, data, or case evidence connects to the standard. Bring your notes, the evidence you used, and the exact step where your reasoning became unclear.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Naming a body part or process without explaining how the visual, graph, data, or case evidence proves the answer.

Correct Reasoning

Correct: Name the part or process, cite the evidence, explain the structure-function relationship, and connect that relationship to the lesson standard.
