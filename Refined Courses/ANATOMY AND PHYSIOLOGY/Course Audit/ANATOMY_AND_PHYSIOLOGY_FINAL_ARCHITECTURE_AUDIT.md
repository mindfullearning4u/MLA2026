## 3. Strengths

- Course identity is tightly controlled as Anatomy and Physiology.
- The sequence moves logically from foundations to microscopic structures, support/movement systems, regulation systems, transport/defense/exchange systems, and whole-body integration.
- Lab and virtual lab expectations are visible in multiple architecture documents.
- Every unit includes lab/data emphasis.
- Lesson-level mapping preserves manageable workload while supporting mastery-based production.
- Health benchmarks are handled as medical and physiological support, not as a separate Health course.
- Biology, Chemistry, and Physics concepts are used only as needed to explain human structure and function.

---

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Drift into general Biology | Architecture limits cell, enzyme, macromolecule, mutation, and cancer content to human physiology context. |
| Drift into Health | Health standards are mapped only as medical evidence, heredity, disease prevention, and physiology support. |
| Overloading Unit 4 | Nervous and endocrine content is sequenced across seven instructional lessons before synthesis. |
| Lab designation invisibility | Lab and virtual lab requirements are stated in overview, Phase 1B, mapping framework, unit map, and lesson map. |
| Lesson 8 becoming a catch-all | Lesson 8 validation confirms no new primary standard is introduced. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

Recommended before lesson production:

- Use the lesson-level lab/data evidence column to guide future lab and virtual lab tasks.
- Use `PHASE_3A_B_3_LAB_VISUAL_SIMULATION_MAPPING.md` as the required lab, visual, simulation, resource, and assessment-stimulus gate.
- Maintain model/case/data evidence in every unit.
- Preserve structure-before-function sequencing when producing lessons.
- Keep health, chemistry, biology, and physics support content subordinate to Anatomy and Physiology outcomes.

These are production guidance recommendations, not architecture deficiencies.
