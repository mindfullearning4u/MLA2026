# Math for College Readiness Curriculum Analysis

## Purpose

This document analyzes the strongest MLA course architecture for Math for College Readiness. It evaluates Florida B.E.S.T. mathematics expectations represented in the existing course materials, College Algebra readiness, SAT readiness, ACT readiness, quantitative reasoning, financial literacy, and workforce readiness.

This is an architecture recommendation only. It does not modify existing lessons, standards, benchmarks, or unit materials.

---

## Source Context Reviewed

### Local MLA Course Materials

The existing Math for College Readiness course is organized around six applied units:

1. Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving
2. Algebra in Real-World Contexts
3. Data Literacy and Interpretation
4. Financial Literacy and Personal Finance
5. Probability and Decision Making
6. Measurement, Modeling, and Applied Geometry

The course currently draws from selected Florida B.E.S.T. benchmark areas including:

- Financial Literacy: `MA.912.FL`
- Algebraic Reasoning: `MA.912.AR`
- Functions: `MA.912.F`
- Data Analysis and Probability: `MA.912.DP`
- Geometric Reasoning: `MA.912.GR`
- Trigonometry: `MA.912.T`

### External Readiness Sources

College Board describes SAT Math as focused on Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry. It also notes that approximately 30% of SAT Math questions are set in science, social studies, or real-world contexts.

ACT describes its Math section as assessing skills typically acquired by the beginning of grade 12 and emphasizes prerequisites for entry-level college mathematics. ACT reporting categories include Preparing for Higher Math, Integrating Essential Skills, and Modeling, with subcategories for number and quantity, algebra, functions, geometry, statistics, and probability.

## Primary Purpose of the Course

The primary purpose of Math for College Readiness should be:

**To prepare students who have completed core high school mathematics but need strengthened readiness for college-level quantitative work, placement expectations, personal finance, workforce decision making, and applied mathematical reasoning.**

This course should not function as:

- A repeat of Algebra I
- A replacement for Algebra II
- A Precalculus bridge for STEM-bound students
- A narrow test-prep course

It should function as:

- A senior-year or post-Algebra II readiness course
- A bridge to College Algebra, Liberal Arts Mathematics, Statistics, technical programs, and workforce training
- A mastery-based course emphasizing interpretation, modeling, decision making, and quantitative communication

---

## Target Student Population

The strongest target population is:

- Students who have completed Algebra I and Geometry and may have completed or be completing Algebra II
- Students who need stronger college placement readiness before entering College Algebra, Liberal Arts Mathematics, Statistics, or technical mathematics
- Students who benefit from applied, contextualized mathematics rather than a purely symbolic algebra sequence
- Students preparing for SAT/ACT math improvement through applied algebra, data interpretation, probability, and modeling
- Students entering career, technical, business, healthcare, trades, public service, or general college pathways

The course is especially appropriate for:

- Grade 11 or 12 students needing readiness reinforcement
- Students with uneven algebra retention
- Students who can solve procedures in isolation but struggle to interpret mathematical results in context
- Students needing financial, data, and quantitative reasoning skills for postsecondary life

---

## Major Instructional Domains

The strongest MLA architecture should include seven major instructional domains:

1. Quantitative Reasoning
   - Units, rates, ratios, percents, proportional relationships, weighted averages, estimation, and reasonableness

2. Applied Algebra and Functions
   - Linear equations, inequalities, systems, exponential models, piecewise relationships, formulas, function notation, constraints, and interpretation

3. Data Literacy and Statistical Reasoning
   - Data displays, distributions, center, variability, bivariate data, correlation, causation, sampling, misleading representations, and evaluation of claims

4. Financial Literacy
   - Net worth, income, budgets, taxes, interest, credit, debt, insurance, retirement, and spreadsheet-supported decision making

5. Probability and Risk
   - Sample spaces, conditional probability, empirical probability, probability rules, counting, expected value, risk, and fair decisions

6. Measurement and Applied Geometry
   - Similarity, scale, area, volume, surface area, density, right-triangle modeling, coordinate geometry, and units

7. Mathematical Modeling and Communication
   - Assumptions, constraints, representation choice, reasonableness, explanation, comparison of options, and decision justification

The seventh domain should be embedded across the course rather than isolated in a single unit.

## Relationship to Other MLA Math Courses

### Algebra I

Math for College Readiness reinforces Algebra I concepts in context:

- Linear equations
- Linear functions
- Inequalities
- Systems
- Exponential models
- Data displays
- Modeling and interpretation

It should not reteach Algebra I as a procedural course. Algebra should be used as a tool for applied reasoning.

### Geometry

The course extends Geometry through applied measurement and modeling:

- Similarity
- Coordinate geometry
- Scale factors
- Area, volume, and surface area
- Density and units
- Right-triangle modeling

Geometry content should be practical and contextual rather than proof-based.

### Algebra II

The course supports Algebra II readiness and retention through:

- Function notation
- Exponential models
- Piecewise models
- Systems and constraints
- Interpreting key features
- Data and regression-style reasoning

It should not become an Algebra II replacement. Advanced symbolic manipulation should be limited to what supports college and workplace readiness.

### Precalculus

Math for College Readiness should not be a Precalculus substitute. It may support students who later enter Precalculus by strengthening:

- Function interpretation
- Modeling
- Data reasoning
- Average rate of change
- Algebraic fluency in context

It should not emphasize trigonometric identities, polar relationships, vectors, conics, or Calculus readiness.

## Model 1: College Algebra Prep Model

### Description

This model prioritizes algebraic readiness for College Algebra placement and success. It emphasizes functions, equations, graphs, systems, exponential models, inequalities, formulas, and algebraic fluency.

### Recommended Unit Structure

1. Algebra Readiness and Quantitative Foundations
2. Linear Equations, Inequalities, and Systems
3. Functions, Graphs, and Modeling
4. Exponential and Nonlinear Models
5. Data, Regression, and Interpretation
6. College Algebra Readiness Applications

### Strengths

- Strong alignment to College Algebra readiness
- Supports SAT Algebra and Advanced Math
- Supports ACT Algebra and Functions categories
- Useful for students at risk of college placement gaps

### Limitations

- Underemphasizes financial literacy
- Underemphasizes workforce quantitative reasoning
- Can become Algebra II review rather than a distinct readiness course
- Less aligned to broad real-life decision making
- May not fully serve students headed to Liberal Arts Mathematics, Statistics, CTE, or workforce pathways

### Best Use

This model is strongest for students whose main risk is college algebra placement or algebraic fluency.

---

## Model 2: Quantitative Reasoning Model

### Description

This model prioritizes practical mathematical reasoning, interpretation, financial literacy, data literacy, probability, risk, and decision making.

### Recommended Unit Structure

1. Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving
2. Data Literacy and Statistical Claims
3. Financial Literacy and Personal Finance
4. Probability, Risk, and Decision Making
5. Measurement and Applied Geometry
6. Civic, Workplace, and Personal Quantitative Modeling

### Strengths

- Strong alignment to real-world quantitative reasoning
- Strong alignment to workforce readiness
- Strong alignment to financial literacy
- Supports ACT Integrating Essential Skills and Modeling
- Supports SAT Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
- Useful for non-STEM college pathways and career preparation

### Limitations

- May not provide enough College Algebra preparation
- Algebraic fluency may remain uneven
- SAT Advanced Math readiness may be underdeveloped
- Students entering College Algebra may still need targeted function and equation support

### Best Use

This model is strongest for students whose primary need is practical adult quantitative literacy rather than College Algebra placement.

---

## Model 3: Hybrid Model

### Description

This model combines applied College Algebra readiness with quantitative reasoning, data literacy, financial literacy, probability, measurement, modeling, and workforce decision making.

It treats algebra as a tool for real-world reasoning rather than as isolated symbolic manipulation.

### Recommended Unit Structure

1. Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving
2. Algebra in Real-World Contexts
3. Data Literacy and Interpretation
4. Financial Literacy and Personal Finance
5. Probability and Decision Making
6. Measurement, Modeling, and Applied Geometry

### Strengths

- Best matches the current MLA course architecture
- Balances College Algebra readiness with practical quantitative literacy
- Supports SAT Algebra, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry/Trigonometry readiness
- Supports ACT Algebra, Functions, Statistics and Probability, Geometry, Integrating Essential Skills, and Modeling
- Strongly supports personal finance and workforce readiness
- Serves a broad student population without becoming narrow remediation or narrow test prep
- Preserves the applied identity of Math for College Readiness

### Limitations

- Requires careful standards crosswalk design to ensure College Algebra readiness is not diluted
- Requires strong mastery checks for algebraic fluency in Unit 2
- Requires clear messaging that the course is not a replacement for Algebra II or Precalculus
- Advanced students may need optional enrichment tasks involving more complex modeling, regression, spreadsheet analysis, and SAT/ACT-style mixed problems

### Best Use

This model is strongest for MLA because it supports the broadest range of college, career, and life-readiness outcomes while still preserving academic rigor.

## Recommended MLA Unit Structure

The existing six-unit structure is already close to the strongest model and should be retained as the recommended MLA architecture:

### Unit 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving

Purpose: Build foundational readiness through quantities, units, percent reasoning, ratios, weighted averages, formulas, function notation, and average rate of change.

Rationale: This unit establishes the reasoning habits students need before applying algebra, finance, data, probability, or geometry.

### Unit 2: Algebra in Real-World Contexts

Purpose: Strengthen College Algebra readiness through linear equations, functions, inequalities, systems, exponential models, constraints, and piecewise relationships.

Rationale: Algebra should come early because it supports SAT/ACT readiness and provides the modeling language used throughout later units.

### Unit 3: Data Literacy and Interpretation

Purpose: Develop interpretation of data displays, distributions, center, variability, bivariate data, categorical data, correlation, causation, and statistical claims.

Rationale: Data literacy is central to college, career, and civic readiness and strongly supports SAT Problem-Solving and Data Analysis and ACT Statistics and Probability.

### Unit 4: Financial Literacy and Personal Finance

Purpose: Apply quantitative, algebraic, data, and spreadsheet reasoning to budgets, income, tax, interest, credit, debt, insurance, and retirement.

Rationale: Financial literacy is a high-value readiness domain for all students and gives practical meaning to percent, exponential, and data reasoning.

### Unit 5: Probability and Decision Making

Purpose: Develop probability, conditional reasoning, counting, expected value, risk interpretation, and fair decision making.

Rationale: Probability belongs after data literacy because students need table, frequency, and categorical data reasoning before conditional probability and expected value.

### Unit 6: Measurement, Modeling, and Applied Geometry

Purpose: Apply geometry and measurement through similarity, scale, coordinate geometry, area, volume, surface area, density, right-triangle modeling, and units.

Rationale: This unit synthesizes proportional reasoning, algebra, measurement, and modeling in practical spatial contexts.

## Readiness Alignment Review

### Florida Math for College Readiness Standards

The existing benchmark pattern is appropriate for an MLA Math for College Readiness course because it draws from applied Florida B.E.S.T. high school benchmarks across financial literacy, algebraic reasoning, functions, data/probability, geometry, and trigonometry.

The course should be documented as a selected-benchmark readiness course rather than as a single traditional sequence like Algebra II or Precalculus.

### College Algebra Readiness

The course supports College Algebra readiness through:

- Linear equations and inequalities
- Systems and constraints
- Function notation
- Average rate of change
- Exponential models
- Piecewise relationships
- Graph, table, and context interpretation

Recommended improvement: Unit 2 should remain rigorous and should include enough symbolic fluency to prevent the course from becoming only interpretation-based.

### SAT Readiness

The course supports SAT readiness through:

- Algebra: Units 1 and 2
- Advanced Math: Unit 2, especially exponential and piecewise models
- Problem-Solving and Data Analysis: Units 1, 3, 4, and 5
- Geometry and Trigonometry: Unit 6

The hybrid model is especially strong because SAT Math includes context-based questions and quantitative interpretation.

### ACT Readiness

The course supports ACT readiness through:

- Number and Quantity: Unit 1
- Algebra: Unit 2
- Functions: Units 1 and 2
- Geometry: Unit 6
- Statistics and Probability: Units 3 and 5
- Integrating Essential Skills: all units
- Modeling: all units

The hybrid model aligns well with ACT's emphasis on applying skills across varied contexts.

### Quantitative Reasoning

Quantitative reasoning should be the central identity of the course. Students should consistently:

- Identify relevant quantities
- Track units
- Estimate and check reasonableness
- Interpret results in context
- Compare options
- Justify decisions

### Financial Literacy

Financial literacy should remain a full unit. It is not a minor application topic. It provides high-value readiness for:

- Personal finance
- Workforce decisions
- Postsecondary independence
- Spreadsheet and technology use
- Real-world modeling

### Workforce Readiness

The course supports workforce readiness through:

- Financial decision making
- Data interpretation
- Measurement and estimation
- Risk and probability
- Spreadsheet-supported analysis
- Communication of mathematical reasoning
- Evaluating constraints and tradeoffs

This is a major reason the hybrid model is stronger than a narrow College Algebra Prep model.

## Architecture Recommendation

### Recommended Model for MLA

**Hybrid Model**

### Rationale

The Hybrid Model is the most appropriate architecture for MLA because it best matches the purpose of Math for College Readiness: preparing students for postsecondary math expectations while also developing the quantitative reasoning needed for personal finance, workforce decisions, data interpretation, and real-world problem solving.

The College Algebra Prep Model is too narrow for the course's likely student population. It risks becoming Algebra II remediation and does not sufficiently address financial literacy, data literacy, risk, or workforce applications.

The Quantitative Reasoning Model is valuable but may underprepare students for College Algebra placement and SAT/ACT algebra expectations if algebra is not given enough focused attention.

The Hybrid Model provides the best balance:

- College Algebra readiness through applied algebra and functions
- SAT/ACT readiness through algebra, data, geometry, probability, and modeling
- Quantitative reasoning through percent, ratio, units, formulas, interpretation, and reasonableness
- Financial literacy through a dedicated personal finance unit
- Workforce readiness through data, probability, measurement, spreadsheets, constraints, and decision making

---

## Final Recommendation

MLA should adopt the **Hybrid Model** as the official Math for College Readiness architecture.

The existing six-unit structure is instructionally strong and should be retained with future refinement through a formal standards crosswalk and unit mapping process:

1. Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving
2. Algebra in Real-World Contexts
3. Data Literacy and Interpretation
4. Financial Literacy and Personal Finance
5. Probability and Decision Making
6. Measurement, Modeling, and Applied Geometry

This model is the strongest fit for MLA because it is rigorous, practical, standards-aligned, college-oriented, workforce-relevant, and consistent with MLA's mastery-based philosophy.
