==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — ENTREPRENEURSHIP
==================================================

Teach:

Entrepreneurship is the economic function of:

- identifying opportunities;
- organizing resources;
- making production decisions;
- accepting uncertainty and risk;
- introducing or expanding goods, services, or production methods.

An entrepreneur may combine:

LAND

LABOR

CAPITAL

into a productive activity.

Do not define an entrepreneur simply as:

“someone who owns a company.”

Ownership alone does not fully explain the economic function.

==================================================
ENTREPRENEUR VS INVENTOR
==================================================

Teach explicitly:

INVENTOR

creates or develops a new idea, process, or product.

ENTREPRENEUR

organizes resources and takes economic risk to bring a product, service, process, or business opportunity into use or into a market.

A person may be:

- an inventor only;
- an entrepreneur only;
- both.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — INNOVATION
==================================================

Innovation may involve:

- new product;
- improved product;
- new production process;
- new distribution process;
- organizational change;
- application of existing technology in a new way.

Do not teach:

innovation = invention only.

Innovation can involve improving or applying something that already exists.

==================================================
REQUIRED INNOVATION MODEL
==================================================

Use a fictional producer.

BEFORE:

10 workers produce 500 units per day using older equipment.

AFTER:

The firm invests in improved equipment and worker training.

10 workers now produce 700 units per day.

Ask:

What changed?

Not:

number of workers.

Changed:

- physical capital;
- human capital;
- production process.

Result:

higher output per worker.

This demonstrates increased labor productivity.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — CAPITAL INVESTMENT
==================================================

Teach:

Capital investment means committing resources toward productive assets or capabilities expected to support future production.

Examples:

- machinery;
- tools;
- factories;
- software systems;
- infrastructure;
- worker training where human-capital investment is being analyzed.

Distinguish from:

consumption.

Do NOT confuse:

financial money used to buy equipment

with

the physical capital itself.

==================================================
CURRENT CONSUMPTION VS INVESTMENT TRADEOFF
==================================================

Reconnect Unit 1 Lesson 2.

Resources used to create:

CAPITAL GOODS

cannot simultaneously be used for:

CONSUMER GOODS.

This creates a present tradeoff.

Investment may:

reduce current consumption possibilities

while potentially increasing:

future productive capacity.

Do not imply every investment succeeds.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — PRODUCTIVITY
==================================================

Teach:

Productivity measures output relative to input.

For introductory labor productivity:

Labor Productivity
=
Output ÷ Labor Input

Possible labor input:

- worker;
- worker-hour;
- total labor hours.

Always specify the unit.

==================================================
REQUIRED PRODUCTIVITY MODEL
==================================================

Factory A:

800 units

100 worker-hours

Productivity:

8 units per worker-hour.

Factory B:

900 units

150 worker-hours

Productivity:

6 units per worker-hour.

Critical conclusion:

Factory B produces MORE total output.

But Factory A has HIGHER labor productivity.

This distinction is mandatory.

==================================================
PRODUCTIVITY IS NOT PRODUCTION
==================================================

Teach:

PRODUCTION

= amount produced.

PRODUCTIVITY

= output relative to inputs used.

A firm can increase production by simply using more inputs without increasing productivity.

==================================================
FACTORS THAT MAY INCREASE PRODUCTIVITY
==================================================

Teach:

- improved physical capital;
- education;
- training;
- experience;
- technology;
- improved management;
- specialization;
- infrastructure;
- better production organization.

Do not state that any one factor guarantees productivity growth.

==================================================
PRODUCTIVITY AND WAGES BOUNDARY
==================================================

Students may learn conceptually that productivity is important to long-run earning capacity and living standards.

Do NOT claim:

“Higher productivity automatically causes every worker’s wage to rise proportionally.”

Distribution depends on additional economic conditions taught later.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — ECONOMIC GROWTH
==================================================

At this introductory stage define economic growth as:

an increase in an economy’s ability to produce goods and services over time.

Connect to:

productive capacity.

Later Unit 3 lessons will teach macroeconomic measurement in greater depth.

Do not fully teach GDP here.

==================================================
ECONOMIC GROWTH AND PPF
==================================================

Required connection to Lesson 2:

Initial productive capacity:

PPF 1

Improved resources / capital / technology / human capital:

PPF 2 farther outward.

Teach:

An OUTWARD shift indicates increased productive capacity under the model.

Do not confuse:

moving from an interior point toward the frontier

with

economic growth.

Moving from an interior point to the existing frontier means:

better use of existing capacity.

Growth means:

the frontier itself expands.

==================================================
REQUIRED VISUAL
==================================================

Show:

PPF 1

and

PPF 2

with PPF 2 outside PPF 1.

Label:

Increase in Productive Capacity

Include accessible alt text.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — ECONOMIC GROWTH VS WELL-BEING
==================================================

Teach carefully:

Economic growth can support:

- greater production;
- higher potential income;
- additional goods/services;
- improved productive capacity.

But growth alone does not tell us everything about:

- distribution;
- environmental effects;
- health;
- leisure;
- security;
- quality of life.

This prepares students for later macroeconomic analysis.

Do NOT turn this into an ideological critique of growth.

Teach measurement limitations.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — RISK AND ENTREPRENEURSHIP
==================================================

Entrepreneurs face uncertainty.

Possible outcomes include:

- profit;
- loss;
- growth;
- failure;
- adaptation.

Do not romanticize business failure or portray entrepreneurship as guaranteed wealth.

==================================================

==================================================

To satisfy the Florida standard, include several economically meaningful examples of entrepreneurs, inventors, and innovators from different backgrounds.

Selection criteria:

- clear connection to innovation/production/productivity;
- diversity across gender/social/ethnic backgrounds;
- factual documentation.

Potential examples may include individuals associated with:

- manufacturing;
- agriculture;
- transportation;
- communications;
- technology;
- consumer products;
- services.

Do NOT build a celebrity biography lesson.

For each selected individual analyze:

1. Economic problem/opportunity.
2. Innovation or entrepreneurial action.
3. Resources organized.
4. Economic effect.
5. Limitation or context.

WORKED CALCULATION 1
==================================================

Output:
600 units

Labor:
75 worker-hours

Productivity:

600 ÷ 75
=
8 units per worker-hour.

Interpretation:

The production process generates an average of 8 units for each worker-hour of labor input.

==================================================
WORKED CALCULATION 2
==================================================

400 units
80 worker-hours

Productivity:
5 units per worker-hour.

After training:

520 units
80 worker-hours

Productivity:
6.5 units per worker-hour.

Interpretation:

Output increased while labor input remained constant, so labor productivity increased.

==================================================
P04 — GROWTH AND PPF
==================================================

Reconnect the production possibilities frontier.

Teach three distinct situations:

CASE A:

Economy operates inside existing PPF.

Moves toward frontier.

Interpretation:

better use of existing capacity.

NOT necessarily economic growth.

---

CASE B:

Economy reallocates resources along existing frontier.

Interpretation:

tradeoff.

NOT growth.

---

CASE C:

Frontier shifts outward.

Interpretation:

increase in productive capacity.

This represents economic growth in the model.

==================================================
CAUSES OF OUTWARD PPF SHIFT
==================================================

Examples:

- increase in productive resources;
- technological improvement;
- physical-capital investment;
- human-capital development;
- improved infrastructure.

Students must connect:

CAUSE

→ PRODUCTIVE CAPACITY

→ PPF EFFECT.

==================================================
INNOVATION TRADEOFFS
==================================================

Teach:

Innovation can create gains while also creating adjustment costs.

Possible effects:

- higher productivity;
- lower production cost;
- new products;
- displaced tasks/jobs;
- need for retraining;
- startup/investment costs;
- uncertainty.

Do NOT frame innovation as automatically good or bad.

Analyze effects.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 5
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

An entrepreneur is simply anyone who owns a business.

CORRECTION:

Entrepreneurship involves organizing resources and making decisions under uncertainty.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Inventor and entrepreneur always mean the same thing.

CORRECTION:

The roles can overlap but are distinct.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Money is physical capital.

CORRECTION:

Money can finance capital purchases but is not itself the machine/tool/factory used in production.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

More production always means higher productivity.

CORRECTION:

Productivity depends on output relative to inputs.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Economic growth means moving from inside the PPF to the frontier.

CORRECTION:

That reflects fuller use of existing resources. Growth expands productive capacity and shifts the frontier outward.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Every capital investment increases productivity.

CORRECTION:

Investment involves risk and may not produce the expected result.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Innovation always reduces employment.

CORRECTION:

Innovation can eliminate some tasks, create others, change skills demanded, and affect industries differently.

---

MISCONCEPTION 8:

Economic growth means everyone automatically becomes better off.

CORRECTION:

Growth measures productive capacity/output, not every dimension of individual well-being or distribution.
