==================================================
IMPORTANT COURSE BOUNDARY
==================================================

Economics is separate from:

Personal Financial Literacy and Money Management.

Do not describe this course as a combined Economics and Financial Literacy course.

Economics focuses on:

- economic reasoning;
- scarcity;
- choice;
- opportunity cost;
- incentives;
- production;
- markets;
- supply and demand;
- firms;
- labor;
- national economic performance;
- inflation;
- unemployment;
- business cycles;
- money and banking;
- government economic policy;
- fiscal policy;
- monetary policy;
- international trade;
- global economic relationships.

Personal budgeting, credit management, banking decisions, investing for personal goals, insurance, and similar household-finance topics belong primarily in MLA's separate Personal Financial Literacy and Money Management course.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
==================================================

Write a concise student-facing course description.

Use language similar in meaning to:

Economics examines how individuals, businesses, governments, and societies make decisions when resources are limited.

Students will learn how economists analyze:

- choices;
- incentives;
- opportunity costs;
- production;
- markets;
- prices;
- employment;
- inflation;
- economic growth;
- government policy;
- international trade.

Students will work with:

- economic graphs;
- tables;
- models;
- data;
- real-world and fictional economic scenarios.

The course emphasizes economic reasoning rather than memorization.

Students will be expected to explain:

- what happened;
- why it happened;
- what economic concept applies;
- what the likely consequences are.

Keep this description approximately 2–3 short paragraphs.

Do not turn it into a full syllabus.

==================================================
WHAT YOU WILL LEARN
==================================================

Include a concise list.

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- explain scarcity, tradeoffs, and opportunity cost;
- use economic reasoning to evaluate choices;
- interpret production possibilities models;
- analyze supply and demand;
- determine market equilibrium;
- explain how firms and market structures affect competition;
- analyze wages and labor markets;
- interpret economic indicators such as output, inflation, and unemployment;
- explain business cycles;
- explain the role of money and financial institutions in the economy;
- analyze taxation and government spending;
- explain fiscal and monetary policy;
- analyze comparative advantage and international trade;
- interpret economic graphs, data, and models;
- distinguish economic evidence from opinion.

==================================================

The Unit Pretest helps determine current knowledge.

A score of 90% or higher may qualify the student for acceleration according to MLA policy.

==================================================
LESSON WORKFLOW
==================================================

Explain that a typical Economics lesson follows:

Lesson Overview and Vocabulary

→ Learn the Economic Concept

→ Model / Worked Example

→ Guided Practice

→ Independent Work

→ Mastery Checkpoint

→ Notebook Submission

→ Checkpoint Submission

→ Quiz if Assigned

Some graph/data-intensive lessons may include a separate page for:

- learning the economic graph/model;
- additional data analysis.

Students must follow the actual Moodle sequence.

==================================================
VOCABULARY
==================================================

Explain:

Economics uses precise terminology.

Students should record and understand vocabulary because similar terms may have different meanings.

Examples:

- demand versus quantity demanded;
- supply versus quantity supplied;
- deficit versus debt;
- absolute advantage versus comparative advantage.

Students are expected to use correct economic vocabulary in explanations.

==================================================
ECONOMIC GRAPHS
==================================================

Explain:

When a graph is taught, students should pay attention to:

- horizontal axis;
- vertical axis;
- curve labels;
- equilibrium;
- movement along a curve;
- curve shifts;
- direction of change.

Students should not rely only on memorizing whether a line moves left or right.

They must understand WHY the change occurs and what it means economically.

==================================================
WORKED EXAMPLES
==================================================

Explain:

Worked examples show the reasoning process.

Students should study:

QUESTION

→ ECONOMIC CONCEPT

→ GRAPH / DATA / EVIDENCE

→ REASONING

→ CONCLUSION.

Do not simply copy the final answer.

==================================================
GUIDED PRACTICE
==================================================

Explain:

Guided Practice provides support while students are learning.

Students should use:

- hints;
- partial examples;
- explanations;
- feedback.

Guided Practice prepares students for Independent Work.

==================================================
INDEPENDENT WORK
==================================================

Explain:

Independent Work occurs after Guided Practice.

It is the student's opportunity to apply the lesson concept without step-by-step assistance.

Independent Work may include:

- calculations;
- graph interpretation;
- economic scenarios;
- data analysis;
- short written explanations.

Independent Work is submitted as part of Notebook Evidence.

It is NOT normally a separate assignment.

==================================================
NOTEBOOK EVIDENCE
==================================================

Explain:

Notebook Evidence demonstrates the student's work throughout the lesson.

Each Notebook Submission identifies exactly what must be submitted.

Typical required evidence may include:

- vocabulary;
- required lesson organizers;
- graph/model work;
- calculations;
- Independent Work.

Students should submit only the specifically required lesson evidence plus any optional notes they wish to include.

==================================================
MASTERY CHECKPOINT
==================================================

Explain:

The Checkpoint is separate from Notebook Evidence.

It uses a new problem, graph, data set, or economic scenario.

Students must complete it independently.

Checkpoint Submission should contain only the required checkpoint response.

Do not resubmit the entire Notebook unless specifically instructed.

==================================================
QUIZZES
==================================================

Explain:

Not every Economics lesson includes a Quiz.

When assigned:

- quiz questions are selected from a larger question bank;
- the quiz measures lesson mastery;
- students receive one initial attempt;
- 80% is the mastery threshold.

Additional attempts follow MLA remediation/reassessment policy.

==================================================
UNIT ASSESSMENT
==================================================

Explain:

Each unit ends with a Unit Assessment.

The assessment includes questions from across the entire unit.

Students should be prepared to:

- interpret graphs;
- interpret data;
- apply economic concepts;
- explain cause and effect;
- distinguish similar concepts.

==================================================
CALCULATORS
==================================================

Explain:

Calculators may be used when allowed.

The purpose of calculations is not just to produce a number.

Students must often explain what the answer means economically.

Example:

A correct inflation calculation is incomplete if the student cannot explain what the inflation rate represents.

==================================================
WHEN TO ASK FOR HELP
==================================================

Students should contact the TOR when:

- instructions remain unclear after rereading;
- a graph/model is not understood;
- feedback is unclear;
- they do not understand why an answer is incorrect;
- remediation is required.

End with:

Next:
Read "Expectations and Policies."

==================================================

Create Moodle page titled:

Economics — Expectations and Policies

==================================================
STUDENT RESPONSIBILITIES
==================================================

Students are expected to:

- complete course work in order;
- read all instructional pages;
- complete Guided Practice;
- complete Independent Work;
- maintain required Notebook Evidence;
- submit Notebook and Checkpoint work separately;
- complete quizzes and Unit Assessments;
- review TOR feedback;
- revise work when required;
- ask for help when needed.

==================================================
MASTERY EXPECTATION
==================================================

MLA mastery threshold:

80%.

A score below mastery may require:

- review;
- remediation;
- revision;
- reassessment.

Mastery means students can apply the concept, not simply recognize vocabulary.

==================================================
ECONOMIC REASONING EXPECTATION
==================================================

Students should explain:

- what economic concept applies;
- what evidence supports the answer;
- what economic mechanism causes the result.

Avoid unsupported statements such as:

"Because that's how the economy works."

Students must explain reasoning.

==================================================
GRAPH EXPECTATIONS
==================================================

When a graph is required:

Students must correctly identify:

- axes;
- labels;
- curves;
- direction of movement/shift;
- equilibrium where applicable.

A graph without an economic explanation may be incomplete.

==================================================
CALCULATION EXPECTATIONS
==================================================

When calculations are required:

Show enough work to demonstrate:

- correct relationship/formula;
- values used;
- final answer;
- units where appropriate.

Students may also be required to explain the economic meaning of the answer.

==================================================
POSITIVE VS NORMATIVE ECONOMICS
==================================================

Explain briefly:

Economics sometimes examines controversial public-policy questions.

Students are not graded on holding a particular political viewpoint.

Students are graded on:

- accurate economic concepts;
- evidence;
- reasoning;
- identification of tradeoffs.

Students should distinguish:

positive claims

from

normative opinions.

==================================================
POLITICAL NEUTRALITY
==================================================

Students may encounter topics such as:

- taxes;
- government spending;
- regulation;
- trade;
- monetary policy.

The purpose is economic analysis.

Students are not required to support any:

- political party;
- candidate;
- ideology;
- policy position.

==================================================
NOTEBOOK SUBMISSIONS
==================================================

Notebook Evidence must:

- include required components;
- be readable;
- identify the lesson;
- show the student's own work.

Missing required components may result in revision.

==================================================
CHECKPOINT SUBMISSIONS
==================================================

Checkpoint submissions must:

- answer all required questions;
- use complete reasoning where requested;
- use correct economic vocabulary;
- use evidence/graphs/data where required.

Do not combine Notebook and Checkpoint unless Moodle explicitly instructs otherwise.

==================================================
ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
==================================================

Students must submit their own work and reasoning.

External tools, including AI, may not replace:

- calculations;
- graph interpretation;
- Independent Work;
- Checkpoint reasoning;
- assessments.

Students should follow MLA academic-integrity rules regarding permitted assistance.

==================================================
GRADING AND FEEDBACK
==================================================

Use the standard MLA student-facing policy:

TOR grading and feedback are generally completed within 24 business hours during:

Monday–Friday
8:00 AM–5:00 PM Eastern Time

There is generally no routine grading on:

- weekends;
- school holidays.

Do not promise an exact turnaround where exceptional circumstances may occur.

==================================================
TECHNICAL EXPECTATIONS
==================================================

Students need:

- reliable internet access;
- access to Moodle;
- ability to view graphs/tables;
- ability to upload required work.

A basic calculator may be used when permitted.

No specialized economics software is required unless specifically supplied by MLA.

==================================================
ACCESSIBILITY
==================================================

Students who use approved accommodations should follow the accommodations established by MLA.

Course visuals should include accessible alternatives.

If a graph or visual cannot be accessed properly, the student should contact the TOR.

==================================================
CURRENT ECONOMIC INFORMATION
==================================================

Some lessons may use real economic data.

Students should pay attention to:

- source;
- date;
- whether data are current or historical.

Economic conditions change over time.

A historical example should not automatically be treated as a current statistic.

==================================================
COMMUNICATION
==================================================

Students should communicate respectfully and clearly with the TOR.

When asking for help, identify:

- Unit;
- Lesson;
- Page or activity;
- specific question.

NEED HELP?
