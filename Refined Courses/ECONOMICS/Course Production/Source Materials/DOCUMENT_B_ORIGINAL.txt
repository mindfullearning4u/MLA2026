IMPORTANT COURSE-SEPARATION RULE
==================================================

MLA is intentionally separating:

ECONOMICS — 0.5 CREDIT

from

PERSONAL FINANCIAL LITERACY AND MONEY MANAGEMENT — 0.5 CREDIT

The Economics course must therefore remain an economics course.

Florida’s current Economics strand still contains several financial-literacy-heavy benchmarks:

SS.912.E.1.14
Compare credit, savings, and investment services available to the consumer from financial institutions.

SS.912.E.1.15
Describe risk and return profiles of investment vehicles and the importance of diversification.

SS.912.E.1.16
Construct a one-year budget plan for a specific career path including expenses and construction of a credit plan for purchasing a major item.

MLA CROSS-COURSE POLICY:

These three benchmarks must NOT drive substantive Economics lessons.

They will be mastered in the separate course:

PERSONAL FINANCIAL LITERACY AND MONEY MANAGEMENT.

In the Economics crosswalk, mark them:

CROSS-COURSE MASTERY — PERSONAL FINANCIAL LITERACY AND MONEY MANAGEMENT

Economics may make limited contextual reference to:

- financial institutions;
- savings;
- investment;
- credit;
- capital markets

when necessary to explain macroeconomic systems.

However:

Do NOT create personal budgeting lessons.

Do NOT create credit-score lessons.

Do NOT create personal investment-planning lessons.

Do NOT create household debt-management lessons.

Do NOT create major-purchase financing assignments.

Those belong in Personal Financial Literacy.

==================================================
IMPORTANT DEMAND/SUPPLY SEQUENCING RULE
==================================================

Demand must be taught before Supply.

Supply must be taught before Equilibrium.

Equilibrium must be taught before:

- price controls;
- taxes/subsidies where used;
- externalities;
- complex policy intervention.

Do not reorder these concepts.

==================================================
POSITIVE VS NORMATIVE ECONOMICS
==================================================

Document B detailed lessons must distinguish:

POSITIVE CLAIM

from

NORMATIVE CLAIM

when public-policy issues are discussed.

Example:

Positive:

“A binding price ceiling below equilibrium creates a shortage in the standard competitive-market model.”

Normative:

“The government should never use price ceilings.”

Do not present the second statement as a scientific fact.

==================================================
ECONOMICS VS FINANCIAL LITERACY — FINAL CONTROL
==================================================

Economics MAY teach:

- role of financial institutions;
- savings/investment in macroeconomic capital formation;
- money supply;
- interest rates as economic variables;
- banking within the circular flow;
- investment as capital formation.

Economics must NOT become instruction in:

- choosing a checking account;
- building personal credit;
- managing a credit card;
- household budgeting;
- personal retirement planning;
- selecting individual stocks/funds;
- obtaining a mortgage;
- insurance shopping;
- personal tax filing.

Those belong in:

PERSONAL FINANCIAL LITERACY AND MONEY MANAGEMENT.
