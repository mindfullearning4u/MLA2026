==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — WHY TRADE BARRIERS EXIST
==================================================

Teach possible stated policy goals:

- protect domestic industries;
- protect employment in targeted sectors;
- national-security concerns;
- infant-industry rationale;
- respond to unfair-trade allegations;
- protect health/safety/environment;
- raise revenue;
- bargaining/retaliation.

Do NOT state these rationales are automatically valid or invalid.

Students analyze:

goal

vs

economic effect.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — TARIFF
==================================================

Tariff:

tax on imported goods/services.

At introductory level:

tariff raises cost of imported good.

Potential effects:

- domestic price may rise;
- quantity of imports may fall;
- domestic producers may gain market share/output;
- domestic consumers may pay higher prices / buy less;
- government may collect tariff revenue;
- foreign producers may lose sales;
- resources may shift domestically.

Do not claim all effects are identical in every market.

$20

higher price / lower consumption.

producer effect:
higher domestic production.

import effect:
falls.

government:
tariff revenue if applicable.

==================================================
TARIFF REVENUE
==================================================

If tariff:

$5 per imported unit

and imports after tariff:

35 units

Tariff revenue:

$5 × 35
=
$175.

Do not imply this equals total economic benefit.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — DISTRIBUTIONAL EFFECTS OF TARIFF
==================================================

Teach:

Potential domestic winners:

- protected domestic producers;
- some workers/owners in protected sector;
- government receiving revenue.

Potential losers:

- consumers paying higher prices;
- firms using imported inputs;
- foreign exporters;
- downstream industries.

Do not make moral judgment from winner/loser labels.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — QUOTA
==================================================

Quota:

legal limit on quantity/value of imports.

Unlike tariff:

quota directly limits amount.

Potential result:

- imports constrained;
- domestic price may rise;
- domestic production may rise;
- consumption may fall.

Government does NOT automatically receive revenue unless quota rights/licenses are auctioned or otherwise priced.

This distinction is important.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — TARIFF VS QUOTA
==================================================

Required comparison:

TARIFF

- tax on imports;
- changes price/cost;
- may produce government revenue.

QUOTA

- quantity restriction;
- caps imports;
- quota rents may accrue depending on allocation.

Do not treat them as identical.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — EMBARGO
==================================================

Embargo:

broad prohibition or severe restriction on trade with a country or in specified goods/services.

Often linked to:

- foreign policy;
- national security.

Do not frame current geopolitical examples unless sourced and necessary.

Prefer historical or fictional examples.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — FREE TRADE
==================================================

Free trade means reducing/removing trade barriers so exchange occurs more freely across borders.

Potential gains:

- specialization;
- lower opportunity-cost sourcing;
- competition;
- variety;
- larger markets.

Potential adjustment costs:

- import-competing sectors;
- geographic/worker transitions;
- strategic dependence.

Do not teach:

free trade = no rules whatsoever.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 8 — FREE-TRADE AGREEMENTS / ZONES
==================================================

Teach conceptually:

Countries may agree to reduce certain trade barriers among members.

Possible effects:

- more trade among members;
- supply-chain integration;
- potential trade creation;
- potential trade diversion.

Do not require detailed current treaty law.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — RETALIATION
==================================================

A country affected by trade barriers may respond with its own barriers.

Possible result:

- escalation;
- lower trade volumes;
- higher costs;
- supply-chain disruption.

Do not claim retaliation always occurs.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — TRADE BARRIERS AND COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE
==================================================

Reconnect Lessons 1–2.

Barriers can reduce extent to which trade follows comparative advantage.

This may protect certain sectors while reducing some gains from specialization.

Students must see:

policy tradeoff.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 11 — GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAINS
==================================================

Teach:

A final product may involve:

- design in one country;
- inputs from several countries;
- assembly elsewhere;
- global distribution.

Therefore:

trade barriers on intermediate inputs can affect domestic producers as well as foreign suppliers.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 12 — INTERNATIONAL EXTERNALITY
==================================================

International externality:

a cross-border cost or benefit not fully reflected in market transactions.

Example negative:

air pollution produced in one country crosses border and harms another.

Example positive:

international disease surveillance / technological knowledge spillover can benefit others.

Use neutral examples.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 13 — NEGATIVE CROSS-BORDER EXTERNALITY
==================================================

Possible examples:

- transboundary air pollution;
- water pollution;
- greenhouse-gas emissions;
- overuse of shared resources.

Do not turn this into a climate-policy advocacy lesson.

Analyze:

source

third party

external cost.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 14 — POSITIVE CROSS-BORDER EXTERNALITY
==================================================

Possible examples:

- scientific knowledge;
- disease-control information;
- environmental restoration benefiting downstream regions;
- open technology standards.

Students must identify:

direct participant

and

external beneficiary.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 15 — POLICY RESPONSES TO INTERNATIONAL EXTERNALITIES
==================================================

Possible approaches:

- international agreements;
- coordinated standards;
- taxes/fees;
- subsidies;
- monitoring;
- property/resource agreements;
- information sharing.

No one response is universally best.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 16 — INTERDEPENDENCE
==================================================

Trade creates links.

A shock in one economy may affect others through:

- input shortages;
- energy prices;
- financial markets;
- demand for exports;
- shipping/logistics.

Use fictional supply-chain examples.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 17 — POSITIVE VS NORMATIVE TRADE ANALYSIS
==================================================

Positive:

“A $10 tariff raises the importer’s cost by $10 per unit before behavioral adjustments.”

Normative:

“The tariff is justified.”

Students must distinguish.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 3
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Tariffs are paid only by foreign producers.

CORRECTION:

Economic burden can be distributed among importers, consumers, producers, and foreign sellers depending on market conditions.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

A tariff always increases government revenue indefinitely.

CORRECTION:

Revenue depends on tariff rate and remaining import quantity.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Quota and tariff are identical.

CORRECTION:

One taxes imports; the other directly limits quantity.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Trade barriers benefit everyone domestically.

CORRECTION:

They create distributional winners and losers.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Free trade guarantees every worker benefits.

CORRECTION:

Aggregate gains may coexist with adjustment costs.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

An international externality is simply any effect on another country.

CORRECTION:

It must be a spillover cost/benefit not fully reflected in the transaction.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Protectionism is automatically good or bad.

CORRECTION:

Economic analysis examines objectives, effects, tradeoffs, and evidence.

TERMINOLOGY CONTROL
==================================================

Terms such as:

developed

developing

emerging

are broad classifications.

They can obscure variation.

Teach students to rely on:

specific indicators

rather than stereotypes.

Do not describe countries as:

“advanced people”

or

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — GROWTH VS DEVELOPMENT
==================================================

ECONOMIC GROWTH:

increase in real output/productive capacity over time.

ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT:

broader process involving improvements in economic capacity and living conditions, which may include:

- income;
- health;
- education;
- infrastructure;
- institutional capacity;
- productivity.

Growth can contribute to development but is not identical.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — GDP LEVEL VS GDP PER CAPITA
==================================================

GDP:

total domestic output.

GDP per capita:

GDP ÷ population.

A large-population economy may have:

high total GDP

but

lower GDP per capita than a smaller economy.

Required calculation examples.

==================================================
REQUIRED EXAMPLE
==================================================

Country A:

GDP:
$2 trillion

Population:
200 million

GDP per capita:
$10,000.

Country B:

GDP:
$500 billion

Population:
25 million

GDP per capita:
$20,000.

Conclusion:

A has larger total economy.

B has higher GDP per capita.

Neither fact alone establishes overall quality of life.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — LEVEL VS GROWTH RATE
==================================================

This distinction is mandatory.

COUNTRY A:

GDP per capita:
$60,000

Growth:
2%.

COUNTRY B:

GDP per capita:
$12,000

Growth:
6%.

Country B grows faster.

Country A remains at higher income/output level.

Do not say:

“Country B is richer because it grows faster.”

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — PRODUCTIVITY
==================================================

Higher productivity can support:

- higher real output;
- higher potential income;
- long-run growth.

But productivity alone does not describe:

- distribution;
- health;
- education;
- environmental quality.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — INFLATION
==================================================

High or unstable inflation can complicate:

- planning;
- saving;
- investment;
- purchasing power.

But low inflation alone does not establish high development.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — LABOR MARKET
==================================================

Employment/unemployment may provide useful information.

However:

cross-country comparisons can be difficult because:

- labor-force definitions;
- informal work;
- data quality;
- participation patterns

may differ.

Students must interpret cautiously.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — POVERTY
==================================================

Teach conceptually.

Poverty measures identify population shares below defined thresholds.

Important:

Thresholds and methodologies vary.

Do not compare countries using poverty percentages without checking definitions.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 8 — INCOME DISTRIBUTION
==================================================

GDP per capita is an average.

It does NOT show how income/output is distributed.

Two economies with same GDP per capita can have very different distributions.

==================================================
OPTIONAL LORENZ CURVE INTRODUCTION
==================================================

If useful and accessible:

introduce Lorenz curve conceptually.

Do not require Gini coefficient calculation unless separately approved.

Purpose:

show that averages do not reveal distribution.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — HEALTH INDICATORS
==================================================

Possible indicators:

- life expectancy;
- infant mortality where carefully handled;
- healthcare access.

Do not treat health outcomes as caused solely by income.

Use correlation/causation caution.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — EDUCATION / HUMAN CAPITAL
==================================================

Possible indicators:

- literacy;
- years of schooling;
- completion rates;
- skill levels.

Education can support human capital/productivity.

Do not claim schooling alone causes all economic development.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 11 — INFRASTRUCTURE
==================================================

Infrastructure may include:

- transportation;
- energy;
- communications;
- water/sanitation;
- digital access.

It can support productivity and market access.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 12 — INSTITUTIONS
==================================================

At introductory level:

institutions can include:

- legal systems;
- property rights;
- contract enforcement;
- public administration;
- financial institutions.

These can influence economic incentives and investment.

Do not turn into political-system ranking.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 13 — MULTIDIMENSIONAL COMPARISON
==================================================

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 14 — DATA QUALITY
==================================================

Teach:

International data may differ because of:

- measurement method;
- reporting quality;
- informal economy;
- exchange-rate conversion;
- purchasing-power differences;
- definitions;
- time periods.

Students must check source/date.

==================================================
PURCHASING POWER PARITY — CONCEPTUAL ONLY
==================================================

Introduce if useful:

Purchasing-power adjustments attempt to account for differences in price levels across countries.

Do NOT require PPP calculations.

Explain only why market-exchange-rate comparisons may not fully reflect domestic purchasing power.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 15 — CORRELATION VS CAUSATION
==================================================

Example:

Countries with more infrastructure also show higher GDP per capita.

Valid:

association.

Not enough:

“roads alone caused the income difference.”

Potential confounders:

- history;
- institutions;
- education;
- resources;
- investment;
- geography.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 16 — CONVERGENCE BOUNDARY
==================================================

Introduce only conceptually:

Lower-income economies can sometimes grow faster as they adopt technologies/capital.

But convergence is NOT automatic.

Do not teach advanced growth theory.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 4
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Largest GDP means highest living standard.

CORRECTION:

Population size matters; GDP per capita provides different information.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Fastest-growing economy is the richest.

CORRECTION:

Growth rate and income level are different.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

GDP per capita shows what every person earns.

CORRECTION:

It is an average output measure, not individual salary.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

High GDP per capita proves income is evenly distributed.

CORRECTION:

Averages do not show distribution.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

One indicator can determine whether an economy is developed.

CORRECTION:

Development is multidimensional.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Higher education levels prove education alone caused economic growth.

CORRECTION:

Correlation does not establish sole causation.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Developing economies are all alike.

CORRECTION:

They vary substantially across income, institutions, health, resources, and growth.
