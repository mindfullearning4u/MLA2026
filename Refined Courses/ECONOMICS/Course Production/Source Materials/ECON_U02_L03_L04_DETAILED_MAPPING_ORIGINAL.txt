CORE CONCEPT 1 — COMBINING DEMAND AND SUPPLY
==================================================

For the first time in the course, place:

DEMAND

and

SUPPLY

on the SAME graph.

Required graph conventions:

Vertical axis:
PRICE

Horizontal axis:
QUANTITY

Demand:
D

Supply:
S

Intersection:
E

Label:

P*
for equilibrium price

Q*
for equilibrium quantity

Explain:

The equilibrium point is where:

QUANTITY DEMANDED
=
QUANTITY SUPPLIED.

==================================================
REQUIRED EQUILIBRIUM TABLE
==================================================

Use a fictional market.

Example:

PRICE | QD | QS

$10 | 90 | 20
$20 | 70 | 35
$30 | 50 | 50
$40 | 30 | 65
$50 | 10 | 80

Equilibrium:

Price = $30

Quantity = 50

Students must identify equilibrium from the table before graphing.

==================================================
EQUILIBRIUM IS NOT “PERFECT”
==================================================

Critical misconception prevention:

Do NOT define equilibrium as:

- fair;
- morally ideal;
- best for everyone;
- permanent;
- no one ever wants anything different.

Equilibrium means:

under the model, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at that price.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — SHORTAGE
==================================================

Teach:

A shortage occurs when:

QD > QS

at a given price.

Formula:

Shortage Size
=
QD − QS

Example:

At $20:

QD = 70

QS = 35

Shortage:

35 units.

==================================================
SHORTAGE MECHANISM
==================================================

In the standard competitive market model:

Shortage

→ buyers compete for limited units

→ sellers have incentive to raise price

→ quantity demanded falls

→ quantity supplied rises

→ market moves toward equilibrium.

Teach as a model mechanism.

Do not say:

“Price always instantly rises.”

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — SURPLUS
==================================================

Teach:

A surplus occurs when:

QS > QD.

Formula:

Surplus Size
=
QS − QD.

Example:

At $40:

QS = 65

QD = 30

Surplus:

35 units.

==================================================
SURPLUS MECHANISM
==================================================

In the standard model:

Surplus

→ unsold inventory / excess supply

→ sellers have incentive to lower price

→ quantity demanded rises

→ quantity supplied falls

→ market moves toward equilibrium.

Again:

Do not claim adjustment is instantaneous in real markets.

==================================================
PRICE ABOVE / BELOW EQUILIBRIUM
==================================================

Teach:

PRICE BELOW EQUILIBRIUM

usually produces:

shortage.

PRICE ABOVE EQUILIBRIUM

usually produces:

surplus.

Students must reason from QD and QS rather than memorize only position.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — PRICE AS A SIGNAL
==================================================

Teach:

Prices can communicate information about:

- relative scarcity;
- consumer willingness to buy;
- producer willingness to sell.

Changing prices can influence:

- consumer decisions;
- producer decisions.

Do not say prices contain perfect information.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — DEMAND SHIFT AND NEW EQUILIBRIUM
==================================================

Case:

Demand increases.

Supply unchanged.

Standard result:

Equilibrium price:
rises.

Equilibrium quantity:
rises.

Required visual:

D1 → D2 right

S unchanged

E1 → E2.

==================================================
DEMAND DECREASE
==================================================

Demand decreases.

Supply unchanged.

Standard result:

Equilibrium price:
falls.

Equilibrium quantity:
falls.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — SUPPLY SHIFT AND NEW EQUILIBRIUM
==================================================

Supply increases.

Demand unchanged.

Standard result:

Equilibrium price:
falls.

Equilibrium quantity:
rises.

Supply decreases.

Demand unchanged.

Standard result:

Equilibrium price:
rises.

Equilibrium quantity:
falls.

REQUIRED FOUR-SHIFT MATRIX
==================================================

Create a permanent student reference:

CHANGE | PRICE | QUANTITY

Demand ↑ | ↑ | ↑

Demand ↓ | ↓ | ↓

Supply ↑ | ↓ | ↑

Supply ↓ | ↑ | ↓

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — SIMULTANEOUS SHIFTS
==================================================

Introduce carefully.

Case:

Demand ↑
AND
Supply ↑

Quantity:
increases.

Price:
ambiguous unless relative shift magnitude is known.

Case:

Demand ↓
AND
Supply ↓

Quantity:
decreases.

Price:
ambiguous.

Case:

Demand ↑
AND
Supply ↓

Price:
increases.

Quantity:
ambiguous.

Case:

Demand ↓
AND
Supply ↑

Price:
decreases.

Quantity:
ambiguous.

This is important reasoning.

Do NOT force a determinate answer when the model does not support one.

==================================================
AMBIGUOUS DOES NOT MEAN UNKNOWN EVERYTHING
==================================================

Teach:

Sometimes one variable is predictable while another is not.

Example:

Demand increases
and
Supply decreases.

Both put upward pressure on price.

Therefore:

PRICE definitely rises.

But quantity effect depends on relative shift sizes.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 3
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Equilibrium means fair price.

CORRECTION:

It means QD = QS.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Any price below equilibrium is a surplus.

CORRECTION:

Below equilibrium typically creates a shortage.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Shortage means supply curve shifted left.

CORRECTION:

A shortage can occur simply because current price is below equilibrium.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Surplus means demand decreased.

CORRECTION:

A surplus can occur because price is above equilibrium without either curve shifting.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

If demand increases, only price changes.

CORRECTION:

With supply unchanged, both equilibrium price and quantity rise.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

If supply increases, price and quantity both rise.

CORRECTION:

Price falls while quantity rises.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Every simultaneous shift has one fully predictable result.

CORRECTION:

One variable may be ambiguous unless shift magnitude is known.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — WHAT IS A FIRM?
==================================================

Teach:

A firm is an economic organization that combines resources to produce and/or sell goods and services.

Firms use:

- land;
- labor;
- capital;
- entrepreneurship.

Their decisions include:

- what to produce;
- how much;
- how to produce;
- what resources to use;
- how to organize ownership and control.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — SOLE PROPRIETORSHIP
==================================================

Teach:

Owned by one person.

Potential advantages:

- simple formation;
- direct control;
- owner receives residual profit.

Potential limitations:

- unlimited personal liability in many contexts;
- limited access to capital;
- owner bears substantial risk;
- continuity may depend heavily on owner.

Do not provide legal advice.

Explain conceptually.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — PARTNERSHIP
==================================================

Teach:

Two or more owners share ownership under an agreed structure.

Potential advantages:

- pooled resources;
- shared expertise;
- shared responsibility.

Potential limitations:

- conflicts;
- shared profits;
- liability depending on structure;
- decision complexity.

Important:

Do not imply all partnerships have identical liability rules.

State:

specific legal treatment depends on jurisdiction and partnership form.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — CORPORATION
==================================================

Teach:

A corporation is a legal entity distinct from its owners.

Potential characteristics:

- ownership through shares;
- limited liability for shareholders;
- continuity beyond individual owners;
- ability to raise substantial capital.

Potential limitations:

- more complex organization;
- regulatory/administrative requirements;
- separation of ownership and management.

Do not turn this into corporate-law instruction.

CORE CONCEPT 5 — LIABILITY
==================================================

Teach:

LIABILITY

refers to legal responsibility for debts or obligations.

UNLIMITED LIABILITY:

Owner may be personally responsible for business obligations depending on legal form.

LIMITED LIABILITY:

Owner/shareholder financial exposure is generally limited according to legal structure.

Do not provide legal planning advice.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — OWNERSHIP VS CONTROL
==================================================

Teach:

In a small sole proprietorship:

ownership and management may be the same person.

In a large corporation:

ownership may be dispersed among shareholders while managers make daily decisions.

This distinction prepares students for later firm behavior analysis.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — FIRM REVENUE, COST, PROFIT
==================================================

TOTAL REVENUE

money received from sales.

TR

TOTAL COST

resources expended to produce output.

PROFIT

=
Total Revenue − Total Cost

If negative:

loss.

CORE CONCEPT 8 — FIXED AND VARIABLE COST
==================================================

FIXED COST:

does not change with short-run output in the simplified period.

Examples:

- monthly facility lease;
- certain insurance payments.

VARIABLE COST:

changes with output.

Examples:

- materials;
- hourly production labor where tied to output;
- packaging.

Do not claim a cost is permanently fixed forever.

The distinction depends on the relevant time period.

==================================================

Teach:

Choosing a business form involves tradeoffs.

Example:

Sole proprietor may gain:

control

but face:

greater personal risk / limited capital access.

Corporation may gain:

capital access / continuity / limited shareholder liability

but face:

greater complexity and separation of ownership/control.

Do not tell students which structure they personally should choose.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 4
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

A sole proprietorship has no risk because one person controls it.

CORRECTION:

Control does not eliminate financial or legal risk.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Partnership always means equal ownership.

CORRECTION:

Ownership arrangements can vary.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Corporation means government-owned company.

CORRECTION:

A corporation is a legal business form and may be privately owned through shares.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Limited liability means no one is ever legally responsible.

CORRECTION:

It limits owner/shareholder exposure under the legal structure; it does not eliminate all obligations or misconduct liability.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Revenue and profit are the same.

CORRECTION:

Profit equals revenue minus cost.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Fixed costs never change.

CORRECTION:

They are fixed relative to output during the modeled period.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Higher sales always mean higher profit.

CORRECTION:

Costs matter.

---

MISCONCEPTION 8:

A firm should produce as much as physically possible.

CORRECTION:

Additional output should be evaluated using costs, benefits, demand, and constraints.

AUTOMATIC REVISION TRIGGERS
==================================================

Require revision if:

- profit calculated as cost minus revenue;

6. Surplus size is QS − QD.

12. Business forms are described accurately but not as legal advice.

14. Corporation is not equated with government ownership.
