LESSON 5 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
==================================================

How have major economic thinkers explained markets, production, trade, government, and economic instability differently, and how can economic evidence be used to evaluate competing explanations and policy choices?

==================================================
LESSON 5 LEARNING OBJECTIVES
==================================================

Students will be able to:

1. Define economic thought.
2. Explain why economic ideas develop in historical context.
3. Identify major ideas associated with selected historical economists.
4. Distinguish economic ideas from political slogans.
5. Explain Adam Smith’s emphasis on specialization, exchange, competition, and self-interest within institutions.
6. Explain David Ricardo’s comparative-advantage contribution.
7. Explain Karl Marx’s critique of ownership, class, labor, and capitalist production at an introductory level.
8. Explain John Maynard Keynes’s analysis of aggregate demand and economic instability.
9. Explain Friedrich Hayek’s emphasis on dispersed knowledge, prices, and decentralized coordination.
10. Explain Milton Friedman’s emphasis on monetary conditions and limited discretionary stabilization.
11. Compare at least four economic thinkers using common analytical dimensions.
12. Explain how economists may disagree because of assumptions, evidence, models, and values.
13. Distinguish positive economic claims from normative claims.
14. Distinguish description from prescription.
15. Identify the economic problem addressed by a theory.
16. Identify the mechanism proposed by a theory.
17. Identify the role assigned to markets.
18. Identify the role assigned to government.
19. Identify limitations of simplified representations of economic thought.
20. Apply competing economic perspectives to a fictional economic problem.
21. Evaluate economic evidence rather than selecting a theory by preference.
22. Integrate microeconomic, macroeconomic, policy, and international concepts in one analysis.
23. Identify tradeoffs and unintended consequences.
24. Explain when available evidence is insufficient to choose confidently among alternatives.
25. Produce a final evidence-based economic recommendation.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 5
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Adam Smith argued government should never have any economic role.

CORRECTION:

His thought was more nuanced; avoid reducing it to absolute laissez-faire.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Ricardo argued the most productive nation should produce everything.

CORRECTION:

Comparative advantage depends on relative opportunity cost.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Marx's economic thought can be summarized simply as government ownership.

CORRECTION:

His analysis included production, class, labor, ownership, and distribution.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Keynes believed government spending should always increase.

CORRECTION:

His stabilization analysis focused substantially on insufficient aggregate demand and downturns.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Hayek argued all government action is economically harmful.

CORRECTION:

His key economic concern involved dispersed information and limits of centralized coordination.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Friedman believed every economic problem is caused only by money supply.

CORRECTION:

His work was broader; monetary stability was a major emphasis.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Economic theory is just political opinion.

CORRECTION:

Economic theories contain mechanisms and claims that can be evaluated using evidence.

---

MISCONCEPTION 8:

One economic theory explains every economic problem best.

CORRECTION:

Different frameworks emphasize different mechanisms and may be more or less useful depending on the question.
