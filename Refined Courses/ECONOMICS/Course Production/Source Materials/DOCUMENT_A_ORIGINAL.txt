==================================================
PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
==================================================

You are creating Document A for the Mindful Learning Academy (MLA) high-school course:

ECONOMICS

Document A controls HOW the Economics course is constructed.

Document A does NOT determine the final unit titles, lesson titles, exact lesson standards, or detailed instructional content.

Those belong in Document B.

The purpose of Document A is to establish the permanent production architecture, instructional rules, assessment rules, Moodle requirements, quantitative reasoning expectations, economic-model requirements, TOR grading expectations, accessibility requirements, and QA controls for the entire Economics course.

Document A must be written specifically for Economics.

Do NOT copy a generic architecture from U.S. History, World History, PE/Health, or another MLA course.

Reuse MLA-wide policies only when appropriate.

- banking and money;
- international trade;

==================================================
ECONOMIC REASONING MODEL
==================================================

Use the following MLA economic reasoning process throughout the course when appropriate:

1. IDENTIFY THE ECONOMIC QUESTION

What decision, market behavior, policy, or economic outcome is being analyzed?

2. IDENTIFY THE RELEVANT CONCEPT

Examples:

- scarcity;
- opportunity cost;
- incentives;
- supply;
- demand;
- elasticity;
- externality;
- unemployment;
- inflation.

3. IDENTIFY THE ACTORS

Examples:

- households;
- firms;
- workers;
- consumers;
- producers;
- government;
- financial institutions;
- foreign buyers/sellers.

4. IDENTIFY INCENTIVES

What factors encourage or discourage particular behavior?

5. IDENTIFY TRADEOFFS

What is gained?

What is sacrificed?

6. INTERPRET THE EVIDENCE

Use:

- graph;
- data;
- table;
- scenario;
- economic model.

7. PREDICT THE ECONOMIC EFFECT

What is likely to happen?

8. EXPLAIN THE MECHANISM

Why does the effect occur?

9. IDENTIFY LIMITATIONS

What assumptions or missing information affect the conclusion?

This process should be modeled repeatedly.

==================================================
QUANTITATIVE REASONING
==================================================

Economics must include substantial quantitative reasoning.

Students must learn how to:

- interpret tables;
- read economic graphs;
- compare rates;
- calculate percentage changes where appropriate;
- calculate simple economic indicators when mapped;
- interpret index values;
- analyze price and quantity changes;
- interpret equilibrium;
- compare costs and benefits;
- analyze economic trends.

Calculators may be used when appropriate.

Do NOT make arithmetic the primary objective when economic interpretation is the actual standard.

Required pattern:

CALCULATE
→ INTERPRET
→ EXPLAIN ECONOMIC MEANING.

Possible models include:

==================================================
GRAPH INSTRUCTION RULE
==================================================

Students must be explicitly taught how to read economic graphs.

Do NOT assume students already understand:

- axes;
- curves;
- slope;
- equilibrium;
- movement along a curve;
- curve shift;
- intersection;
- quantity;
- price;
- index values;
- time-series graphs.

Whenever a new graph type appears:

1. identify axes;
2. identify variables;
3. explain the relationship;
4. model interpretation;
5. show one worked example;
6. provide guided practice;
7. require independent interpretation.

==================================================
SUPPLY / DEMAND CONTROL
==================================================

When supply and demand are eventually taught, maintain strict conceptual distinctions:

MOVEMENT ALONG DEMAND CURVE

caused by:

change in price of the good itself.

DEMAND SHIFT

caused by a non-price determinant.

MOVEMENT ALONG SUPPLY CURVE

caused by:

change in price of the good itself.

SUPPLY SHIFT

caused by a non-price determinant.

Codex must not use imprecise language such as:

“Demand increased because the price fell”

when describing a movement along the same demand curve.

Economic vocabulary must remain technically accurate.

==================================================
MODEL ASSUMPTIONS
==================================================

Students must understand that economic models simplify reality.

Each major model should identify relevant assumptions or limitations where appropriate.

Do NOT teach:

“The graph proves this always happens.”

Teach:

“The model predicts this outcome under the stated conditions.”

==================================================
CURRENT ECONOMIC DATA
==================================================

Static economics instruction should not depend unnecessarily on rapidly changing current data.

When real-world economic data are used:

- clearly identify date;
- identify source;
- distinguish historical example from current condition;
- use authoritative sources.

Potential authoritative sources include:

- U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis;
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics;
- Federal Reserve;
- U.S. Census Bureau;
- Congressional Budget Office where appropriate;
- World Bank;
- IMF;
- U.S. government economic data.

Do NOT build permanent lesson conclusions around current figures that will quickly become outdated.

Prefer fictionalized or historical datasets when the instructional objective is stable.

==================================================
POLITICAL / POLICY NEUTRALITY
==================================================

Economics includes contested public-policy questions.

The course must remain analytically neutral.

Do NOT:

- advocate for a political party;
- advocate for a candidate;
- tell students what political position they should hold;
- present one economic school of thought as morally correct;
- characterize disagreement as ignorance.

When appropriate, students should examine:

- intended policy goal;
- economic mechanism;
- predicted effects;
- tradeoffs;
- distributional effects;
- assumptions;
- evidence;
- limitations.

Different economic perspectives may be presented when academically relevant.

==================================================
NORMATIVE VS POSITIVE ECONOMICS
==================================================

The course must explicitly distinguish:

POSITIVE ECONOMICS

Statements about:

- what is;
- what happened;
- what a model predicts.

NORMATIVE ECONOMICS

Statements involving:

- what should happen;
- values;
- policy preferences.

Students must not present normative preferences as empirical facts.

LESSON COUNT

This page must contain substantive instruction.

==================================================
MODELED REASONING
==================================================

Economics requires explicit modeling.

Examples must show:

QUESTION

→ RELEVANT CONCEPT

→ EVIDENCE / GRAPH / DATA

→ ECONOMIC MECHANISM

→ CONCLUSION

Students must see the reasoning.

Do not simply reveal answers.

==================================================
GUIDED PRACTICE
==================================================

Guided Practice must occur BEFORE Independent Work.

Guided Practice should include:

- prompts;
- scaffolds;
- partially completed examples;
- graph interpretation;
- data interpretation;
- immediate feedback/explanations.

When a student selects an incorrect answer in interactive practice, the feedback should explain WHY.

==================================================
INDEPENDENT WORK
==================================================

Independent Work is required in every instructional lesson.

It must occur:

AFTER Guided Practice

and

BEFORE the Mastery Checkpoint.

Independent Work should require students to apply the lesson concept without step-by-step scaffolding.

Possible tasks include:

- scenario analysis;
- graph analysis;
- data interpretation;
- economic comparison;
- calculation plus explanation;
- short constructed response;
- policy-tradeoff analysis.

Independent Work is NOT a separate Moodle assignment.

It is submitted as part of Notebook Evidence.

==================================================
NOTEBOOK SUBMISSION
==================================================

Every lesson must include a separate Moodle Notebook Submission.

Notebook Evidence contains:

1. Vocabulary
2. Specifically mapped lesson evidence
3. Independent Work

Do NOT automatically require every minor activity from every page.

Document B identifies required evidence.

==================================================
NOTEBOOK RUBRIC
==================================================

Every Notebook Submission must have an objective rubric.

Use measurable criteria.

Avoid subjective terms such as:

- excellent;
- thoughtful;
- strong understanding;
- high-quality response.

Prefer:

- all required vocabulary included;
- graph correctly labeled;
- equilibrium correctly identified;
- calculation correct;
- explanation names the correct economic mechanism;
- required evidence cited.

==================================================
TOR NOTEBOOK GRADING KEY
==================================================

Document B must include a TOR grading key for every Notebook.

For objective questions:

Provide exact answer.

For calculations:

Provide:

- formula;
- work;
- correct result;
- acceptable rounding.

For graphs:

Provide:

- required axes;
- curve labels;
- direction of shift;
- equilibrium movement;
- expected interpretation.

For open responses:

Provide:

- required elements;
- acceptable alternatives;
- unacceptable reasoning;
- common misconceptions;
- automatic revision triggers.

TOR should not be required to independently invent the grading standard.

==================================================
MASTERY CHECKPOINT
==================================================

Every instructional lesson must include a Mastery Checkpoint.

The Checkpoint must:

- assess the Essential Question;
- use a NEW scenario/data set;
- require independent reasoning;
- contain no major new content.

Checkpoint Submission must contain ONLY the checkpoint response.

Do not resubmit Notebook work.

==================================================
CHECKPOINT RUBRIC
==================================================

Every Checkpoint must have an objective rubric and TOR grading key.

Checkpoint tasks should usually prioritize:

- correct economic concept;
- graph/data interpretation;
- economic mechanism;
- evidence;
- reasoning.

==================================================
QUIZZES
==================================================

Do NOT automatically create a quiz for every lesson.

Document B determines which lessons require quizzes.

A quiz is useful when a lesson contains substantial:

- vocabulary;
- graph interpretation;
- model interpretation;
- quantitative reasoning;
- concept discrimination.

Major applied/capstone lessons may not need a quiz when Notebook + Checkpoint + Unit Assessment already provide sufficient evidence.

==================================================
ECONOMIC CALCULATIONS
==================================================

When calculations are part of the lesson:

Students must be taught:

1. what the variables mean;
2. the formula or relationship;
3. units;
4. worked example;
5. interpretation.

Example pattern:

CALCULATION

→ ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION.

A correct number without understanding may not demonstrate full mastery.

==================================================
FORMULA RULE
==================================================

Do not overload students with formulas that are not standards-relevant.

Formulas should serve economic reasoning.

Possible calculations may include, where mapped:

- opportunity cost;
- percent change;
- inflation rate;
- unemployment rate;
- GDP/value-added;
- real versus nominal comparisons;
- simple productivity calculations;
- exchange-rate conversions.

Document B determines exact requirements.

==================================================
SPREADSHEETS
==================================================

Economics may use spreadsheets when they meaningfully improve:

- data organization;
- trend analysis;
- calculations;
- graph construction.

Do NOT require a spreadsheet when a simple table/calculation is sufficient.

If used:

- provide exact instructions;
- provide template or data;
- make the task accessible;
- do not assume advanced spreadsheet skill.

==================================================

==================================================
POLITICAL BALANCE
==================================================

- taxes;
- government spending;
- regulation;
- minimum wage;

- monetary policy;

- welfare programs;

==================================================
MISCONCEPTIONS
==================================================

Every detailed lesson mapping in Document B must identify likely misconceptions.

Examples:

- scarcity means poverty;
- opportunity cost is only money;
- demand means desire;
- a fall in price shifts demand;
- supply means quantity supplied;
- equilibrium means morally fair;
- GDP measures all well-being;
- inflation means every price rises;
- unemployment rate includes every adult without a job;
- imports are automatically harmful;
- trade requires one country to lose;
- printing money automatically creates prosperity.

Each misconception should include an explicit correction.
