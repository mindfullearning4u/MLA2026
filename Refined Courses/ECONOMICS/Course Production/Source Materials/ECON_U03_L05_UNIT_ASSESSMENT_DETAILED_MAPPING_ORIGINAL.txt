==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — BUSINESS CYCLE
==================================================

Teach:

The business cycle refers to recurring fluctuations in broad economic activity around a longer-term growth trend.

Critical:

The cycle is NOT:

- perfectly regular;
- identical in duration;
- identical in severity;
- mechanically predictable.

Do not draw a perfect sine wave and imply real economies behave exactly that way.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — EXPANSION
==================================================

Teach:

During an expansion, broad economic activity is generally increasing.

Typical patterns MAY include:

- rising real output;
- increasing employment;
- declining cyclical unemployment;
- increased production;
- increased spending;
- possible upward inflation pressure.

Important:

Do not teach:

“Expansion means inflation must always be high.”

==================================================
EXPANSION IS ABOUT DIRECTION
==================================================

Critical:

A country can be in expansion even if economic activity remains below a previous peak.

If activity is rising after a trough:

the economy is expanding.

Students must classify based on direction, not merely absolute level.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — PEAK
==================================================

Peak:

the upper turning point where an expansion ends and contraction begins.

Do not define:

“highest economic level ever recorded.”

A peak is a local turning point in the cycle.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — CONTRACTION
==================================================

During contraction:

broad economic activity generally declines.

Possible patterns:

- falling real output;
- weaker production;
- reduced hiring;
- rising cyclical unemployment;
- slower spending.

Inflation may:

- slow;
- remain elevated;
- occasionally coexist with weak output

depending on economic circumstances.

Do not teach an inflexible inflation rule.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — TROUGH
==================================================

Trough:

the lower turning point where contraction ends and expansion begins.

Do not define:

“lowest economic level in history.”

It is the low turning point of that specific cycle.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — BUSINESS CYCLE VS LONG-RUN GROWTH
==================================================

Teach explicitly:

BUSINESS CYCLE

=
shorter-run fluctuations in economic activity.

LONG-RUN ECONOMIC GROWTH

=
increase in productive capacity / real output potential over longer periods.

An economy can:

grow over decades

while still experiencing temporary contractions.

==================================================
REQUIRED VISUAL
==================================================

Show:

upward long-run trend line

with fluctuations around it.

Students must distinguish:

TREND

from

CYCLE.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — REAL GDP AND BUSINESS CYCLE
==================================================

Reconnect Lesson 2.

Real GDP is a major measure of actual national output.

Typical relationship:

Expansion:
real output rising.

Contraction:
real output declining.

But:

one small quarterly movement should not automatically be treated as sufficient evidence of an official recession.

==================================================
RECESSION PRECISION
==================================================

Do NOT teach:

“A recession is always exactly two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP”

as a universal official definition.

At course level explain:

The two-quarter rule is a common shorthand, but recession identification can consider a broader range of economic activity indicators.

For permanent Moodle content:

avoid depending on one current institutional dating decision.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 8 — UNEMPLOYMENT AND THE CYCLE
==================================================

Reconnect Lesson 4.

During contraction:

cyclical unemployment may increase as:

- sales weaken;
- output falls;
- firms reduce labor demand.

During expansion:

cyclical unemployment may decline.

Do NOT claim:

all unemployment is cyclical.

Frictional and structural unemployment can exist throughout the cycle.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — INFLATION AND THE CYCLE
==================================================

Reconnect Lesson 3.

In some expansions:

strong demand may contribute to rising inflation pressure.

During contractions:

inflationary pressure may weaken.

But:

supply shocks and other conditions can complicate the relationship.

==================================================
IMPORTANT STAGFLATION BOUNDARY
==================================================

Introduce conceptually if appropriate:

An economy can experience:

weak/stagnant output

and

high inflation

at the same time.

This demonstrates why students should not memorize:

contraction = low inflation

as a universal rule.

Do not require advanced macroeconomic modeling.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — MULTIPLE INDICATORS
==================================================

Students should assess multiple measures.

Possible indicators:

- real GDP;
- employment;
- unemployment;
- labor-force participation;
- inflation;
- production;
- income;
- spending.

Do not use one indicator alone when the scenario provides multiple data points.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 11 — INDICATOR TIMING
==================================================

At an introductory level, teach:

LEADING INDICATOR

may change before broader economic activity.

COINCIDENT INDICATOR

moves roughly with current economic activity.

LAGGING INDICATOR

may respond after broader changes occur.

Do not require memorization of large indicator lists.

Use simple examples.

==================================================
POSSIBLE EXAMPLES
==================================================

Leading:
new orders or expectations measure.

Coincident:
current production/employment measure.

Lagging:
some unemployment or price effects may adjust after changes.

Important:

Actual classification of indicators can depend on the source/model.

Do not overstate.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 12 — MIXED SIGNALS
==================================================

Real economies do not always provide perfectly aligned indicators.

Example:

Real output:
rising slowly.

Employment:
rising.

Inflation:
falling.

This can still be consistent with expansion plus disinflation.

Another example:

Real output:
declining.

Inflation:
still high.

Unemployment:
starting to rise.

This may indicate contraction with persistent inflation.

Students must interpret evidence.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 13 — CORRELATION VS CAUSATION
==================================================

Business-cycle indicators often move together.

That does NOT mean:

one indicator automatically caused another.

Example:

Output declines and unemployment rises.

Valid:

The indicators are consistent with contraction.

Do not automatically claim:

unemployment caused output decline

without additional evidence.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 14 — BUSINESS-CYCLE CAUSES
==================================================

At this stage, introduce broad possible sources only:

- changes in consumer/business spending;
- investment changes;
- financial conditions;
- supply disruptions;
- external shocks;
- policy changes;
- expectations.

Do NOT teach one universal cause.

Detailed policy response belongs in Unit 4.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 15 — ECONOMIC GOALS
==================================================

Reconnect Unit 1.

Major national goals include:

- growth;
- price stability;
- full employment.

Business cycles can make these goals harder to achieve simultaneously.

Example:

Strong expansion may reduce unemployment while increasing inflation pressure.

Do not present this as an automatic fixed tradeoff.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 5
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Business cycles occur at fixed intervals.

CORRECTION:

Timing and severity vary.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Expansion means the economy is at its highest level ever.

CORRECTION:

Expansion means broad activity is rising.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Peak means the economy is permanently at maximum capacity.

CORRECTION:

Peak is a turning point between expansion and contraction.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Trough means the lowest output ever recorded.

CORRECTION:

It is the low turning point of a particular cycle.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Every contraction has deflation.

CORRECTION:

Inflation may slow, persist, or occasionally remain elevated.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

All unemployment during contraction is cyclical.

CORRECTION:

Frictional and structural unemployment remain.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

A recession is always officially defined as exactly two quarters of declining GDP.

CORRECTION:

That is a common shorthand; broader measures can be used to identify recessions.

---

MISCONCEPTION 8:

If unemployment falls, the economy must be expanding strongly.

CORRECTION:

Participation and employment levels also matter.

---

MISCONCEPTION 9:

Economic growth means the economy never contracts.

CORRECTION:

Long-run growth can coexist with shorter-run contractions.
