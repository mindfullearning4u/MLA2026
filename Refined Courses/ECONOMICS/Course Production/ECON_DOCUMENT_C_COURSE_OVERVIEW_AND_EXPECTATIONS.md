# Economics: Document C — Course Overview and Expectations

**Organization:** Mindful Learning Academy (MLA)  
**Course:** Economics (`ECON`)  
**Credit:** 0.5 high-school credit  
**Subject:** Social Studies  
**Delivery:** 100% online, asynchronous, mastery-based, through Moodle  
**Placement:** Before Unit 1  
**Status:** Student-facing orientation content approved for Moodle implementation

## Moodle implementation notes

Create exactly four activities in the order shown below. Pages 1–3 are Moodle **Page** resources. Page 4 is a required acknowledgement activity configured to record completion before Unit 1. Copy only the HTML inside each page's code block into Moodle's HTML editor. Do not copy this introduction, implementation notes, or QA record into student-facing Moodle content.

Use Moodle activity completion to require Pages 1–3 to be viewed and Page 4 to be submitted before Unit 1 opens. Do not use a visual that conveys instructional information unless it has meaningful alternative text. The supplied pages require no images.

    <ol class="mla-econ-steps">
      <li>Lesson Overview and Vocabulary</li>
      <li>Learn the Economic Concept</li>
      <li>Model / Worked Example</li>
      <li>Guided Practice</li>
      <li>Independent Work</li>
      <li>Mastery Checkpoint</li>
      <li>Notebook Submission</li>
      <li>Checkpoint Submission</li>
      <li>Quiz, if assigned</li>
    </ol>
    <p>Some graph- or data-intensive lessons include a separate graph/model page or additional data analysis. Always follow the sequence displayed in Moodle.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-vocabulary-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-vocabulary-heading">Use precise vocabulary</h3>
    <p>Record and learn each lesson's vocabulary. Similar terms can have different meanings—for example, <em>demand</em> versus <em>quantity demanded</em>, <em>supply</em> versus <em>quantity supplied</em>, <em>deficit</em> versus <em>debt</em>, and <em>absolute advantage</em> versus <em>comparative advantage</em>. Use correct economic vocabulary in your explanations.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-reading-graphs-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-reading-graphs-heading">Read economic graphs for meaning</h3>
    <p>When a graph is introduced, identify the horizontal and vertical axes, curve labels, equilibrium when applicable, movements along curves, curve shifts, and the direction of change. Do not memorize only whether a curve moves left or right. Explain why the change occurs and what it means economically.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-examples-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-examples-heading">Study worked examples</h3>
    <p>Worked examples reveal the reasoning process:</p>
    <p class="mla-econ-workflow" aria-label="Question, then economic concept, then graph data or evidence, then reasoning, then conclusion"><strong>Question</strong> → <strong>Economic Concept</strong> → <strong>Graph / Data / Evidence</strong> → <strong>Reasoning</strong> → <strong>Conclusion</strong></p>
    <p>Study each step rather than copying only the final answer.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-practice-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-practice-heading">Guided Practice and Independent Work</h3>
    <p><strong>Guided Practice</strong> provides hints, partial examples, explanations, and feedback while you learn. It prepares you for <strong>Independent Work</strong>, where you apply the lesson concept without step-by-step assistance.</p>
    <p>Independent Work may include calculations, graph interpretation, economic scenarios, data analysis, or short explanations. It is submitted as part of Notebook Evidence and is not normally a separate Moodle assignment.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-evidence-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-evidence-heading">Notebook Evidence</h3>
    <p>Notebook Evidence demonstrates your work throughout the lesson. Each Notebook Submission tells you exactly what to provide. Required evidence may include vocabulary, organizers, graph/model work, calculations, and Independent Work. Submit the specified evidence; you may also include optional notes that help show your learning.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-checkpoint-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-checkpoint-heading">Mastery Checkpoint</h3>
    <p>The Checkpoint is separate from Notebook Evidence. It uses a new problem, graph, data set, or scenario and must be completed independently. The Checkpoint Submission should contain only the required checkpoint response. Do not resubmit your entire Notebook unless Moodle specifically instructs you to do so.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-quizzes-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-quizzes-heading">Quizzes</h3>
    <p>Not every lesson includes a quiz. When assigned, questions are selected from a larger bank, you receive one initial attempt, and 80% is the mastery threshold. Additional attempts follow MLA's remediation and reassessment policy.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-unit-assessment-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-unit-assessment-heading">Unit Assessment</h3>
    <p>Each unit ends with an assessment covering the whole unit. Be prepared to interpret graphs and data, apply economic concepts, explain cause and effect, and distinguish similar concepts.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-calculator-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-calculator-heading">Calculators</h3>
    <p>You may use a calculator when allowed. Calculations are not only about producing a number: you must often explain its economic meaning. For example, an inflation calculation may be incomplete if you cannot explain what the inflation rate represents.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-help-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-help-heading">When to ask for help</h3>
    <p>Contact your TOR if instructions remain unclear after rereading, you do not understand a graph or model, feedback is unclear, you do not understand why an answer is incorrect, or remediation is required.</p>
  </section>

  <nav class="mla-econ-next" aria-label="Next orientation step">

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-mastery-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-mastery-heading">Mastery expectation</h3>
    <p>MLA's mastery threshold is <strong>80%</strong>. A score below mastery may require review, remediation, revision, or reassessment. Mastery means you can apply a concept, not merely recognize its vocabulary.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-reasoning-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-reasoning-heading">Economic reasoning</h3>
    <p>Explain which economic concept applies, what evidence supports your answer, and what economic mechanism causes the result. “Because that's how the economy works” is not a supported explanation.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-graph-expectations-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-graph-expectations-heading">Graph expectations</h3>
    <p>When a graph is required, correctly identify its axes, labels, curves, direction of movement or shift, and equilibrium when applicable. A graph without an economic explanation may be incomplete.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-calculation-expectations-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-calculation-expectations-heading">Calculation expectations</h3>
    <p>Show enough work to identify the correct relationship or formula, values used, final answer, and units when appropriate. Be prepared to explain the economic meaning of your result.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-positive-normative-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-positive-normative-heading">Positive and normative economics</h3>
    <p>Economics sometimes examines controversial public-policy questions. A <strong>positive claim</strong> describes what is, what happened, or what evidence or a model predicts. A <strong>normative claim</strong> expresses a value judgment about what should happen. You are graded on accurate concepts, evidence, reasoning, and tradeoffs—not on holding a particular political viewpoint.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-neutrality-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-neutrality-heading">Political neutrality</h3>
    <p>Topics may include taxes, government spending, regulation, trade, and monetary policy. The purpose is economic analysis. You are not required to support any political party, candidate, ideology, or policy position.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-submission-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-submission-heading">Notebook and Checkpoint submissions</h3>
    <p><strong>Notebook Evidence</strong> must include all required components, be readable, identify the lesson, and show your own work. Missing components may result in revision.</p>
    <p><strong>Checkpoint submissions</strong> must answer every required question, provide complete reasoning when requested, use correct economic vocabulary, and use evidence, graphs, or data when required. Do not combine the Notebook and Checkpoint unless Moodle explicitly instructs otherwise.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-integrity-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-integrity-heading">Academic integrity</h3>
    <p>Submit your own work and reasoning. External tools, including artificial intelligence (AI), may not replace your calculations, graph interpretation, Independent Work, Checkpoint reasoning, or assessments. Follow MLA's academic-integrity rules for permitted assistance.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-feedback-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-feedback-heading">Grading and feedback</h3>
    <p>TOR grading and feedback are generally completed within 24 business hours during Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. Eastern Time. Routine grading generally does not occur on weekends or school holidays. Exceptional circumstances may affect turnaround time.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-technical-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-technical-heading">Technical expectations</h3>
    <p>You need reliable internet access, Moodle access, the ability to view graphs and tables, and the ability to upload required work. You may use a basic calculator when permitted. No specialized economics software is required unless MLA supplies it.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-accessibility-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-accessibility-heading">Accessibility</h3>
    <p>Follow accommodations approved by MLA. Course visuals should provide accessible alternatives. Contact your TOR if you cannot access a graph, table, model, or other visual properly.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-current-data-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-current-data-heading">Current economic information</h3>
    <p>Some lessons use real economic data. Pay attention to the source, date, and whether the evidence is current or historical. Economic conditions change, so a historical example should not automatically be treated as a current statistic.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mla-econ-section" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-communication-heading">
    <h3 id="mla-econ-communication-heading">Communication</h3>
    <p>Communicate respectfully and clearly with your TOR. When asking for help, identify the unit, lesson, page or activity, and your specific question.</p>
  </section>

  <aside class="mla-econ-help-box" aria-labelledby="mla-econ-need-help-heading">
