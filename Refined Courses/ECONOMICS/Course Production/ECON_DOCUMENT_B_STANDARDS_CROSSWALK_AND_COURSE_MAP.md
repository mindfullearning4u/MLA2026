## Authority and scope

Document A controls how the course is produced. This document controls what the course teaches. MLA Economics standards define the course's instructional expectations. Florida and other external standards are crosswalked to demonstrate alignment.

This front matter authorizes a five-unit, 27-lesson curriculum map. It does **not** authorize detailed lesson pages, Moodle construction, quizzes, or question banks. Those artifacts begin only after this map is approved and detailed lesson specifications are completed.

## Economics–financial-literacy boundary

Economics may teach financial institutions as macroeconomic actors, savings and investment as capital formation, money supply, interest rates, banking in circular flow, and investment as an economic variable. It must not teach checking-account selection, personal credit, credit-card management, household budgeting, retirement planning, individual investment selection, mortgages, insurance shopping, or personal tax filing. Those are reserved for Personal Financial Literacy and Money Management.

## Sequencing controls

- Demand precedes supply; supply precedes equilibrium; equilibrium precedes price controls, taxes/subsidies, externalities, and complex intervention.
- Circular flow precedes national output; national output precedes inflation and unemployment; those precede business cycles; business cycles precede fiscal and monetary policy.
- Opportunity cost precedes comparative advantage; comparative advantage precedes gains from trade; gains from trade precede trade barriers.

These dependencies may not be reordered without documented instructional justification and MLA approval.

## Required core models and data representations

The course must include, when instructionally appropriate: production possibilities frontier; circular flow; demand and supply curves; market equilibrium; demand and supply shifts; price ceiling; price floor; externality model; business cycle; CPI/inflation table or model; GDP/value-added model; labor/unemployment data; fiscal-policy reasoning model; monetary-policy transmission model; comparative-advantage table; international-trade model; and development-indicator comparison.

Every new graph type explicitly teaches axes, variables, relationships, interpretation, a worked example, guided practice, and independent interpretation. Models must state relevant assumptions and limitations. Quantitative work follows **calculate → interpret → explain economic meaning**.

## Assessment architecture

### Unit pretests

Each unit receives a 10-question, one-attempt diagnostic pretest worth 0% with a 90% acceleration threshold. It assesses only that unit's content.

### Lesson quizzes

The preliminary map contains 22 quizzes. Each mapped quiz uses a 25-question pool, delivers five questions, permits one attempt, and requires 80%. Another attempt requires remediation and TOR authorization. The recommended pool is five foundational, ten interpretation, and ten application/reasoning questions. Detailed mapping must confirm that each quiz adds useful independent evidence.

### Unit assessments

Each unit uses a 40-question bank, delivers ten questions, permits one attempt, and requires 80%. Another attempt requires remediation and TOR authorization. The default bank contains eight foundational, 16 interpretation, and 16 application/reasoning items. Detailed unit specifications must define lesson/domain representation and stratified delivery so unrestricted random selection cannot omit a major domain.

### Grade weights

- Notebook Evidence — 20%
- Mastery Checkpoints — 20%
- Lesson Quizzes — 30%
- Unit Assessments — 30%
- Unit Pretests — diagnostic, 0%

## Policy-analysis and neutrality controls

Policy tasks require students to identify the objective, mechanism, affected actors, incentives, short- and long-term effects, tradeoffs, unintended consequences, evidence, and limitations. Lessons must distinguish positive claims from normative claims and must not advocate for a party, candidate, or required policy preference.
