==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — WHY USE A CIRCULAR-FLOW MODEL?
==================================================

Teach:

An economy contains millions of transactions.

Economists use models to simplify these interactions and identify major relationships.

The circular-flow model helps show:

- who participates;
- what each sector provides;
- what each sector receives;
- how resources move;
- how money moves.

Critical:

The model is a simplification.

It is NOT a literal map of every transaction.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — HOUSEHOLDS
==================================================

Teach:

Households act as:

CONSUMERS

in product markets

and

RESOURCE OWNERS / SUPPLIERS

in factor markets.

Households may supply:

- labor;
- land/resources;
- capital resources/financial saving;
- entrepreneurial activity.

At introductory level:

emphasize labor as the clearest example.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — FIRMS
==================================================

Firms:

- hire/use productive resources;
- organize production;
- produce goods/services;
- sell output;
- pay income to resource providers.

Reconnect:

Unit 1 factors of production

and

Unit 2 firm behavior.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — PRODUCT MARKET
==================================================

Product market:

market where final goods and services are exchanged.

In the basic two-sector model:

FIRMS

supply goods/services.

HOUSEHOLDS

demand/buy goods/services.

Money moves:

HOUSEHOLDS → FIRMS

as consumer spending.

Goods/services move:

FIRMS → HOUSEHOLDS.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — FACTOR / RESOURCE MARKET
==================================================

Factor market:

market where productive resources/services are exchanged.

In the basic model:

HOUSEHOLDS

supply resources such as labor.

FIRMS

demand productive resources.

Real flow:

HOUSEHOLDS → FIRMS

Monetary income:

FIRMS → HOUSEHOLDS.

REAL VS MONETARY FLOWS
==================================================

Teach explicit distinction.

REAL FLOW:

- labor/resources;
- goods/services.

MONETARY FLOW:

- wages;
- income;
- spending;
- revenue.

Required principle:

Real and monetary flows generally move in opposite directions.

Example:

Worker provides labor to firm.

Firm provides wage payment to worker.

==================================================
SAVING VS INVESTMENT — ECONOMICS TERMINOLOGY
==================================================

Teach carefully:

In macroeconomics:

INVESTMENT

generally refers to spending on productive capital such as:

- equipment;
- structures;
- inventories.

It does NOT simply mean:

“buying stocks.”

This distinction is essential before GDP.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — INTERNATIONAL SECTOR
==================================================

Introduce:

Domestic households/firms interact with other economies through:

- imports;
- exports.

EXPORT:

domestically produced good/service sold abroad.

IMPORT:

foreign-produced good/service purchased domestically.

Do not teach comparative advantage yet.

That belongs in Unit 5.

==================================================

- exports.

Do NOT turn Lesson 1 into Keynesian equilibrium accounting.

Students need conceptual understanding only.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — OPEN ECONOMY
==================================================

Teach:

An open economy interacts economically with the rest of the world.

The expanded circular flow can include:

- domestic households;
- domestic firms;
- government;
- financial sector;
- foreign sector.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 11 — MODEL LIMITATIONS
==================================================

Circular flow simplifies:

- millions of firms;
- different household types;
- different levels of government;
- financial instruments;
- informal activity;
- international capital flows.

Required conclusion:

Models help isolate relationships but omit details.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 1
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Households only consume.

CORRECTION:

Households also supply productive resources, especially labor.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Firms supply labor.

CORRECTION:

Workers/households supply labor; firms demand labor.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Product markets sell productive resources to firms.

CORRECTION:

Factor markets handle productive-resource exchanges.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Real flow means cash.

CORRECTION:

Real flow means goods, services, and productive resources.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Saving means money disappears from the economy.

CORRECTION:

Financial systems can channel saving toward borrowing and investment.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Investment means buying stocks.

CORRECTION:

Macroeconomic investment generally refers to spending on productive capital/inventory.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

Imports are produced domestically.

CORRECTION:

Imports are produced abroad and purchased domestically.

CORE CONCEPT 1 — GDP

==================================================
WHAT GDP DOES NOT MEAN
==================================================

GDP is NOT:

- all wealth owned by residents;
- the amount of money in the economy;
- government revenue;
- stock-market value;
- every transaction that occurs;
- a complete measure of human well-being.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — FINAL VS INTERMEDIATE GOODS
==================================================

Teach:

FINAL GOOD/SERVICE:

purchased for final use.

INTERMEDIATE GOOD:

used as an input in producing another good/service.

Reason:

Avoid double counting.

==================================================
REQUIRED PRODUCTION CHAIN
==================================================

Use:

WHEAT

→ FLOUR

→ BREAD.

Example sale values:

Farmer sells wheat:
$1

Miller sells flour:
$2

Bakery sells final bread:
$4

Do NOT add:

$1 + $2 + $4 = $7 GDP

if earlier values are embodied in the final product.

Using final-good approach:

GDP contribution = $4.

==================================================
VALUE ADDED
==================================================

Introduce:

Value added at each production stage equals:

value of output

minus

value of intermediate inputs purchased.

Example:

Farmer:
$1 value added.

Miller:
$2 − $1 = $1.

Bakery:
$4 − $2 = $2.

Total value added:

$1 + $1 + $2 = $4.

Matches final value.

This reinforces why double counting is avoided.

CONSUMPTION
==================================================

Household spending on final goods/services.

Examples:

- restaurant meal;
- clothing;
- haircut;
- new consumer durable.

Do not overcomplicate housing treatment.

==================================================
INVESTMENT
==================================================

At introductory level include:

- business equipment;
- structures;
- inventories;
- new residential construction if included in course examples.

Do not teach detailed national accounting categories beyond necessary rigor.

==================================================
GOVERNMENT PURCHASES
==================================================

Government purchases of currently produced goods/services.

Examples:

- road construction;
- public employee services;
- equipment.

Critical:

TRANSFER PAYMENTS are NOT counted as government purchases in GDP because they are not payment for current production.

Example:

A benefit payment itself is a transfer.

Subsequent household spending may enter consumption when final goods/services are purchased.

==================================================
EXPORTS
==================================================

Domestically produced output purchased by foreigners.

Exports are added because they are domestic production.

==================================================
IMPORTS
==================================================

Foreign-produced goods/services purchased domestically.

Imports are subtracted in the expenditure identity because they may already appear within C, I, or G but are not domestic production.

Critical:

Do NOT teach:

“Imports reduce GDP because imports are bad.”

The subtraction is an accounting adjustment.

==================================================

Example:

Consumption:
$700 billion

Investment:
$150 billion

Government purchases:
$200 billion

$100 billion

$125 billion

$100 − $125
=

=

category classification

and

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — NOMINAL GDP
==================================================

Nominal GDP measures output using current-period prices.

Therefore nominal GDP can rise because:

- more output was produced;
- prices increased;
- or both.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — REAL GDP
==================================================

Real GDP adjusts for changes in the overall price level using constant/base-year prices.

Purpose:

better isolate changes in actual production.

Do not make full GDP-deflator calculation a mandatory objective unless later Document B explicitly adds it.

==================================================
REQUIRED NOMINAL VS REAL EXAMPLE
==================================================

Simple one-product economy.

YEAR 1:

100 units
$10 each

Nominal value:
$1,000.

YEAR 2:

100 units
$12 each

Nominal value:
$1,200.

Physical output:

unchanged.

Conclusion:

Nominal output increased because prices increased, not because more goods were produced.

Then provide second example where:

quantity rises.

==================================================
NOMINAL VS REAL PRECISION
==================================================

Students must understand:

Nominal GDP growth

does NOT necessarily equal

real economic growth.

Real GDP is generally more useful for evaluating changes in actual output over time.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — GDP PER CAPITA
==================================================

Teach:

GDP per capita

=
GDP ÷ population.

Purpose:

roughly compares output/income scale per person across populations or over time.

Required example:

GDP:
$500 billion

Population:
10 million

GDP per capita:

$50,000.

Ensure units are handled correctly.

==================================================
GDP PER CAPITA LIMITATIONS
==================================================

GDP per capita does NOT show:

- actual income of each individual;
- income distribution;
- household circumstances;
- unpaid work;
- environmental quality;
- health;
- leisure;
- happiness.

Do not call GDP per capita:

“the average person's salary.”

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — GDP LIMITATIONS
==================================================

Teach that GDP omits or incompletely captures:

- unpaid household work;
- volunteer activity;
- some informal/unreported production;
- environmental costs;
- leisure;
- distribution of output/income;
- some quality improvements;
- broader health/social outcomes.

Critical:

GDP is useful.

GDP is not complete.

Do not frame GDP as either:

“the perfect measure”

or

“useless.”

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 8 — PRODUCTIVITY
==================================================

Reconnect Unit 1 Lesson 5.

Productivity:

output relative to input.

Labor productivity:

Output ÷ Labor Input.

Required:

Students must still distinguish:

TOTAL OUTPUT

from

PRODUCTIVITY.

==================================================
REQUIRED PRODUCTIVITY EXAMPLE
==================================================

Year 1:

1,000 units

200 worker-hours

Productivity:
5 units/hour.

Year 2:

1,200 units

200 worker-hours

Productivity:
6 units/hour.

Productivity increased 20%.

If percent change is required:

(6 − 5) ÷ 5 × 100
=
20%.

Teach percent-change formula explicitly before requiring it.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — ECONOMIC GROWTH
==================================================

Define at course level:

Economic growth refers to sustained increase in an economy's real productive output/capacity over time.

Use:

REAL OUTPUT

not merely nominal-dollar increases.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — SOURCES OF ECONOMIC GROWTH
==================================================

Teach major contributors:

1. Physical capital
2. Human capital
3. Technology
4. Productivity
5. Labor-force growth
6. Natural resources where relevant
7. Institutions/infrastructure where appropriate
8. Entrepreneurship/innovation.

Do not state any one factor guarantees growth.

==================================================
CAPITAL INVESTMENT
==================================================

Reconnect Florida alignment.

Investment in:

- machinery;
- technology;
- infrastructure;
- human capital

may increase productive capacity or productivity.

But investment has:

- current costs;
- opportunity costs;
- risk.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 11 — GROWTH AND PPF
==================================================

Reconnect Unit 1.

PPF outward shift:

increase in productive capacity.

Real GDP growth:

increase in actual national output.

Students must distinguish:

CAPACITY

from

ACTUAL PRODUCTION.

An economy could gain productive capacity yet temporarily operate below it.

==================================================
REQUIRED GROWTH MODEL
==================================================

Show:

PPF 1

→ capital/technology/human-capital improvement

→ PPF 2 outward.

Then pair with:

real-output time series.

Teach:

PPF is a conceptual capacity model.

Real GDP measures actual output.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 12 — ECONOMIC GROWTH VS WELL-BEING
==================================================

Higher real GDP per capita can be associated with greater material capacity.

But it does not automatically describe:

- distribution;
- environment;
- safety;
- health;
- leisure;
- quality of life.

Students must avoid:

“GDP rose, therefore every person became better off.”

==================================================
CORRELATION / CAUSATION CONTROL
==================================================

Example:

Countries with greater education levels may have higher productivity.

Valid:

There is an association in the provided dataset.

Not automatically valid:

Education alone caused the entire productivity difference.

Teach students to identify:

- additional variables;
- direction of causation;
- limitations.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 2
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

GDP measures a country's total wealth.

CORRECTION:

GDP measures current production of final goods/services during a period.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Every sale counts in GDP.

CORRECTION:

GDP focuses on current production and avoids double counting.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Used goods are counted every time they are resold.

CORRECTION:

The original production was counted when newly produced; current service fees associated with resale may count separately.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Government transfer payments are government purchases.

CORRECTION:

Transfers are not payments for current production.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Imports are subtracted because they harm the economy.

CORRECTION:

Imports are subtracted to remove foreign production already included in domestic spending categories.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Nominal GDP growth means the country produced more.

CORRECTION:

Nominal GDP can rise because prices rose.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

GDP per capita is everyone's salary.

CORRECTION:

It is national output divided by population.

---

MISCONCEPTION 8:

Higher GDP means every citizen is better off.

CORRECTION:

GDP does not show distribution or every dimension of well-being.

---

MISCONCEPTION 9:

Higher total production automatically means higher productivity.

CORRECTION:

Productivity measures output relative to inputs.

---

MISCONCEPTION 10:

Moving from inside a PPF to the frontier is growth.

CORRECTION:

That is increased utilization of existing capacity; growth expands the frontier.
