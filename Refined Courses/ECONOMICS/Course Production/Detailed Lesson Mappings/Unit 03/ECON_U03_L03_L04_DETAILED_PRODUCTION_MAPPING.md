==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — PRICE LEVEL
==================================================

Teach:

The PRICE LEVEL represents the general level of prices across a broad set of goods and services in an economy.

It is not the price of one item.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — INFLATION
==================================================

Inflation is:

a sustained increase in the general price level over time.

Critical precision:

Inflation does NOT mean:

- every price rises;
- every price rises by the same percentage;
- one expensive product became more expensive;
- prices are “high” in an absolute sense.

Inflation concerns CHANGE in the general price level.

==================================================
INFLATION VS HIGH PRICES
==================================================

Required example:

Economy A:
Prices are high but unchanged this year.

Economy B:
Prices are lower than Economy A but rising rapidly.

Economy B may have:

higher inflation

even though its absolute prices remain lower.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — DEFLATION
==================================================

Deflation:

a sustained decline in the general price level.

Do not teach:

“deflation = inflation slowing.”

That is incorrect.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — DISINFLATION
==================================================

Disinflation:

inflation remains positive, but the rate of inflation decreases.

Example:

Year 1:
8%

Year 2:
5%

Prices are still rising, but more slowly.

Do not confuse with deflation.

==================================================
REQUIRED THREE-WAY COMPARISON
==================================================

INFLATION:
price level rises.

DISINFLATION:
price level still rises, but at slower rate.

DEFLATION:
price level falls.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 5 — PURCHASING POWER
==================================================

Purchasing power:

the amount of goods/services a given amount of money can purchase.

If income remains unchanged while the general price level rises:

purchasing power generally falls.

Example:

Income:
$1,000.

Original basket:
$900.

Later basket:
$990.

Same nominal income buys less remaining consumption.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 6 — CPI
==================================================

Teach introductory CPI.

CPI measures changes in the cost of a representative consumer market basket relative to a base period.

At course level:

CPI =
Cost of Current Basket
÷
Cost of Base-Year Basket
×
100

==================================================
REQUIRED CPI MODEL
==================================================

Base-year basket cost:
$200

Current-year basket cost:
$230

CPI:

230 ÷ 200 × 100
=
115.

Interpretation:

The basket costs 15% more than in the base year.

Do NOT automatically say:

“Inflation this year is 15%”

unless the comparison is specifically from base year to current year and that is the requested interval.

==================================================
CPI INDEX INTERPRETATION
==================================================

Teach:

CPI = 100
means:

same basket cost as base year.

CPI = 120
means:

basket cost is 20% above base-year level.

CPI = 95
means:

basket cost is 5% below base-year level.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — INFLATION RATE
==================================================

Teach:

Inflation Rate
=
(Current CPI − Previous CPI)
÷ Previous CPI
× 100

==================================================
REQUIRED INFLATION CALCULATION
==================================================

Previous CPI:
125

Current CPI:
130

Inflation rate:

(130 − 125)
÷ 125
× 100

=
4%.

Require students to:

- subtract correctly;
- divide by previous period;
- multiply by 100;
- interpret.

==================================================
INDEX CHANGE VS INDEX LEVEL
==================================================

Critical distinction:

CPI level:

130.

Inflation rate:

4%.

Do not confuse the index number with the rate of change.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 8 — NOMINAL VS REAL
==================================================

Reconnect Lesson 2.

NOMINAL:

measured in current dollars.

REAL:

adjusted for changes in purchasing power/price level.

At this stage, use conceptual comparisons.

Example:

Worker's nominal wage rises 3%.

Inflation is 5%.

Approximate implication:

real purchasing power of that wage has declined.

Do not require precise real-wage formula unless mapped.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — WHO IS AFFECTED BY INFLATION?
==================================================

Teach that effects differ.

Inflation can affect:

- workers;
- savers;
- borrowers;
- lenders;
- firms;
- governments.

Do NOT say inflation harms everyone equally.

==================================================
BORROWERS AND LENDERS
==================================================

Teach unexpected inflation conceptually.

If a fixed-rate loan was agreed before unexpectedly high inflation:

BORROWER:

may repay with dollars that have less purchasing power.

LENDER:

may receive repayment worth less in real purchasing power than expected.

Important:

Do not say borrowers always benefit.

Loan terms, interest rates, income, and expected inflation matter.

==================================================
FIXED NOMINAL INCOME
==================================================

A person whose nominal income does not rise while prices increase may experience lower purchasing power.

Examples:

- fixed contractual payment;
- fixed salary during short period.

Avoid using personal retirement planning.

==================================================
SAVERS
==================================================

If savings earn a return below inflation:

real purchasing power of those savings may decline.

Do not turn this into investment advice.

==================================================
FIRMS
==================================================

Inflation may affect firms through:

- input costs;
- pricing decisions;
- uncertainty;
- wage negotiations;
- borrowing costs;
- planning.

Effects differ by firm.

==================================================
EXPECTED VS UNEXPECTED INFLATION
==================================================

Teach:

Expected inflation can be incorporated into:

- wage agreements;
- interest rates;
- contracts.

Unexpected inflation creates greater redistribution and planning problems.

Do not require advanced expectations theory.

==================================================
CAUSES OF INFLATION — INTRODUCTORY ONLY
==================================================

Lesson 3 may introduce broad conceptual categories:

DEMAND-PULL:

aggregate spending pressure exceeds available productive capacity.

COST-PUSH:

major production costs rise, contributing to broader price increases.

MONETARY CONDITIONS:

sustained monetary expansion can contribute to inflation under relevant macroeconomic conditions.

Do NOT teach a partisan or simplistic one-cause explanation.

Full monetary policy belongs in Unit 4.

==================================================
CAUSE CONTROL
==================================================

Do NOT teach:

- “corporate greed causes all inflation”;
- “government spending causes all inflation”;
- “money supply causes all inflation”;
- “wages cause all inflation.”

Inflation can have multiple contributing mechanisms depending on context.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — CPI LIMITATIONS
==================================================

Introduce:

CPI is useful but imperfect.

Potential issues:

- consumer baskets differ;
- substitution changes behavior;
- quality changes;
- new products appear;
- geographic differences;
- household spending patterns differ.

Do not imply CPI is invalid.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 3
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Inflation means every price rises.

CORRECTION:

Inflation refers to the broad price level.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

High prices and high inflation are the same.

CORRECTION:

Inflation measures rate of price-level change.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Disinflation means prices are falling.

CORRECTION:

Prices are still rising, but more slowly.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

CPI of 120 means inflation is 120%.

CORRECTION:

The index is relative to the base year; the inflation rate depends on change from the previous comparison period.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Inflation always hurts borrowers.

CORRECTION:

Unexpected inflation can reduce the real burden of fixed nominal debt under some conditions.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Inflation benefits every borrower.

CORRECTION:

Terms, interest rates, income, and expected inflation matter.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

If nominal income rises, purchasing power automatically rises.

CORRECTION:

It depends on how nominal income changes relative to the price level.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — EMPLOYED
==================================================

At course level:

Employed individuals are people who meet official employment criteria during the reference period.

Do not oversimplify into:

“anyone who has ever worked.”

Use fictional classifications rather than requiring current legal/statistical technical detail beyond instructional need.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — UNEMPLOYED
==================================================

Teach:

Unemployed generally means:

- does not currently have employment;
- is available for work;
- is actively seeking work

under the standard official measure.

Critical:

A person without a job is NOT automatically classified as unemployed.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — LABOR FORCE
==================================================

Labor Force
=
Employed
+
Unemployed.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 4 — NOT IN LABOR FORCE
==================================================

Examples may include people who:

- are not working;
- are not actively seeking work;
- may be retired;
- may be full-time students not seeking employment;
- may be caregivers not seeking employment;
- may be discouraged workers.

Do not assume why an individual is outside labor force unless scenario provides it.

==================================================
REQUIRED CLASSIFICATION MODEL
==================================================

Employed.

PERSON B:

Classification:
Unemployed.

PERSON C:
not working and not seeking work.

Classification:
Not in labor force.

==================================================

==================================================

Formula:

IMPORTANT:

Denominator is NOT:

==================================================

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 7 — UNEMPLOYMENT RATE CAN FALL FOR DIFFERENT REASONS
==================================================

This is essential.

Scenario A:

Unemployed workers find jobs.

Unemployment rate falls.

Positive labor-market signal.

Scenario B:

Some unemployed workers stop looking and leave the labor force.

Measured unemployment rate may also fall.

Different interpretation.

Therefore:

Do not evaluate labor-market health from unemployment rate alone.

==================================================

100

1,000

UR:
10%.

900

Unemployed:
50

Labor Force:
950

UR:

Employment did NOT increase.

Some people left labor force.

Students must recognize:

lower unemployment rate does not necessarily mean more people found jobs.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 8 — FRICTIONAL UNEMPLOYMENT
==================================================

Short-term unemployment associated with:

- job search;
- transitions;
- entering/re-entering labor market.

Example:

Recent graduate searching for first job.

Do not portray as inherently harmful or avoidable.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 9 — STRUCTURAL UNEMPLOYMENT
==================================================

Mismatch between:

worker skills/location

and

available jobs.

Possible causes:

- technological change;
- industry change;
- geographic shifts;
- changing skill requirements.

Do not say:

technology always causes permanent unemployment.

Workers/markets can adapt.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 10 — CYCLICAL UNEMPLOYMENT
==================================================

Unemployment associated with downturns in overall economic activity.

Reconnect:

Lesson 5 business cycle.

During contractions:

firms may reduce output and employment.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 11 — SEASONAL UNEMPLOYMENT
==================================================

Changes due to predictable seasonal patterns.

Examples:

- seasonal tourism;
- agriculture;
- holiday retail.

Important:

Official unemployment data are often seasonally adjusted.

Do not overfocus.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 12 — FULL EMPLOYMENT
==================================================

Teach:

Full employment does NOT mean:

zero unemployment.

Some frictional and structural unemployment can exist even when economy is near full employment.

Avoid assigning a fixed numerical “full-employment rate” unless a sourced dataset explicitly defines one.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 13 — NATURAL RATE
==================================================

At introductory level:

Natural unemployment broadly reflects frictional + structural unemployment when cyclical unemployment is absent or minimal in the simplified model.

Do not turn into advanced macro theory.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 14 — DISCOURAGED WORKERS
==================================================

A discouraged worker may want a job but has stopped actively searching after believing no suitable work is available.

Under standard official measures:

they may not be counted as unemployed if not actively searching.

This contributes to limitations of unemployment rate.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 15 — UNDEREMPLOYMENT
==================================================

Conceptually:

A worker may be employed but:

- work fewer hours than desired;
- work in a role below available skills/capacity.

Standard unemployment rate does not fully capture this.

Do not create subjective personal judgments about whether someone's occupation is “beneath” them.

Use scenario-provided information.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 4
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Anyone without a job is unemployed.

CORRECTION:

Official unemployment requires labor-force criteria including active job search under the standard measure.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Unemployment rate uses total population as denominator.

CORRECTION:

It uses the labor force.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Lower unemployment always means more people got jobs.

CORRECTION:

It can also fall when people leave the labor force.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Full employment means zero unemployment.

CORRECTION:

Frictional and structural unemployment can remain.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Structural unemployment occurs only during recessions.

CORRECTION:

It arises from mismatch in skills/location/job structure.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

Seasonal unemployment is the same as cyclical unemployment.

CORRECTION:

Seasonal changes are predictable within the year; cyclical changes relate to economy-wide fluctuations.

---

MISCONCEPTION 7:

An employed person cannot be underemployed.

CORRECTION:

Underemployment may exist even when a person is officially employed.
