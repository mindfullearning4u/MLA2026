==================================================
CRITICAL TECHNICAL LANGUAGE CONTROL
==================================================

This distinction is NONNEGOTIABLE.

CHANGE IN QUANTITY DEMANDED

means:

movement along the SAME demand curve caused by a change in the good’s own price.

CHANGE IN DEMAND

means:

the ENTIRE demand curve shifts because a non-price determinant changed.

Therefore:

DO NOT write:

“Demand increases when price decreases.”

Correct:

“When price decreases, quantity demanded increases, all else equal.”

Likewise:

CHANGE IN QUANTITY SUPPLIED

means:

movement along the SAME supply curve caused by a change in the good’s own price.

CHANGE IN SUPPLY

means:

the ENTIRE supply curve shifts because a non-price determinant changed.

DO NOT write:

“Supply increases because the price of the good increased.”

Correct:

“When the good’s own price increases, quantity supplied increases, all else equal.”

==================================================
CETERIS PARIBUS
==================================================

Teach the concept explicitly.

Ceteris paribus:

“other relevant factors held constant.”

Students do not need Latin memorization as the central objective.

They must understand WHY the assumption matters.

Example:

The law of demand predicts that a lower price leads to a greater quantity demanded only when relevant non-price determinants are held constant.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — WHAT IS DEMAND?
==================================================

Teach:

Demand represents consumers’ willingness AND ability to purchase various quantities of a good or service at different prices during a given period, other relevant factors held constant.

Critical distinction:

WANTING a product

does not automatically create economic demand.

Example:

A person may strongly want a $5 million home but lack the ability to purchase it.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 2 — QUANTITY DEMANDED
==================================================

Quantity demanded is:

the amount consumers are willing and able to purchase at a SPECIFIC PRICE during a given period.

Distinguish:

DEMAND
= entire relationship.

QUANTITY DEMANDED
= one quantity associated with one particular price.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 3 — LAW OF DEMAND
==================================================

Teach:

Other things equal:

when price rises,
quantity demanded tends to fall.

When price falls,
quantity demanded tends to rise.

This is an inverse relationship.

Do not teach:

price ↓ = demand ↑.

Correct terminology:

price ↓
→ quantity demanded ↑.

==================================================
WHY DEMAND SLOPES DOWNWARD
==================================================

Provide an introductory explanation.

Possible mechanisms:

1. SUBSTITUTION EFFECT

When a good becomes relatively more expensive, consumers may switch toward alternatives.

2. PURCHASING-POWER / INCOME EFFECT

A higher price reduces the purchasing power of a fixed amount of income for that good and other purchases.

3. DIMINISHING MARGINAL BENEFIT / UTILITY CONNECTION

Additional units may provide less additional benefit, so consumers may require a lower price to purchase more units.

Do not turn this into advanced utility theory.

==================================================
MOVEMENT ALONG DEMAND CURVE
==================================================

Teach:

OWN PRICE CHANGE

→ MOVEMENT ALONG CURVE

→ CHANGE IN QUANTITY DEMANDED.

Use arrows along the same curve.

Do not draw a new curve.

==================================================
CHANGE IN DEMAND
==================================================

Teach:

A non-price determinant changes.

→ entire demand relationship changes.

→ curve shifts.

INCREASE IN DEMAND:

shift RIGHT.

DECREASE IN DEMAND:

shift LEFT.

Required labels:

D1
D2

==================================================
MAJOR DETERMINANTS OF DEMAND
==================================================

Teach at minimum:

1. Consumer income
2. Consumer tastes/preferences
3. Number of buyers/population
4. Prices of related goods
5. Consumer expectations

==================================================
INCOME
==================================================

For a NORMAL GOOD:

Income rises
→ demand tends to increase.

Income falls
→ demand tends to decrease.

For an INFERIOR GOOD:

Income rises
→ demand may decrease.

Income falls
→ demand may increase.

Important:

“Inferior” is technical economic terminology.

It does NOT mean:

poor quality.

==================================================
SUBSTITUTES
==================================================

Goods used in place of one another.

Example:

Tea and coffee.

If price of coffee rises:

demand for tea may increase.

Critical:

The price change is for ANOTHER good.

Therefore:

tea's demand curve shifts.

==================================================
COMPLEMENTS
==================================================

Goods commonly consumed together.

Example:

Printers and compatible ink cartridges.

If printer prices fall substantially and more printers are purchased:

demand for compatible cartridges may increase.

Again:

This is a demand shift for cartridges.

==================================================
PREFERENCES
==================================================

Changes in:

- tastes;
- trends;
- perceived usefulness;
- information;
- popularity

may affect demand.

Avoid assuming trends always persist.

==================================================
NUMBER OF BUYERS
==================================================

More buyers in a market generally:

increase market demand.

Fewer buyers generally:

decrease market demand.

Do not confuse this with one consumer moving along a curve.

==================================================
EXPECTATIONS
==================================================

Expected future:

- prices;
- income;
- product availability;
- economic conditions

may affect current demand.

Example:

Consumers expect a product's price to rise significantly next week.

Current demand may increase.

Do not present expectations as guaranteed behavior.

==================================================
MARKET DEMAND
==================================================

Introduce conceptually:

Market demand combines quantities demanded by individual buyers at each price.

Provide a small table.

Example:

PRICE | BUYER A | BUYER B | MARKET QD

$10 | 1 | 2 | 3
$8  | 2 | 3 | 5
$6  | 3 | 5 | 8

Market quantity demanded is the horizontal sum of individual quantities at the same price.

Keep calculations simple.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 1
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Demand means wanting something.

CORRECTION:

Demand requires willingness AND ability to purchase.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

When price falls, demand increases.

CORRECTION:

A change in the good's own price causes a change in quantity demanded.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Demand curve shifts when the product's price changes.

CORRECTION:

Own-price change causes movement along the curve.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Inferior good means low-quality good.

CORRECTION:

Inferior refers to how demand responds to income changes.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

If coffee price rises, movement occurs along tea's demand curve.

CORRECTION:

Coffee is another good; if they are substitutes, tea's demand shifts.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

A rightward shift means price fell.

CORRECTION:

A demand shift means a non-price determinant changed.

==================================================
CORE CONCEPT 1 — WHAT IS SUPPLY?
==================================================

Supply represents producers’ willingness AND ability to offer various quantities of a good or service for sale at different prices during a given period, other relevant factors constant.

==================================================
QUANTITY SUPPLIED
==================================================

Quantity supplied is:

the amount producers are willing and able to sell at ONE specific price.

Distinguish:

SUPPLY:
entire relationship.

QUANTITY SUPPLIED:
one amount associated with one price.

==================================================
LAW OF SUPPLY
==================================================

Other things equal:

higher price
→ greater quantity supplied.

lower price
→ smaller quantity supplied.

This is a direct relationship.

Correct language:

“When price rises, quantity supplied rises.”

Do NOT say:

“Supply increases when price rises.”

==================================================
WHY SUPPLY CURVES SLOPE UPWARD
==================================================

Teach introductory producer reasoning.

Higher prices can:

- make additional production worthwhile;
- cover higher marginal costs associated with increasing output;
- create stronger incentive to offer more units for sale.

Do not teach full marginal-cost curve derivation yet.

That comes with firm decisions.

==================================================
INPUT PRICES
==================================================

Example:

Cost of flour falls for bakeries.

Production becomes less costly.

Supply of baked goods may increase.

Curve:

right.

If input costs rise:

supply may decrease.

Curve:

left.

==================================================
TECHNOLOGY AND PRODUCTIVITY
==================================================

Reconnect Unit 1 Lesson 5.

Improved production technology may:

- increase productivity;
- reduce per-unit resource requirements;
- increase supply.

Shift:

right.

Do not state every technology automatically increases supply.

Use scenario evidence.

==================================================
NUMBER OF SELLERS
==================================================

More firms entering a market:

market supply generally increases.

Fewer firms:

market supply generally decreases.

==================================================
PRODUCER EXPECTATIONS
==================================================

Expectations about future prices may affect current supply.

Example:

Producers expect price to rise substantially next month and can store the product.

They may reduce current supply to sell later.

Important:

Effect depends on ability to delay sale/storage and scenario conditions.

Do not teach expectations as mechanically identical in every market.

==================================================
TAXES
==================================================

At introductory supply level:

A tax that increases producers’ per-unit costs may reduce supply under the simplified model.

Curve:

left.

Do NOT conduct tax-incidence analysis yet.

That belongs later.

==================================================
SUBSIDIES
==================================================

At introductory level:

A production subsidy may reduce effective producer costs or increase returns to production.

Supply may shift right.

Do NOT turn this into political advocacy.

==================================================
REGULATION
==================================================

Regulation can affect:

- costs;
- required production processes;
- entry;
- resource use.

Effects depend on the specific rule.

Do not teach:

“Regulation always decreases supply.”

Supply of the crop decreases.

Curve:

left.

MARKET SUPPLY
==================================================

Introduce:

Market supply combines quantities supplied by individual producers at each price.

Example:

PRICE | FIRM A | FIRM B | MARKET QS

$3 | 10 | 5 | 15
$5 | 20 | 15 | 35
$7 | 30 | 25 | 55

Market quantity supplied is the horizontal sum.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 2
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

If price rises, supply increases.

CORRECTION:

Own-price increase causes quantity supplied to increase.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Supply means how much is currently in a store.

CORRECTION:

Supply is the relationship between price and quantities producers are willing and able to sell.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Higher input prices increase supply.

CORRECTION:

Higher production costs generally reduce supply under the standard model.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Any new technology automatically shifts supply right.

CORRECTION:

It must improve relevant production conditions/productivity.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

More sellers cause movement along a supply curve.

CORRECTION:

More sellers change market supply and shift the curve.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

A leftward shift means the product's own price fell.

CORRECTION:

A supply shift reflects a non-price determinant.
