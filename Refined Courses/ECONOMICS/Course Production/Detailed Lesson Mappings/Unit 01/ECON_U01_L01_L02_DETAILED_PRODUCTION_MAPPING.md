==================================================
LESSON 1 CRITICAL DEFINITIONS
==================================================

ECONOMICS:

The study of how people and societies make choices about using limited resources to satisfy wants and needs.

SCARCITY:

The condition that exists because resources are limited while human wants for goods and services exceed what can be produced with those resources.

Do NOT define scarcity simply as:

“not having enough money.”

SHORTAGE:

A situation in which the quantity available of a specific good or resource is temporarily less than the quantity desired or demanded under particular conditions.

Required distinction:

SCARCITY
is fundamental and persistent.

SHORTAGE
is specific and may be temporary.

TRADEOFF:

What is given up or accepted when choosing among alternatives.

OPPORTUNITY COST:

The value of the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

Critical rule:

Opportunity cost is NOT:

- every alternative;
- necessarily money;
- the price of the chosen item;
- the total of all sacrificed alternatives.

==================================================
SCARCITY EXAMPLES
==================================================

Use multiple levels.

INDIVIDUAL:

A student has two hours available after school but wants to:

- exercise;
- work;
- study;
- socialize.

The scarce resource is:

time.

HOUSEHOLD:

A household has limited income and must choose among competing expenditures.

Do NOT turn this into a personal-budgeting lesson.

FIRM:

A business has limited:

- workers;
- equipment;
- time;
- production space.

It must choose what and how much to produce.

GOVERNMENT:

A local government cannot fund every proposed project with existing resources.

Choices may involve:

- roads;
- parks;
- emergency services;
- libraries.

Remain politically neutral.

==================================================
NEEDS AND WANTS
==================================================

Teach carefully.

A basic instructional distinction may identify needs as goods/services essential to basic functioning and wants as additional desired goods/services.

However:

Do NOT teach that the classification is always absolute.

Context can matter.

Example:

Transportation may be essential for one person's employment circumstances but take different forms.

The economic point is not moral judgment.

The economic point is:

limited resources require choices among competing uses.

==================================================
SCARCITY VS SHORTAGE MODEL
==================================================

Required comparison table:

SCARCITY

- broad economic condition;
- resources are limited;
- persistent;
- forces choice.

SHORTAGE

- applies to a specific item/resource;
- quantity available is insufficient under current conditions;
- may be temporary;
- may result from disruptions, controls, or unusual demand conditions.

Examples:

SCARCITY:
limited land in a city.

SHORTAGE:
a temporary shortage of bottled water after a hurricane.

Do not introduce formal supply/demand curves yet.

P03 — TRADEOFFS AND OPPORTUNITY COST
==================================================

Teach:

A decision may involve several alternatives.

The opportunity cost is the next-best alternative that was not chosen.

==================================================
REQUIRED MODEL 1
==================================================

A student has Saturday afternoon available.

Choices:

A. Work a four-hour shift and earn $60.
B. Attend a soccer game with friends.
C. Stay home and play video games.

The student ranks:

1. Work
2. Soccer game
3. Video games

If the student chooses Work:

Opportunity cost:

the soccer game.

Do NOT answer:

$60

because $60 is the benefit of the chosen alternative.

Do NOT answer:

soccer game + video games

because opportunity cost is the next-best forgone alternative.

==================================================
REQUIRED MODEL 2
==================================================

A town has enough available funding for ONE project:

A. Repair a bridge
B. Build a new playground
C. Expand a library

Decision:

Repair the bridge.

If the decision-makers ranked the library expansion second:

Opportunity cost:

the library expansion.

Required reasoning:

Opportunity cost depends on the ranking of alternatives.

There may not be enough information to determine opportunity cost unless the next-best alternative is known.

==================================================
MONETARY VS NONMONETARY COST
==================================================

Teach that economic cost can involve:

- money;
- time;
- convenience;
- forgone experiences;
- use of resources.

Example:

A free concert still has an opportunity cost if attending requires giving up something else valuable.

==================================================
SUNK COST BOUNDARY
==================================================

Do NOT fully teach sunk-cost analysis in Lesson 1 unless used as a short misconception warning.

If included, state only:

Costs that cannot be recovered should not automatically determine the best future choice.

Full marginal decision-making belongs in Lesson 3.

==================================================
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — LESSON 1
==================================================

MISCONCEPTION 1:

Scarcity means poverty.

CORRECTION:

Scarcity exists whenever limited resources have competing uses, including in wealthy economies.

---

MISCONCEPTION 2:

Scarcity means a temporary shortage.

CORRECTION:

Scarcity is a fundamental condition; shortages are specific and may be temporary.

---

MISCONCEPTION 3:

Opportunity cost is the money spent.

CORRECTION:

It is the value of the next-best alternative forgone.

---

MISCONCEPTION 4:

Opportunity cost includes everything not chosen.

CORRECTION:

It refers to the next-best alternative.

---

MISCONCEPTION 5:

Something free has no cost.

CORRECTION:

A zero-dollar price does not eliminate time or alternative-use opportunity costs.

---

MISCONCEPTION 6:

If a choice has benefits, it has no tradeoff.

CORRECTION:

Choosing one use of scarce resources generally means giving up another use.

P04 — MODELED AND GUIDED ECONOMIC DECISION-MAKING
==================================================

Teach the MLA introductory decision model:

1. Identify the scarce resource.
2. Identify the alternatives.
3. Identify the decision.
4. Identify benefits of the chosen option.
5. Identify alternatives forgone.
6. Determine the next-best alternative.
7. State the opportunity cost.
8. Explain the tradeoff.

==================================================
MODELED EXAMPLE
==================================================

A small bakery has one commercial oven available for the next hour.

It can produce:

- 60 loaves of bread;
- 120 muffins;
- 40 trays of pastries.

Assume the bakery ranks:

1. Bread
2. Muffins
3. Pastries

It chooses bread.

Model:

Scarce resource:
oven time.

Choice:
bread.

Benefit:
60 loaves available for sale.

Next-best forgone alternative:
120 muffins.

Opportunity cost:
the 120 muffins that could have been produced.

Economic reasoning:
Using the oven for bread prevents the bakery from using the same hour to produce its next-best alternative.

GUIDED PRACTICE EXAMPLE

Jordan has $25.

Options:

==================================================
CRITICAL FACTORS OF PRODUCTION CONTENT
==================================================

Teach four factors:

LAND

Natural resources used in production.

Examples:

- land;
- water;
- minerals;
- timber;
- energy resources.

Do not define land as only physical acreage.

---

LABOR

Human effort used in production.

Examples:

- physical work;
- technical work;
- professional services;
- management labor.

---

CAPITAL

Produced resources used to create other goods/services.

Examples:

- machinery;
- tools;
- factories;
- computers;
- equipment.

Critical misconception:

Money itself is generally not classified as physical capital in the factor-of-production model.

Financial resources can purchase capital, but:

$50,000 in a bank account

is not the same thing as:

$50,000 machine used in production.

---

ENTREPRENEURSHIP

The organizing and risk-bearing function that combines resources, makes decisions, identifies opportunities, and may create new products/processes.

Do not reduce entrepreneurship to:

“owning a business.”

==================================================

==================================================

Teach:

HUMAN CAPITAL

refers to productive knowledge, education, skills, training, and experience embodied in people.

Depending on the economic framework, human capital is often analyzed as enhancing labor productivity rather than as physical capital.

Avoid confusing:

human capital

with

physical capital.

==================================================
P02/P03 PPF FOUNDATIONS
==================================================

Teach the purpose of a PPF.

A PPF is a simplified economic model showing the maximum combinations of two categories of goods/services that can be produced with:

- available resources;
- existing technology;
- full and efficient resource use

under the assumptions of the model.

Do not say the PPF is literally a complete picture of the entire economy.

It is a model.

==================================================
PPF ASSUMPTIONS
==================================================

At introductory level, teach:

The model typically assumes:

- a fixed amount of resources during the period;
- fixed technology during the period;
- two categories of output;
- resources are used efficiently for points on the frontier.

Later changes may relax these assumptions to show shifts.

PPF POINT CLASSIFICATION
==================================================

“impossible forever.”

They are:

currently unattainable under current conditions.

==================================================
INCREASING OPPORTUNITY COST
==================================================

Teach conceptually:

If resources are not equally suited to producing both goods, shifting more resources toward one output may require increasingly larger sacrifices of the other.

This can produce a:

bowed-out PPF.

Do not merely tell students:

“bowed curve means increasing opportunity cost.”

Show the numeric pattern.

==================================================
NUMERIC INCREASING-COST MODEL
==================================================

Example:

Moving from A → B costs 10 bicycles.

B → C costs 15 bicycles.

C → D costs 20 bicycles.

D → E costs 25 bicycles.

E → F costs 30 bicycles.

Explain:

Each additional equal increase in computers requires giving up more bicycles.

This represents increasing opportunity cost.

==================================================
CONSTANT OPPORTUNITY COST
==================================================

Briefly distinguish:

A straight-line PPF may represent constant opportunity cost when resources are equally adaptable between the two outputs under the simplified model.

Do not make this a major lesson objective unless needed for graph interpretation.

==================================================
PPF SHIFT — OUTWARD
==================================================

Teach:

An outward shift may result from increased productive capacity.

Possible causes:

- more productive resources;
- improved technology;
- increased physical capital;
- improvements in human capital;
- discoveries of resources.

Do NOT state that every technological improvement shifts the entire PPF equally.

A change affecting only one sector may rotate or shift the frontier asymmetrically.

This can be introduced conceptually.

==================================================
PPF SHIFT — INWARD
==================================================

Teach:

A reduction in productive capacity may move the PPF inward.

Examples:

- destruction of productive capital;
- loss of labor/resources;
- severe natural disaster affecting productive resources.

Do not turn this into current-event disaster analysis.

==================================================
MOVEMENT ALONG VS SHIFT
==================================================

This distinction is essential.

MOVEMENT ALONG PPF:

Different allocation of existing resources.

Represents:

choice/tradeoff.

SHIFT OF PPF:

Change in productive capacity.

Represents:
