# MLA Algebra I Unit and Lesson Mapping v3

**Reference Files:** `mla_algebra_1_standards_crosswalk.xlsx`; `mla_algebra_1_unit_mapping_v2.md`  
**Revision Basis:** Algebra I Sequencing Audit Findings  
**Purpose:** This document refines the Algebra I instructional sequence while preserving the MLA standards crosswalk, 100% benchmark coverage, mastery-based progression, six-unit structure, and eight lessons per unit.

---

## Course Sequencing Principles

The standards crosswalk establishes **what** must be taught. This unit mapping establishes **when** and **in what order** concepts should be taught.

This sequence is organized by prerequisite skill progression, conceptual development, student readiness, cognitive scaffolding, best instructional practices, support for struggling learners, and college/career readiness. It does not follow the order of Florida B.E.S.T., Common Core, SAT, or ACT standards documents.

The instructional progression is:

Foundations → Expressions → Equations → Linear Relationships → Graphing → Slope → Inequalities → Functions → Quadratic Models → Exponential Models → Systems → Data Analysis → Financial Literacy → Algebraic Modeling

## Unit 1: Foundations of Algebra and Expressions

**Unit Purpose:** Develop prerequisite skills and algebra readiness.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A1.FND.1, MLA.A1.FND.2, MLA.A1.FND.4

**Unit Justification:** Unit 1 is a readiness unit for students who enter Algebra I with skill gaps. The revised sequence keeps expression structure, algebraic language, exponent properties, combining like terms, and the distributive property in Unit 1, but moves advanced polynomial and radical operations closer to quadratics in Unit 5 to reduce cognitive load.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Whole-number operations, arithmetic facts, number line familiarity, reading directions, and willingness to show work.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports equation solving, graphing, functions, polynomial operations, quadratics, exponentials, systems, data interpretation, and financial literacy.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** SAT-style expression structure questions; ACT-style integer/rational-number fluency sets; error analysis on order of operations; multiple-representation vocabulary tasks; Desmos number line investigations; written justification of equivalent expressions.

## Unit 2: Solving Linear Equations

**Unit Purpose:** Develop procedural fluency and conceptual understanding of equations.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A1.AR.1, MLA.A1.FND.5

**Unit Justification:** Unit 2 progresses from one-step equations to two-step, multi-step, variables on both sides, literal equations, and applications. The revised prerequisites explicitly carry forward distributive property and combining like terms from Unit 1.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Integer/rational operations, order of operations, variables, expression interpretation, combining like terms, distributive property, and equivalent expressions.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports inequalities, systems, slope-intercept reasoning, formula use, model interpretation, and financial applications.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Equation error analysis; equations with rational coefficients; SAT-style linear equation word problems; ACT-style formula manipulation; student-created equation models; justification of inverse operations.

**Unit Justification:** Unit 3 begins with coordinate-plane and relationship meaning, then moves into graphing, slope, slope-intercept form, equation writing, and inequalities. Explicit coordinate vocabulary and unit-rate prerequisites reduce graphing overload.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Equation solving, coordinate plane vocabulary, plotting ordered pairs, x-axis/y-axis interpretation, unit rate, proportional reasoning, integer/rational operations, and expression evaluation.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports functions, systems, scatterplots, trend analysis, financial growth comparisons, and algebraic modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Desmos linear investigations; SAT-style slope/rate questions; ACT-style graph interpretation; compare two linear models from different representations; write equations from constraints; explain common slope errors.

**Unit Justification:** Functions are introduced after students have concrete linear experiences. This lets function notation, domain/range, behavior, representations, transformations, absolute value, and comparison develop conceptually.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Linear graphs, slope/rate of change, coordinate plane vocabulary, expression evaluation, equation solving, and interpreting contexts.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports quadratic functions, exponential functions, model selection, data trend analysis, and college-readiness function reasoning.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Multiple representation sorting; function/non-function justifications; SAT-style function notation questions; ACT-style graph interpretation; Desmos transformations; create examples and nonexamples of functions.

## Unit 5: Quadratic and Exponential Models

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Desmos quadratic transformations; SAT-style nonlinear model selection; ACT-style function comparison; error analysis for factoring and zeros; open-ended projectile or growth modeling; justify which model best fits a context.

## Systems Placement Review

### Option A: Keep Systems in Unit 6

**Instructional Advantages:** Systems remain an application of linear equations, graphing, inequalities, and modeling after students have developed the full linear toolkit. This placement allows systems to function as a real-world constraint and modeling capstone.

**Instructional Disadvantages:** Systems may arrive later than many traditional Algebra I sequences, which can delay practice with a major Algebra I topic. Students may also have to reactivate graphing and equation-writing skills from Unit 3.

**Impact on Struggling Learners:** Keeping systems late may help students because they have more time to build equation and graphing fluency. The risk is retention: students may need deliberate review before systems.

**Impact on Mastery Learning:** This placement supports mastery if Unit 6 includes prerequisite review and intervention before systems. It also allows systems mastery evidence to draw from prior units.

**Impact on SAT Readiness:** SAT Algebra includes systems and constraints, so late placement requires cumulative review to keep SAT readiness active before the end of the course.

**Impact on ACT Readiness:** ACT Preparing for Higher Math benefits from systems as applied algebra; late placement is acceptable if students receive mixed review throughout Units 4 and 5.

### Option B: Move Systems Immediately After Unit 3

**Instructional Advantages:** Systems would follow graphing, slope, linear equations, and inequalities while those skills are fresh. This is the most natural traditional instructional placement.

**Instructional Disadvantages:** Moving systems immediately after Unit 3 would require reorganizing the six-unit architecture or replacing some function/nonlinear time. It may also make the linear block feel long for students who need variety.

**Impact on Struggling Learners:** This placement reduces skill reactivation demands because graphing and inequalities are fresh. However, it may create a dense linear sequence before students see broader function ideas.

**Impact on Mastery Learning:** Earlier placement could simplify intervention because systems errors usually trace directly to graphing, equation solving, or slope. It may also allow repeated systems review later.

**Impact on SAT Readiness:** Earlier systems instruction strengthens SAT Algebra readiness sooner and provides more time for spiral review.

**Impact on ACT Readiness:** Earlier systems instruction supports ACT algebra readiness and frees Unit 6 to focus more cleanly on data, financial literacy, and modeling.

### Recommendation

Do not move systems in v3 without approval because the requested revision specifically asks for analysis rather than automatic relocation. Instructionally, the stronger long-term recommendation is to move systems immediately after Unit 3 in a future v4 if the academy approves a larger course architecture revision. For v3, keep systems in Unit 6 and add explicit prerequisite review of graphing, slope-intercept form, inequalities, and ordered-pair solution meaning.

The revision places cognitively demanding polynomial and radical operations closer to the quadratic unit where those skills have immediate purpose. It also gives nonlinear model comparison its own lesson so students are not asked to learn major model-selection content during a synthesis lesson.

**Impact on Struggling Learners**

The revision reduces early overload, especially in Unit 1. It makes prerequisite skills more explicit and delays advanced polynomial work until students have more algebraic maturity.

**Impact on Advanced Learners**

Advanced students receive optional extension pathways in every unit through SAT/ACT-style challenges, error analysis, Desmos investigations, multiple-representation tasks, open-ended modeling, and mathematical justification.

**Impact on Mastery-Based Instruction**

The sequence supports mastery by keeping new learning focused, reserving Lesson 8 for synthesis, and ensuring that major standards appear in dedicated instructional lessons before students are asked to demonstrate integrated mastery.
