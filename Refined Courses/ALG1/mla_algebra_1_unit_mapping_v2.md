# MLA Algebra I Unit and Lesson Mapping v2

**Reference File:** `mla_algebra_1_standards_crosswalk.xlsx`  
**Purpose:** This document establishes when and in what order MLA Algebra I concepts are taught. The standards crosswalk establishes what must be taught; this mapping establishes the instructional sequence.

---

## Course Sequencing Principles

This course is organized by prerequisite skill progression, conceptual development, student readiness, cognitive scaffolding, best instructional practices, support for struggling learners, and college and career readiness.

The course does not follow the order of Florida B.E.S.T., Common Core, SAT, or ACT standards documents. Standards are placed where they best support student learning.

The instructional progression is:

Foundations → Expressions → Equations → Linear Relationships → Graphing → Slope → Inequalities → Functions → Quadratic Models → Exponential Models → Systems → Data Analysis → Financial Literacy → Algebraic Modeling

## Unit 1: Foundations of Algebra and Expressions

**Unit Purpose:** Develop prerequisite skills and algebra readiness.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.A1.FND.4, MLA.A1.FND.1, MLA.A1.FND.2, MLA.A1.FND.3, MLA.A1.FND.6, MLA.A1.FND.7

**Unit Justification:** This unit intentionally begins below the first advanced Algebra I benchmark because many students need number sense, expression fluency, and algebraic language before formal equation solving. The sequence moves from readiness thinking to rational numbers, operations, variables, exponents, and equivalent expressions.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Whole-number operations, basic arithmetic facts, number line familiarity, and willingness to show work.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports all later equation solving, function notation, graph interpretation, nonlinear expressions, data calculations, and financial literacy.

## Instructional Sequencing Validation

This sequence is educationally sound because it starts with readiness and number fluency before asking students to manage formal Algebra I abstraction. Unit 1 gives struggling learners time to rebuild integer, rational-number, expression, variable, exponent, and equivalent-expression foundations. Unit 2 then develops equation fluency in a controlled progression from one-step equations to contextual applications and formulas.

Unit 3 connects equations to graphs, slope, rate of change, linear modeling, and inequalities. This placement supports SAT Algebra readiness and ACT Preparing for Higher Math because students learn to move among symbolic, graphical, and contextual representations before formal function abstraction. Unit 4 introduces functions after students have concrete linear experiences, allowing function notation, domain and range, behavior, transformations, absolute value, and comparison to develop conceptually.

Unit 5 moves into nonlinear models only after students understand functions and graph features. Quadratics are introduced before exponentials because they extend polynomial and graphing foundations; exponentials then build from exponent properties and multiplicative change. Unit 6 is intentionally application-heavy: systems, constraints, data displays, trend analysis, financial literacy, and multi-step modeling require the full Algebra I toolkit and prepare students for college and career quantitative reasoning.

Mastery-based learning is supported because each lesson has clear prerequisites, a defined reason for placement, and future-learning connections. Lesson 8 in every unit is reserved for "Putting It All Together" so students can consolidate learning through Notebook Evidence, Checkpoint Submission, and Lesson Quiz evidence before progressing.
