P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the structure, applies the correct algebraic process, and checks the result.

How to Use These Examples

Read each example slowly. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to understand how each step connects to analyzing scatterplots, association, trend lines, and correlation versus causation.

Worked Example 1: Describe association

  1. Look at the overall direction of the points.
  2. A rising pattern suggests positive association.
  3. A falling pattern suggests negative association.

Worked Example 2: Use a trend line

  1. Draw or identify a line that follows the middle of the data pattern.
  2. Use the line to estimate a value.
  3. Remember that predictions are estimates, not exact values.

Worked Example 3: Separate correlation and causation

  1. Correlation means two variables are related.
  2. Causation means one variable causes a change in the other.
  3. Do not claim causation unless the situation gives strong evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: If two variables are correlated, one must cause the other.

Correct: Correlation shows association, but it does not prove causation. Extra evidence is needed to make a cause-and-effect claim.
