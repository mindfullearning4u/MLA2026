P04 Worked Example

How to Use These Examples

Read each example slowly. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to understand how each step connects to applying linear and exponential models to financial literacy situations.

Worked Example 1: Identify simple interest

  1. Simple interest grows by adding the same amount each time period.
  2. Use the rate, principal, and time to find interest.
  3. Explain the total amount as original principal plus interest.

Worked Example 2: Identify compound interest

  1. Compound interest grows by multiplying by a growth factor.
  2. The interest earns more interest over time.
  3. Connect the model to exponential growth.

Worked Example 3: Compare financial choices

  1. Find the starting amount, rate, and time for each option.
  2. Use the correct model structure for each option.
  3. Compare final amounts and explain which choice is better for the goal.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use simple interest and compound interest the same way.

Correct: Simple interest is repeated addition, while compound interest is repeated multiplication by a growth factor.

When you check your own work, ask whether your reasoning matches the definition, model, display, or context being used in the lesson.
