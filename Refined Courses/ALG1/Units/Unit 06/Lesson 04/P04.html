P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the structure, applies the correct algebraic process, and checks the result.

How to Use These Examples

Read each example slowly. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to understand how each step connects to selecting and interpreting displays for numerical and categorical data.

Worked Example 1: Choose a display

  1. Identify whether the data are numerical or categorical.
  2. Use bar charts for categories and dot plots, histograms, or box plots for numerical data.
  3. Choose the display that makes the comparison easiest to see.

Worked Example 2: Read a scale

  1. Check the axis labels and units.
  2. Notice the interval between tick marks.
  3. Use the scale before estimating a value.

Worked Example 3: Interpret center and spread

  1. Describe where most data values are located.
  2. Notice whether values are close together or spread out.
  3. Use the display to support the interpretation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Choose a graph because it looks familiar, without checking the data type.

Correct: The best display depends on the type of data and the question being answered.

When you check your own work, ask whether your reasoning matches the definition, model, display, or context being used in the lesson.
