P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the structure, applies the correct algebraic process, and checks the result.

Study all three worked examples. Each example shows the steps and reasoning you should use before working independently.

Worked Example 1: Graph y > 2x + 1

Problem: Graph y > 2x + 1.

Step 1: Use y = 2x + 1 as the boundary line.

Step 2: Use a dashed line because the symbol is >.

Step 3: Shade above the line.

Mathematical Visual: y > 2x + 1

Worked Example 2: Graph y ≤ -x + 4

Problem: Graph y ≤ -x + 4.

Step 1: Use y = -x + 4 as the boundary line.

Step 2: Use a solid line because the symbol is ≤.

Step 3: Shade below the line.

Mathematical Visual: y ≤ -x + 4

Worked Example 3: Test a Point

Problem: Determine whether (2, 5) is a solution to y ≤ 2x + 1.

Step 1: Substitute x = 2 and y = 5.

5 ≤ 2(2) + 1

Step 2: Simplify the right side.

5 ≤ 5

Step 3: Decide whether the statement is true. Since 5 ≤ 5 is true, (2, 5) is a solution.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Using a solid boundary for y > 2x + 1.

Correct: Use a dashed boundary for < or > because points on the line are not included.

Teachable Explanation: The worked examples model both the calculation and the reasoning needed to justify the answer.
