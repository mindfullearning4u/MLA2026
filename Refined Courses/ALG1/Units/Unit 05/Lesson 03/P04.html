P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the structure, applies the correct algebraic process, and checks the result.

How to Use These Examples

Read each example slowly. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to understand how each step connects to recognizing and representing quadratic functions.

Worked Example 1: Recognize a quadratic equation

  1. Look for a squared variable term.
  2. A rule with x2 as the highest power is quadratic.
  3. Quadratic graphs form parabolas.

Worked Example 2: Use second differences

  1. Find first differences in the output values.
  2. Find differences of those differences.
  3. Constant second differences suggest a quadratic relationship.

Worked Example 3: Write a quadratic from context

  1. Identify the changing quantity.
  2. Use a quadratic model when the situation has curved or parabolic behavior.
  3. Define variables before writing the function.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Any curved graph is automatically quadratic.

Correct: A quadratic has specific structure, such as an x2 term or constant second differences in a table.

When you check your own work, ask whether your reasoning matches the definition, feature, or model type being used in the lesson.
