P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the structure, applies the correct algebraic process, and checks the result.

How to Use These Examples

Read each example slowly. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to understand how each step connects to exponential growth and decay models.

Worked Example 1: Identify exponential growth

  1. Look for repeated multiplication by a factor greater than 1.
  2. The output increases by a constant percent or ratio.
  3. Classify the model as growth.

Worked Example 2: Identify exponential decay

  1. Look for repeated multiplication by a factor between 0 and 1.
  2. The output decreases by a constant percent or ratio.
  3. Classify the model as decay.

Worked Example 3: Interpret a growth factor

  1. In y = a(b)x, b is the growth factor.
  2. If b = 1.08, the model increases by 8% each step.
  3. Explain the percent change, not just the number.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A model that adds the same amount each time is exponential.

Correct: Exponential models multiply by the same factor each step. Adding the same amount describes a linear pattern.

When you check your own work, ask whether your reasoning matches the definition, feature, or model type being used in the lesson.
