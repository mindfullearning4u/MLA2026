P04 Worked Example

How to Use These Examples

Read each example slowly. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to understand how each step connects to solving and modeling quadratic equations.

Worked Example 1: Solve by factoring

  1. Write the quadratic equal to 0.
  2. Factor the expression.
  3. Use the zero-product property to solve each factor.

Worked Example 2: Interpret solutions

  1. Quadratic solutions often represent x-intercepts.
  2. In a context, decide which solutions make sense.
  3. Reject impossible values, such as negative time when time cannot be negative.

Worked Example 3: Use a quadratic model

  1. Identify what the input and output represent.
  2. Use the model to answer the question.
  3. Explain the answer in context with units.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Keep every algebraic solution even when it does not make sense in the situation.

Correct: After solving, check the context. A mathematically correct solution may be unreasonable for the situation.

When you check your own work, ask whether your reasoning matches the definition, feature, or model type being used in the lesson.
