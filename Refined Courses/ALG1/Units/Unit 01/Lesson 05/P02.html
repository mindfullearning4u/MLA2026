Lesson 5: Variables, Expressions, and Algebraic Language

Why Algebraic Language Matters

Algebra uses symbols to describe quantities and relationships. Before solving equations, you need to understand what the symbols and parts of an expression mean.

Algebraic language helps connect a calculation to its meaning.

When you know what a variable, coefficient, term, factor, and constant represent, you can interpret formulas, functions, and real-world models more accurately.

Key Vocabulary

  • Variable: A letter or symbol that represents a quantity.
  • Expression: A mathematical phrase with numbers, variables, and operations.
  • Term: A part of an expression separated by addition or subtraction.
  • Coefficient: The number multiplied by a variable.
  • Constant: A number that does not change.
  • Factor: A number, variable, or expression being multiplied.

Expression Parts

In the expression 4x + 7:

  • 4x and 7 are terms.
  • 4 is the coefficient of x.
  • x is the variable.
  • 7 is the constant.

Example 1: Identify Parts

Expression: 6n + 12

Variable: n

Coefficient: 6

Constant: 12

Terms: 6n and 12
