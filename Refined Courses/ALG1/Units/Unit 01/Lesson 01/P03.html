How to Work Through a Readiness Problem

Example: A student earns 8 points on each practice set. The student completes 5 practice sets. How many points does the student earn?

Step 1: Identify what the problem is asking.

The problem asks for the total number of points.

Step 2: Choose an operation that matches the situation.

There are 5 equal groups of 8 points, so multiplication matches the situation.

8 × 5 = 40

Step 3: Write the answer with meaning.

The student earns 40 points.

Step 4: Check reasonableness.

The answer should be more than 8 because the student completed more than one practice set.

How to Explain a Mistake

When you find a mistake, your explanation should identify what happened and how to correct it.

Problem: Evaluate 6 + 2 × 4.

Incorrect:

6 + 2 × 4 = 8 × 4 = 32

Misconception: The student added before multiplying.

Correct:

6 + 2 × 4 = 6 + 8 = 14

Correction: Multiplication comes before addition, so 2 × 4 is completed first.

Readiness Reflection Categories

As you complete readiness work, sort your thinking into three categories.

  • Ready: I can do this skill accurately and explain my work.
  • Review Needed: I understand part of the skill, but I need more practice.
  • Support Needed: I am not sure how to start, or I keep making the same kind of mistake.

These categories help you take responsibility for your learning without guessing about what you need.
