P03 Concept Instruction

Rational exponents are another way to write roots and powers. They let you move between exponent form and radical form.

Prerequisite Review: What You Need First

Exponent: A number that tells how many times to use the base as a factor.

3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81

Root: A number that reverses a power.

√36 = 6 because 6² = 36

∛8 = 2 because 2³ = 8

New Concept: Rational Exponents

A rational exponent is an exponent written as a fraction, such as 1/2, 1/3, 2/3, or 3/4.

The denominator tells the root. The numerator tells the power.

a to the power of 1/2 means:

a1/2 = √a

a to the power of 1/3 means:

a1/3 = ∛a

a to the power of m/n means:

am/n = (nth root of a)m

Vocabulary

  • Base: The number being raised to a power.
  • Exponent: The number that tells the power or operation on the base.
  • Rational exponent: A fractional exponent.
  • Radical: A root expression, such as √25.
  • Equivalent expressions: Expressions that have the same value.

How to Evaluate a Fractional Exponent

Example: 64 to the power of 2/3

642/3

Step 1: Take the root. The denominator is 3, so take the cube root.

∛64 = 4

Step 2: Apply the power. The numerator is 2, so square the result.

42 = 16

Final Answer:

642/3 = 16

In general:

  1. Identify the base.
  2. Identify the denominator. This tells the root.
  3. Identify the numerator. This tells the power.
  4. Take the root first when possible.
  5. Apply the power.
  6. Check that the final value makes sense.

Why This Matters Mathematically

Rational exponents give mathematicians a compact way to write roots and powers. This is important later when working with radicals, polynomial expressions, exponential functions, quadratic relationships, and equivalent forms of expressions.
