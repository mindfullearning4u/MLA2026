P04 Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Evaluate a Square Root Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 49 to the power of 1/2, which means 491/2.

Step 1: Identify the exponent. 1/2 means square root.

Step 2: Rewrite using radical form.

491/2 = √49

Step 3: Evaluate the root.

√49 = 7

Answer:

491/2 = 7

Reasoning: The square root of 49 is 7 because 7² = 49.

Example 2: Evaluate a Rational Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 27 to the power of 2/3, which means 272/3.

Step 1: The denominator is 3, so take the cube root.

272/3 = (∛27)²

Step 2: Evaluate the cube root.

∛27 = 3

Step 3: Apply the numerator as a power.

3² = 9

Answer:

272/3 = 9

Reasoning: The denominator names the root; the numerator names the power.

Example 3: Use Exponent Laws to Generate an Equivalent Form

Problem: Simplify 2 to the power of 1/3 times 2 to the power of 2/3, which means 21/3 × 22/3.

Step 1: Notice the bases are the same: both bases are 2.

Step 2: When multiplying powers with the same base, add exponents.

21/3 × 22/3 = 21/3 + 2/3

Step 3: Add the fractions.

1/3 + 2/3 = 3/3 = 1

Step 4: Evaluate.

2¹ = 2

Answer:

21/3 × 22/3 = 2

Common Mistake

Mistake: Thinking 27 to the power of 2/3, which means 272/3, means 27² / 3.

Why it is wrong: The denominator of a rational exponent tells the root. It does not mean divide the expression by that number.

Correct:

272/3 = (∛27)² = 3² = 9
