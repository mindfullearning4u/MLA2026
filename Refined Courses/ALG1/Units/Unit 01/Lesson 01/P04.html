P04 Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Evaluate a Square Root Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 49^(1/2).

Step 1: Identify the exponent. 1/2 means square root.

Step 2: Rewrite using radical form. 49^(1/2) = sqrt(49)

Step 3: Evaluate the root. sqrt(49) = 7

Answer: 49^(1/2) = 7

Reasoning: The square root of 49 is 7 because 7^2 = 49.

Example 2: Evaluate a Rational Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 27^(2/3).

Step 1: The denominator is 3, so take the cube root.

27^(2/3) = (cuberoot(27))^2

Step 2: Evaluate the cube root.

cuberoot(27) = 3

Step 3: Apply the numerator as a power.

3^2 = 9

Answer: 27^(2/3) = 9

Reasoning: The denominator names the root; the numerator names the power.

Common Mistake

Mistake: Thinking 27^(2/3) means 27^2 / 3.

Why it is wrong: The denominator of a rational exponent tells the root. It does not mean divide the expression by that number.

Correct: 27^(2/3) = (cuberoot(27))^2 = 3^2 = 9
