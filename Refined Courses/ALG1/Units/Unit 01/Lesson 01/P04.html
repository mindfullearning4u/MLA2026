P04 Worked Example

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Evaluate a Square Root Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 49 to the power of 1/2.

491/2

Step 1: Identify the exponent. 1/2 means square root.

Step 2: Rewrite using radical form.

491/2 = √49

Step 3: Evaluate the root.

√49 = 7

Answer:

491/2 = 7

Reasoning: The square root of 49 is 7 because 7² = 49.

Example 2: Evaluate a Rational Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 27 to the power of 2/3.

272/3

Step 1: Identify the denominator. This tells the root.

Step 2: Rewrite using radical form.

272/3 = (∛27)2

Step 3: Evaluate the root.

∛27 = 3

Step 4: Apply the numerator as a power.

32 = 9

Answer:

272/3 = 9

Reasoning: The denominator names the root; the numerator names the power.

Example 3: Use Exponent Laws to Generate an Equivalent Form

Problem: Simplify 2 to the power of 1/3 times 2 to the power of 2/3.

21/3 × 22/3

Step 1: Notice the bases are the same: both bases are 2.

Step 2: When multiplying powers with the same base, add exponents.

21/3 × 22/3 = 21/3 + 2/3

Step 3: Add the exponents (fractions).

1/3 + 2/3 = 3/3 = 1

Step 4: Evaluate.

21 = 2

Answer:

21/3 × 22/3 = 2

Reasoning: When multiplying powers with the same base, you add the exponents.

Common Mistake

27 to the power of 2/3 means:

272/3

Incorrect:

272/3 = 272 ÷ 3

Why it is wrong: The denominator tells which root to take. It does not mean divide the expression.

Correct:

272/3 = (∛27)2 = 32 = 9
