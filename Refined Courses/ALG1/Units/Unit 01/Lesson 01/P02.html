Lesson 1: Rational Exponents and Equivalent Numerical Expressions

Prerequisite Review: What You Need First

Exponent: A number that tells how many times to use the base as a factor.

3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81

Root: A number that reverses a power.

√36 = 6 because 6² = 36

∛8 = 2 because 2³ = 8

New Concept: Rational Exponents

A rational exponent is an exponent written as a fraction, such as 1/2, 1/3, 2/3, or 3/4.

The denominator tells the root. The numerator tells the power.

a to the power of 1/2 means:

a1/2 = √a

a to the power of 1/3 means:

a1/3 = ∛a

a to the power of m/n means:

am/n = (nth root of a)m

Vocabulary

  • Base: The number being raised to a power.
  • Exponent: The number that tells the power or operation on the base.
  • Rational exponent: A fractional exponent.
  • Radical: A root expression, such as √25.
  • Equivalent expressions: Expressions that have the same value.

4. Review: Whole-Number Exponents

An exponent tells how many times a base is used as a factor.

2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16

Important exponent laws:

  • Multiply powers with the same base.

    am × an = am+n

  • Divide powers with the same base, when a ≠ 0.

    am / an = am-n

  • Raise a power to a power.

    (am)n = amn

5. Review: Roots

A root asks what number was used as a repeated factor.

  • √25 = 5 because 5² = 25.
  • ∛27 = 3 because 3³ = 27.
  • fourth root of 16 = 2 because 2⁴ = 16.

6. New Concept: Rational Exponents

A rational exponent is a fraction used as an exponent.

a to the power of 1/n means:

a1/n = nth root of a

a to the power of m/n means:

am/n = (nth root of a)m or nth root of am

The denominator tells the root. The numerator tells the power.

7. How to Evaluate a Fractional Exponent

Example: 64 to the power of 2/3

642/3

Step 1: Take the root. The denominator is 3, so take the cube root.

∛64 = 4

Step 2: Apply the power. The numerator is 2, so square the result.

42 = 16

Final Answer:

642/3 = 16

In general:

  1. Identify the base a.
  2. Identify the denominator n. This tells the root.
  3. Identify the numerator m. This tells the power.
  4. Take the root first when possible.
  5. Apply the power.
  6. Check that the final value makes sense.

8. Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not treat the fraction in the exponent as ordinary division of the whole expression.

27 to the power of 2/3 means:

272/3

Incorrect:

272/3 = 272 ÷ 3

Correct:

272/3 = (∛27)2 = 32 = 9
