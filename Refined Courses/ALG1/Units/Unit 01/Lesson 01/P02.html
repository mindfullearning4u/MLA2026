Lesson 1: Rational Exponents and Equivalent Numerical Expressions

1. Review: Whole-Number Exponents

An exponent tells how many times a base is used as a factor.

2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16

Important exponent laws:

  • aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
  • aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ, when a ≠ 0
  • (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

2. Review: Roots

A root asks what number was used as a repeated factor.

  • √25 = 5 because 5² = 25.
  • ∛27 = 3 because 3³ = 27.
  • fourth root of 16 = 2 because 2⁴ = 16.

3. New Concept: Rational Exponents

A rational exponent is a fraction used as an exponent.

a to the power of 1/n is written compactly as:

a¹ᐟⁿ = nth root of a

a to the power of m/n is written compactly as:

aᵐᐟⁿ = (nth root of a)ᵐ or nth root of aᵐ

The denominator tells the root. The numerator tells the power.

4. Step Process for Evaluating a to the power of m/n

  1. Identify the base a.
  2. Identify the denominator n. This tells the root.
  3. Identify the numerator m. This tells the power.
  4. Take the root first when possible.
  5. Apply the power.
  6. Check that the final value makes sense.

5. Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not treat the fraction in the exponent as ordinary division of the whole expression.

27 to the power of 2/3 is written compactly as 27²ᐟ³.

Incorrect: 27²ᐟ³ = 27² / 3
Correct: 27²ᐟ³ = (∛27)² = 3² = 9
