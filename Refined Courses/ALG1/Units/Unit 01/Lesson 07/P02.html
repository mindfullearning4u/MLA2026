Lesson 7: Equivalent Expressions and Simplifying Expressions

Why Equivalent Expressions Matter

Equivalent expressions have the same value, even when they look different. Simplifying expressions helps you see structure and prepares you to solve equations.

Simplifying an expression does not change its value. It rewrites the expression in a clearer form.

Key Vocabulary

  • Term: A number, a variable, or a product of numbers and variables in an expression.
  • Coefficient: The numerical factor multiplied by a variable.
  • Constant: A term with no variable.
  • Like terms: Terms with the same variable part and the same exponent.
  • Equivalent expressions: Expressions that have the same value for every value of the variable.

Like Terms

Like terms can be combined because they describe the same kind of quantity.

  • 3x and 5x are like terms.
  • 7a and 2a are like terms.
  • 4 and 9 are like terms because they are both constants.
  • 2x and 2x2 are not like terms because the exponents are different.

Example 1: Combine Like Terms

Problem: Simplify 3x + 5x.

3x + 5x = 8x

Reasoning: Both terms are x-terms, so add the coefficients: 3 + 5 = 8.

Example 2: Combine Variables and Constants

Problem: Simplify 7a + 2 + 4a.

7a + 2 + 4a = 11a + 2

Reasoning: Combine 7a and 4a. The constant 2 stays separate.
