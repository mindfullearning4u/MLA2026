Lesson 2: Integers and Rational Numbers

Why Signed Numbers Matter

Algebra uses positive and negative numbers in equations, inequalities, graphs, slope, data, and real-world models.

A sign tells direction or value compared with zero. A positive number is greater than zero. A negative number is less than zero.

-5 < 0 < 5

Vocabulary

  • Integer: A whole number, its opposite, or zero.
  • Rational number: A number that can be written as a fraction of two integers, with a nonzero denominator.
  • Opposites: Numbers the same distance from zero on opposite sides of the number line.
  • Absolute value: A number's distance from zero.

Integers and Rational Numbers

Integers include numbers such as:

..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...

Rational numbers also include fractions and decimals that can be written as fractions.

-3⁄4, 0.5, 2, -7

Number Line Reasoning

Numbers farther to the right on a number line are greater. Numbers farther to the left are less.

-8 < -2 because -8 is farther left.

A negative number with a larger absolute value is actually smaller.

Absolute Value

Absolute value tells distance from zero, so it is never negative.

|-6| = 6

|6| = 6

Both -6 and 6 are 6 units away from zero.
