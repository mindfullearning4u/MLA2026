Notebook Title

Lesson 2: Equivalent Algebraic Expressions with Exponent Properties

Review: Base, Exponent, and Like Bases

Base: The repeated factor in a power.

Exponent: The number that tells how many times the base is used as a factor.

Like bases: Powers that have the same base.

Example: x to the power of 4 and x to the power of 3 have the same base.

x4 and x3

New Concept: Equivalent Algebraic Expressions

Equivalent algebraic expressions have the same value for every allowed value of the variable.

Exponent properties help rewrite expressions without changing their value.

Product of Powers Property

x to the power of 3 times x to the power of 2 means:

x3 × x2

Because both powers have the same base, add the exponents.

x3 × x2 = x3+2 = x5

Rule:

am × an = am+n

Quotient of Powers Property

x to the power of 7 divided by x to the power of 3 means:

x7 ÷ x3

Because both powers have the same base, subtract the exponents.

x7 ÷ x3 = x7-3 = x4

Rule:

am ÷ an = am-n, where a is not 0

Power of a Power Property

x squared raised to the third power means:

(x2)3

A power is being raised to another power, so multiply the exponents.

(x2)3 = x2×3 = x6

Rule:

(am)n = amn

Power of a Product Property

2x raised to the third power means:

(2x)3

The exponent applies to each factor inside the parentheses.

(2x)3 = 23x3 = 8x3

Connection: Rational Exponents in Algebraic Expressions

6x to the power of 3/2 means:

6x3/2

The exponent 3/2 can be separated into 1 and 1/2.

x3/2 = x1x1/2

x to the power of 1/2 means the square root of x.

6x3/2 = 6x√x

Common Mistake

x to the power of 3 times y to the power of 2 means:

x3 × y2

Incorrect:

x3 × y2 = xy5

Correct:

x3 × y2 = x3y2

The bases are different, so the exponents cannot be combined.
