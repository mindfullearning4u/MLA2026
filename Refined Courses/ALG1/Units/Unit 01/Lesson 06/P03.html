Rational Exponents and Roots

A rational exponent can represent a root. The denominator of the exponent tells the type of root.

  • 361⁄2 = √36 = 6
  • 271⁄3 = ∛27 = 3
  • 163⁄2 = (√16)3 = 43 = 64

Example: Quotient of Powers

Problem: Rewrite x8⁄x3.

x8⁄x3 = x8-3 = x5

Reasoning: The bases are the same, so subtract the exponents.

Example: Power of a Power

Problem: Rewrite (a2)5.

(a2)5 = a2 · 5 = a10

Reasoning: A power raised to a power uses multiplication of exponents.

Why This Matters Mathematically

Exponent laws let you rewrite expressions efficiently while keeping the same value.

Rational exponents prepare you to understand roots and radical expressions later in the course.

Common Mistake

A common mistake is multiplying exponents when the bases are multiplied.

Problem: Rewrite x3 · x4.

Incorrect:

x3 · x4 = x12

Why it is wrong: The exponents were multiplied, but product of powers uses addition of exponents.

Correct:

x3 · x4 = x3+4 = x7

Reasoning: When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents.

Teachable Explanation: Algebra errors usually happen when a step is skipped or a rule is used without checking the expression, equation, graph, or context first.
