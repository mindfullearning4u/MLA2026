P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the structure, applies the correct algebraic process, and checks the result.

Study each example carefully. Notice whether the expression is numerical or algebraic, then choose the matching exponent property.

Worked Example 1: Numerical Exponents

Problem: Evaluate 23 · 24.

23 · 24 = 23+4 = 27 = 128

Answer: 128

Reasoning: The bases are the same, so add the exponents. Then evaluate the numerical power.

Worked Example 2: Algebraic Exponents

Problem: Rewrite y6 ÷ y2.

y6 ÷ y2 = y6-2 = y4

Answer: y4

Reasoning: The bases are the same, so subtract the exponents.

Worked Example 3: Rational Exponent

Problem: Evaluate 811⁄2.

811⁄2 = √81 = 9

Answer: 9

Reasoning: An exponent of 1⁄2 represents the square root.

Common Mistake

A common mistake is treating a zero exponent as zero.

Problem: Evaluate 70.

Incorrect:

70 = 0

Why it is wrong: A nonzero number raised to the zero power equals 1, not 0.

Correct:

70 = 1

Reasoning: The zero exponent property says x0 = 1 when x is not 0.

Teachable Explanation: The worked examples model both the calculation and the reasoning needed to justify the answer.
