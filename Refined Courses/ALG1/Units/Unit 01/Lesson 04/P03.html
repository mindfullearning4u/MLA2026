How to Evaluate an Algebraic Expression

To evaluate an algebraic expression, replace each variable with the given value. Then use the order of operations.

Step 1: Write the expression.

Step 2: Substitute the given value for the variable.

Step 3: Use order of operations to simplify.

Step 4: Check whether the answer is reasonable.

Example: Substitute and Simplify

Problem: Evaluate 4x + 6 when x = 5.

4x + 6

4(5) + 6

20 + 6 = 26

Answer: 26

Reasoning: The value 5 replaces x. Then multiplication is completed before addition.

Expression Evaluation Reminders

  • Substitute carefully before simplifying.
  • Use parentheses when substituting a value into a multiplication expression.
  • Evaluate exponents before addition or subtraction.
  • Work left to right for multiplication and division.

Why This Matters Mathematically

Evaluating expressions prepares you to use formulas, function notation, financial models, and equation-solving steps later in Algebra 1.

When your steps are organized, another person can see exactly how you used substitution and order of operations.

Common Mistake

A common mistake is adding before evaluating an exponent.

Problem: Evaluate 6 + 22.

Incorrect:

6 + 22 = 82 = 64

Why it is wrong: The addition was completed before the exponent.

Correct:

6 + 22 = 6 + 4 = 10

Reasoning: Exponents are evaluated before addition unless grouping symbols change the order.

Teachable Explanation: Algebra errors usually happen when a step is skipped or a rule is used without checking the expression, equation, graph, or context first.
