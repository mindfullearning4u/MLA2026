Learn Alcohol, Impairment, and Health Effects

Effect Pathway

Alcohol → brain/CNS → judgment, coordination, reaction time, attention → safety and behavior. Impairment can occur before someone appears severely intoxicated or recognizes the impairment.

Standard Drink and BAC Concepts

A U.S. standard drink contains about 14 grams (0.6 fluid ounces) of pure alcohol. Container size or beverage type alone does not reveal the exact amount. BAC generally rises as alcohol enters the bloodstream and is influenced by amount, rate, physiology, food, metabolism, medications, and other substances. These concepts are for risk interpretation—not personal predictions.

No Quick Sobering Method

Coffee, cold showers, fresh air, exercise, and vomiting do not rapidly remove alcohol from the bloodstream. Time and metabolism are required.

Short- and Long-Term Effects

Short-term risks include impaired judgment, falls, crashes, violence, drowning, unsafe decisions, injury, and overdose. Repeated misuse can increase risk for liver disease, cardiovascular disease, some cancers, neurological harm, dependence, and alcohol-use disorder.

Driving and Safe Transportation

Do not drive after drinking and do not ride with an impaired driver. Use a sober trusted driver, safe ride service, trusted adult, or emergency assistance when necessary.

Alcohol Overdose

Serious signs include confusion, inability to stay conscious or wake, vomiting, seizures, trouble or irregular breathing, clammy/pale/bluish skin, and dangerously low body temperature. Call emergency services immediately, follow instructions, and do not leave the person alone. Do not wait for every sign and do not “let them sleep it off.”
