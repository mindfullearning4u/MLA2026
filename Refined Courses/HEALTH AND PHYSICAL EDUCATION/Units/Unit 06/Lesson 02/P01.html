Essential Question

How can alcohol affect the brain, body, judgment, safety, and long-term health, and why are abstinence from underage drinking and appropriate emergency responses important protective decisions?

Learning Objectives

  • explain alcohol as a psychoactive central-nervous-system depressant;
  • interpret standard-drink and BAC concepts without personal calculations;
  • analyze impairment, underage-use consequences, driving risk, and long-term harm;
  • recognize alcohol-overdose warning signs and select immediate emergency help.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.SUA.01: Analyze alcohol use/misuse, impairment, underage use, risk behavior, consequences, emergencies, and prevention.

MLA.PEH.SUA.03: Apply protective decisions, refusal, emergency response, and help-seeking.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 6 Lesson 2 Vocabulary and copy all terms and definitions.

Alcohol: psychoactive substance found in alcoholic beverages. Ethanol: the type of alcohol in alcoholic beverages. Central nervous system: brain and spinal cord. Depressant: drug class that slows or disrupts CNS functioning; it is not the same as a depression diagnosis. Standard drink: U.S. comparison unit containing about 14 grams or 0.6 fluid ounces of pure alcohol. Blood alcohol concentration (BAC): concentration of alcohol in blood. Intoxication: altered normal functioning. Impairment: reduced judgment, reaction, coordination, attention, or safe performance. Binge drinking: a pattern that raises BAC to a high-risk level. Heavy drinking: repeated high alcohol consumption associated with increased health risk. Alcohol misuse: unsafe, harmful, underage, or otherwise inappropriate alcohol use. Underage drinking: alcohol use below the legal age. Judgment: ability to evaluate choices and consequences. Coordination: ability to control movements together. Reaction time: time needed to respond. Alcohol poisoning/overdose: emergency in which alcohol disrupts vital brain functions. Unconsciousness: inability to remain awake or respond normally. Vomiting: forceful emptying of stomach contents; it does not make overdose safe. Breathing: movement of air needed for life; abnormal breathing is an emergency sign. Driving under the influence: driving while affected by alcohol or another substance. Impaired driving: driving with reduced safe abilities. Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders: effects that can result from alcohol exposure during pregnancy. Dependence: adaptation that may involve withdrawal. Alcohol-use disorder: problematic alcohol-use pattern requiring qualified assessment. Recovery: ongoing improvement in health and functioning.

Privacy and Safety

All scenarios are fictional. Do not disclose personal alcohol use or family alcohol history. Never calculate a personal drinking limit or BAC. Suspected overdose requires emergency services—not the TOR as first contact.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to clarify vocabulary or a fictional scenario without sharing personal information.
