Marijuana, THC, and Impairment

THC is psychoactive. It can affect attention, memory, perception, coordination, reaction time, and judgment. Those changes matter for driving, sports, work, and other safety-sensitive tasks.

Products differ in potency and route. Delayed effects—especially with edible products—can cause a person to take more before the first amount has fully affected them. “Natural” and “legal somewhere” do not mean harmless. Medical use under qualified care is different from nonmedical use.

Repeated use may contribute to tolerance, dependence, or cannabis use disorder. Effects vary, so a person cannot guarantee safe performance after use.

Worked Example: Driving Claim

Claim: “I feel calm, so I can drive.”

Evidence: THC can slow reaction time, divide attention, and alter judgment even when a person feels calm.

Reasoning: A feeling is not an objective measure of driving ability. Safe driving requires several functions that THC may impair.

Conclusion: Do not drive or ride with an impaired driver; use a safe alternative.

Required Notebook Summary

Create a pathway with arrows: THC exposure → brain functions affected → performance/safety consequence → protective decision. Include at least three brain functions and two consequences. Finish with two sentences explaining why “natural = safe” is faulty reasoning.
