Essential Question

How can evidence about nicotine, vaping, marijuana, marketing, and impairment support safer health decisions?

Learning Objectives

  • compare tobacco, nicotine, vaping aerosol, marijuana, and THC;
  • analyze dependence, impairment, health, social, and financial consequences;
  • evaluate marketing claims and perceived norms with evidence;
  • connect protective choices to personal goals.

MLA Standard

MLA.PEH.SUA.02: Analyze the health, safety, social, legal, and performance consequences of tobacco, nicotine, vaping, marijuana, and other substance use.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 6 Lesson 3 Vocabulary. Copy each term and definition: tobacco—plant whose leaves contain nicotine; nicotine—addictive stimulant drug; dependence—adaptation that can produce cravings or withdrawal; withdrawal—symptoms after reducing or stopping an adapted substance; combustion—burning; secondhand smoke—smoke breathed from another person's tobacco use; vaping—inhaling aerosol from an electronic device; aerosol—suspension of particles and droplets, not harmless water vapor; dual use—using cigarettes and e-cigarettes; cessation—ending substance use; marijuana/cannabis—cannabis plant products; THC—main intoxicating cannabis chemical; psychoactive—affecting brain function, mood, or perception; impairment—reduced ability to perform safely; tolerance—needing more for a similar effect; potency—strength or concentration; delayed effect—effect that begins later than expected; perceived norm—belief about how common a behavior is; target audience—group a message is designed to influence; marketing claim—persuasive statement promoting a product.

Notebook check: 20 complete entries.

Privacy and Safety

Use only fictional examples. Do not disclose personal or family substance use. Never test a product or accept an unknown substance.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to clarify vocabulary or help distinguish a product, substance, claim, and health effect.
