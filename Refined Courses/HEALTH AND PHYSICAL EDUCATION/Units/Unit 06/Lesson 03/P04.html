Apply the Skill — Marketing, Norms, and Comparative Risk

Marketing can use flavors, colors, influencers, lifestyle images, selective facts, and vague words such as “clean” or “natural.” A perceived norm can also be wrong: seeing many posts does not prove most people use a product.

MessageEvidence to seekReasoned response
“Everybody vapes.”Representative prevalence dataOnline visibility does not establish a population norm.
“Natural marijuana cannot hurt performance.”Evidence about THC and impairmentOrigin does not determine safety; effects on performance do.

Required Guided-Practice Notebook Task

Copy the table headings Claim | Persuasive technique | Missing evidence | Health evidence | Conclusion. Analyze these three fictional claims: (1) “Fruit flavor means safe,” (2) “Everyone at school uses it,” and (3) “Legal means harmless.” Write one complete row for each.

How to reason: identify the exact claim; name the technique; ask what reliable evidence is absent; connect known health evidence; write a conclusion that does not overstate.

TOR Help
