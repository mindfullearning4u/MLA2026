Tobacco, Nicotine, and Vaping

Product → exposure → effect: cigarettes burn tobacco and produce smoke; e-cigarettes heat liquid and produce aerosol. Both may deliver nicotine. Aerosol is not harmless water vapor, and cigarette smoke contains products of combustion.

Nicotine activates reward pathways. Repeated exposure may create a cycle: use → short-term reward → craving/withdrawal → repeated use. Developing brains are especially vulnerable to nicotine's effects on attention, learning, and impulse control.

Consequences can be physical (heart and lung stress), mental/emotional (cravings, irritability), social (conflict or restrictions), and financial (repeated purchasing). Dual use does not erase risk.

Worked Example: “It is only vapor.”

Evidence: A vaping device creates an aerosol that can contain nicotine, ultrafine particles, flavoring chemicals, and other substances.

Reasoning: Calling it “vapor” makes the product sound like plain water, but composition—not the nickname—determines exposure.

Conclusion: The claim is misleading; vaping is not harmless.
