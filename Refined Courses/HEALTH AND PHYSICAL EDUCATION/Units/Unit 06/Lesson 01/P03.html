Modeled Analysis and Guided Practice

Worked Example

Scenario: Jordan is stressed, believes “everyone drinks,” has limited coping strategies, and has a trusted coach. Jordan reports needing more of a substance to feel the same effect.

EvidenceReasoningDefensible Conclusion
Stress, inaccurate perceived norm, limited copingThese may increase vulnerability.They are risk factors, not proof of addiction.
Trusted coachA supportive adult can help with coping and help-seeking.This is a protective factor.
More needed for similar effectThis matches the tolerance concept.It raises concern but cannot independently diagnose addiction.

How the conclusion was derived: We named only the evidence provided, matched each detail to a taught concept, and limited the conclusion to what that evidence can support. We did not turn probability into certainty or make a diagnosis.

Guided Practice

Analyze this new fictional profile: Casey takes a prescribed medication exactly as directed, faces pressure to share it, wants to remain eligible for a team, and can contact a caregiver and school counselor. Identify use versus misuse, two risk factors, three protective factors, three possible consequences of sharing, and one conclusion that cannot be made.

Required Notebook Task

Copy the worked-example table. Then create and complete a second Evidence / Reasoning / Defensible Conclusion table for Casey. Finish with: Because [evidence], I can conclude [supported conclusion]; however, I cannot conclude [unsupported diagnosis or certainty].

TOR Help

Your TOR may check one row and help you distinguish appropriate prescription use from sharing/misuse.
