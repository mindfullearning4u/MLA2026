Essential Question

How can understanding substance use, misuse, dependence, addiction, risk factors, protective factors, and health consequences help a person make safer and more informed decisions?

Quick Lesson Overview

You will distinguish related substance-use concepts, analyze fictional evidence without diagnosing anyone, and select prevention and help-seeking strategies.

Learning Objectives

  • distinguish use, misuse, tolerance, dependence, addiction, poisoning, and overdose;
  • analyze risk and protective factors without treating risk as certainty;
  • evaluate physical, mental/emotional, social, performance, and safety consequences;
  • apply prevention and professional help-seeking strategies without stigma.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.SUA.02: Analyze health consequences associated with tobacco, nicotine, vaping, marijuana/THC, prescription-drug misuse, opioids, and other drugs.

MLA.PEH.SUA.03: Apply decision-making, refusal, protective-factor, emergency-response, credible-information, and help-seeking strategies.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 6 Lesson 1 Vocabulary. Copy every term and definition into your notebook.

Substance: material that may affect body or brain function. Drug: chemical substance affecting body or brain function. Psychoactive substance: substance affecting mood, perception, thinking, or behavior. Substance use: broad term for using a substance; it does not automatically mean misuse. Substance misuse: unsafe, inappropriate, unlawful, harmful, or contrary-to-directions use. Prescription drug: medication prescribed for a particular person and purpose. Illicit drug: drug whose possession or use is unlawful. Legal drug: regulated substance whose possession or use may be lawful under defined conditions. Underage use: use prohibited because a person is below the legal age. Intoxication: altered normal functioning caused by a substance. Impairment: reduced thinking, reaction, coordination, judgment, or safe performance. Tolerance: reduced response after repeated exposure; it does not mean safety. Dependence: physical or psychological adaptation that may involve withdrawal or difficulty stopping. Withdrawal: symptoms after a dependent person stops or reduces use. Addiction: health condition involving a broader problematic pattern causing significant harm or impairment. Substance-use disorder: clinical term for a problematic substance-use pattern requiring qualified assessment. Craving: strong urge to use a substance. Risk factor: condition that may increase vulnerability but does not guarantee an outcome. Protective factor: condition that may reduce vulnerability or support safety. Peer influence: effect peers may have on choices. Perceived norm: belief about what others commonly do, which may be inaccurate. Recovery: ongoing process of improving health and functioning. Treatment: professional care intended to address a health condition. Relapse: return to use during recovery; it is not proof that recovery is impossible. Overdose: dangerous toxic effects beyond what the body can safely handle. Poisoning: illness or injury caused by a harmful substance exposure.

Privacy and Safety

All scenarios are fictional. Do not disclose personal or family substance use, prescriptions, treatment, or recovery. This lesson provides health education, not diagnosis or individualized medical advice.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to clarify a term or fictional scenario. Your TOR can help you separate evidence from assumptions without asking for personal disclosure.
