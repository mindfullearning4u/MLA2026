These Terms Are Related—but Not Interchangeable

ConceptMeaningReasoning Boundary
UseBroad categoryDoes not automatically equal misuse.
MisuseUnsafe, inappropriate, unlawful, harmful, or contrary to directionsDoes not by itself prove dependence or addiction.
DependenceAdaptation that may involve withdrawalIs not automatically addiction in every medical context.
Addiction/SUDBroader problematic pattern causing significant harm or impairmentRequires qualified assessment; one sign does not diagnose it.

Risk Web

Risk may involve biology, stress, mental/emotional health, peers, family or social environment, media, access, and perceived norms. Protective factors include supportive relationships, healthy coping, accurate information, goals, refusal skills, trusted adults, and access to help. Risk changes probability—not destiny.

Health Dimensions

A substance-related decision can affect physical health, mental/emotional health, relationships, school or athletic performance, finances, legal/safety responsibilities, and future goals. Psychoactive substances may affect reward, judgment, coordination, attention, memory, and emotion; effects vary by substance, amount, pattern, and other factors.

Prevention, Treatment, and Recovery

Prevention includes accurate information, healthy coping, supportive relationships, avoiding high-risk situations, refusal skills, and help-seeking. Substance-use disorders can be treated through qualified medical, behavioral, counseling, and recovery-support services. Recovery is ongoing and may include setbacks; relapse is not a moral failure or permanent defeat.
