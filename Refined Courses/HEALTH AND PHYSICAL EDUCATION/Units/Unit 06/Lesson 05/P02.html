Prevention and Refusal Skills

Effective prevention works at several levels: personal knowledge and coping skills; supportive peers/family; safe school/community policies; and access to qualified care. Protective factors can interrupt risk pathways.

An assertive refusal has five parts: clear no + brief reason/value + alternative + exit + support. It does not require debate or personal disclosure.

Worked Example

Pressure: “Take this pill; it will help you focus.”

Response: “No. I do not take medicine that was not prescribed to me. I’m going to study in the library. I’m leaving now and calling my ride.”

Reasoning: The response sets a boundary, ties it to safety, offers an alternative, exits, and activates support.

Required Notebook Task

Create a table labeled Pressure | Clear refusal | Reason/value | Alternative | Exit | Support. Write complete responses for: offered a vape, invited to ride with an impaired driver, offered an unknown pill, and pressured to drink at a party. Then identify two risk factors and three protective factors across the scenarios.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to check whether each refusal is direct and includes an achievable exit and backup support.
