Emergency Response and Help-Seeking

Emergency sequence: recognize danger → call emergency services → give accurate location/signs/known substance → naloxone if opioid overdose is possible and available → follow dispatcher directions/CPR training → recovery position when appropriate → stay and monitor → transfer information to responders.

Never delay to avoid embarrassment or punishment. Never leave, force food/drink, induce vomiting, or use a cold shower. Naloxone's effect may wear off, and other substances may still cause harm.

Resource Match

  • Immediate life threat: emergency services.
  • Possible poisoning while responsive: Poison Control/medical professional; emergency services if severe signs.
  • Ongoing substance concern: licensed medical/behavioral-health provider or treatment referral.
  • School support: TOR, counselor, nurse, or administrator within their role.
  • Crisis or self-harm danger: emergency services or an appropriate crisis service.

Required Notebook Task

Draw the emergency sequence in arrows. Then create a five-row table labeled Situation | Urgency | First resource | Backup resource | Information to communicate using the five situations above.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to verify your resource matches. In an actual emergency, do not wait for TOR review.
