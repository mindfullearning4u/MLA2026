Prescription Drugs and Medication Safety

Appropriate prescription use follows five controls: right person, right medicine, right dose, right time/route, right purpose. Taking extra, using someone else's medication, changing the route, or using for another purpose is misuse.

ClassLegitimate purposeKey misuse risk
OpioidSome pain treatmentRespiratory depression, dependence, overdose
StimulantCertain diagnosed conditionsHeart strain, agitation, toxicity
DepressantCertain anxiety/sleep/seizure careSedation and dangerous breathing suppression, especially with alcohol/opioids

OTC does not mean risk-free. Follow labels and ask a pharmacist or provider about interactions.

Worked Example

A friend offers a prescription stimulant for studying. Evidence: it was not prescribed to the student, and dose/health history are unknown. Reasoning: a legitimate medicine becomes unsafe when controls are removed. Conclusion: refuse, do not share, and use safe study/support strategies.

Required Notebook Task

Copy the three-row class table. Then make a two-column list with five examples of appropriate use and five of misuse. Finish with an evidence → reasoning → conclusion response to the worked example.
