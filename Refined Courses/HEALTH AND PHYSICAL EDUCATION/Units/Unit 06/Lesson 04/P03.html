Opioids, Fentanyl, Counterfeit Pills, and Mixing Risks

Opioids can reduce pain and also slow breathing. Fentanyl is extremely potent. Illicitly manufactured fentanyl may appear in counterfeit pills or other drugs without the user's knowledge. Appearance cannot verify contents.

Polysubstance use makes effects less predictable. Combining opioids, alcohol, or other depressants can compound sedation and respiratory depression. Stimulant plus depressant does not “balance out”; both can stress the body while masking warning signs.

Unknown pill rule: do not take it, do not taste/test it, keep others away, and seek qualified help.

Worked Example: Counterfeit Pill

Evidence: A pill came from social media rather than a licensed pharmacy; its source and contents cannot be verified. Reasoning: shape, color, or imprint can be copied, and fentanyl contamination can be lethal. Conclusion: do not take or share it; contact a trusted adult or qualified service.
