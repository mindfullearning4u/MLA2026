Apply the Skill — Recognize and Respond to Overdose

Possible opioid-overdose signs

Unresponsiveness, very slow/irregular/stopped breathing, choking or gurgling sounds, limp body, and pale/blue/gray skin or lips are emergency signs.

Immediate response

  1. Call emergency services now; state the location, signs, and known substances.
  2. Give naloxone if available and you are trained/legally permitted; follow device directions.
  3. Support breathing or CPR as directed by the dispatcher and your training.
  4. Place the person in the recovery position when appropriate and monitor continuously.
  5. Stay until help arrives. A return of breathing does not cancel emergency care.

Do not make the person walk, force food/drink, induce vomiting, put them in a cold shower, or leave them alone.

Worked Example

A person will not wake and has slow, gurgling breaths beside an unknown pill. Evidence: unresponsiveness and abnormal breathing are life-threatening signs. Reasoning: waiting for certainty risks oxygen loss. Conclusion: call emergency services immediately, use naloxone if available, follow dispatcher directions, and stay.
