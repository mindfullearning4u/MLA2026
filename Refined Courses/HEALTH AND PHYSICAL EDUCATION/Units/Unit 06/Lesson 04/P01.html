Essential Question

How do medication-use decisions, drug interactions, overdose recognition, and immediate help-seeking reduce preventable harm?

Learning Objectives

  • distinguish appropriate prescription use from misuse;
  • compare opioids, stimulants, depressants, OTC medicines, and illicit drugs;
  • analyze fentanyl, counterfeit-pill, mixing, and overdose risks;
  • select safe emergency, storage, disposal, and help-seeking actions.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.SUA.02: Analyze substance risks and consequences. MLA.PEH.SUA.03: Apply protective decisions, emergency response, and help-seeking.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 6 Lesson 4 Vocabulary and copy: prescription—provider-authorized medication order; appropriate use—correct person, dose, time, route, and purpose; misuse—use in a way not directed; opioid—pain-relieving drug class that can slow breathing; stimulant—drug class that increases CNS activity; depressant—drug class that slows CNS activity; OTC medicine—medicine available without a prescription; drug interaction—one substance changing another's effect; polysubstance use—using multiple substances; respiratory depression—dangerously slowed or stopped breathing; fentanyl—very potent synthetic opioid; counterfeit pill—fake pill made to resemble legitimate medicine; overdose—toxic amount or combination disrupting vital functions; naloxone—medicine that can temporarily reverse opioid overdose; Poison Control—professional poison-exposure guidance service; safe storage—secure storage away from unauthorized users; safe disposal—approved removal of unused medicine; stigma—negative judgment that can block help; help-seeking—contacting qualified support; Good Samaritan protection—legal protection that may encourage emergency calls, varying by jurisdiction.

Safety Boundary

Never take another person's pill or an unknown pill. Suspected overdose requires emergency services immediately. Naloxone does not replace emergency care.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to clarify terms or fictional scenarios. In an emergency, call emergency services first—not the TOR.
