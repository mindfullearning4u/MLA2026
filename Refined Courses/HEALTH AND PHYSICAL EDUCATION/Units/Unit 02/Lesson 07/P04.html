Worked Example 1

  1. Identify the student goal or problem.
  2. Match it to the lesson concept: adjust fitness plans for barriers, interests, and resources.
  3. Use the chart or model to choose a safe first step.

Model answer: The safest first step is the one that uses evidence and can be monitored.

Worked Example 2

  1. Look for risk factors.
  2. Look for protective factors.
  3. Choose the option that reduces risk and supports wellness.

Model answer: A stronger plan names the risk, removes or reduces it, and checks progress.

Worked Example 3

  1. Evaluate whether the plan is realistic.
  2. Adjust the plan so it fits time, access, safety, and health needs.
  3. Explain how the adjustment improves success.

Model answer: A realistic plan is specific enough to follow and safe enough to continue.

Common Mistake

A plan that is extreme, vague, unsafe, or not connected to the evidence does not show mastery. Revise it by naming the exact action, reason, and check.
