P03 — Examples and Guided Practice
Worked Example 1: Brisk Walking
Primary: cardiorespiratory endurance because effort is sustained. Supporting: muscular endurance because the legs contract repeatedly; balance and coordination support safe movement. Reasoning: identify duration and repeated movement evidence before naming components.
Worked Example 2: Basketball Sequence
A short sequence of running, stopping, changing direction, jumping, and responding to a pass uses cardiorespiratory endurance, speed, agility, power, coordination, reaction time, and balance. The primary component depends on the specific task being analyzed—not the activity name alone.
Guided Practice — notebook table
Create columns: Activity Evidence; Primary Component; Two Supporting Components; Health- or Skill-Related; Reasoning. Analyze: (1) steady cycling for 20 minutes; (2) holding a controlled stable position; (3) quickly changing direction around safe markers. For each, cite the exact movement or duration evidence that supports your conclusion.
Reasoning Check
Steady cycling → cardiorespiratory endurance is primary because “steady for 20 minutes” signals sustained effort. Muscular endurance and coordination support repeated pedaling. This conclusion comes from the evidence, not from guessing based on familiarity.
