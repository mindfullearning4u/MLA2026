LESSON 2 — CARDIORESPIRATORY FITNESS: AEROBIC ACTIVITY AND EXERCISE INTENSITY
P01 — Overview and Vocabulary
Essential Question
How can you determine whether aerobic activity is challenging your cardiorespiratory system at an appropriate intensity and adjust activity safely?
Overview and Why It Matters
You will compare aerobic and anaerobic activity, calculate an instructional target heart-rate range, combine heart rate, talk test, RPE, and physiological response, and make safe intensity decisions. No wearable is required.
Learning Objectives
Classify aerobic/anaerobic/mixed activity; distinguish intensity from duration; calculate and interpret 220 − age and a consistent 60%–80% instructional range; use talk test and 0–10 RPE; analyze physical evidence; and maintain, increase, decrease, modify, or stop safely.
MLA Standards
MLA.PEH.FIT.02 intensity and physiological response; MLA.PEH.FIT.05 lifetime activity; supporting MLA.PEH.FIT.01, MLA.PEH.SAF.01, MLA.PEH.GOL.02.
Notebook — copy all 19 terms and definitions
Cardiorespiratory endurance: sustained support from heart, lungs, vessels, and related systems. Aerobic activity: sustained activity substantially supported by oxygen-based energy processes. Anaerobic activity: shorter, higher-intensity activity with rapid energy demand. Exercise intensity: how hard the body works. Heart rate: heartbeats per minute. Pulse: felt arterial beat representing heart rate. Resting heart rate: heart rate at rest. Estimated maximum heart rate: instructional estimate using 220 − age. Target heart-rate zone: estimated range for an intended intensity. Moderate intensity: noticeable but controlled effort. Vigorous intensity: hard effort below deliberate maximal work. Talk test: speech-based intensity check. Rate of perceived exertion: personal effort rating. RPE: abbreviation for rate of perceived exertion. Physiological response: observable body response to activity. Recovery: gradual movement toward resting levels. Duration: how long activity lasts. Monitoring: collecting evidence. Adjustment: change based on evidence.
