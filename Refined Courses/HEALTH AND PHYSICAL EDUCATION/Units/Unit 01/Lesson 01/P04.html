P04 — Supported Notebook Practice

Classify evidence, explain interactions, and select a supported action.

This is guided notebook practice—not Independent Work. Record your responses in your notebook under the heading P04 Supported Practice. The prompts and reasoning supports on this page are here to teach you the analysis process. Page 5 is where you will use the process independently.

Health Interrelationship Organizer

Copy this organizer for each scenario.

Scenario detailDimension(s)Possible interaction

Then add: one protective factor, one risk factor, one realistic action, and a one-sentence evidence-based justification.

Practice Scenario 1 — Schedule Pressure

Elena has added several activities to an already busy week. She skips her usual lunch break to finish tasks, stays online late, and has difficulty concentrating the next morning. A trusted adult helps Elena list which commitments are required and which can be rescheduled.

Watch the first conclusion being built

  1. Locate the exact evidence: “stays online late” and “has difficulty concentrating the next morning.”
  2. Classify it: Staying online late may affect sleep, which belongs to the physical dimension. Difficulty concentrating belongs to the mental dimension.
  3. Connect the evidence: A defensible conclusion is: Elena's late schedule may reduce sleep or recovery, which could make concentration more difficult the next morning.
  4. Check the wording: The conclusion says may and could because the scenario supports a possible relationship but does not prove a medical cause.

Now complete the remaining analysis

  1. Identify a third relevant dimension and cite its exact evidence.
  2. Identify one risk factor. Use the frame: _____ is a risk factor because _____.
  3. Identify the protective factor provided near the end of the scenario.
  4. Recommend one realistic action. Use the frame: Elena could _____; this may support the _____ and _____ dimensions because _____.

Practice Scenario 2 — Unsafe Route

Dev wants to walk after sunset, but the usual route has poor lighting and no sidewalk. Dev chooses an indoor movement option and asks a family member to help identify a safer daylight route for another day.

Reasoning hint: Begin with the environmental evidence, not with a judgment about Dev. Poor lighting and no sidewalk increase exposure to a hazard. Dev's goal is still to be active, so the analysis should consider how to preserve the health-supporting behavior while reducing risk.

  1. Quote the two environmental details.
  2. Explain how each detail changes the safety of the physical-health behavior.
  3. Identify both protective choices Dev makes.
  4. Complete this reasoning frame: The indoor option is a responsible modification because it preserves _____ while reducing _____.
  5. Explain why asking a family member is an example of using a resource rather than avoiding responsibility.

Practice Scenario 3 — Team Communication

During an online project, Kai misunderstands a short message and responds angrily. After taking a break, Kai rereads the thread, asks what the classmate meant, and suggests a video meeting to clarify the plan. The group then divides the work more clearly.

Less support this time: Use the process you practiced above. Separate the sequence into initial problem → response → resource or strategy → result. This helps you avoid mixing the risk with the protective actions.

  1. Identify the mental, emotional, and social evidence. Quote or closely paraphrase one detail for each.
  2. Explain two possible interactions using because, therefore, or which may.
  3. Identify one initial risk factor and two later protective factors.
  4. Recommend one action that could support more than one dimension and explain the connection.
  5. Check your conclusion: Did you use only scenario evidence? Did you avoid diagnosing Kai or claiming a guaranteed result?

What Goes in Your Notebook

Keep the completed Health Interrelationship organizer and your responses to all three supported-practice scenarios. These are guided-practice components of the single U1 L1 Notebook Submission. They are not a separate Moodle assignment and they are not the Independent Work.

Stop and check: Your P04 notes should show evidence, classification, interaction, risk/protective factors, and reasoning—not answers copied without explanation.
