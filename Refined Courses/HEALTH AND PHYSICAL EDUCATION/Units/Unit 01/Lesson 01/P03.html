P03 — Examples and Guided Practice

Use scenario details as evidence; do not make assumptions.

A Reliable Analysis Process

  1. Locate specific scenario details.
  2. Classify each detail by wellness dimension.
  3. Explain how two dimensions may interact.
  4. Identify a protective factor and a risk factor.
  5. Recommend one realistic action supported by evidence.
  6. Use may, can, or could when an outcome is possible rather than certain.

Notebook load: Study the two modeled examples, but do not copy them. Your written work begins in Guided Practice and uses one concise table—not three essays.

Modeled Example 1: Several Nights of Poor Sleep

Scenario: Jordan has slept about five hours on several school nights. Jordan reports low energy, has difficulty concentrating, becomes impatient during a group chat, and postpones an assignment.

How the conclusion is derived

  1. Evidence: five hours of sleep, low energy, difficulty concentrating, impatience, and a postponed task.
  2. Classification: sleep and energy are physical; concentration and task completion are mental; impatience may involve emotional regulation and social communication.
  3. Interaction: reduced sleep may contribute to lower energy and focus, which could affect communication and decisions.
  4. Claim limit: the scenario does not prove a diagnosis or a single cause, so the conclusion uses may.
  5. Action: a consistent wind-down routine addresses the repeated short sleep; seeking support is appropriate if difficulty continues.

Defensible conclusion: Repeated insufficient sleep is a risk factor that may affect several dimensions of Jordan's wellness.

Modeled Example 2: Walking with a Friend

Scenario: Priya and a friend take a comfortable 20-minute walk after school three days each week. Priya enjoys the conversation, says the routine creates a break between tasks, and prepares water before leaving.

How the conclusion is derived

  1. Physical evidence: walking and hydration preparation.
  2. Social evidence: shared activity and conversation with a friend.
  3. Mental/emotional evidence: the reported break may support transition or stress management.
  4. Interaction: social support may help maintain the physical routine, while the routine creates time for connection.
  5. Conclusion check: every claim uses stated evidence; nothing depends on appearance or athletic ability.

Defensible conclusion: Priya's planned routine combines several protective factors that may reinforce one another.
