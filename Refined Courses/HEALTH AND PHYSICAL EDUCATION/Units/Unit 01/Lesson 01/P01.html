Essential Question

How do different dimensions of health interact to influence a person's overall wellness?

Quick Lesson Overview

Health is more than physical fitness or the absence of illness. It includes interconnected physical, mental, emotional, social, and environmental dimensions. In this lesson, you will learn how those dimensions influence one another and how behaviors, circumstances, risks, and supports can affect overall wellness.

You will use fictional scenarios rather than private medical information. Your final work will show that you can identify evidence, explain interactions, and recommend realistic health-supporting actions.

Why This Lesson Matters

Every later unit—fitness, nutrition, mental wellness, relationships, safety, substance-use prevention, and lifetime wellness—depends on understanding that one choice or circumstance may affect several parts of health at the same time.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • define health, wellness, and the five dimensions of health;
  • distinguish health from wellness;
  • classify scenario evidence by wellness dimension;
  • explain at least two interactions among dimensions;
  • identify protective and risk factors;
  • distinguish factors a person can influence from factors that may require adaptation or support; and
  • recommend a realistic health-supporting action using evidence.

Vocabulary

Required notebook directions: Open your Physical Education and Health notebook. Write the heading Unit 1 Lesson 1 Vocabulary. Then write every bold vocabulary term below and its complete provided definition. This vocabulary page is required evidence for your Notebook Submission. Do not shorten the definitions or replace them with Internet definitions.

Health
A person's overall condition across physical, mental, emotional, social, and environmental dimensions.
Wellness
An active, ongoing process of making informed choices and using supports to improve or maintain health.
Physical health
The condition and functioning of the body, including activity, sleep, nutrition, safety, and recovery.
Mental health
How a person thinks, learns, focuses, solves problems, and responds to challenges.
Emotional health
How a person recognizes, expresses, and manages feelings.
Social health
The quality of relationships, communication, belonging, boundaries, and support.
Environmental health
How surroundings—such as safety, air, noise, resources, and living or learning conditions—affect health.
Health behavior
An action or pattern of actions that can affect health.
Protective factor
A condition, skill, relationship, or resource that can reduce risk or support health.
Risk factor
A condition or behavior that can increase the likelihood of harm or a negative health outcome.
Lifestyle
A person's regular pattern of choices, habits, and daily activities.
Self-awareness
The ability to recognize one's patterns, strengths, needs, feelings, and influences.
Health status
A description of health at a particular time based on multiple kinds of information—not appearance alone.
