P02 — Learn the Concept

Health is multidimensional, dynamic, and interconnected.

Health and Wellness

Health describes a person's condition across several dimensions at a particular time. Wellness is the ongoing process of making informed choices, responding to circumstances, and using resources to support health.

Health is not a fixed label. It can change as behaviors, relationships, surroundings, resources, stressors, and life circumstances change. A person may have strengths in some dimensions and needs in others. A health challenge does not mean the person has failed at wellness.

Important distinction: being free from a diagnosed illness is not the same as having complete wellness, and having a health condition does not prevent a person from making health-supporting choices.

The Five Dimensions

Physical health

Includes body functioning, sleep, movement, nutrition, hydration, hygiene, safety, recovery, and access to appropriate care. Physical appearance or athletic ability alone cannot establish physical or overall health.

Mental health

Includes thinking, concentration, learning, decision-making, and responding to challenges. Mental health is related to, but not identical with, emotional health.

Emotional health

Includes identifying feelings, expressing them appropriately, regulating responses, coping with stress, and seeking help when needed.

Social health

Includes communication, boundaries, belonging, conflict management, supportive relationships, and access to trusted people.

Environmental health

Includes the conditions surrounding a person: physical safety, air and water quality, noise, weather, transportation, housing or learning spaces, and access to resources.

Interrelationships: Follow the Evidence

Insufficient sleep → lower concentration → irritability → strained communication → weaker decision-making

Regular activity → physical response → possible stress relief → possible sleep support → broader wellness

These arrows show possible relationships, not guaranteed outcomes. People respond differently. In an analysis, use words such as may, can, or could unless the scenario gives definite evidence.

Notebook organizer: Draw two chains. For each chain, record the starting behavior or circumstance, two dimensions affected, the evidence, and one possible result.

Influences, Responsibility, and Support

Health is influenced by choices and habits, but also by values, attitudes, relationships, culture, media, heredity, available resources, and environmental conditions. Responsible wellness means recognizing what a person can influence while responding appropriately to what cannot be controlled immediately.

Support may include a trusted adult, Teacher of Record, qualified healthcare professional, school resource, or community service. Seeking appropriate help is a protective action, not a failure of responsibility.

Correct the Misconceptions

  • “Health means not being sick.” Health includes multiple dimensions and behaviors.
  • “Appearance proves health.” Appearance alone cannot establish health status.
  • “Dimensions work separately.” Evidence often shows interaction.
  • “A health challenge means failure.” Wellness includes informed management, support, and adaptation.

Required Notebook Summary

Write the heading “P02 Quick Summary” in your notebook. Under it, copy the five numbered ideas below. These are your required essential lesson notes.

  1. Health describes a person's condition across physical, mental, emotional, social, and environmental dimensions.
  2. Wellness is an active, ongoing process of making informed choices, adapting to circumstances, and using appropriate support.
  3. The five dimensions interact; a behavior or circumstance in one dimension may affect several others.
  4. Health is dynamic, and one indicator—especially appearance—cannot define total wellness.
  5. Personal responsibility includes acting on factors a person can influence and seeking appropriate help or adapting when a factor is not under immediate control.

Then add one original example: Write a two- or three-step chain showing how one behavior or circumstance could affect at least two wellness dimensions. Use may, can, or could rather than claiming that the result is guaranteed.
