PHYSICAL EDUCATION AND HEALTH
UNIT 1 — FOUNDATIONS OF HEALTH, WELLNESS, AND SAFE INDEPENDENT PARTICIPATION
LESSON 5 — BUILDING YOUR PERSONAL HEALTH AND ACTIVITY PLAN
P01 — Lesson Overview and Vocabulary Review
Essential Question
How can health goals, decision-making, safety, and self-monitoring be combined into a realistic personal plan?
Quick Lesson Overview
Combine Lessons 1–4 into one realistic initial health and activity plan. This is not yet a scientific fitness program; Unit 2 teaches that design.
Why This Lesson Matters
A usable plan connects a goal to safe actions, barriers, monitoring, support, and adjustment so you can make responsible independent decisions.
Learning Objectives
Select a non-sensitive baseline; write a SMART goal; choose safe actions; match barriers with strategies; select evidence; and justify plan adjustments.
MLA Standards Connection
MLA.PEH.GOL.01 goal setting; MLA.PEH.GOL.02 monitoring and modification; MLA.PEH.SAF.01 safe activity; MLA.PEH.WEL.03 decision-making.
Notebook — copy these 15 terms and provided definitions
Wellness: active pursuit of balanced health. Goal: desired result. SMART goal: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound. Baseline: starting evidence. Barrier: obstacle. Strategy: planned action. Monitoring: collecting evidence. Progress: movement toward a goal. Safety: practices reducing preventable harm. Risk: possibility of harm. Modification: safer or more workable change. Physical activity: body movement using energy. Support: appropriate help. Resource: useful tool or service. Adjustment: evidence-based plan change.

Write all 15 bold terms and definitions in your notebook. Do not search for alternate definitions.

Lesson Path and Required Work
P01 vocabulary → P02 framework → P03 model → P04 guided plan → P05 independent notebook plan → P06 checkpoint. Submit the separate Notebook and Mastery Checkpoint assignments. There is no Lesson 5 quiz; the Unit Assessment follows.
Privacy Reminder
Use a non-sensitive baseline. Do not include diagnoses, medication, weight, trauma, or private family information.
