Essential Question

How can media, social comparison, advertising, and body-image messages influence food and health decisions, and how can those messages be evaluated critically?

Overview, Purpose, and Objectives

Fictional examples will help you identify media influence, persuasion, image limitations, testimonials, conflicts, and evidence without discussing your own body or diet.

  • explain how media messages may influence beliefs and decisions;
  • distinguish appearance/popularity/testimonials from evidence;
  • identify audiences and persuasion techniques;
  • evaluate qualifications, conflicts, data, and credible comparisons; and
  • correct restriction, food-moralization, and exercise-punishment messages.

Body image: thoughts, perceptions, attitudes, and feelings about body/appearance. Self-image: overall perception of self. Body ideal: culturally promoted appearance standard. Social comparison: comparing self with others. Media literacy: critically analyzing media. Health literacy: finding, understanding, evaluating, and using health information. Influencer: person whose online reach may affect audiences. Advertisement: message promoting a product/idea. Marketing: strategies encouraging interest/action. Target audience: intended group. Persuasion: attempt to influence belief/action. Claim: asserted statement. Evidence: support for a conclusion. Testimonial: reported personal experience. Endorsement: public support for a product/idea. Edited image: digitally altered image. Filter: tool changing image appearance. Algorithm: system influencing repeated content exposure. Diet culture: media/social pattern moralizing food or elevating restriction/appearance. Diet trend: eating pattern rapidly promoted through media. Misinformation: false/inaccurate information shared without necessarily intending harm. Disinformation: false information intentionally created/shared to mislead. Credibility: trustworthiness based on expertise/evidence/context. Conflict of interest: interest that may influence presentation.
