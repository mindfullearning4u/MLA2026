Essential Question

How do nutrients, hydration, and overall dietary patterns support health, physical activity, and normal body function?

Why This Lesson Matters

Nutrition claims appear in advertising, social media, sports culture, and everyday conversation. A sound foundation helps you make decisions based on function, evidence, access, culture, and overall patterns rather than fear or appearance.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • identify the six broad nutrient categories and explain their general functions;
  • distinguish macronutrients from micronutrients;
  • explain the roles of water, fiber, and electrolytes;
  • analyze factors that influence hydration needs;
  • evaluate dietary variety, balance, adequacy, and context; and
  • use evidence to correct common nutrition misconceptions.

Vocabulary

Required notebook directions: Write the heading Unit 3 Lesson 1 Vocabulary. Copy every bold term and its complete provided definition. These 18 entries are required Notebook evidence; do not replace them with Internet definitions.

Nutrition
The process and study of obtaining and using food and nutrients to support body function and health.
Nutrient
A substance in food or drink that supports energy, growth, repair, regulation, or other body functions.
Macronutrient
A nutrient needed in relatively larger amounts: carbohydrate, protein, or fat.
Micronutrient
A vitamin or mineral needed in smaller amounts for important body processes.
Carbohydrate
A macronutrient that is an important energy source and supports physical activity.
Protein
A macronutrient that supports growth, maintenance, tissue repair, enzymes, and other functions.
Fat
A macronutrient that supplies energy and supports cells and absorption of certain vitamins.
Vitamin
An organic micronutrient that helps regulate and support body processes.
Mineral
An inorganic micronutrient with structural or regulatory roles.
Water
An essential nutrient that supports fluid balance, transport, and temperature regulation but provides no calories.
Hydration
Maintaining enough body fluid to support normal function.
Dehydration
A condition in which fluid loss exceeds replacement enough to interfere with normal function.
Fiber
A carbohydrate component in plant foods that supports digestive and other health functions.
Dietary pattern
The overall pattern of foods and drinks consumed over time.
Nutrient density
The useful nutrients a food provides relative to its overall energy and content context.
Energy
The capacity to perform body functions and physical activity.
Calorie
A unit used to measure energy in food and energy used by the body.
Electrolyte
A mineral involved in fluid balance and other body functions.
