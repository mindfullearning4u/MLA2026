Essential Question

How can understanding transmission, prevention, detection, and treatment help reduce communicable disease?

Quick Lesson Overview

You will trace fictional transmission chains, separate exposure from infection and diagnosis, and evaluate individual and public-health prevention.

Learning Objectives

  • identify pathogens and transmission routes;
  • select methods that interrupt transmission;
  • distinguish exposure, infection, symptoms, testing, and diagnosis;
  • explain why treatment depends on qualified evaluation.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.HLT.01: Analyze communicable and chronic disease prevention and health decisions.

MLA.PEH.HLT.02: Apply hygiene and preventive-health practices.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 5 Lesson 2 Vocabulary. Copy all terms and definitions.

Communicable disease: illness capable of spreading through a transmission route. Infectious disease: illness caused by a pathogen. Pathogen: organism or agent capable of causing disease. Virus: infectious agent that reproduces in host cells. Bacterium: single-celled organism, some forms of which cause disease. Fungus: organism that can cause certain infections. Parasite: organism living on or in a host and obtaining resources from it. Transmission: movement of a pathogen between sources and susceptible hosts. Direct contact: person-to-person physical transmission. Indirect contact: transmission through a contaminated object or surface. Droplet: short-range respiratory particles. Airborne transmission: spread through smaller particles that remain suspended. Vector: organism that carries a pathogen between hosts. Exposure: contact with a possible source; it does not prove infection. Infection: invasion and multiplication of a pathogen. Incubation period: time between infection and symptom onset. Symptom: experienced or observable sign of illness. Asymptomatic: infected without noticeable symptoms. Contagious: capable of transmitting infection. Prevention: action reducing disease risk or spread. Vaccination: use of a vaccine to develop protection. Immunity: the body’s ability to resist a particular infection. Screening: checking for possible disease or risk before diagnosis. Testing: analysis used to detect evidence. Diagnosis: qualified identification of a health condition. Treatment: care intended to manage or cure disease. Public health: organized efforts to protect community health.
