Unit 5, Lesson 5: Personal Safety, Exploitation Prevention, and Human Trafficking Awareness

Page 1 — Lesson Overview and Vocabulary

Essential Question

How can a person recognize possible exploitation or trafficking risks, maintain boundaries and personal security, and seek appropriate help without investigating danger alone?

Objectives

  • Recognize evidence-based warning patterns without treating a warning sign as proof.
  • Distinguish force, fraud, and coercion and apply personal-security boundaries.
  • Choose safe disengagement, trusted help, reporting, or emergency response.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.DIG.02 — Evaluate online predatory behavior and protect digital privacy and safety.

MLA.PEH.SAF.03 — Select safe responses and appropriate levels of help.

Required Notebook Vocabulary

Write each bold term and definition in your notebook.

Personal safety: actions that reduce exposure to harm. Exploitation: unfairly taking advantage of a person for another's benefit. Human trafficking: exploitation involving prohibited means or circumstances, including forced labor or commercial sexual exploitation. Labor trafficking: exploitation involving compelled labor or services. Sex trafficking: commercial sexual exploitation under prohibited circumstances. Force: physical compulsion or threatened violence. Fraud: deception used to gain control or compliance. Coercion: threats, intimidation, pressure, or manipulation used to control behavior. Manipulation: unfair influence that distorts another person's choices. Recruitment: approaching or drawing someone into an opportunity or relationship. Isolation: separating a person from support. Control: restricting another person's choices, movement, communication, money, or resources. Threat: a stated or implied promise of harm. Debt manipulation: using a claimed debt to control or exploit. Grooming: gradual trust-building or boundary testing for later exploitation. Predatory behavior: conduct aimed at exploiting vulnerability. Boundary: a stated personal limit. Personal security: protecting identity, location, communication, and access to support. Suspicious behavior: conduct that raises a safety concern. Trusted adult: a reliable adult able to help protect safety. Reporting: sharing a concern with an appropriate responsible resource. Victim: a person harmed by exploitation or crime. Survivor: a person living after or beyond harm. Resource: a person or service that provides help. Emergency: an immediate serious danger requiring urgent response.

Privacy and safety: Use only fictional scenarios. Never submit names, screenshots, messages, or real concerns as graded work. If a real concern exists, contact a trusted adult or appropriate safety resource; immediate danger requires emergency help.
