Page 2 — Learn Exploitation and Trafficking Safety Concepts

Trafficking does not always involve kidnapping or a stranger. Exploitation can develop through deception, pressure, threats, isolation, or control by someone the person knows or initially trusts. It can affect people of different genders, backgrounds, and communities.

ConceptNon-graphic example
ForceThreatened physical harm is used to compel behavior.
FraudA promised job is intentionally misrepresented.
CoercionThreats, debt, isolation, or manipulation are used to control choices.

Warning sign is not proof

Secrecy, isolation, threats, controlling communication, unrealistic offers, requests for identifying information or location, and escalating boundary violations raise concern. One sign does not prove trafficking. The student's job is to protect safety and seek help—not investigate or accuse.

Notebook Summary Box — Write This

“Trafficking can involve force, fraud, coercion, manipulation, or control and does not require kidnapping. Warning signs raise concern but are not proof. The safe pathway is recognize, protect personal security, disengage when possible, seek trusted help, and use emergency assistance for immediate danger.”
