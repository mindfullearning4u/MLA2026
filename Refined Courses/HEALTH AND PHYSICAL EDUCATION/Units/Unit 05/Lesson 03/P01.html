Essential Question

How can heredity, environment, behavior, preventive care, and early detection influence chronic-disease risk without determining a person’s outcome?

Quick Lesson Overview

You will evaluate fictional risk patterns without diagnosis or blame.

Learning Objectives

  • distinguish chronic and communicable disease;
  • classify modifiable, nonmodifiable, environmental, and protective factors;
  • apply primary and secondary prevention;
  • distinguish screening from diagnosis.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.HLT.01: Analyze communicable and chronic disease prevention and health decisions.

MLA.PEH.HLT.02: Apply hygiene and preventive-health practices.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 5 Lesson 3 Vocabulary. Copy all 25 terms and definitions.

Chronic disease: long-lasting condition requiring ongoing prevention or management. Noncommunicable disease: disease not spread person to person. Risk factor: characteristic associated with greater risk. Protective factor: characteristic reducing risk or impact. Modifiable risk factor: factor that may be changed. Nonmodifiable risk factor: factor not changeable, such as age or inherited traits. Heredity: transmission of traits from biological parents. Genetics: study of genes and inheritance. Gene: unit of inherited biological information. Family history: health patterns among biological relatives. Environment: surrounding physical and social conditions. Lifestyle: recurring behaviors and routines. Prevention: action reducing risk or impact. Screening: checking for possible risk or disease before diagnosis. Early detection: identifying possible disease at an earlier stage. Diagnosis: qualified identification of a condition. Treatment: professional care to manage disease. Healthcare provider: qualified professional providing health services. Blood pressure: force of blood against artery walls. Cholesterol: waxy blood substance needed by the body but associated with risk at unhealthy levels. Cancer: group of diseases involving uncontrolled abnormal cell growth. Cardiovascular disease: disease of the heart or blood vessels. Diabetes: disease affecting blood-glucose regulation. Respiratory disease: disease affecting breathing structures or function. Neurological disease: disease affecting the brain, spinal cord, or nerves.
