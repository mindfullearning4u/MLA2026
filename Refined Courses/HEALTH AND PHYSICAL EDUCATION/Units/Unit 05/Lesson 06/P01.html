Unit 5, Lesson 6: Preventive Care and Accessing Appropriate Health Services

Page 1 — Lesson Overview and Vocabulary

Essential Question

How can a person choose an appropriate preventive, routine, urgent, emergency, or specialized service and evaluate whether health information and services are credible and accessible?

Objectives

  • Classify preventive, routine, prompt, urgent, emergency, and specialized needs.
  • Distinguish screening, diagnosis, and treatment.
  • Evaluate access barriers, provider fit, credentials, evidence, safety, transparency, and claims.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.HLT.02 — Evaluate preventive-health practices, screening, access, and behaviors that reduce preventable risk.

MLA.PEH.CON.01 — Evaluate the credibility and appropriateness of health information, providers, and services.

Required Notebook Vocabulary

Write each bold term and definition in your notebook.

Healthcare: services that prevent, evaluate, or treat health needs. Healthcare access: the ability to obtain appropriate care. Preventive care: care intended to reduce risk or detect concerns early. Primary care: first-contact general healthcare. Routine care: scheduled non-emergency care. Urgent care: prompt care for a non-life-threatening need. Emergency care: immediate response for serious danger. Emergency department: hospital service for urgent, potentially life-threatening needs. Specialist: provider with focused expertise. Mental-health professional: qualified provider for mental and emotional health. Dental care: oral-health services. Reproductive healthcare: qualified services related to reproductive health. Screening: checking for possible risk or signs before diagnosis. Diagnostic service: evaluation used to determine a condition. Vaccination: preventive immunization. Prescription: authorized direction for a medicine or treatment. Referral: direction to another provider or service. Telehealth: healthcare delivered remotely through technology. Insurance: coverage that helps pay eligible healthcare costs. Coverage: services or costs included in a plan. Copayment: set amount paid for a covered service. Deductible: amount paid before some coverage begins. Network: contracted providers in a health plan. Community health center: community-based service offering healthcare. Public health department: government agency supporting community health services. Pharmacy: service that dispenses medicines and may provide selected preventive services. Patient: person receiving healthcare. Provider: qualified person or organization delivering care. Credential: verified qualification or license. Health claim: statement about health benefit, risk, or treatment. Transparency: clear disclosure of qualifications, costs, risks, and limits.

Privacy: This lesson teaches general resource navigation. It does not diagnose conditions or provide individualized medical advice. Use fictional scenarios; do not disclose diagnoses, insurance status, or family finances.
