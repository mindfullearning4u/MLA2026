Page 6 — Mastery Checkpoint

New fictional household scenario

Person A needs a preventive screening. Person B has a prompt but non-emergency concern. Person C needs a mental- or reproductive-health service. Person D has an immediate serious emergency. The household has limited transportation and scheduling barriers. Two qualified providers differ in distance and scope. An online service has no visible credentials, and an advertisement promises one secret cure for multiple unrelated diseases.

Exactly What to Submit

Submit one checkpoint document, separate from Page 5.

Part A: A healthcare decision matrix for Persons A–D with need, urgency, service, reason, barrier, strategy, and credibility evidence.

Part B: A 300–350 word analysis addressing all 12 requirements:

  1. classify urgency for each person;
  2. identify appropriate service category;
  3. justify each selection;
  4. distinguish screening from diagnosis;
  5. identify access barriers;
  6. propose realistic strategies;
  7. evaluate the questionable online service;
  8. identify three credibility indicators or red flags;
  9. identify the emergency;
  10. explain why emergency response cannot be delayed;
  11. explain access and outcomes without blame;
  12. use at least eight vocabulary terms accurately.
TOR Help: Mastery is 80%. The immediate serious emergency must receive immediate emergency response.
