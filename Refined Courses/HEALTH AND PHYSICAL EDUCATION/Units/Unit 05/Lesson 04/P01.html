Unit 5, Lesson 4: Reproductive Health, STI Prevention, Abstinence, and Pregnancy Prevention

Page 1 — Lesson Overview and Vocabulary

Essential Question

How can accurate information, abstinence, healthy decision-making, protective behaviors, and professional healthcare reduce risks associated with STIs and teenage pregnancy?

Learning Objectives

  • Explain reproductive health and the difference between STI risk and pregnancy risk.
  • Explain why maintained abstinence avoids pregnancy from intercourse and STIs from sexual exposure.
  • Evaluate prevention claims, misinformation, peer pressure, consent, refusal, testing, and credible support.

MLA Standards

MLA.PEH.HLT.01 — Analyze disease prevention, detection, risk reduction, and professional support.

MLA.PEH.CON.01 — Apply consent, boundary, refusal, and help-seeking skills.

Required Notebook Vocabulary

Write every bold term and its definition in your notebook.

Reproductive health: well-being related to the reproductive system and informed, safe decisions. Sexually transmitted infection (STI): an infection that can spread through sexual contact. Asymptomatic: present without noticeable symptoms. Transmission: movement of an infection from one person to another. Abstinence: choosing not to participate in sexual activity. Risk avoidance: avoiding the exposure that creates a risk. Risk reduction: lowering risk without necessarily eliminating it. Contraception: methods intended to reduce pregnancy risk. Condom/barrier method: a barrier that can reduce pregnancy risk and risk of many STIs when used correctly and consistently. Testing: professional health procedures used to identify a possible infection. Consent: voluntary agreement free from pressure or coercion. Coercion: pressure, threats, or manipulation used to obtain compliance. Refusal skill: a clear boundary statement followed by repetition, disengagement, or support-seeking. Credible source: information from qualified, evidence-based authorities. Stigma: unfair shame or judgment attached to a health condition or identity. HIV: a virus that affects the immune system. HPV: a common group of viruses, some types of which can cause health problems. Pregnancy prevention: actions or methods that reduce the chance of pregnancy. Professional healthcare: qualified medical evaluation, testing, counseling, or treatment.
