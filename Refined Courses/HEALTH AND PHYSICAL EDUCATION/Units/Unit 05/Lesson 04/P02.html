Page 2 — Learn: Reproductive Health, Pregnancy, and STI Concepts

Reproductive-health decisions require accurate information, personal boundaries, and appropriate healthcare. STIs may be bacterial, viral, or parasitic; some can be asymptomatic, so appearance and lack of symptoms cannot establish infection status.

QuestionPregnancy riskSTI risk
What creates the risk?Sexual intercourse that can result in pregnancyExposure through transmission routes associated with the infection
How does abstinence apply?Maintained abstinence from intercourse prevents pregnancy from intercourseMaintained abstinence from relevant sexual exposure prevents sexual transmission; some infections also have nonsexual routes
What else matters?Contraceptive methods differ in effectivenessBarrier methods, testing, and vaccination where appropriate can reduce particular risks

Critical distinction

A method that reduces pregnancy risk does not automatically reduce STI risk. Risk reduction is not zero risk. Medical questions and possible exposure require qualified healthcare/testing—not self-diagnosis.

Notebook Summary Box — Write This

Copy this summary: “Pregnancy risk and STI risk overlap in some situations, but they are not identical. Maintained abstinence avoids pregnancy from intercourse and sexual transmission from avoided exposure. Other methods reduce particular risks but may not eliminate them. Because some STIs are asymptomatic, testing and professional care may be necessary.”
