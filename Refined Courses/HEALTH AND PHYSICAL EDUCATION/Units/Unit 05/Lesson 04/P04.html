Page 4 — Apply Decision-Making, Consent, and Refusal Skills

Use the MLA decision process: identify the decision, facts, influences, options, risks/consequences, resources, safest health-supporting option, reasoning, and possible outcome.

Fictional scenario

Jordan has chosen abstinence to support personal goals. A partner says, “If you cared, you would change your mind,” and shares an anonymous post claiming there is no STI risk when a person looks healthy.

Reasoning model

  1. Boundary: Jordan has already stated a voluntary decision.
  2. Influence: The partner uses emotional pressure; pressure is not consent.
  3. Evidence check: Appearance cannot establish STI status because infection may be asymptomatic.
  4. Response: State the boundary clearly, repeat it once, leave/disengage if pressure continues, and seek trusted support.
  5. Conclusion: The safest choice protects the stated goal, rejects misinformation, and prioritizes consent.

Required Notebook Task

Write a five-row decision table for Jordan: goal; accurate facts; pressure/misinformation; boundary/refusal response; support or healthcare resource. Then write 4–6 sentences explaining how the evidence led to your conclusion. Do not write about your own experiences.

TOR Help: A clear refusal does not require a long justification. Contact your TOR if you need help applying the steps.
