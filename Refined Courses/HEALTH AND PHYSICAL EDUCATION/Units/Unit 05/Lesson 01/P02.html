Learn Injury Prevention and Emergency Decisions

Prevention First

Use this hierarchy: remove or avoid the hazard when possible; reduce exposure; use protective equipment and safe procedures; prepare supervision and emergency plans.

Three Levels of Help

LevelEvidenceResponse
Non-emergencyMinor, stable, no serious warning signsBasic care within training; monitor
Professional evaluationPersistent, significant, uncertain, or beyond routine self-managementPrompt qualified healthcare assessment
EmergencyUnresponsiveness, severe breathing difficulty, severe bleeding, serious poisoning/allergic reaction, or immediate dangerEnsure scene safety and call emergency services now

Never enter an unsafe scene, give an unapproved poisoning remedy, move a seriously injured person without necessity, or perform care beyond your training.

Required Notebook Summary

Copy the three-level table. Then write 4–6 sentences explaining the prevention hierarchy, why scene safety comes first, and why worsening evidence can change the correct help level.

TOR Help

Ask your TOR to check whether you connected each help level to observable evidence, not a diagnosis.
