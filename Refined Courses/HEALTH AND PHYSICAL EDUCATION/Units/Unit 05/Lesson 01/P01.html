Essential Question

How can a person recognize preventable risks, respond appropriately to injury or illness, and determine when professional or emergency help is needed?

Quick Lesson Overview

You will analyze fictional safety situations, prevent hazards, choose the correct level of help, and recognize when an emergency must be handled by trained professionals.

Learning Objectives

  • distinguish a hazard from risk;
  • select prevention and protective behaviors;
  • classify non-emergency, urgent professional-care, and emergency situations;
  • apply scene-safety and emergency-response steps without exceeding training.

MLA Standard

MLA.PEH.SAF.03: Analyze injury and illness risks and determine preventive, professional-care, and emergency-response actions.

Required Vocabulary Notebook Task

Write Unit 5 Lesson 1 Vocabulary. Copy all 25 bold terms and definitions.

Injury
Physical harm to the body.
Illness
A condition that disrupts normal health or functioning.
Prevention
Action taken to reduce the likelihood or severity of harm.
Risk
The likelihood and possible severity of harm under particular circumstances.
Hazard
Something with the potential to cause harm.
Protective behavior
An action that reduces exposure to a hazard or lowers risk.
Emergency
An immediate serious threat requiring emergency help.
Urgent
Requiring prompt professional attention but not necessarily emergency services.
Non-emergency
A situation without signs of immediate serious danger.
First aid
Immediate basic care within a person’s training while appropriate help is obtained.
Professional healthcare
Assessment or treatment by a qualified healthcare professional.
Emergency medical services
Trained responders who provide emergency medical assistance.
911
The United States emergency telephone number for immediate danger.
Warning sign
Observable evidence that a situation may be serious or worsening.
Unconscious
Not awake and not responding normally.
Severe bleeding
Heavy or uncontrolled blood loss requiring emergency help.
Breathing difficulty
Trouble getting enough air or breathing normally.
Head injury
Trauma involving the head that may require evaluation.
Heat illness
Health harm caused by excessive heat exposure.
Dehydration
Insufficient body fluid for normal functioning.
Poisoning
Harmful exposure to a toxic substance.
Allergic reaction
An immune response that may range from mild to life-threatening.
Concussion
A brain injury caused by a force or impact; it requires appropriate evaluation.
Distracted driving
Operating a vehicle while attention is diverted from driving.
Protective equipment
Safety gear designed to reduce injury risk.
