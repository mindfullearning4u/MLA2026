# Digital Citizenship and Online Safety

## Course Description

Digital Citizenship and Online Safety is a 0.5-credit mastery-based course that helps students make safe, ethical, informed, and healthy choices in digital spaces. Students analyze digital identity, privacy, security, online communication, cyberbullying, source credibility, misinformation, AI-generated content, copyright, ethical publishing, and digital wellness.

## Standards Alignment

The course uses MLA Digital Citizenship and Online Safety standards aligned to CPALMS/FDOE Computer Science expectations and supported by CSTA, ISTE, Florida B.E.S.T. ELA, Common Core literacy, SAT, ACT, and ELD/accessibility expectations.

## Learning Objectives / Outcomes

Students will evaluate digital risks and opportunities, protect personal data, communicate respectfully, respond to unsafe situations, verify information before sharing, respect creative rights, manage digital wellness, and produce a final digital citizenship portfolio.

## Prerequisite Knowledge / Skills

Students should be able to read grade-level informational text, use basic online tools, write short evidence-based explanations, and follow step-by-step safety procedures.

## Course Structure

This 0.5-credit course has 6 units with 5 lessons per unit. Lessons 1-4 include guided practice and a lesson quiz. Lesson 5 is the synthesis lesson and includes guided practice plus the unit assessment, but no lesson quiz.

## Lesson Workflow

Each lesson includes overview, notebook instruction, misconception repair, worked examples, guided practice preparation, independent work, and a checkpoint.

## Assessment Structure

Each unit includes a pretest, guided practice for every lesson, lesson quizzes for Lessons 1-4, and a unit assessment in Lesson 5.

## Mastery & Progression Criteria

Students demonstrate mastery by earning at least 80%, completing required evidence tasks, using feedback for revision, and meeting checkpoint criteria.

## College / Skill Readiness Integration

The course supports college, career, and life readiness by developing information literacy, responsible technology use, source evaluation, privacy/security habits, ethical communication, and evidence-based decision-making.
