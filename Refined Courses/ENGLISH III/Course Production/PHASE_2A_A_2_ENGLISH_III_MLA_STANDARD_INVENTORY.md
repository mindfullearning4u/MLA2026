# Phase 2A-A-2: English III MLA Standard Assignment

## Executive Summary

This report establishes the official MLA English III standard inventory using the approved MLA English numbering framework:

`MLA.[COURSE].[DOMAIN].[NUMBER]`

For English III, the course identifier is `ENG3`. Standards are numbered within the approved MLA English domains:

- `R` = Reading
- `W` = Writing
- `L` = Language
- `V` = Vocabulary
- `RS` = Research
- `C` = Communication

This inventory preserves the approved English I and English II production frameworks while advancing the grade-level benchmark codes from Grade 10 English II to Grade 11 English III. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, GIFT files, lesson JSON files, or student-facing instructional content.

Source-control note: Benchmark codes and wording were verified against the public CPALMS standards search endpoint before final certification. CPALMS returned benchmark cards for all 24 English III benchmark codes.

## English II to English III Comparison Findings

### Standards Unique to English III

| Category | Finding |
|---|---|
| Course-code uniqueness | Every English III standard is uniquely identified by `ENG3` and aligned to Grade 11 benchmark codes. |
| Structural uniqueness | English III does not add new MLA domains beyond English I and English II; rigor increases through evaluation, literary research, Classical Period texts, complex text analysis, and rhetorical presentation. |
| Literary progression | English III moves beyond English II poetic ambiguity and coming-of-age perspectives into literary evaluation, universal themes across time and place, juxtaposition, time-period poetry, allegory, and contemporaneous authors. |
| Informational progression | English III moves from English II historical American speeches and essays to speeches and essays from the Classical Period. |
| Writing progression | English III emphasizes literary analyses, complex text analysis, complex narratives with multiple perspectives, and revision for clarity, structure, and style. |

### Increased Rigor Requirements

| Strand | English II Baseline | English III Rigor Increase |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Analyze comparative themes, conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, historical American texts, and shared-information argument moves. | Evaluate literary elements, analyze Classical Period speeches and essays, compare contemporaneous authors, and evaluate rhetoric. |
| Writing | Write formal arguments, source-based expository texts, and narratives with pacing/tone. | Write literary analyses, complex text analyses, and complex narratives with multiple perspectives. |
| Research | Refine research scope and synthesize information from multiple reliable and valid sources. | Conduct literary research using primary and secondary sources aligned to text interpretation. |
| Vocabulary | Apply vocabulary strategies independently to nuanced literary, rhetorical, and source-based contexts. | Apply vocabulary strategies in complex literary, Classical Period, allegorical, rhetorical, and research contexts. |
| Communication | Present synthesized claims and findings with stronger audience awareness and evidence control. | Present with rhetorical devices and create digital presentations that improve audience experience. |
