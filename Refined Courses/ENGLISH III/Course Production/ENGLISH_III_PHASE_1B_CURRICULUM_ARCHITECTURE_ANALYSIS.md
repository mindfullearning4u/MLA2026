# English III Phase 1B: Curriculum Architecture Analysis

## Executive Summary

This document establishes the English III curriculum architecture using the approved English I and English II production frameworks as the master template. It preserves MLA curriculum methodology, naming conventions, audit phases, documentation format, course production artifact expectations, and mastery-based sequencing.

This is architecture only. It does not create lesson content, assessments, HTML files, quiz files, GIFT files, lesson JSON files, or LMS pages.

## Approved Unit Architecture

| Unit | Unit Title | Architecture Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Foundations of Grade 11 Close Reading, Evidence, and Academic Voice | Establish Grade 11 paraphrase, academic vocabulary, evidence, conventions, and analytical response readiness. |
| Unit 2 | Literary Analysis, Juxtaposition, Poetry, and Complex Narrative | Develop literary analysis through key elements, universal themes across time and place, juxtaposition and character perspective, poetry in time-period context, allegory, and complex narrative perspectives. |
| Unit 3 | Classical Speeches, Essays, Rhetoric, and Argument | Develop informational and rhetorical analysis through Classical Period speeches and essays, structure, author purpose, multiple arguments, shared information, and rhetoric. |
| Unit 4 | Literary Research and Complex Text Analysis | Connect literary inquiry, primary and secondary sources, research synthesis, literary analysis writing, and complex text analysis. |
| Unit 5 | Language, Vocabulary, Style, and Revision | Refine standard English, word analysis, connotation, denotation, clarity, structure, style, and Grade 11 writing precision. |
| Unit 6 | Synthesis, Oral Presentation, and Digital Publication | Synthesize reading, literary research, writing, rhetoric, language, vocabulary, oral presentation, digital presentation, multimedia integration, and publication. |

## English II to English III Progression

| Area | English II Foundation | English III Progression |
|---|---|---|
| Literary analysis | Comparative themes, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, adaptation. | Evaluation of key elements, universal themes across time and place, juxtaposition defining character perspective, poetry in time-period context, allegory, and contemporaneous authors. |
| Informational text | Historical American speeches and essays, author choices, opposing arguments, shared information. | Speeches and essays from the Classical Period, multiple arguments, reasoning, validity, and same-information rhetorical uses. |
| Writing | Formal argument, source-based expository writing, narrative pacing, audience-focused revision. | Literary analysis writing, complex text analysis, complex narratives with multiple perspectives, and revision for clarity, structure, and style. |
| Research | Research synthesis from reliable and valid sources. | Literary research using primary and secondary sources to support interpretations of texts. |
| Communication | Oral and digital communication of findings, reasoning, and evidence. | Oral presentation with effective rhetorical devices and digital presentations designed to improve audience experience. |

## Architecture Controls

- Preserve the MLA mastery model.
- Preserve the approved six-unit, eight-lesson course structure.
- Preserve Lesson 8 as Putting It All Together.
- Preserve standard numbering format `MLA.ENG3.[DOMAIN].[NUMBER]`.
- Preserve the approved MLA English domains: Reading, Writing, Language, Vocabulary, Research, Communication.
- Preserve audit phase separation: architecture, inventory, crosswalk, mapping, then future production.
- Do not create student-facing instructional content in architecture documents.
- Do not introduce lesson content, assessments, HTML pages, quiz JSON, lesson JSON, or GIFT files in this phase.
