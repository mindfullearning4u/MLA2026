P04 Worked Example

Study how to evaluate structure and features instead of only identifying them.

Worked Example Text

Why the Harbor Changed

For decades, fishing boats filled the harbor before sunrise. When the shipping company built a larger dock, the water traffic changed. Small boats waited longer to enter, fuel costs rose, and several fish sellers moved inland. As a result, the town created separate morning hours for fishing boats and afternoon hours for cargo ships.

Example 1: Identify Structure

The passage uses cause and effect. The larger dock causes changed water traffic, longer waits, higher costs, and moved sellers.

Example 2: Identify Features

The title previews the explanation, and the transition "As a result" signals the effect and response.

Example 3: Evaluate Effectiveness

The cause-and-effect structure is effective because it helps readers understand why the town changed harbor hours instead of presenting the decision without context.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The passage has a title, so it is effective.

Why it is weak: It names a feature but does not explain its effect.

Correct: The title is effective because it prepares readers for an explanation of the harbor's change, and the cause-and-effect structure shows why the schedule changed.

Why it is correct: It connects feature, structure, purpose, and reader understanding.
