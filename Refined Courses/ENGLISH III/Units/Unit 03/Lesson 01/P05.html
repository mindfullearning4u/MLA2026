Independent Work: “A Library After Sunset”

Directions

Read the complete essay closely. Answer all 18 questions in complete sentences. For Question 2, infer meaning first, then verify with a reputable online dictionary or encyclopedia and record the source title or link. Submit responses with lesson vocabulary.

Passage

The Locked Hour

At 5:55 each evening, the East Ward Library’s computers display a warning: save your work. Five minutes later, students gathering sources, nursing assistants completing certification modules, and parents filling out employment forms must leave. The building is not empty because its services are unwanted; it closes while demand remains visible.

Why the Schedule Persisted

The current hours were established when the neighborhood contained fewer apartment buildings and most municipal offices closed at five. Officials describe the schedule as customary, a word that can make an inherited decision sound inevitable. Yet custom is not evidence of present need. Attendance records show the library’s busiest hour begins at four, precisely when many users arrive from school or work.

The Cost Objection

Extending every branch until midnight would be financially untenable. That objection deserves more than dismissal. But it creates a false choice between the current schedule and unlimited hours. A six-month pilot could keep two branches open until nine on alternating evenings. Security and utility costs could be measured against attendance, program participation, and completed service appointments.

What Access Means

Some residents can continue their work at home. Others depend on library internet, quiet space, translation assistance, or staff guidance. Equal opening hours do not create equitable access when people have unequal alternatives. The question is not whether every resident needs an evening library. It is whether a public resource should close at the moment it becomes most necessary for those with the fewest substitutes.

A Measured Proposal

Begin with two branches near transit routes. Publish monthly costs and use. Survey both participants and residents who still cannot attend. After six months, expand, revise, or discontinue the pilot according to stated criteria. Public institutions should not preserve a schedule merely because it is familiar; neither should they change without evidence. They should test whether their practices still serve the public they name.
