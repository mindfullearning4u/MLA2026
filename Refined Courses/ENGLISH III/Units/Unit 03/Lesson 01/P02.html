Notebook Title

Evaluating Text Structures and Features

Vocabulary

  • Structure: the way a text is organized.
  • Feature: a visible tool that guides readers, such as a heading, list, chart, bold word, caption, or transition.
  • Purpose: what the author is trying to accomplish.
  • Evaluate: judge how well something works and explain why.
  • Reader understanding: how clearly the audience can follow the author's ideas.

Direct Teaching

To evaluate structure, use three steps.

  1. Name the structure or feature. Is the text organized by cause and effect, comparison, problem and solution, sequence, or claim and evidence?
  2. Explain what the structure does. Does it show causes, compare ideas, present a problem, or build an argument?
  3. Judge effectiveness. Does the structure help the reader understand the author's purpose? Why or why not?

Evaluation requires judgment. Do not only say, "The text has headings." Explain whether the headings help the reader follow the main ideas.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Evaluating text structures and features

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.R.05

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Read the draft as an outside reader. Mark where the claim, reasoning, organization, sentence control, or style becomes unclear.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: first meaning, then organization, then transitions, then sentence precision, then conventions.
  3. Keep the original analytical purpose unless the task asks for a new claim. Revision should strengthen the argument, explanation, or analysis.
  4. Reread the revised version and ask whether the audience can follow the reasoning without extra explanation from a teacher.
