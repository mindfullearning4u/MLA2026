Lesson 1: Evaluating Text Structures and Features

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.R.05 - Evaluate Text Structures and Features.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.10 - Paraphrase Grade-Level Texts; MLA.ENG3.V.01 - Integrate Academic Vocabulary; MLA.ENG3.L.01 - Apply Standard English Conventions.

What You Will Learn

  • How informational text structure controls how ideas are presented.
  • How headings, lists, transitions, and examples guide readers.
  • How to evaluate whether a structure or feature is effective.
  • How to explain the connection between structure, purpose, and reader understanding.

Why This Matters

Readers do not only ask what a text says. Strong readers also ask how the text is built. A writer may use cause and effect, comparison, problem and solution, sequence, or claim and evidence to guide the reader toward a purpose.

This lesson helps you evaluate the design of informational texts before Unit 3 moves into Classical Period speeches, essays, argument, and rhetoric.

What You Will Do

  • Identify common informational text structures.
  • Study how features guide a reader.
  • Evaluate whether structure and features support the author's purpose.
  • Write a checkpoint response using evidence from a passage.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will explain how a text's structure and features help or limit reader understanding, using specific evidence from the text.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard asks you to judge how well a text's organization and features help the reader understand the author's ideas.

Ask TOR for Help
