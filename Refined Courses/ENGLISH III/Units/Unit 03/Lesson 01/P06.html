Checkpoint: “The Case for a Quiet Car”

Directions

Read the different essay and answer all 12 questions. Infer passage vocabulary from context; verify only when directed. Submit separately.

Passage

Morning trains carry phone calls, videos, announcements, and conversations into one narrow space. Complaints about noise usually produce an unenforceable request that everyone “be considerate.” A designated quiet car offers a clearer solution.

Critics argue that quiet cars privilege passengers who can choose where to sit and may burden conductors. Those concerns are legitimate. A trial should therefore designate only the first car during two peak periods, post specific expectations, and collect conductor reports rather than demand perfect silence.

Rail systems elsewhere report that clearly marked quiet areas reduce disputes because passengers choose an expectation before boarding. The feature does not declare conversation wrong; it classifies space according to different needs.

After sixty days, the agency should compare complaints, ridership distribution, and staff reports. If confusion exceeds benefit, revise the signs or end the trial. Courtesy is difficult to enforce as a vague moral demand. It becomes easier to practice when structure gives people a meaningful choice.
