Worked Example: Analyzing a Pattern of Choices

Model Passage — “The Night Bus”

At ten each evening, the last bus leaves the hospital district. It carries nurses whose shifts ran late, cooks closing restaurant kitchens, students leaving the library, and parents returning from second jobs. The route is not glamorous. Its seats are worn; its windows rattle; its schedule fits on one narrow column. Yet for the people aboard, this modest line is not a convenience but a connection.

Remove one trip, and a nurse waits alone after midnight. Remove one trip, and a student chooses between class and a safe ride home. Remove one trip, and a parent loses an hour that cannot be returned to a child. A budget records a route as a cost. A community must also record what disappears when the route does.

Step 1 — Purpose, Audience, Context

Purpose: urge local decision makers to consider human consequences before reducing service.

Audience: budget officials and community members who may view the route mainly as an expense.

Step 2 — Trace the Diction

Words such as “worn,” “rattle,” and “modest” acknowledge the route’s limitations. “Connection” reframes its value in social rather than luxurious terms. Because the writer admits imperfections, the praise sounds measured rather than promotional.

Step 3 — Analyze Syntax and Accumulation

The first paragraph’s lists accumulate different riders, expanding the route’s importance across occupations and responsibilities. In the second paragraph, three parallel sentences beginning “Remove one trip” slow the reader and attach a concrete consequence to an abstract cut.

Step 4 — Explain the Antithesis

The final two sentences balance “A budget records…” against “A community must also record….” This antithesis does not reject financial analysis; it argues that responsible accounting must include social loss.

Step 5 — State the Cumulative Effect

The modest diction establishes credibility, the rider list broadens relevance, the anaphora creates urgency, and the antithesis reframes the decision. Together, the choices move the audience from seeing a line item to considering a network of human consequences.

Alternative Interpretation and Evaluation

A reader might argue that the emotional examples distract from missing cost data. That objection is reasonable because the passage provides no budget figures. Still, the stronger text-based interpretation is that the writer deliberately supplies the human evidence a financial spreadsheet omits, not that cost is irrelevant.

Second Quick Model

Text: “The garden did not erase the vacant lot’s history; it rooted that history in something living.”

The antithesis between “erase” and “rooted” turns preservation into growth. The metaphor also connects memory to the garden’s physical action, making the claim compact and memorable.
