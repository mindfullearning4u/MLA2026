P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Set up your notebook to evaluate rhetorical choices.

Notebook Title

Rhetorical Choices and Audience Effect

Vocabulary

  • Rhetoric: The way language is used to persuade, inform, or affect an audience.
  • Rhetorical choice: A deliberate author move, such as repetition, contrast, imagery, direct address, or a question.
  • Effect: What the choice does for the audience.
  • Purpose: What the author wants to accomplish.
  • Evaluate: Judge how well the choice works.

Direct Teaching

Do not stop at naming a device. A strong rhetorical evaluation has three parts: identify the choice, explain the effect, and connect the effect to purpose.

For example, if a speaker repeats "we must act," the device is repetition. The effect may be urgency. The purpose may be to move listeners toward action.

Use this frame: The author uses ____ to create ____, which helps achieve the purpose of ____.

Notebook Setup

Create a four-row chart:

  1. Author's purpose
  2. Rhetorical choice
  3. Effect on audience
  4. Evaluation of effectiveness

Leave space for one paragraph after the chart.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Review the vocabulary and identify the skill being practiced.
  2. Step 2: Read the model or passage carefully before answering.
  3. Step 3: Find the exact evidence, word, sentence, or structural choice that supports your answer.
  4. Step 4: Explain how that evidence connects to meaning, effect, or purpose.
  5. Step 5: Check the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: work one step at a time. Standard path: complete the whole task with evidence and explanation. Accelerated path: compare alternatives or add a second evidence-based explanation.
