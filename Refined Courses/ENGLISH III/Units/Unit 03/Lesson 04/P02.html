Notebook Title

Comparing Multiple Arguments: Claims, Validity, and Reasoning

Vocabulary

  • Claim: the author's position or argument.
  • Reasoning: the explanation of why the claim makes sense.
  • Evidence: facts, examples, or details used to support a claim.
  • Validity: whether reasoning is logical and well-supported.
  • Effectiveness: how well the argument achieves its purpose for the audience.

Direct Teaching

When comparing arguments, do not stop at agreement or disagreement. Ask how each author builds the argument.

  1. Identify the shared topic.
  2. Find each claim.
  3. Explain each author's reasoning.
  4. Evaluate validity. Is the reasoning logical and supported?
  5. Evaluate effectiveness. Does the argument work well for its audience and purpose?

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Review the vocabulary and identify the skill being practiced.
  2. Step 2: Read the model or passage carefully before answering.
  3. Step 3: Find the exact evidence, word, sentence, or structural choice that supports your answer.
  4. Step 4: Explain how that evidence connects to meaning, effect, or purpose.
  5. Step 5: Check the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: work one step at a time. Standard path: complete the whole task with evidence and explanation. Accelerated path: compare alternatives or add a second evidence-based explanation.
