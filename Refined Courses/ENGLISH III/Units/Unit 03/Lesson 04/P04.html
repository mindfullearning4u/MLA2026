P04 Worked Example

Study how to evaluate claims, validity, and reasoning.

Worked Example Arguments

Argument A: The council should keep public debates short because shorter meetings allow more citizens to attend after work.

Argument B: The council should allow longer debates because complex issues need time for evidence, questions, and careful response.

Example 1: Claims

Argument A claims meetings should be shorter. Argument B claims debates should be longer.

Example 2: Reasoning

Argument A reasons from access: shorter meetings may help more people attend. Argument B reasons from complexity: important issues need enough time for careful discussion.

Example 3: Evaluation

Both arguments can be valid because each connects a claim to a reasonable public purpose. Argument A may be more effective for participation, while Argument B may be more effective for decision quality.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Argument A is wrong because I like longer debates.

Why it is weak: Personal preference is not evaluation.

Correct: Argument A is valid if the goal is wider attendance because it links shorter meetings to citizens' ability to attend after work.

Why it is correct: It evaluates reasoning based on claim, purpose, and support.
