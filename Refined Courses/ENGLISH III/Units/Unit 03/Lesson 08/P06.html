Checkpoint: Two Views of a Civic Data Portal

Directions

Read both passages and answer all 12 questions. Submit these responses only in the separate Checkpoint Submission.

Source A — Open by Default

Public decisions depend on public information. The city should publish budgets, inspection results, and meeting data in a searchable portal. A resident should not need specialized knowledge or repeated records requests to understand how money is spent. Openness will not eliminate disagreement, but it can ensure that disagreement begins with shared evidence.

The portal must include plain-language explanations and accessible formats. A warehouse of unreadable files is technically open but practically closed. Transparency is not the mere release of information; it is the creation of a usable path to it.

Source B — Privacy by Design

Government transparency is essential, but publication can create new harm when records contain personal details or combine into revealing patterns. Removing names does not always guarantee anonymity. A portal should therefore begin with a privacy review, publish categories rather than individual-level data when necessary, and explain what has been withheld.

Trust requires both visibility and restraint. Citizens should be able to inspect public power without having their own lives made inspectable in the process.
