The Ledger and the Lantern

Neighbors, we gather tonight beside a river that has carried both our commerce and our grief. Ten years ago, the flood crossed Harbor Street before dawn. It entered workshops, kitchens, and the lower rooms of this very hall. We remember the losses because memory is honest. We also remember the rescue boats, the school gym turned shelter, and the bakery ovens that worked through the night because memory, when complete, records more than injury.

The council proposes a stone memorial on the east bank. Some residents ask why money should be spent on stone when culverts still need repair. Their question is not cynical. A town that carves names but neglects drains would honor the dead with ceremony and endanger the living through policy. We must refuse that contradiction.

Yet we should also refuse the claim that remembrance is an ornament. The flood changed where we build, how we warn, and whom we notice when sirens sound. A memorial can make those lessons visible. It can hold the names that official reports reduce to totals. It can remind a child born tomorrow that safety was purchased not only with concrete but with the courage of ordinary people.

Therefore, let the memorial begin not with a sculptor’s sketch but with a public ledger. Publish the construction estimate. Publish the drainage schedule. Publish the source of every donated dollar. If the east-bank repairs are delayed, delay the monument. If the repairs proceed, reserve a small portion of private donations for a place of memory designed with survivors, engineers, and students.

Our choice is not between a town that remembers and a town that prepares. That is a false and impoverished choice. The lantern we raise to the past should illuminate the road ahead. Build the culvert so water may pass safely. Build the memorial so responsibility may pass from one generation to the next. Let the river see that we have learned both to measure its height and to measure our obligations to one another.
