P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Author Purpose in Classical Period Speeches and Essays

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.R.07 - Analyze Author Purpose in Classical Texts.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.06 - Analyze Central Ideas in Classical Texts; MLA.ENG3.V.03 - Determine Word Meaning; MLA.ENG3.C.01 - Present with Rhetorical Devices.

What You Will Learn

  • How to identify an author's purpose in a Classical Period-style speech or essay.
  • How central idea, word choice, structure, and examples help establish purpose.
  • How to explain the difference between topic, central idea, and purpose.
  • How to use evidence to show how the author achieves that purpose.

Why This Matters

Speeches and essays are written to do something. An author may want to advise, warn, persuade, defend, correct, inspire, or challenge an audience.

When you understand purpose, you can explain why the author chose certain examples, words, and structure.

What You Will Do

  • Read Classical Period-style speeches and essays.
  • Identify the author's purpose.
  • Explain how choices in structure, diction, and examples help achieve purpose.
  • Write a checkpoint response using passage evidence.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will explain an author's purpose and show how the author uses choices in the text to establish and achieve that purpose.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard asks you to explain why the author wrote the speech or essay and how the author's choices help accomplish that goal.

Ask TOR for Help

Ask TOR for help if you can identify the central idea but are unsure what the author wants the audience to think or do.
