P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Set up your author purpose analysis notes.

Notebook Title

Author Purpose in Classical Speeches and Essays

Vocabulary

  • Purpose: what the author wants the text to accomplish.
  • Audience: the people the author is addressing.
  • Author choice: a decision about structure, evidence, wording, example, or tone.
  • Establish purpose: make the goal of the text clear.
  • Achieve purpose: use choices that help the text accomplish its goal.

Direct Teaching

Author purpose is not the same as topic. A topic is what the text is about. Purpose is what the author wants the text to do.

Topic: leadership

Central idea: leaders should listen to wise counsel.

Purpose: to warn leaders against pride and persuade them to seek advice.

Use this process:

  1. Identify the audience.
  2. Identify the central idea.
  3. Ask what the author wants the audience to think, feel, or do.
  4. Find author choices that help accomplish that purpose.

Notebook Task

Copy this frame into your notebook.

  1. The audience is _____.
  2. The central idea is _____.
  3. The author's purpose is to _____.
  4. One author choice that supports this purpose is _____.
  5. This choice helps because _____.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Author purpose in Classical Period speeches and essays

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.R.07

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Identify the speaker or writer, audience, purpose, and occasion before judging the argument or rhetoric. These details shape what the choices are meant to do.
  2. Separate claim, evidence, reasoning, and rhetorical technique. A strong analysis names the move and explains how it affects the audience.
  3. Evaluate effectiveness, not just presence. Ask whether the structure, appeal, diction, repetition, or contrast actually helps the author achieve the purpose.
  4. Use precise academic language such as claim, validity, reasoning, appeal, effect, audience, and purpose so your analysis sounds mature and defensible.
