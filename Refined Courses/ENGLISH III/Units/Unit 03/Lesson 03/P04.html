Worked Example: “Before the Gates Open”

Model Speech

Neighbors, the reservoir gates will open tomorrow whether we are prepared or not. Officials assure us the release is routine. Routine, however, describes a schedule; it does not measure the condition of our drainage channels.

I have walked the southern ditch with engineers and residents. Three culverts remain blocked. The city has promised crews by morning, but promises cannot carry water.

Some will say that public concern creates panic. Panic acts without evidence. Preparation acts because of it. Bring tools to the community hall at six. Check on residents who cannot lift sandbags. Record every blocked drain so the city must answer a list rather than a rumor.

We are not gathering because failure is certain. We are gathering because responsibility begins before certainty arrives.

Purpose Map

  • Occasion/exigence: scheduled water release plus unresolved drainage risk.
  • Primary purpose: mobilize specific preparation.
  • Secondary purpose: redefine concern as evidence-based responsibility.
  • Audience: neighbors, including skeptics and potential volunteers.

Modeled Evaluation

The speaker builds ethos through direct inspection, uses logos by naming three blocked culverts, and limits pathos by distinguishing preparation from panic. Imperative sentences convert reasoning into clear action. The final maxim makes uncertainty—not fear—the reason to prepare.

Alternative Interpretation

The repeated contrast with officials may also undermine trust before their response is known. For an audience dependent on city coordination, this strategy could create defensiveness. Effectiveness therefore depends on whether the named evidence is accurate and current.

Second Model

A philosophical essay may seek not immediate action but intellectual revision. Its success should be judged by whether definitions and counterexamples change the reader’s framework, not whether it includes a call to action.
