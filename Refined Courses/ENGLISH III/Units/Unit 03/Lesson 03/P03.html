P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Apply purpose analysis to a Classical Period-style speech.

Read the Speech

Speech to Young Magistrates

You have been given seats above the crowd, but do not mistake height for wisdom. A judge who cannot hear the poor has climbed too far from justice. Bend your ear before you lift your hand. Ask what was lost, who was harmed, and what repair can restore peace. Authority is honorable only when it kneels long enough to understand.

Step-by-Step Thinking

  1. Audience: young magistrates, or new judges/public officials.
  2. Central idea: authority must listen carefully to be just.
  3. Purpose: to warn officials against pride and persuade them to listen before judging.
  4. Author choice: the command "Bend your ear before you lift your hand" turns the purpose into a memorable instruction.

Notebook Task

  1. Copy the purpose-analysis frame from P02.
  2. Complete the frame for "Speech to Young Magistrates."
  3. Quote one author choice that helps establish purpose.
  4. Explain how the choice helps the author achieve the purpose.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author's purpose is judges.

Why it is weak: "Judges" is a topic or audience, not a purpose.

Correct: The author's purpose is to persuade young judges to listen carefully before using authority.

Why it works: It states what the author wants the audience to do.
