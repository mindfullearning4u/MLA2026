Independent Work: “A Speech for the Closed Clinic”

Directions

Read the full speech and answer all 18 questions. For Question 2, infer the passage word, verify it with a reputable dictionary or encyclopedia, and record the source. Submit with vocabulary.

Speech

Members of the board, the North Clinic’s doors are locked tonight, but illness has not observed your schedule. Parents will still wake to fever; workers will still cut hands after the urgent-care office closes.

You describe consolidation as efficient. Efficiency is a worthy goal when it removes waste. It becomes a euphemism when it removes service and leaves the cost uncounted because patients pay it elsewhere—in missed work, longer travel, delayed treatment, and crowded emergency rooms.

I do not ask you to reopen every program exactly as it was. The clinic’s laboratory operated below capacity, and its aging equipment requires replacement. Keep those services centralized. But restore evening triage three nights each week, staffed through rotation, and publish monthly data on visits, wait times, and emergency referrals.

Some will call this proposal a retreat from modernization. It is the opposite. A modern system measures whether its efficiencies improve human outcomes. If the spreadsheet becomes cleaner while the waiting room across town grows fuller, we have moved a cost, not solved a problem.

Vote tonight not for a building, but for a standard: no reform deserves the name unless the people most dependent on a public service can still reach it.

Questions

  1. Identify the occasion, audience, and exigence.
  2. Infer euphemism; verify its definition, record the source, and explain its effect.
  3. State the speaker’s precise primary purpose.
  4. Identify a secondary purpose.
  5. How does the opening use pathos without abandoning evidence?
  6. Analyze the direct address.
  7. Explain the efficiency/euphemism contrast.
  8. What concession appears in paragraph 3?
  9. How does the concession build ethos?
  10. Map the proposal’s logos.
  11. Analyze the spreadsheet/waiting-room contrast.
  12. What assumption about modernization does the speaker challenge?
  13. Explain the purpose shift in the conclusion.
  14. Write a precise purpose statement using audience and method.
  15. Support it with evidence from three paragraphs.
  16. Evaluate effectiveness for a budget-focused board.
  17. Identify one ethical strength or risk.
  18. Offer and test a counterinterpretation.
