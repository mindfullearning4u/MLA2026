TOR ONLY — Answer Guide

Independent Work — Suggested Responses

  1. Resilient: able to recover or withstand difficulty; context links wetlands to reduced storm damage. Verify with a reputable source.
  2. Shared facts include cost, construction time, parking removal, and lower damage after two storms.
  3. The city emphasizes success and public benefit.
  4. The business account foregrounds disruption and parking loss.
  5. The environmental review foregrounds ecological outcomes and limits.
  6. The city targets residents/taxpayers with a reassuring civic tone.
  7. The association targets merchants/customers with economic caution.
  8. The review targets decision makers with qualified analysis.
  9. Selection highlights facts useful to each purpose.
  10. Omissions include competing costs, benefits, or uncertainties.
  11. Loaded language creates approval or concern.
  12. Different time frames privilege short-term disruption or longer-term benefit.
  13. Qualifications limit certainty and strengthen credibility.
  14. Baselines influence whether improvement appears large or modest.
  15. Each account can be accurate yet incomplete.
  16. A fair synthesis retains disruption, benefits, and evidence limits.
  17. Defensible evaluation depends on question and evidence standard.
  18. A responsible conclusion separates facts from interpretation and names evidence still needed.

Checkpoint — Suggested Responses

  1. Trails: remains behind; the state rate is the clue.
  2. A frames improvement and credits mentoring.
  3. B frames the remaining state gap.
  4. “Climbs” signals progress; “still trails” signals insufficiency.
  5. The report supports an increase but not proven causation.
  6. Voluntary participation creates selection concerns.
  7. The state rate is a comparative baseline.
  8. Both headlines use true data but omit context.
  9. A overreaches if it implies causation; B can minimize growth.
  10. A complete summary includes growth and remaining gap.
  11. Needed evidence includes participant outcomes, prior trends, and comparison groups.
  12. Balanced: “District Graduation Rate Rises Four Points but Remains Below State Average.”

Scoring Note

Accept equivalent evidence-based responses. Apply the student-visible rubric exactly. Require TOR-guided corrections and resubmission below 80%.
