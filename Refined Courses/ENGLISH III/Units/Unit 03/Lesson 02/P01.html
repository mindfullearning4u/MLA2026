Lesson 2: Central Ideas in Classical Period Speeches and Essays

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.R.06 - Analyze Central Ideas in Classical Texts.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.05 - Evaluate Text Structures and Features; MLA.ENG3.R.10 - Paraphrase Grade-Level Texts; MLA.ENG3.V.01 - Integrate Academic Vocabulary.

What You Will Learn

  • How to identify central ideas in speeches and essays.
  • How Classical Period-style texts often build ideas through civic duty, virtue, justice, reason, and public life.
  • How structure helps a central idea develop.
  • How to paraphrase central ideas without copying the author's wording.

Why This Matters

Classical speeches and essays often ask readers to think about public responsibility, leadership, citizenship, and reasoned judgment. Central ideas hold those texts together.

This lesson prepares you for later Unit 3 work with author purpose, multiple arguments, and rhetoric.

What You Will Do

  • Read short original speeches and essays modeled after Classical Period civic writing.
  • Identify central ideas and supporting details.
  • Explain how structure develops a central idea.
  • Write a checkpoint response using evidence.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will identify a central idea in a Classical Period-style speech or essay and explain how details and structure develop that idea.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard asks you to figure out the main idea a Classical Period speech or essay develops and explain how the text builds that idea.
