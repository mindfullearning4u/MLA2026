P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Learn how to separate topic, central idea, and supporting details.

Notebook Title

Central Ideas in Classical Speeches and Essays

Vocabulary

  • Central idea: the main idea the text develops.
  • Topic: the subject the text is about.
  • Supporting detail: evidence, example, or explanation that develops the central idea.
  • Classical Period text: a speech or essay connected to ancient civic, philosophical, or public reasoning traditions.
  • Paraphrase: restate an idea accurately in your own words.

Direct Teaching

A topic is usually one or two words. A central idea is a full thought about that topic.

Topic: leadership

Central idea: A leader earns trust by serving the common good before personal comfort.

To find a central idea, ask:

  1. What subject repeats across the passage?
  2. What claim or lesson does the author build about that subject?
  3. Which details support that claim?
  4. How does the structure move the idea forward?

Notebook Task

Copy this frame into your notebook.

  1. The topic of the passage is _____.
  2. The central idea is _____.
  3. One detail that supports the central idea is _____.
  4. This detail supports the central idea because _____.

You will use this frame on the next page.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Review the vocabulary and identify the skill being practiced.
  2. Step 2: Read the model or passage carefully before answering.
  3. Step 3: Find the exact evidence, word, sentence, or structural choice that supports your answer.
  4. Step 4: Explain how that evidence connects to meaning, effect, or purpose.
  5. Step 5: Check the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: work one step at a time. Standard path: complete the whole task with evidence and explanation. Accelerated path: compare alternatives or add a second evidence-based explanation.
