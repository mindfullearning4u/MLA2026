P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Apply the central idea frame to a Classical Period-style speech.

Read the Speech

Speech to the Council

Citizens, a city is not strengthened by marble columns alone. It is strengthened when each person guards justice even when no crowd is watching. If the baker weighs bread honestly, if the judge listens before deciding, and if the soldier protects the weak before seeking praise, then the city stands firm. Walls may slow an enemy, but virtue keeps a city from falling from within.

Step-by-Step Thinking

  1. Topic: what makes a city strong.
  2. Repeated idea: justice and virtue matter more than physical strength.
  3. Supporting details: honest baker, fair judge, protective soldier.
  4. Central idea: A city is truly strong when its citizens practice virtue and justice.

Notebook Task

  1. Copy the central idea frame from P02.
  2. Complete it for "Speech to the Council."
  3. Quote one supporting detail.
  4. Explain how the detail develops the central idea.
  5. Write one sentence explaining how the speech structure moves from examples to conclusion.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The central idea is city.

Why it is weak: "City" is only a topic, not a complete idea.

Correct: The central idea is that a city becomes strong when its citizens practice justice and virtue.

Why it works: It states a full idea and matches the passage's examples.
