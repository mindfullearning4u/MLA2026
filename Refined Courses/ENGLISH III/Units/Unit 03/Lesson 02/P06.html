Checkpoint: “The Discipline of Listening”

Directions

Read the different essay and answer all 12 questions. Submit separately.

Essay

Listening is often praised as courtesy, but courtesy is only its surface. To listen seriously is to permit another person’s evidence to alter the shape of one’s conclusion.

This does not require accepting every claim. A judge listens and still weighs testimony; a physician listens and still tests symptoms against knowledge. In both cases, attention is disciplined by standards rather than replaced by agreement.

The greater danger is performative listening: silence maintained while a reply is prepared unchanged. Such silence imitates respect but protects certainty from contact.

A community capable of correction must value listening not as passivity but as a method of inquiry. The strongest conviction is not the one never challenged; it is the one that knows what evidence could revise it.
