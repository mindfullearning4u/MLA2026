Independent Work: “On Public Memory and Honest Praise”

Directions

Read the full speech and answer all 18 questions. For Question 2, infer the passage-specific word from context, then verify with a reputable dictionary or encyclopedia and record the source. Submit with vocabulary.

Speech

We gather to dedicate a monument, and dedication invites praise. Praise, however, becomes flattery when it remembers achievement but edits out cost. A city that celebrates only its victories teaches future citizens to inherit confidence without judgment.

Our founders built roads across the marsh and opened a harbor where none had stood. They also displaced families whose homes did not appear on the first official maps. Both statements belong to the same history. To speak one without the other is not loyalty; it is amnesia arranged in stone.

Some fear that acknowledging injustice will make gratitude impossible. The fear is understandable but mistaken. Mature gratitude does not require innocent heroes. We may honor courage while examining whom that courage failed to protect. Indeed, honest examination enlarges praise by making it chosen rather than compulsory.

Consider the monument before us. Its eastern panel names engineers and donors. Its western panel is blank. Let that blankness remain visible until the archive committee completes interviews with displaced families. An unfinished monument may appear indecisive, but false completion would be worse.

Therefore dedicate this place not as a final answer but as a public promise: when evidence changes our understanding, memory must have the courage to change its form.
