Checkpoint: “The Orchard of Names”

Directions

Read the different allegorical passage and answer all 12 questions. Submit them separately.

Passage

In Mira’s country, every child planted a tree and carved a name into its trunk. At age eighteen, officials inspected the orchard. Trees judged straight and productive remained; twisted trees were cut down, and their owners received new names chosen from a list.

Mira’s tree bent toward the river, where its roots prevented the bank from washing away. The inspector measured only its height. “Unsuccessful,” he said, marking the trunk with red paint. That night a storm flooded the orchard. The tallest trees fell in the softened soil, but Mira’s roots held the bank. At dawn, the red mark had washed into the river.

The inspector offered Mira the first name on his list. She wrote it on a wooden sign and placed the sign beside the fallen trees. Then she carved her original name deeper into the living trunk.

Analysis

  1. Summarize the literal situation.
  2. What does the tree-name relationship symbolize?
  3. What does the inspection system represent?
  4. Analyze the contrast between height and roots.
  5. Explain the red paint’s meaning.

Allegory and Synthesis

  1. How does the storm function in the allegory?
  2. Why does Mira put the assigned name beside fallen trees?
  3. Explain the final carving.
  4. Identify an ambiguity or complication.
  5. Write an objective summary.
  6. Write a supported allegorical claim.
  7. Evaluate how the passage comments on imposed standards.
