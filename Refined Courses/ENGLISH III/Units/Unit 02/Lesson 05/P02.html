Notebook Title

Figurative Language and Allegory: Literal Meaning, Deeper Meaning, Evidence

Vocabulary

  • Figurative language: language that means more than its literal words.
  • Symbol: a person, place, object, or action that represents an idea beyond itself.
  • Allegory: a story or passage in which characters, objects, or events represent larger ideas.
  • Literal meaning: what the words directly say.
  • Deeper meaning: the larger idea suggested by the words, image, or symbol.

Direct Teaching

When you analyze figurative language, do not stop after naming the device. A strong answer explains what the language does.

Use this three-step process:

  1. Identify the exact words. Quote the image, comparison, or symbol.
  2. Explain the literal picture. Say what the words make the reader imagine.
  3. Explain the deeper meaning. Connect the image to a theme, conflict, character, or issue.

Allegory uses the same thinking, but it works across more of the text. A single object can be symbolic. An allegory usually creates a pattern of symbols that points to a larger idea.
