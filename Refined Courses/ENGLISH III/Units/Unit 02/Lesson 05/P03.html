P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now apply the same process to an allegorical passage.

Read the Passage

Every citizen in the valley carried a small mirror. The mayor said the mirrors helped everyone see clearly. But the mirrors were polished only on the outside, so each person saw the sky behind them instead of the road ahead. One child turned her mirror around and saw a crack running through the glass. By sunset, others began turning their mirrors too.

Step-by-Step Thinking

  1. Literal level: People carry mirrors that do not help them see the road.
  2. Pattern: The mayor claims the mirrors help, but the mirrors actually distract people from what is ahead.
  3. Possible deeper meaning: The mirrors may represent false information, public image, or a system that keeps people from seeing reality.
  4. Evidence: The child turns the mirror around and sees a crack, which suggests someone begins questioning what everyone else accepts.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, answer each prompt in complete sentences.

  1. What object seems symbolic in the passage?
  2. What does the object literally do?
  3. What larger idea might the object represent?
  4. Which sentence from the passage best supports your interpretation?
  5. Write one paragraph explaining how the passage works as an allegory.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The passage is about mirrors.

Why it is weak: That only repeats the literal object.

Correct: The mirrors may represent false clarity because the people believe the mirrors help them see, but the mirrors actually keep them from looking at the road ahead.

Why it works: It names the symbol, explains the deeper meaning, and uses the text's contrast.
