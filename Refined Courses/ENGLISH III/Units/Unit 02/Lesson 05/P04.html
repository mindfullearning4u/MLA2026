Worked Example: “The Town of Borrowed Shadows”

Model Passage

In the town of Vey, citizens borrowed shadows from the courthouse each morning. Long shadows were assigned to officials; short ones went to laborers and children. A shadow determined where its wearer could stand at public meetings. One afternoon, clouds covered the sun, and every borrowed shadow disappeared. The mayor postponed the meeting, claiming no one could know where to stand. A child named Sena lit a lantern. The new shadows changed length whenever people moved. “Then perhaps our places can move too,” she said.

Literal Level

A town distributes artificial shadows that control public position. Weather disrupts the system, and a child demonstrates that shadows naturally change.

Allegorical Pattern

  • Borrowed shadows: assigned social status rather than inherent identity.
  • Courthouse: institutional authority that legitimizes hierarchy.
  • Meeting positions: access to civic voice.
  • Clouds: disruption exposing the system’s dependence on belief.
  • Lantern: inquiry creating an alternative way of seeing.

Model Claim

The passage allegorizes socially assigned rank: by making status as changeable and insubstantial as a shadow, it argues that institutions preserve hierarchy by treating constructed differences as fixed.

Complication and Evaluation

Sena does not destroy shadows; her lantern creates new ones. This prevents the simplistic message that all difference can disappear. Instead, the passage values flexible arrangements that respond to human movement rather than permanently determining it.

Accuracy Check

The interpretation accounts for several connected details, respects the literal plot, and explains a complication instead of forcing a rigid code.
