Checkpoint: “At the Center of Progress”

Directions

Read the passage and answer all 12 questions. Submit only these responses in the separate Checkpoint Submission.

Passage

The gallery placed two photographs at the entrance. In the first, a factory owner stood before a new textile mill, one hand resting on a polished machine. The caption read, Prosperity Arrives, 1928. In the second, seamstress Laila Ortiz sat beside an apartment window, wrapping cloth around swollen fingers. Its caption read, End of Shift, 1928.

Visitor Jonah admired the first photograph’s sharp lines and bright machinery. His friend Celeste remained near the second. “They are the same year,” she said. Jonah replied that progress always required difficult work. Celeste asked why the owner’s name appeared in the first caption while Laila’s name appeared only in a handwritten note on the frame’s back.

Later, Jonah returned alone. He noticed that the mill’s windows reflected rows of workers too small to identify. In Laila’s window, the dark glass reflected the photographer. Jonah revised his exhibit comment: The photographs do not cancel each other. Together they ask who is allowed to stand at the center of progress.

Analysis

  1. Describe Jonah’s initial perspective.
  2. Describe Celeste’s perspective.
  3. How does the juxtaposition of the photographs shape the passage?
  4. Analyze the two captions.
  5. Why does Celeste focus on naming?

Perspective and Meaning

  1. What does Jonah initially assume about progress?
  2. How do the window reflections complicate both photographs?
  3. Explain Jonah’s perspective shift.
  4. Why does he say the photographs do not cancel each other?

Synthesis

  1. Write an objective summary.
  2. Write and support a claim about how juxtaposition reveals bias.
  3. Evaluate Jonah’s revised comment as a statement of the passage’s central idea.
