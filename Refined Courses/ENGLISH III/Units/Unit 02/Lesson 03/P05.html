Independent Work: “Two Views of the Bridge”

Directions

Read the complete passage and answer all 18 questions. Submit these responses with your vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Passage

On the morning the river bridge reopened, two reports appeared in the local paper. Councilor Venn called the bridge “a silver promise joining neighborhoods after years of delay.” From the council balcony, he watched ribbons lift in the wind and saw a line of bicycles crossing the new span.

Below, mechanic Dara stood beside the old ferry landing. She noticed that the bridge entrance had no safe turn for delivery trucks and that the ferry workers’ booth was already boarded shut. Her father had piloted the ferry for twenty-six years. “A bridge can connect two banks,” she told a reporter, “and still leave people stranded.”

Venn toured the landing after reading her comment. He pointed to the shorter travel time and new pedestrian path. Dara pointed to three shop owners unloading supplies by hand. Their disagreement sharpened until a school bus approached the narrow turn and stopped traffic in both directions.

At the next meeting, Venn proposed widening the entrance. Dara asked that former ferry workers be hired to manage traffic during construction. The revised plan passed. At the second ceremony, Venn stood at street level. Dara watched from the balcony, where the bridge looked smooth and complete. “It is easier up here,” she admitted, “to mistake distance for the whole story.”

Part A — Perspective

  1. Summarize Venn’s initial perspective.
  2. Summarize Dara’s initial perspective.
  3. How does each character’s location shape what is noticed?
  4. What does Dara’s family history add to her perspective?
  5. Identify one limitation in Venn’s perspective.
  6. Identify one limitation or potential bias in Dara’s perspective.

Part B — Juxtaposition

  1. Explain the effect of placing the bridge ceremony beside the boarded ferry booth.
  2. Analyze the contrast between “silver promise” and “leave people stranded.”
  3. How does the school bus incident change the disagreement?
  4. Explain how the balcony and street operate symbolically.
  5. Identify a structural parallel between the first and final paragraphs.
  6. How does the reversal of locations affect meaning?

Part C — Analysis and Synthesis

  1. Write a claim about how juxtaposition reveals incomplete perspectives.
  2. Support the claim with paired evidence.
  3. Explain why neither perspective is presented as entirely wrong.
  4. State a central idea.
  5. Explain how Venn and Dara each change.
  6. Evaluate the final quotation’s contribution to the passage.
