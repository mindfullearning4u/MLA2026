P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Explain how the contrast defines perspective.

Same Passage

Lena entered the banquet hall in a patched coat. The silver tables were crowded with flowers, but she noticed the kitchen door first, where a tired boy carried a tray too heavy for his arms.

Response Frame

Sentence 1: Identify the juxtaposition.

Sentence 2: Explain Lena's perspective.

Sentence 3: Use evidence from the passage.

Sentence 4: Explain why the author's choice matters.

Notebook Task

Write a four-sentence response explaining how the author uses juxtaposition to define Lena's perspective.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Lena sees tables and a boy.

Why it is weak: It identifies details but does not explain the contrast or perspective.

Correct: The author juxtaposes the "silver tables" with the "tired boy" to show that Lena notices hardship beneath luxury.

Why it is stronger: It connects contrast to character perspective.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Juxtaposition and character perspective

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.R.03

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Start with the exact literary choice: structure, character contrast, time-period issue, imagery, allegory, perspective, or narrative technique.
  2. Connect that choice to meaning. English III analysis should explain how the choice develops theme, style, issue, or reader effect.
  3. Use evidence from the text before making an interpretation. The evidence should be specific enough to prove the point.
  4. Move beyond identification by evaluating significance. Ask why this choice matters in the work as a whole or in its time-period context.
