Independent Work: “Signals Carried Forward”

Directions

Read both connected narratives. Answer all 18 questions in complete sentences using evidence from both sections when requested. Submit these responses with your vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Paired Narrative

In the mountain town of San Aurelio, the bell keeper rang three notes whenever fog covered the road. Travelers understood the pattern: stop, listen, and wait for a guide. After a new highway diverted traffic, the council voted to sell the bronze bell. Lucía, the keeper’s daughter, argued that an object no longer useful for travelers could still guide the town. On the night before the sale, fog entered the valley, and a wildfire’s smoke hid the lower road. Lucía rang the old warning. Farmers working beyond the ridge heard it and led their animals toward the stone square. By dawn, soot covered the bell, but every family had arrived safely.

Years later and far north, Arun lived beside a river that froze each winter. His grandfather had taught him to place a blue flag above the boathouse when the ice began to weaken. New residents relied on phone alerts and laughed at the flag until a storm damaged the communication tower. Arun climbed through sleet to raise the cloth. A group of skaters saw it, turned back, and avoided a newly opened channel. Afterward, the town installed an electronic sign—but it kept the blue flag above it.

Neither Lucía nor Arun opposed change. Lucía helped record the bell’s warning patterns for emergency workers, and Arun designed symbols for the new sign. Each protected an inherited practice by adapting its purpose. The bell no longer guided travelers through fog, and the flag no longer served only longtime residents. Both became public promises: knowledge survives when a community chooses to carry it forward.

Part A — Individual Texts

  3. What does Lucía’s action reveal about her character?
  4. Summarize Arun’s conflict with the new residents’ assumptions.
  5. How does the damaged tower affect the plot?
  6. What does Arun’s decision reveal about his values?

Part B — Theme and Context

  1. Write a theme statement for Lucía’s narrative.
  2. Write a theme statement for Arun’s narrative.
  3. Identify the universal human concern shared by both.
  4. Explain how the mountain and river settings create different risks.
  5. How do technological changes shape each conflict?
  6. Why is it important that neither character opposes change?

Part C — Comparative Analysis

  1. Compare the bell and flag as symbols.
  2. Identify one structural parallel and explain its effect.
  3. Explain one important difference between the outcomes.
  4. Write a comparative claim about tradition and adaptation.
  5. Support that claim with one detail from each narrative and explain the reasoning.
  6. Evaluate the final sentence as a statement of universal theme without ignoring the texts’ distinct contexts.
