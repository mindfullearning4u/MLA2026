Checkpoint: “Measured Water, Shielded Light”

Directions

Paired Narrative

Beside a desert railway, Samir’s family maintained a shaded water jar for strangers. The trains stopped coming, yet his mother refilled the jar each morning. “A road can return before the travelers do,” she said. During a year of severe drought, officials asked residents to remove public jars. Samir instead placed a small cup beside the jar and painted a line showing how much one person should take. Months later, surveyors arrived to plan a solar field. They found the measured water waiting and included a public well in their proposal.

Across an ocean, Elin lived on an island where families left porch lights burning during winter storms. Modern navigation made the custom seem unnecessary, and the island council requested darkness to protect migrating birds. Elin fitted her lamp with a shield that directed light toward the harbor path and away from the sky. When a ferry lost power, passengers followed the low row of shielded lights to shelter. The council later adopted Elin’s design throughout the harbor.

Samir preserved welcome by limiting water; Elin preserved guidance by limiting light. Their choices suggest that a tradition’s deepest value may survive even when its familiar form must change.

  3. State the central conflict in Elin’s section.

Comparison

  2. Explain one parallel between Samir’s and Elin’s solutions.
  3. Explain one meaningful difference in cultural or environmental context.
  4. How do the officials or councils contribute to each conflict?

Synthesis

  2. Write a comparative claim and support it with evidence from both sections.
  3. Evaluate the final sentence: how accurately does it express a universal theme while preserving the texts’ differences?
