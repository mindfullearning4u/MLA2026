Worked Example: One Concern, Different Worlds

Text A — “The Clay Cup”

In a drought-stricken village, Amara’s family owns one painted cup used only when a traveler arrives. Though the family measures every drop, Amara fills the cup completely for a stranger. Her brother protests until the stranger reveals a map to a hidden spring.

Text B — “The Winter Window”

In a crowded northern city, Tomas keeps his apartment window shut to conserve heat. When a newly arrived neighbor becomes lost in the snow, Tomas opens the window and places a lamp on the sill. The room grows colder, but the neighbor follows the light home.

Individual Theme Statements

Text A: Generosity during scarcity can create possibilities that self-protection cannot.

Text B: A person may accept private discomfort to protect another member of the community.

Compare Elements and Context

  • Shared concern: responsibility toward a stranger.
  • Different settings: water scarcity makes giving tangible; dangerous cold makes guidance urgent.
  • Parallel symbols: the cup and lamp convert limited resources into invitations.
  • Different outcomes: Amara’s generosity unexpectedly benefits the village; Tomas acts without promised reward.

Model Comparative Claim and Reasoning

Claim: Both texts suggest that community begins when individuals risk a scarce resource for a stranger, but “The Clay Cup” connects generosity to reciprocal discovery while “The Winter Window” presents aid as a moral duty independent of reward.

Reasoning: The cup and lamp are parallel symbols of welcome. Yet the distinct outcomes prevent a shallow “kindness is rewarded” interpretation and reveal two culturally situated versions of responsibility.

Accuracy Check

The comparison states a shared universal concern, preserves the texts’ differences, uses evidence from both, and explains why the contrast matters.
