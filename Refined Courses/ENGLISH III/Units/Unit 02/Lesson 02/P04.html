P04 Worked Example

Study how to compare theme without forcing the texts to be identical.

Worked Example Texts

Text A: A traveler in an old desert tale shares his last water with a stranger and later survives because that stranger knows the way to the city.

Text B: A student in a modern school story gives her study notes to a classmate and later receives help preparing for a speech.

Worked Example 1: Shared Theme

Both texts develop the universal theme that generosity can create unexpected support.

Worked Example 2: Difference in Development

Text A develops the theme through survival in a harsh physical setting. Text B develops the same theme through academic and social support in a school setting.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Both stories are the same because people help people.

Why it is weak: It identifies a shared idea but ignores how each text develops the idea differently.

Correct: Both texts show that generosity can lead to support, but one develops the theme through physical survival while the other develops it through school collaboration.

Why it is correct: It names the theme and tracks how the theme changes across time and place.

Worked Example Review

Example 1: If the question asks for meaning, identify the claim or central idea first.

Example 2: If the question asks for evidence, choose the exact detail that proves the claim.

Example 3: If the question asks for analysis, explain how the detail affects the reader, argument, or interpretation.

Common Mistake: A response that only summarizes the topic does not show mastery because it does not explain the author's choices.
