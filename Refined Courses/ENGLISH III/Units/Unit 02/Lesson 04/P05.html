Independent Work: “Factory Measure”

Directions

Read the context and poem. Answer all 18 questions in complete sentences. Submit these responses with vocabulary.

Poem

Context: During the early twentieth century, rapid urban growth brought electric streetlights, crowded factories, and new debates about labor and time.

At five, the windows bloom with hands,
each pane a square of borrowed day.
The whistle cuts the morning loose;
we enter while the stars still stay.

My mother counted hours by bread,
by rising yeast and cooling stone.
I count them now by gears that turn
and bells that will not let me own

the minute after lunch, the breath
between one metal sheet and two.
Yet through the roof a sparrow calls—
a sound no foreman taught it to.

Tonight I carry home that note,
small contraband beneath my tongue.
The city measures what I make;
I measure what remains unsung.

Close Reading

  1. Identify the speaker and situation.
  2. Paraphrase stanza 1.
  3. Analyze “windows bloom with hands.”
  4. Explain the effect of “borrowed day.”
  5. How does the whistle function?
  6. Analyze the contrast between the mother’s and speaker’s timekeeping.

Craft and Context

  1. How does rhyme contribute to the controlled rhythm?
  2. Explain the enjambment between stanzas 2 and 3.
  3. What does “own” suggest about labor and time?
  4. Analyze the sparrow as a symbol.
  5. How does the historical context clarify the factory imagery?
  6. Which concern extends beyond the poem’s period?

Theme and Synthesis

  1. State an enduring theme.
  2. Support it with two lines and reasoning.
  3. Explain “small contraband beneath my tongue.”
  4. Analyze the final contrast between what the city and speaker measure.
  5. Write an objective summary.
  6. Evaluate how form, context, and imagery work together.
