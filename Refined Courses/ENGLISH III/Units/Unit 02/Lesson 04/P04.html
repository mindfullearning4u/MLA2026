Worked Example: “Night Shift, 1943”

Context

During World War II, many women entered industrial jobs while family members served abroad. Public praise for wartime production often existed beside exhaustion, danger, and uncertainty.

Model Poem

The siren combs the midnight air,
and we answer in borrowed shoes.
Steel flowers open under sparks;
our shadows learn the machines.

At lunch, Mae reads one line from home—
the rest is censored into snow.
We fold our worry with the letter,
then feed the furnace morning.

Close Reading

  • Speaker: a collective “we” emphasizes shared labor.
  • Imagery: “steel flowers” joins creation with danger.
  • Metaphor: censored words become “snow,” suggesting blankness, distance, and emotional cold.
  • Structure: the stanza break moves from public labor to private worry.
  • Final line: workers manufacture the conditions for “morning,” suggesting hope and relentless demand.

Model Claim

Through industrial imagery and a shift from collective work to private uncertainty, the poem reflects wartime factory life while developing an enduring theme: communities can transform fear into purposeful labor, but public achievement often depends on unrecognized emotional sacrifice.

Evidence and Reasoning

“Borrowed shoes” suggests workers occupying unfamiliar roles, while “fold our worry with the letter” turns emotional restraint into a physical action. The historical context clarifies wartime censorship, but the poem’s concern with hidden sacrifice extends beyond that period.

Accuracy Check

The interpretation identifies craft, uses context to clarify—not replace—evidence, and states both period-specific and enduring significance.
