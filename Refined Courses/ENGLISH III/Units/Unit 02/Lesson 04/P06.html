Checkpoint: “The Watch Behind the Wall”

Directions

Read the context and poem. Answer all 12 questions and submit them separately.

Poem

Context: The poem imagines a speaker during a period of wartime rationing, when households saved metal and food for military use.

We washed the foil and tied it flat,
saved sugar in a labeled tin.
The poster smiled above the sink:
“Your smallest gift will help us win.”

My sister mailed her wedding ring.
My father would not send his watch.
At night it ticked behind the wall—
a private, unpatriotic scratch.

Years later, peace filled every square,
but he still wound that guarded spring.
Some victories are counted loud;
some losses keep on measuring.

Analysis

  1. Identify the speaker and situation.
  2. What does the poster represent?
  3. Analyze the contrast between the sister’s ring and father’s watch.
  4. Explain “private, unpatriotic scratch.”
  5. How does sound imagery shape tone?

Context and Theme

  1. How does rationing context clarify the family’s choices?
  2. Why might the father keep the watch?
  3. Explain the shift in the final stanza.
  4. State an enduring theme.

Synthesis

  1. Write an objective summary.
  2. Support a claim about public duty and private loss with two lines.
  3. Evaluate the final line’s contribution to meaning.
