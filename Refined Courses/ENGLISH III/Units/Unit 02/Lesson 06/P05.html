Independent Work: Two Writers on Night Transit

Directions

Read both contemporaneous texts and answer all 18 questions. Submit responses with vocabulary.

Text A — Policy Commentary

Author A argues that the city should extend late-night bus service. She cites hospital shift schedules, survey data showing missed work, and a pilot route whose fare revenue covered most operating costs. She concedes that empty buses waste resources, then proposes service concentrated around major employers and transfer stations. Her conclusion defines mobility as economic infrastructure rather than convenience.

Text B — Literary Essay

Author B describes waiting after midnight with a hotel dishwasher, nursing assistant, and college custodian. The shelter’s advertising screen changes every ten seconds while the arrival display remains blank. He admits that one delayed night cannot establish a transit policy, but argues that public systems reveal whom a city imagines when it plans an ordinary day.

Comprehension and Reasoning

  1. State A’s central claim.
  2. Identify two forms of evidence A uses.
  3. Explain A’s concession and qualification.
  4. State B’s central claim.
  5. How does B use the waiting passengers as evidence?
  6. Explain B’s concession.
  7. Identify one assumption shared by both authors.
  8. Map A’s line of reasoning.
  9. Map B’s line of reasoning.

Comparison and Evaluation

  1. How does genre shape each author’s evidence?
  2. Compare their definitions of public value.
  3. Analyze the blank display and changing advertisements.
  4. Identify one important difference in audience.
  5. Write a comparative claim.
  6. Support it with evidence from both texts.
  7. Which text is more persuasive to a budget committee? Explain using criteria.
  8. Which is more effective for a general public audience? Explain.
  9. Evaluate what the two texts reveal together that neither reveals alone.
