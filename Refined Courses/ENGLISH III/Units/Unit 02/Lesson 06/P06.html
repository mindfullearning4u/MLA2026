Checkpoint: Two Writers on Community Libraries

Directions

Read both texts and answer all 12 questions. Submit only these responses separately.

Text A

Author A supports longer library hours using attendance records, student internet-access data, and comparisons with nearby districts. She acknowledges staffing costs and proposes rotating evening schedules. She concludes that access must be measured when families are available, not only when buildings are open.

Text B

Author B recalls learning English beside his father at a library table. He concedes that digital collections can reach more readers, yet argues that a shared room offers forms of assistance and belonging that a download cannot provide. He describes a librarian silently placing a bilingual dictionary between them.

Questions

  1. State each author’s central claim.
  2. Identify A’s evidence and reasoning.
  3. Explain A’s concession and solution.
  4. Identify B’s narrative evidence.
  5. Explain B’s concession.
  6. State a shared assumption.
  7. Compare how genre shapes reasoning.
  8. Compare definitions of access.
  9. Write a comparative claim.
  10. Support it with paired evidence.
  11. Evaluate which text better persuades a school board.
  12. Explain what the texts reveal together.
