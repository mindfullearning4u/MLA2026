Checkpoint: “The Bell and the Blueprint”

Directions

Read the different synthesis passage and answer all 12 questions. Submit separately.

Passage

When a school replaced its cracked bell tower, architect Lian proposed displaying the old bell at ground level. Principal Moss preferred a digital recording played each morning. Custodian Reyes remembered that the bell’s uneven rhythm once warned families when the river rose.

Students interviewed residents. Some recalled celebration; others remembered compulsory factory shifts announced by the same sound. In a poem displayed beside the blueprint, student Aya wrote, “A bell is a mouth / that borrows every order / and keeps none.”

The new building opened with the bell suspended above an empty pool designed to collect stormwater. It could not ring, but rain striking its surface produced changing tones. Moss called the sound accidental. Reyes called it the first honest version.

Questions

  1. Summarize the conflict.
  2. Compare Lian, Moss, and Reyes.
  3. How do residents’ memories complicate symbolism?
  4. Analyze Aya’s extended metaphor.
  5. Explain the juxtaposition of bell and blueprint.
  6. How does river history shape interpretation?
  7. What does the empty stormwater pool add?
  8. Analyze “accidental” versus “honest.”
  9. State a central theme.
  10. Write an objective summary.
  11. Write a synthesis claim using three lenses and evidence.
  12. Evaluate the ending’s ambiguity.
