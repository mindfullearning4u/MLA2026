Direct Teaching: Building a Unified Literary Analysis

Choose, Connect, Deepen

Strong synthesis does not use every possible term. It selects the lenses that explain the most important pattern, connects them causally, and deepens the claim by addressing tension or ambiguity.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Establish literal situation, speaker, genre, and context.
  2. Identify the central interpretive problem.
  3. Choose two or three productive lenses.
  4. Trace a pattern across the whole text.
  5. Draft a claim explaining interaction among lenses.
  6. Select varied evidence from beginning, middle, and end.
  7. Elaborate the reasoning after each detail.
  8. Test a counterinterpretation and refine the claim.
  9. Evaluate how structure and ending shape uncertainty.

Synthesis Formula

Authorial choice + interaction + effect + larger meaning + qualification.

Example: By alternating two narrators and repeating the image of an unlocked door, the author turns access into a contested symbol of belonging; however, the unresolved final account prevents the story from treating inclusion as a completed achievement.

Common Mistakes

  • Listing techniques without connecting them.
  • Using context as background rather than reasoning.
  • Ignoring contradictory evidence.
  • Adding a second interpretation without evaluating it.
  • Ending with summary instead of significance.
