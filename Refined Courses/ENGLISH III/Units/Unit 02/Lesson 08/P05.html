Independent Work: “The Museum of Unfinished Maps”

Directions

Read the complete synthesis passage and answer all 18 questions. Submit responses with vocabulary.

Passage

The Museum of Unfinished Maps displayed charts abandoned by explorers, surveyors, and children. Director Vale believed each map should be completed by experts before exhibition. Archivist Noor argued that blank spaces revealed the values and limits of the people who drew them.

At the center hung a coastal map begun after a devastating storm. Wealthy districts appeared in careful blue lines, while the fishing quarter ended in an empty margin. A note in the surveyor’s hand read, “Road inaccessible.” Beside it, residents had pinned a cloth map stitched from memory. Its streets changed width from one panel to the next, but every well and evacuation stair had a name.

Vale called the cloth map inaccurate. Noor called the official map incomplete. During a public forum, storm survivor Amadi said both had guided him differently: the survey map showed which bridges engineers believed existed; the cloth map showed where neighbors knew passage was possible.

The museum placed the maps together. A year later, visitors had covered the wall between them with questions, corrections, and thread. Vale ordered a glass case, but Noor requested a table instead. The board approved neither. It left the maps exposed while members debated.

Questions

  2. Characterize Vale.
  3. Characterize Noor.
  4. How does Amadi complicate both positions?
  5. Analyze the official map as a symbol.
  6. Analyze the cloth map as a symbol.
  7. Explain the juxtaposition of the maps.
  8. How does historical disaster context shape meaning?
  9. Identify one allegorical interpretation.
  10. Identify one limitation of that interpretation.
  11. How do multiple perspectives affect reliability?
  12. Explain the significance of named wells and stairs.
  13. Analyze the wall of questions and thread.
  14. What does glass case versus table represent?
  15. State a central theme.
  16. Write a synthesis claim using three lenses.
  17. Support it with evidence across the passage.
  18. Evaluate the unresolved ending.
