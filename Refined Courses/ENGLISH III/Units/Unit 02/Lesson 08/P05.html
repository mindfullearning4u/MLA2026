Review Passage

The abandoned station stood between the old farms and the new factories. In a poem written during the town's industrial shift, one speaker calls the station "a locked mouth full of departures." A later essay from the same period argues that the station brought wages but carried away memory.

Questions

  1. What figurative meaning is suggested by "a locked mouth full of departures"?
  2. How does the time-period context matter?
  3. What related topic do the poem and essay share?
  4. How do the two texts reason about change differently?
  5. Which narrative perspective would best extend the same conflict?
