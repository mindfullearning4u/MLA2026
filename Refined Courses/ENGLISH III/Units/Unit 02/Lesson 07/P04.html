P04 Worked Example

Study how to plan and revise a multiple-perspective narrative.

Example 1: Start with One Shared Event

Shared event: A class mural is painted over.

Why this works: One event can matter differently to different characters.

Example 2: Establish Two Perspectives

Student artist: notices the covered colors, the missing signatures, and the hours of work.

New student: notices the clean wall, the chance to begin again, and the open space.

The same wall creates loss for one character and possibility for another.

Example 3: Use Clear Transitions

Weak shift: The mural was gone. The wall looked clean. Someone felt angry. Someone felt hopeful.

Stronger shift: From Alia's side of the hallway, the blank wall looked like an eraser mark. From Mateo's side, it looked like a page waiting for a first sentence.

The stronger version signals two perspectives and uses figurative language to show contrast.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Alia was mad. Mateo was not mad. They saw the wall.

Why it is weak: It tells emotions without using narrative techniques.

Correct: Alia saw an "eraser mark" because the painted-over wall erased her work, while Mateo saw "a page waiting" because the blank wall gave him a place to belong.

Why it is correct: It uses details and figurative language to establish multiple perspectives.
