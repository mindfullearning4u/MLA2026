Worked Example: The Missing Key

Account 1 — Caretaker

I locked the archive at six and gave the brass key to Professor Hale. She placed it inside her red notebook. When the fire alarm sounded, I saw her leave without the notebook.

Account 2 — Professor

The caretaker offered me a key, but I told him to keep it. My notebook was blue. During the alarm, a student carried a red notebook down the stairs.

Account 3 — Student, Ten Years Later

I remember carrying two notebooks because the professor’s hands were shaking. One was blue; the other looked brown under the emergency lights. I never saw a key until the inquiry photograph.

Modeled Analysis

  • Agreements: alarm, notebooks, hurried exit.
  • Contradictions: custody of key; notebook color; who carried it.
  • Reliability factors: caretaker’s responsibility may motivate self-protection; professor was distressed; student recalls after ten years.
  • Structural effect: later testimony initially promises resolution but introduces memory uncertainty.

Model Synthesis

The accounts establish that confusion during the alarm separated the professor from at least one notebook, but they do not prove who possessed the key. By adding a delayed witness whose sensory memory complicates color and custody, the narrative shifts the focus from solving a theft to examining how institutions build certainty from partial recollection.
