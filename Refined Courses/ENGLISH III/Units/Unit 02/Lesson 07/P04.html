Worked Example 3: Use Clear Transitions

Step-by-step explanation:

  1. Step 1: Read the worked-example text and identify the task focus: Use Clear Transitions. This step keeps the work tied to Complex Narratives with Multiple Perspectives instead of a general response.
  2. Step 2: Use the exact passage detail, sentence, evidence, or task condition shown in this example. This is necessary because the answer must be supported by the text or prompt students can see on the page.
  3. Step 3: Explain how the selected detail supports the modeled answer. This final step interprets the answer and shows why it demonstrates the lesson skill accurately.

Weak shift: The mural was gone. The wall looked clean. Someone felt angry. Someone felt hopeful.

Stronger shift: From Alia's side of the hallway, the blank wall looked like an eraser mark. From Mateo's side, it looked like a page waiting for a first sentence.

The stronger version signals two perspectives and uses figurative language to show contrast.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Alia was mad. Mateo was not mad. They saw the wall.

Why it is weak: It tells emotions without using narrative techniques.

Correct: Alia saw an "eraser mark" because the painted-over wall erased her work, while Mateo saw "a page waiting" because the blank wall gave him a place to belong.

Why it is correct: It uses details and figurative language to establish multiple perspectives.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.
