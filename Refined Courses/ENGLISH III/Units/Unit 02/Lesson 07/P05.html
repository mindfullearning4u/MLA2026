Independent Work: “The Flood Ledger”

Directions

Read all three accounts, reconstruct events, and answer all 18 questions. Submit with vocabulary.

Account A — Mayor’s Broadcast, Day 1

The eastern levee failed without warning at 4:20. Emergency crews immediately opened the school shelter. No resident was denied transportation.

Account B — Bus Driver’s Journal, Day 3

At four, dispatch told me to wait for official confirmation. By the time the order came, River Street was underwater. I carried twelve people, though the route sheet later listed twenty. We passed the school twice; its doors were locked until nearly six.

Account C — Resident’s Interview, Twelve Years Later

I remember the bus arriving before the water, but my brother says we were already standing on the porch roof. The driver took everyone she could. At the school, a custodian broke a side-door chain. I cannot remember the mayor’s broadcast, only the battery radio repeating music between announcements.

Questions

  1. Create a neutral timeline.
  2. List two agreed facts.
  3. Identify two contradictions.
  4. What does the mayor know or claim directly?
  5. What motive may shape the broadcast?
  6. How does the driver’s journal complicate “immediately”?
  7. What does the route-sheet discrepancy suggest?
  8. How does delayed memory affect Account C?
  9. Why is the brother’s correction important?
  10. Analyze the locked door and broken chain.
  11. What do readers know that each narrator does not?
  12. How does chronology affect judgment?
  13. Write the strongest supported factual conclusion.
  14. Name one unresolved uncertainty.
  15. Write a claim about institutional language and lived experience.
  16. Support it with evidence from all three accounts.
  17. Evaluate which source is most reliable for one specific question.
  18. Explain how multiple perspectives deepen the central idea.
