Notebook Title

Multiple-Perspective Narrative Planning

Vocabulary

  • Perspective: the way a character understands an event.
  • Point of view: the narrator's position in the story.
  • Complex narrative: a story with layered structure, conflict, or perspective.
  • Juxtaposition: placing details side by side to reveal contrast.
  • Transition: a word, phrase, or structure that helps the reader follow shifts.

Direct Teaching

A multiple-perspective narrative does not mean writing the same scene twice with different names. Each perspective should reveal a different understanding of the same event.

Use this sequence:

  1. Choose one shared event. The event must matter to both characters.
  2. Give each character a different concern. One may want safety while another wants independence.
  3. Select details each character would notice. Perspective is revealed through attention.
  4. Signal shifts clearly. Use section breaks, names, time shifts, or transitions.
