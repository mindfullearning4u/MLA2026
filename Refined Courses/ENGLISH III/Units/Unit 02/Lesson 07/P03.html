P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Practice turning a shared event into two perspectives.

Shared Event

A town replaces a cracked stone bridge with a new steel bridge.

Perspective A

Mina touched the stone rail before the workers arrived. Her grandfather's initials were still carved near the bend, shallow from rain but readable if she traced them slowly. The new bridge would be safer, everyone said. Mina only heard the word gone.

Perspective B

Jon checked the warning sign and counted the loose stones under the rail. Last winter, the bridge had shifted after the flood, and every school bus crossed it too quietly. The new steel span would not remember anyone's grandfather, but it would hold.

Notebook Task

  1. What shared event appears in both perspectives?
  2. What detail shows Mina's perspective?
  3. What detail shows Jon's perspective?
  4. How does the juxtaposition reveal different concerns?
  5. Write one sentence explaining how the two perspectives make the narrative more complex.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Mina is sad and Jon is happy, so the story has two perspectives.

Why it is weak: It labels emotions but does not explain what each character notices or values.

Correct: Mina focuses on memory through her grandfather's initials, while Jon focuses on safety through the loose stones and school bus.

Why it works: It uses details to explain perspective.
