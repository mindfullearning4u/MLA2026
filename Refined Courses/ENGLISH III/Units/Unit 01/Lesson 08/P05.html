The Archive of Rain

For generations, Hill County measured drought at one metal gauge behind the courthouse. When the gauge rusted beyond repair, officials planned to replace it and discard the handwritten rain ledgers stored nearby.

Student intern Meena expected the ledgers to prove a simple pattern of worsening drought. Instead, she found missing months, relocated gauges, and notes describing storms too violent for accurate readings. Farm journals corroborated several dry years but contradicted one unusually low courthouse total. A margin note explained that the collector had been ill.

Meena did not conclude that the records were useless. She mapped which claims each source could support. The ledgers documented long-term observation; farm journals supplied local context; modern sensors provided consistent measurements. She proposed preserving the old gauge beside a digital station and scanning the ledgers with annotations explaining gaps and contradictions.

At the exhibit opening, rain tapped the new sensor and the dented gauge. A screen displayed both measurements and unanswered questions. Visitors did not receive one perfect climate story. They saw how responsible knowledge grows by recording evidence, testing it, and making its limits visible.
