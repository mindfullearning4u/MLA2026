The Shared Map

A transit map labeled the South Market stop “low use.” Student analyst Devon compared ticket counts with community surveys. The counts were accurate, but they excluded riders who transferred from free neighborhood shuttles. Surveys also showed that older residents used the stop for the clinic even when they traveled only twice a month.

Devon did not claim the stop was crowded. He argued that frequency alone provided an incomplete measure of importance. His proposal added transfer estimates, destination purpose, and accessibility data to the review. The agency delayed closure and tested a revised schedule.

On the new public map, line thickness still represented frequency, but symbols also identified clinics, schools, and transfer points. The map became more complex because the decision had always been more complex.
