Direct Teaching: The Complete Analytical Cycle

1. Read Closely

Identify central ideas, patterns, shifts, word choices, and tensions. Record both supporting details and evidence that complicates a first impression.

2. Interpret and Qualify

Build a defensible claim that answers the prompt and matches the evidence’s scope. Revise the claim when counterevidence exposes an overstatement.

3. Select and Paraphrase Evidence

Choose the strongest relevant evidence, preserve context, change wording and structure, attribute the source, and check for patchwriting or distortion.

4. Organize Analysis

  1. Focused claim
  2. Necessary context
  3. Integrated evidence
  4. Reasoning
  5. Elaboration and synthesis
  6. Concluding insight

5. Refine Academic Voice

Use exact vocabulary, appropriate qualifications, coherent transitions, and Standard English. Edit boundaries, agreement, parallelism, modifiers, pronouns, and concision without changing meaning.

6. Verify Mastery

Ask: Does every claim have support? Does every detail advance the claim? Is uncertainty visible? Is the reasoning explicit? Is the response clear to its audience?

Common Mistakes

  • Forcing all evidence into the first interpretation.
  • Summarizing instead of analyzing.
  • Using specialized vocabulary without explaining its function.
  • Editing away qualifications.
  • Ending without synthesis.
