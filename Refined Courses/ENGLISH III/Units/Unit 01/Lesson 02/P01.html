Lesson 2: Academic Vocabulary for Literary and Rhetorical Analysis

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.V.01 - Integrate Academic Vocabulary.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.10 - Paraphrase Grade-Level Texts; MLA.ENG3.L.01 - Apply Standard English Conventions.

What You Will Learn

  • How academic vocabulary makes analysis more precise.
  • How to choose words that fit literary and rhetorical thinking.
  • How to use Grade 11 vocabulary in speaking and writing.
  • How to avoid vague words such as thing, good, bad, and stuff.

Why This Matters

English III asks you to evaluate literature, Classical Period texts, rhetoric, research, and presentations. You need precise academic vocabulary so your ideas are clear and credible.

Strong vocabulary does not mean using the longest word possible. It means choosing the word that names the thinking accurately.

What You Will Do

  • Study vocabulary used in literary and rhetorical analysis.
  • Replace vague wording with precise academic wording.
  • Write short analytical sentences using vocabulary correctly.
  • Check whether the vocabulary fits the evidence.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • You choose precise terms for analysis.
  • You use vocabulary accurately in complete sentences.
  • You explain why a word fits the evidence.
  • Your quiz responses identify effective and ineffective vocabulary use.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.ENG3.V.01 in student language: I can use Grade 11 academic vocabulary correctly when I speak and write about texts.

Today, your job is to make your thinking more precise by choosing words that match the text and the task.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help

Ask your Teacher of Record for help if you understand a text but cannot find the academic word that names your idea accurately.
