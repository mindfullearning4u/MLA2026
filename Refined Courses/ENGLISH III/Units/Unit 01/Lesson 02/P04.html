Worked Example: From Word Choice to Analytical Claim

Model Passage

At the dedication, Mayor Ibarra called the restored bridge “a stubborn thread between shores.” She admitted that repairing old stone had cost more than pouring a narrow concrete span. “Efficiency matters,” she said, “but what is a town if every useful thing forgets how it became useful?” Behind her, the new steel braces shone against the weathered arches.

Step 1 — Notice High-Value Language

stubborn thread, admitted, the rhetorical question, and the juxtaposition of new steel with weathered arches carry interpretive weight.

Step 2 — Determine Meaning Precisely

Stubborn denotes resistance to change and connotes persistence. Calling the bridge a thread suggests a delicate but continuing connection. Together, the metaphor presents preservation as resilient connection rather than mere age.

Step 3 — Name Rhetorical Moves

The mayor makes a concession when she acknowledges the higher cost. Her rhetorical question then reframes usefulness to include historical memory. The physical juxtaposition of new braces and old arches embodies her argument.

Step 4 — Build a Qualified Claim

Claim: Through a concession, a rhetorical question, and the juxtaposition of new and weathered materials, the mayor suggests that responsible progress can preserve communal memory while meeting present needs.

Step 5 — Explain Evidence

The concession strengthens credibility because it does not ignore cost. The question expands the audience’s definition of usefulness, while the contrasting materials provide visible evidence that repair and change can coexist.

Second Quick Model

Line: “The hallway did not fall silent; it held its breath.”

Analysis: The personification gives the pause suspenseful agency. Held its breath connotes anticipation rather than emptiness, creating a tense register.
