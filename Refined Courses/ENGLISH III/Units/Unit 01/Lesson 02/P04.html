P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Identify the Analytical Need

Prompt: Explain how the author's word choice affects tone.

Step 1: Identify the task word: explain.

Step 2: Identify the academic focus: word choice and tone.

Step 3: Choose vocabulary that fits: diction, connotation, tone, effect.

Worked Example 2: Build the Sentence

Evidence: The narrator calls the room "narrow," "silent," and "airless."

Weak response: The words make the room seem bad.

Stronger response: The narrator's diction creates a tense tone because words such as "narrow," "silent," and "airless" suggest confinement and discomfort.

Why it works: The sentence uses precise vocabulary and connects the words to an effect.

Worked Example 3: Check the Fit

Check 1: Does diction fit? Yes, because the answer discusses word choice.

Check 2: Does tense tone fit? Yes, because the evidence suggests discomfort.

Check 3: Does confinement fit? Yes, because narrow and airless suggest limited space.

Conclusion: Academic vocabulary is strongest when it is precise and supported by evidence.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The author's rhetoric is nice because it has good words.

Why it is wrong: The sentence uses academic-sounding language without precision. It does not explain the technique or effect.

Correct: The author's diction creates a reflective tone because the repeated references to memory invite readers to think about the past.

Why it is correct: The vocabulary fits the evidence and explains the effect.
