A Name for the Wind — Community Hearing Speech

Some have called the dunes empty land. I understand the convenience of that phrase. Empty land asks nothing of us; it can be measured, divided, and sold without argument. Yet the fishermen call the tallest dune North Marker because its pale grass has guided boats toward the inlet for generations. Biologists call the same ridge a storm barrier. Children call it the place where the wind writes.

These names do not compete so much as accumulate. Each reveals a use that the word empty erases. I concede that our town needs housing, and I do not claim that every grain of sand must remain untouched. But must urgency make our vocabulary smaller? A plan described only as “development” hides both what will be built and what may disappear.

Let us use accurate words before we choose irreversible actions. The proposed eastern site contains forty acres; the protected ridge occupies seven. We can build homes on stable ground, preserve the storm barrier, and create a public path to North Marker. Compromise is not the language of surrender. Here, it is the grammar of belonging.
