P04 Worked Example

Study how the paraphrase, evidence, and explanation work together.

Worked Example 1: Read for Meaning

Passage: The speaker praises courage, but the speech does not define courage as fearlessness. Instead, the speaker describes courage as the choice to act responsibly even when the outcome is uncertain.

Step 1: Identify the topic. The passage is about how the speaker defines courage.

Step 2: Identify the contrast. Courage is not fearlessness; it is responsible action during uncertainty.

Step 3: Keep both sides of the contrast in the paraphrase.

Worked Example 2: Add Evidence

Paraphrase: The speaker presents courage as responsible action in uncertain situations, not as the absence of fear.

Relevant evidence: "the choice to act responsibly even when the outcome is uncertain"

Why this evidence works: The quoted phrase directly supports the paraphrase because it names action, responsibility, and uncertainty.

Worked Example 3: Explain the Connection

Step 1: Name what the evidence says.

The phrase says courage involves responsible action.

Step 2: Connect that meaning to the paraphrase.

Because the phrase says people act even when the result is uncertain, it supports the idea that courage is not simple fearlessness.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The speech says courage means never feeling fear.

Why it is wrong: This reverses the meaning. The passage says courage is not defined as fearlessness.

Correct: The speech defines courage as acting responsibly even when the result is uncertain.

Why it is correct: The wording changes, but the original meaning and evidence are preserved.
