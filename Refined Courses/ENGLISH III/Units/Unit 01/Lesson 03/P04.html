Worked Example: Building an Evidence-Based Paragraph

Model Passage

When the neighborhood council announced that the footbridge would close, Lena complained that no one listened to teenagers. Her brother asked whether she had attended any planning meetings. The next week, Lena studied the repair report, marked the route used by students, and brought a one-page alternative to the council. Her voice shook during the first sentence, but she finished. The council did not accept every suggestion; it did add temporary lighting and a safer detour.

Step 1 — Focused Claim

Lena gains meaningful influence when she replaces a general complaint with informed participation.

Step 2 — Relevant Evidence

The strongest evidence is her study of the report, route map, written alternative, and decision to speak. Her shaking voice is relevant only when used to show action despite fear.

Step 3 — Accurate Paraphrase

Rather than continuing to object from outside the process, Lena researches the bridge problem, develops a documented alternative, and presents it even though she is nervous.

Step 4 — Full Model

Lena gains meaningful influence when she replaces a general complaint with informed participation. After her brother challenges her absence from the meetings, she analyzes the repair report, maps student travel, and presents a written alternative despite visible nervousness. These actions give the council specific information it can use, and the resulting lighting and detour changes demonstrate that evidence-based participation produces a concrete, if partial, response.

Accuracy Check

The model preserves sequence and outcome, changes both wording and syntax, avoids claiming that Lena caused every decision, and explains the evidence’s connection to the claim.
