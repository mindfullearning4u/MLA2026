Direct Teaching: Paraphrase and Relevant Evidence

The Claim–Evidence–Reasoning Chain

A claim states the interpretation. Evidence provides the textual basis. Reasoning explains why the evidence supports the claim. Removing any link produces assertion, summary without purpose, or unexplained quotation.

Selecting Relevant Evidence

  1. Underline the exact idea the claim must prove.
  2. List candidate details.
  3. Reject details that are merely interesting, repetitive, or about a different issue.
  4. Choose the strongest detail or combination.
  5. Record enough context to keep the evidence accurate.

Paraphrase in Four Moves

  1. Read beyond the target sentence.
  2. State its meaning without looking.
  3. Rebuild the idea with a different grammatical structure and precise synonyms.
  4. Compare with the source for copied patterns, omissions, and distortion.

Integrate and Explain

Claim: The narrator’s confidence grows through deliberate action.

Integrated paraphrase: After initially avoiding the public meeting, the narrator prepares evidence and volunteers to speak first.

Reasoning: This change from avoidance to preparation shows that confidence develops through participation rather than appearing suddenly.

Common Mistakes

  • Dropping evidence into a paragraph without attribution or context.
  • Using evidence that matches the topic but not the precise claim.
  • Patchwriting by changing only a few words.
  • Adding motives or causes the source does not establish.
  • Repeating the evidence instead of explaining its significance.
