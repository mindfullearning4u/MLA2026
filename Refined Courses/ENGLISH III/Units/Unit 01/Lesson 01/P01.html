Lesson 1: Grade 11 Close Reading and Accurate Paraphrase

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.R.10 - Paraphrase Grade-Level Texts.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.V.01 - Integrate Academic Vocabulary; MLA.ENG3.L.01 - Apply Standard English Conventions.

What You Will Learn

  • How to slow down when reading complex Grade 11 sentences.
  • How to separate the author's central meaning from extra wording.
  • How to paraphrase without copying sentence structure.
  • How to keep the original meaning accurate while using your own words.

Why This Matters

In English III, you will read literary, rhetorical, historical, and research-based texts. Before you can evaluate a writer's choices, you must first understand what the text actually says.

A strong paraphrase is proof of comprehension. It lets you use evidence honestly because you are not guessing, copying, or changing the author's meaning.

What You Will Do

  • Read short Grade 11 passages included inside the lesson.
  • Identify the topic, central meaning, and required details.
  • Write notebook paraphrases that preserve meaning.
  • Compare correct and incorrect paraphrases.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your paraphrases keep the author's meaning accurate.
  • Your wording and sentence structure are your own.
  • Your checkpoint explains the thinking behind your paraphrase.
  • Your quiz responses identify accurate and inaccurate paraphrases.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.ENG3.R.10 in student language: I can read a Grade 11 text and restate its content accurately in my own words.

Today, your job is to preserve meaning. Do not add opinions, remove key details, or copy the author's sentence pattern.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help

Ask your Teacher of Record for help if you cannot identify the central meaning, if your paraphrase sounds too close to the original, or if you are unsure whether you changed the author's meaning.
