P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continue Your Notebook Work

Now you will move from identifying meaning to checking whether a paraphrase is accurate.

Read the New Passage

Passage: A student who studies a speech from an earlier time period must pay attention to more than the speaker's words. The student should also consider the audience, the occasion, and the problem the speaker hoped to address.

This passage has several connected ideas. A weak paraphrase may mention only the words in the speech and forget audience, occasion, and purpose.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, create a three-column chart:

  1. Original idea: Write each important idea from the passage.
  2. My words: Restate each idea in simpler wording.
  3. Accuracy check: Explain whether your wording keeps the same meaning.

Deeper Application

Write a final paraphrase of the whole passage. Then answer this question in two or three sentences:

How did you make sure your paraphrase included all parts of the author's meaning?

Your answer should mention the speaker's words, audience, occasion, and problem.

Common Mistake Check

Incorrect: Students should only look at the exact words in old speeches.

Why this is incorrect: The passage says students must consider more than words. It specifically names audience, occasion, and problem.

Correct: When studying a speech from the past, students should examine the speaker's language along with the audience, event, and issue that shaped the speech.

Why this is correct: It keeps all major parts of the passage and changes the wording.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Grade 11 close reading and accurate paraphrase

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.R.10

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Identify the exact Grade 11 skill before beginning the task.
  2. Use textual evidence, source evidence, or reasoning instead of general impressions.
  3. Explain how the evidence proves the point, affects the audience, or develops meaning.
  4. Revise for clarity, precision, and academic voice before submitting your work.
