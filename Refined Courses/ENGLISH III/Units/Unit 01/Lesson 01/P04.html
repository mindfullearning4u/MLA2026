Worked Example: From Close Reading to Accurate Paraphrase

Model Passage

At the edge of the harbor, the old signal tower had not guided a ship in twenty years. Developers called it useless and proposed replacing it with a glass observation deck. During the public meeting, historian Amara Cole presented the tower’s logbooks. They recorded not only storms and arrivals but also the names of volunteers who had watched the water during wartime blackouts. A retired fisherman then explained that the tower’s bell once warned families when sudden fog covered the channel. Instead of rejecting development, residents proposed restoring the tower and building the observation deck beside it. The revised plan preserved the original structure, displayed copies of the logbooks, and added a safer viewing platform. When the site reopened, visitors came for the harbor view but remained to read the names and stories inside the tower.

Step 1 — Read for the Gist

The passage explains how evidence about an old signal tower changed a community development plan.

Step 2 — State the Central Idea

Central idea: Communities can meet present needs while preserving meaningful history when decisions are guided by evidence and compromise.

This idea includes both the conflict and its resolution without listing every event.

Step 3 — Select Essential Evidence

  • The logbooks documented volunteers and community service during wartime.
  • The bell had warned local families when fog made the harbor dangerous.
  • The revised plan preserved the tower while adding the new viewing platform.

Together, these details establish the tower’s historical value and show how the compromise addressed both goals.

Step 4 — Make and Support an Inference

Inference: The developers originally judged the tower only by its current function.

Support: They called it useless before the historian and fisherman revealed the community roles recorded in the logbooks and represented by the bell.

Steps 5–7 — Build the Paraphrase

Meaning notes: abandoned signal tower; proposed replacement; records revealed service; compromise preserved tower and added deck; visitors learned history.

Draft: A planned harbor attraction was redesigned after residents presented records showing that an obsolete signal tower had protected and connected the community. The final project restored the historic building, placed the new observation platform nearby, and allowed visitors to encounter both the view and the town’s past.

Accuracy check: The paraphrase preserves the conflict, evidence, compromise, and result while changing the wording and combining the original sentences into a new structure.

Why a Near-Copy Fails

Too close: Developers said the old signal tower was useless and wanted to replace it with a glass deck, but residents showed logbooks and proposed restoring it.

This version follows the source’s sequence and repeats distinctive wording. It also omits why the records mattered and how the final plan served both purposes.
