Worked Example: Testing an Interpretation

Model Passage

Rina kept the theater key after the building closed, although the lock had already been replaced. She told friends the key was useless, yet she rubbed its worn teeth whenever the council discussed demolition. At the final hearing, she did not ask the council to preserve every wall. She presented photographs of community performances and proposed saving the stage arch inside the new recreation center.

Initial Interpretation

The key symbolizes Rina’s refusal to accept change.

Testing the Claim

The worn key supports attachment, but her proposal accepts demolition and adapts one meaningful feature. This counterevidence makes the initial claim too absolute.

Revised Claim

The useless key initially represents Rina’s private attachment, but her evidence-based proposal shows that she transforms nostalgia into selective, forward-looking preservation.

Integrated Model Paragraph

Rina transforms nostalgia into selective, forward-looking preservation. Although she admits that the replaced key no longer opens the theater, she repeatedly touches it during demolition discussions, revealing an attachment she struggles to express. At the hearing, however, she does not demand that every wall remain; instead, she documents community performances and proposes incorporating the stage arch into a new center. The contrast qualifies the key’s meaning: it represents not permanent resistance but the emotional history that motivates a workable compromise.
