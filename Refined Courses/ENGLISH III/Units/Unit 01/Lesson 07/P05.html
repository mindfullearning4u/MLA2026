Names on the Breakwater

After each winter storm, the harbor crew repainted numbers on the concrete breakwater. Lio had assumed the numbers marked repair sections until his grandmother showed him a faded photograph. In it, fishing families stood beside the same blocks, each number paired with a handwritten name.

The official harbor guide described the breakwater as a federal engineering project completed in 1964. The guide was accurate, but incomplete. Newspaper archives showed that local crews had tested currents for two years, while meeting minutes recorded residents arguing for a public walkway along the structure. A storm later destroyed the first path, and the rebuilt wall excluded pedestrians.

Lio wanted to restore every name from the photograph. His grandmother cautioned that some spellings were uncertain and several people could not be identified. Together they compared boat registrations, census records, and oral histories. They confirmed twenty-three names, marked six as probable, and left four blank.

At the redesign meeting, Lio presented the evidence and the uncertainty. Engineers explained that new safety rails could support engraved panels without weakening the wall. The final design included confirmed names, a separate section explaining probable identifications, and four empty spaces. On opening day, visitors read the names while waves struck the numbered blocks below. The blanks did not make the history unfinished; they made its limits visible.
