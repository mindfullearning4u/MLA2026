P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Read the Response

Draft response: the narrator describes the candle as "small, stubborn, and still burning" this image suggests hope that survives difficult conditions.

The ideas are strong, but conventions make the response harder to read.

Notebook Task

  1. Copy the draft response.
  2. Circle the missing capital letter.
  3. Mark where the sentence boundary needs punctuation.
  4. Rewrite the response correctly.

Deeper Application

Write one sentence explaining why the corrected response is clearer. Use the word conventions or clarity.

Accelerated option: Add a second sentence explaining how punctuation helps connect evidence to explanation.

Common Mistake Check

Incorrect: the narrator describes the candle as "small, stubborn, and still burning" this image suggests hope.

Why this is incorrect: The sentence begins without a capital letter and joins two complete ideas without punctuation.

Correct: The narrator describes the candle as "small, stubborn, and still burning." This image suggests hope.

Why this is correct: Capitalization and sentence punctuation make the response readable.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Conventions and clarity in analytical responses

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.L.01

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Read the draft as an outside reader. Mark where the claim, reasoning, organization, sentence control, or style becomes unclear.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: first meaning, then organization, then transitions, then sentence precision, then conventions.
  3. Keep the original analytical purpose unless the task asks for a new claim. Revision should strengthen the argument, explanation, or analysis.
  4. Reread the revised version and ask whether the audience can follow the reasoning without extra explanation from a teacher.
