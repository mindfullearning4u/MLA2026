Direct Teaching: Editing for Clarity and Control

1. Repair Sentence Boundaries

A comma alone cannot join independent clauses. Use a period, semicolon, or comma plus coordinating conjunction. Faulty: The image recurs, it changes meaning. Correct: The image recurs, but it changes meaning.

2. Control Relationships with Clauses

Use subordination to show cause, contrast, condition, or time. Flat: The narrator hesitates. She speaks. Clear: Although the narrator hesitates, she ultimately speaks.

3. Maintain Parallel Structure

Faulty: The speech challenges fear, injustice, and asks citizens to act. Parallel: The speech challenges fear, exposes injustice, and urges citizens to act.

4. Clarify Pronouns and Modifiers

Replace vague pronouns when two possible antecedents exist. Place modifiers beside what they describe. Unclear: After reading the letter, the decision seemed unfair. Clear: After reading the letter, Maya considered the decision unfair.

5. Prefer Active, Concise Analysis

Wordy passive: It is shown by the author that fear is experienced by the speaker. Clear active: The author portrays the speaker’s fear.

6. Edit Without Distorting

  1. Identify the intended claim.
  2. Repair grammar and punctuation.
  3. Remove repetition and vague wording.
  4. Check that evidence and qualifications remain intact.
  5. Read aloud for rhythm and unintended ambiguity.

Common Mistakes

  • Creating fragments while trying to shorten sentences.
  • Changing a qualified claim into an absolute statement.
  • Using a pronoun with no clear antecedent.
  • Making list items grammatically unequal.
  • Choosing passive voice when the actor matters.
