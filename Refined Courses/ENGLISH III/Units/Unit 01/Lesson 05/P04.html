Worked Example: Revising an Analytical Paragraph

Original Draft

The narrator returns to the pier, it reminds her of her brother. Walking through the fog, the broken bell is noticed. This shows how memories are important and it affects her. The bell represents loss, remembering the argument, and she wants reconciliation.

Step 1 — Diagnose

  • Comma splice in sentence 1.
  • Dangling modifier and unnecessary passive voice in sentence 2.
  • Vague pronouns and imprecise wording in sentence 3.
  • Faulty parallel structure in sentence 4.

Step 2 — Preserve the Meaning

The draft intends to argue that the pier and bell activate the narrator’s grief and desire to repair a damaged relationship. Revision should clarify that claim without inventing new evidence.

Step 3 — Revised Paragraph

When the narrator returns to the pier, the setting recalls her unresolved conflict with her brother. As she walks through the fog, she notices the broken bell, whose silence connects physical damage with interrupted communication. The bell therefore represents both loss and the possibility of reconciliation: remembering the argument causes pain, but it also motivates her to seek repair.

Step 4 — Why It Is Clearer

The revision repairs boundaries, identifies the narrator as the observer, gives each pronoun a clear antecedent, uses parallel ideas, and replaces the vague phrase “memories are important” with precise analysis.
