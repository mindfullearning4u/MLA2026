After the Siren

Every Wednesday at noon, the harbor siren sounded for exactly ten seconds. Most residents treated it as a clock. For Captain Reyes, it marked the storm of 1989, when the same siren had warned fishing boats away from the channel.

When the city replaced the emergency system with phone alerts, officials planned to silence the siren permanently. Reyes opposed the decision, but her first letter relied entirely on memory. A council member asked whether an aging signal still served a public purpose.

Reyes returned with maintenance records, emergency-response data, and interviews with residents who lacked reliable cell service. She revised her proposal: test the siren monthly as a backup while using the phone system as the primary warning. The council funded a one-year trial. At the first test, the old sound crossed the harbor while phones lit in hundreds of hands. Neither signal stood alone.
