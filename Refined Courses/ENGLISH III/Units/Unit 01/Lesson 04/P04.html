Worked Example: From Prompt to Paragraph

Prompt and Passage

Prompt: Analyze how the author shows that belonging requires participation.

Mika attended every garden meeting but sat beside the tool shed without speaking. When a storm split the greenhouse roof, she arrived early with measurements and a sketch for temporary braces. She asked two neighbors to test the plan, revised one angle, and stayed until the final panel stopped rattling. At the next meeting, someone moved an empty chair into the circle. Mika carried it the remaining distance herself.

Step 1 — Claim

The author presents belonging as an active process: Mika becomes part of the group when she contributes knowledge, accepts collaboration, and chooses to enter the circle.

Step 2 — Evidence Plan

Use her earlier silence as context, her measured repair work as evidence of participation, and the final chair image as evidence of both invitation and choice.

Step 3 — Model Paragraph

The author presents belonging as an active process rather than automatic physical presence. Although Mika regularly attends meetings, she initially remains silent beside the shed. After the storm, however, she measures the damage, proposes braces, invites neighbors to test the design, and revises it with their help. These actions show that participation includes both offering expertise and accepting other people’s input. The empty chair then symbolizes the group’s invitation, but Mika’s decision to carry it into the circle emphasizes that she must also choose involvement. Thus, the passage defines belonging as a relationship created through contribution, collaboration, and agency.

Why It Works

The claim answers the prompt; transitions clarify change; evidence is contextualized; reasoning interprets actions and symbol; the conclusion extends the idea without repeating the first sentence.
