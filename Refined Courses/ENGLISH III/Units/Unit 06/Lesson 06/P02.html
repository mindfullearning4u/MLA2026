P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Plan quality writing for a publication audience.

Notebook Title

Writing for Online or LAN Publication

Vocabulary

Publication: Sharing writing with an intended audience.

LAN: A local area network where content may be shared within a school or organization.

Export: To save or prepare a file in a format that can be shared or published.

Audience: The readers or viewers the writing is created for.

Quality writing: Writing that is clear, organized, revised, accurate, and appropriate for its audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the audience. Ask, "Who will read this, and what do they already know?"

Step 2: Identify the purpose. Ask, "Am I explaining, analyzing, persuading, informing, or presenting research?"

Step 3: Check the structure. Published writing should guide readers from the title, to the main idea, to evidence, to explanation.

Step 4: Choose multimedia only when it helps readers understand. A chart, image, timeline, or audio clip should support the written message.

Step 5: Check conventions. Spelling, capitalization, punctuation, source labels, and formatting all affect publication quality.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, create a four-row planning chart with these headings: audience, purpose, writing quality, multimedia support.

Choose one piece of writing you have drafted in this unit. Complete each row with one clear sentence.

Example: Audience: My audience is classmates who need a clear summary of my research claim before viewing my presentation.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Quality writing for online or LAN publication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.C.03

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Identify the communication situation: audience, purpose, mode, and evidence. A presentation or publication should be planned before it is designed.
  2. Organize the message so the audience can follow it in real time. Oral and digital communication must be clear without rereading.
  3. Use rhetorical and multimedia choices to clarify meaning, not to decorate. Each visual, example, or phrase should support understanding.
  4. Check that the final communication is accurate, polished, audience-aware, and connected to the evidence or research behind it.
