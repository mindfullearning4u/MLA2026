Worked Example: A Complete Publication Decision

Scenario

A class is preparing a digital story titled The River Steps for residents and city planners. The central claim is that a neglected public stairway is both a flood hazard and a meaningful community landmark. Available evidence includes inspection reports, rainfall records, interviews with longtime residents, photographs from three decades, and a student-recorded soundscape.

1 — Build the Brief

Audience: residents and planners. Purpose: explain both safety concerns and cultural value. Deliverable: a five-minute narrated story page with transcript. Constraint: do not identify minors or publish private addresses.

2 — Map Claims to Evidence

The inspection report supports the hazard claim; rainfall records show frequency; interviews support community meaning; dated photographs demonstrate change. The soundscape establishes place but does not prove historical claims.

3 — Design and Synthesize

The page opens with the present-day problem, moves backward through resident memories, then returns to a proposal. The narration paraphrases reports, identifies uncertainty, and uses a brief quotation only when the speaker’s phrasing matters.

4 — Audit and Test

The team obtains interview releases, credits images, captions audio, writes meaningful alt text, adds a text-only version, checks color contrast, and asks two residents and one planner to complete a navigation task. Testing reveals that the proposal link is hard to find, so it is moved beside the conclusion.

5 — Release Decision

The change log records the moved link, corrected date, shortened narration, new transcript headings, revised alt text, and privacy redaction. The editor, source checker, accessibility reviewer, and project owner sign off. The rationale explains how these changes improve accuracy, coherence, access, and usefulness.
