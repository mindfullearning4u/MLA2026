P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Build a literary research speaking plan.

Speaking Plan Model

Claim: The narrator's repeated doorway imagery shows a conflict between safety and freedom.

Primary evidence: The narrator pauses at a locked doorway before each major decision.

Research support: A source explains that threshold images often signal a moment of transition.

Explanation: Together, these details show the narrator facing change but fearing its cost.

Notebook Task

Create your own four-part speaking plan: claim, primary-text evidence, research support, and explanation. Keep each part short enough to say aloud.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving a broad answer without identifying the exact evidence, rhetorical move, literary choice, or author purpose.

Correct: Name the specific evidence or choice and explain how it supports meaning, argument, style, or reader effect.

Why this matters: English III mastery requires clear reasoning. The reader must see how your evidence proves the claim.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Presenting literary research and complex analysis

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.C.01

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Begin with the interpretive question or claim. In English III, research should help explain literature, not simply collect facts about an author or time period.
  2. Separate primary evidence from secondary-source support. Primary evidence comes from the literary text; secondary support comes from commentary, context, or scholarly explanation.
  3. Synthesize instead of stacking quotations. Explain how the source idea changes, deepens, or supports your interpretation of the text.
  4. Check that every source detail connects back to the literary claim. If the connection is not clear, add reasoning or choose stronger evidence.
