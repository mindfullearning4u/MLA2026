Worked Example: Presenting an Interpretation of “The Winter Window”

Source Set

In the poem, a speaker repeatedly clears frost from a window but sees only a neighbor’s unlit house. The final line says the glass “kept the shape of my hand after the warmth was gone.” Scholar A argues that windows in the poet’s work represent social separation. A local-history article explains that the poem was written during a winter fuel shortage, though it cannot prove the poem’s intended meaning.

Claim and Organization

Claim: The poem turns an ordinary effort to see through frost into an image of connection that leaves evidence but does not guarantee contact.

  1. Repeated clearing establishes effort.
  2. The dark house limits certainty.
  3. The handprint makes absence physically visible.
  4. Historical context sharpens the material stakes without determining the interpretation.

Modeled Synthesis

“Scholar A’s discussion of windows as barriers helps explain the repeated frost, but the final handprint complicates a simple separation reading. The speaker leaves a temporary trace on the barrier itself. The fuel-shortage context makes cold materially significant, yet the poem’s imagery—not biography alone—supports the claim.”

Counterinterpretation and Visual

A reader could interpret the handprint as proof of persistence rather than failed connection. That reading is plausible; however, the unlit house and disappearing warmth make the gesture temporary. Display the final line alone beside a labeled diagram of image, connotation, and claim; include source labels and alternative text.

Delivery Notes

[Pause] after the final quotation. [Point to “kept”] and explain its tension. Slow down for the counterinterpretation. Conclude by restating significance rather than repeating the thesis word for word.
