Independent Work: Presenting “The Clockmaker’s Garden”

Text and Research Set

In a short story, apprentice Nia restores a garden clock that has stopped at 4:17, the hour a factory fire changed the town. Each repair makes the mechanism more accurate, but townspeople disagree about whether it should run again. The final scene shows children planting beans while the clock begins to strike.

Scholar Patel argues that repaired objects in postindustrial fiction expose conflict between remembrance and renewal. Historian Greene documents the town’s actual factory fire but warns that the fictional characters are invented. A museum essay explains that memorial clocks can preserve a public event while also regulating ordinary civic life.

Questions 1–10: Analysis and Sources

  1. Write an interpretive claim.
  2. Identify two textual details and explain their significance.
  3. Define postindustrial in context; verify it with a reputable source.
  4. Evaluate each secondary source’s relevance and limitation.
  5. Explain why the historical source cannot prove authorial intent.
  6. Synthesize Patel and the museum essay around one idea.
  7. Frame and analyze the final image.
  8. Develop a plausible counterinterpretation.
  9. Explain why your claim accounts for more evidence.
  10. State the broader significance of the interpretation.

Questions 11–18: Presentation Plan

  1. Create a six-part oral outline.
  2. Write three signposts.
  3. Write two accurate oral citations.
  4. Plan two readable visuals with source labels and alternative text.
  5. Create five delivery cues.
  6. Write a 220–280 word presentation excerpt integrating the primary text and two secondary sources.
  7. Annotate where synthesis and counterinterpretation occur.
  8. Complete a source-integrity, citation, accessibility, and final-delivery check.
