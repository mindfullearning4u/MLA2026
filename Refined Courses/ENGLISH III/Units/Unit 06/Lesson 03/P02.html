P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook title: Presenting Literary Research

Vocabulary

Literary research uses primary and secondary sources to answer a question about literature. Interpretation is a defensible explanation of meaning. Synthesis combines source information and your analysis. Oral explanation presents ideas in language listeners can understand.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Begin with a research question or interpretation.

Step 2: Select one primary-text detail and one source idea that support the interpretation.

Step 3: Paraphrase source information for oral delivery.

Step 4: Explain how the evidence supports your claim before moving to the next point.

Notebook Task

Write one literary interpretation you could present orally. Then list one primary-text detail and one secondary-source idea that would support it.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Presenting literary research and complex analysis

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.C.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Begin with the interpretive question or claim. In English III, research should help explain literature, not simply collect facts about an author or time period.
  2. Separate primary evidence from secondary-source support. Primary evidence comes from the literary text; secondary support comes from commentary, context, or scholarly explanation.
  3. Synthesize instead of stacking quotations. Explain how the source idea changes, deepens, or supports your interpretation of the text.
  4. Check that every source detail connects back to the literary claim. If the connection is not clear, add reasoning or choose stronger evidence.
