📘 ENG3 | Unit 06 | Lesson 07

P04 Worked Example

Use a final-readiness process for multimedia publication.

Example 1: Audience Check

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Product: A published research summary for classmates.

Step 1: Ask what classmates need first. They need the claim, short context, and evidence.

Step 2: Remove wording that assumes the reader already knows the research.

Result: The writing begins with a clear lead sentence and then explains the evidence.

Example 2: Multimedia Check

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Product: The page includes an image of the text's setting.

Step 1: Ask whether the image helps explain the claim.

Step 2: Add a caption that connects the image to the research summary.

Result: The image supports understanding instead of acting as decoration.

Example 3: Export Check

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Product: The writing is ready to publish to a LAN page.

Step 1: Check title, paragraph breaks, captions, source notes, and conventions.

Step 2: Preview the product as the audience will see it.

Result: The student catches formatting and clarity problems before submitting.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect Example:

My writing, images, and presentation notes are all finished separately, so I can publish without checking whether they work together.

This is incorrect because publication-ready communication requires integration. Separate finished parts may still confuse the audience if they do not connect.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct Thinking

Correct Example:

Before publishing, I will check that the writing, research, multimedia, and final format all support the same purpose for the same audience.

This is correct because it treats publication as a complete communication product, not a collection of separate tasks.
