P04 Worked Example

Use rhetorical devices effectively in oral presentation.

Example 1: Repetition

Line: We revise for clarity, we revise for accuracy, and we revise for readers.

Effect: Repetition emphasizes the purpose of revision.

Example 2: Rhetorical Question

Line: If readers cannot follow our ideas, how can they trust our conclusions?

Effect: The question makes listeners think about clarity and trust.

Example 3: Contrast

Line: A rushed draft may show an idea, but a revised draft communicates that idea clearly.

Effect: The contrast shows why revision matters.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Use five rhetorical questions in a row because they sound dramatic.

Too many devices can distract listeners from the evidence and focus.

Correct: Use one rhetorical question at a key moment to help listeners reflect on the presentation focus.

This keeps the device purposeful and audience-centered.
