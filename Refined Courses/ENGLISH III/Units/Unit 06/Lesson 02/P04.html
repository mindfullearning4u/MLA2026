Worked Example: Revising a Speech for Purposeful Rhetoric

Draft and Evidence

Task: Recommend a one-year city tree-canopy pilot. A municipal heat study found that blocks with less shade averaged 4.2°F warmer on sampled summer afternoons. The study measured 18 blocks and warns that building materials and traffic also affect temperature. A budget memo estimates the pilot at $96,000.

Flat draft: “The city should plant trees in selected low-shade blocks. The study found those blocks were hotter. A pilot would cost money, but it might help.”

Step 1 — Preserve the Qualified Claim

The evidence supports testing targeted shade, not promising that trees alone will remove heat differences. The revised claim should name the pilot, location, and measurement plan.

Step 2 — Add Functional Devices

Anaphora and tricolon: “Measure the heat. Plant where shade is scarce. Measure again.” This emphasizes a testable process.

Antithesis: “This is not a promise that one intervention will cool every street; it is a practical test of where added shade can help.” The contrast protects accuracy.

Analogy: “Treat the pilot as a field test, not a blank check.” The analogy clarifies limits but remains supported by budget and evaluation details.

Step 3 — Integrate Evidence

“The city’s heat study found that 18 low-shade blocks averaged 4.2 degrees warmer during sampled summer afternoons. Researchers also caution that traffic and building materials affect temperature. That limitation is exactly why the city should test, measure, and report rather than overpromise.”

Step 4 — Delivery and Final Version

[Slow pace] State the 4.2-degree result. [Pause] before the limitation. [Emphasize] “test, measure, and report.”

“Measure the heat. Plant where shade is scarce. Measure again. The city’s study found that 18 low-shade blocks averaged 4.2 degrees warmer on sampled summer afternoons, though researchers note that traffic and building materials also matter. This is not a promise that one intervention will cool every street; it is a practical test of where shade can help. Fund the one-year pilot, publish its costs and temperature results, and let evidence—not a slogan—determine what follows.”

Why It Works

The devices make the reasoning memorable while the oral citation, limitation, cost awareness, and measurable recommendation preserve credibility.
