Independent Work: Rhetorical Review of “The River Archive”

Directions

Read the complete source set and answer all 18 questions. Verify the passage-specific vocabulary when directed. Then produce and justify a rhetorical revision that remains evidence-based and ethical.

Speaking Situation and Evidence

A county commission will decide whether to fund a digital archive of river-level records, photographs, and oral histories. A university hydrologist reports that 82 years of gauge records can help researchers compare floods, but warns that early instruments were less precise. A historical society has cataloged 1,400 photographs, although many dates remain uncertain. Residents contributed 63 recorded interviews; participation was voluntary, and the interviews reflect memory rather than measurement. The proposed two-year archive would cost $210,000 and include public transcripts, image descriptions, searchable data, and a process for correcting metadata.

A student speaker drafts this opening: “What kind of community throws its own memory into the current? We must save every voice, preserve every image, and trust every number before history washes away forever.” The language is memorable, but several claims exceed the evidence.

Part A — Meaning, Credibility, and Passage Vocabulary

  1. State the decision and a defensible controlling idea.
  2. Identify the strengths and limitations of each evidence type.
  3. Using context, define metadata. Verify the definition with a reputable online dictionary or encyclopedia and record the source title.
  4. Which claim could the gauge records support?
  5. Which claim would overstate the oral histories?

Part B — Rhetorical Analysis

  1. Identify the rhetorical question, anaphora, and tricolon in the draft.
  2. Explain the intended effect of the river metaphor.
  3. Why is “every voice” inaccurate?
  4. Why is “trust every number” rhetorically forceful but ethically weak?
  5. Explain how kairos could be established without inventing a crisis.

Part C — Revision and Delivery

  1. Write a revised opening that keeps one purposeful device and corrects the overclaims.
  2. Add an antithesis that clarifies a genuine choice.
  3. Write two qualified oral citations.
  4. Create a parallel three-part statement that describes the archive’s actual work.
  5. Add five delivery cues and explain one.

Part D — Extended Speaking Copy

  1. Write a 200–260 word speech section that integrates three sources, uses at least three purposeful rhetorical devices, and acknowledges two limitations.
  2. Annotate each device by naming its function and explaining why it is ethical.
  3. Complete a source-integrity and final-delivery check.
