Worked Example: A Focused Community Proposal

Speaking Task and Source Set

Task: Deliver a four-minute recommendation about whether the town library should remain open two evenings each week.

Source A: A 2025 library report shows that 38% of weekday computer sessions occur during the final hour before closing. The report records use but does not identify every user’s purpose.

Source B: A chamber-of-commerce survey of 22 businesses reports that 15 employers direct job applicants to the library for online forms. The small, voluntary sample limits generalization.

Source C: In an interview, the library director estimates that two later evenings would cost $18,400 annually and says a six-month pilot could measure demand.

Step 1 — Controlling Idea

Claim: The town should fund a six-month trial of two evening openings because current usage and employer reports indicate a need, while a limited pilot would test whether the benefit justifies the cost.

This claim is focused, acknowledges uncertainty, and proposes a measurable action.

Step 2 — Organization and Signposts

  1. Opening: a job application deadline does not move because the library closes.
  2. Reason 1: existing late-hour computer use suggests unmet demand.
  3. Reason 2: employers rely on library access for applicants.
  4. Concession: the business survey is small and the program costs money.
  5. Response: a six-month pilot limits risk and gathers better evidence.
  6. Conclusion: approve the trial and publish usage results.

Step 3 — Evidence With Oral Citation

Model: “According to the library’s 2025 usage report, 38 percent of weekday computer sessions occur in the final hour before closing. That figure does not prove why every visitor needs access, but it shows that computers remain heavily used when the building closes.”

The speaker attributes the source, gives the result, states its limitation, and explains relevance.

Step 4 — Delivery Plan

[Pause] after the opening example. [Show one bar chart] for hourly computer use. Slow down when stating the cost. Emphasize “six-month trial” and “publish the results.” Use note-card phrases rather than a full script.

Model Closing

“The present evidence supports a test, not a permanent promise. A six-month evening schedule would give applicants and other residents more access while allowing the town to measure attendance and cost. Approve the pilot, report the results publicly, and let stronger evidence guide the next decision.”
