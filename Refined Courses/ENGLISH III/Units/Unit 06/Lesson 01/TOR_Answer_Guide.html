Answer Guide: Oral Communication Organization, Focus, and Credible Evidence

Independent Work Guidance

  1. A defensible position may support a limited pilot focused on amplified sound and prolonged idling, with safety exemptions and public reporting; other positions are acceptable when evidence is represented accurately.
  2. Prolonged means continuing for a long time or longer than usual; accept a verified reputable source.
  3. Source A is comparatively strong because its method and data are transparent, but it shows association rather than single-cause proof. Source B has voluntary-response and wording limitations. Source C best supports enforceability. Source D supplies a useful concession but is one person’s perspective.
  4. Strong oral citations identify the university study, marina survey, harbor memorandum, or resident testimony and state relevant limitations.
  5. The 180–230 word excerpt should include a focused claim, explained evidence, a concession, and audible organization. Delivery cues and the integrity check must be specific.

Checkpoint Guidance

  1. A strong claim supports a four-month evening-hours pilot followed by public evaluation, while acknowledging cost and evidence limits.
  2. The special events suggest demand but included free admission and performances. The visitor survey does not represent nonvisitors.
  3. Isolate means to separate one factor from others for examination; accept a verified reputable source.
  4. Oral citations must qualify the finance estimate and survey. The excerpt should remain within 120–160 words and integrate two sources.
  5. Useful visuals show comparable attendance or a clear cost breakdown; misleading visuals omit differing conditions or truncate scales.

Mastery and Feedback

Use the student-visible rubrics. Require 80% mastery. Below 80%, provide specific TOR feedback and require correction and resubmission.
