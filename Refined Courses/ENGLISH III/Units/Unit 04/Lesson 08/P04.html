Worked Example: A Complete Literary Investigation

Model Passage

Every evening, Samin wound the abandoned courthouse clock although the town had replaced it with a digital display in the square. The old clock lost seven minutes each week. Its western face had darkened, and swallows nested behind the numeral four.

When asked why he maintained an inaccurate instrument, Samin said the clock had testified in more disputes than any judge. During the textile strike, workers timed their marches by it; during the blackout, volunteers rang its bell by hand. “It has been wrong in useful ways,” he said.

The council voted to remove the mechanism but preserve the tower. On the final night, Samin stopped winding at eleven fifty-three—the precise error the clock had accumulated. At midnight, the digital display changed without sound. Across the square, several residents looked up at the silent tower anyway.

1. Interpretive Question

How does the story use an inaccurate clock to distinguish measured accuracy from public meaning?

2. Textual Evidence Set

  • The clock loses exactly seven minutes each week.
  • Its physical deterioration coexists with nesting swallows.
  • Workers and volunteers used it during collective events.
  • Samin stops at the exact accumulated error.
  • Residents still look toward the silent tower.

3. Narrow Research Needs

Civic history: Public clocks historically coordinated work, transportation, and civic routines.

Labor history: Control of time and schedules can carry power in industrial settings.

Technology: Digital accuracy can replace a mechanism’s function without replacing its social symbolism.

Appropriate sources might include a museum of technology, labor-history archive, or university history publication.

4. Synthesis Matrix in Prose

The civic-history source confirms the clock’s coordinating role. Labor history extends the strike reference by showing why shared time could matter politically. Technology history complicates nostalgia: the new display performs measurement better, so the interpretation cannot claim that old machinery is practically superior.

5. Claim and Line of Reasoning

Claim: The passage argues that an object can lose practical authority while retaining civic meaning created through shared use.

Reasoning: The clock’s inaccuracy is openly acknowledged, preventing a simple celebration of obsolete technology. Its historical uses and the residents’ final gesture show that collective memory attaches meaning to more than precision. Samin’s exact stopping time unites error and accuracy, suggesting that responsible remembrance must recognize both.

6. Counterinterpretation and Qualification

Counterinterpretation: The residents’ upward glance may show mere habit rather than meaningful remembrance.

Evaluation: Habit remains plausible, but the story’s placement of strike, blackout, and final silence gives the gesture a public-history context.

Qualified conclusion: The story does not prove that preserving the mechanism is wise; it suggests that replacing a function does not automatically replace the meanings communities built around it.
