Independent Work: Synthesizing “The Last Public Well”

Directions

Read the literary passage and source notes. Answer all 18 questions, including passage-vocabulary verification and a source-supported synthesis. Submit with Page 2 vocabulary.

Literary Passage

The town sealed the last public well after tests showed salt in the water. Nera continued to visit it each morning, lowering an empty bucket until the rope touched stone. “A useless ritual,” the mayor said. Nera answered, “A measurement.”

She recorded the depth, temperature, and sound of the bucket against the wall. Children began bringing notebooks. Months later, when rain returned, the well remained unsafe, but Nera’s records revealed a rising water table beneath the abandoned square. The town planted reeds there instead of paving it. The well never reopened. Its measurements changed what the town believed could return.

Source Notes

  • A: Municipal report on salinity and water-table measurements.
  • B: Scholarly article on wells as symbols of communal memory.
  • C: Environmental-history essay on citizen observation after infrastructure failure.
  • D: Unsupported blog declaring that wells always symbolize hope.

Questions 1–6

  1. Infer salinity, verify it, and record the source.
  2. State a provisional interpretation.
  3. Identify two details supporting it.
  4. Summarize each credible source’s relevant claim.
  5. Explain why D is excluded.
  6. Identify one limitation of each credible source.

Questions 7–12

  1. Explain how A supports or complicates the text.
  2. Explain how B extends the symbolic analysis.
  3. Explain how C changes the interpretation of Nera’s ritual.
  4. Identify one point of tension among the sources.
  5. Group evidence into two conceptual categories.
  6. Write two signal phrases introducing sources accurately.

Questions 13–18

  1. Paraphrase one source claim without copying structure.
  2. Identify counterevidence in the passage.
  3. Revise the interpretation to account for it.
  4. Explain why the well’s failure matters to the ending.
  5. Write a source-relationship map.
  6. Write a six-sentence synthesis using the passage and at least two sources.
