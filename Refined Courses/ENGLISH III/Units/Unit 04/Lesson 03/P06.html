Checkpoint: “The Borrowed Bell”

Directions

Read the passage and source notes. Answer all 12 questions and submit separately.

Passage

After the school bell cracked, the village borrowed a ship’s bell from the harbor museum. Its low sound traveled farther than the old bell, reaching farms beyond the ridge. At first, students joked that every lesson now began with a departure. By spring, families were using the sound to coordinate deliveries during washed-out roads.

When the museum asked for the bell back, the school returned it and installed a smaller one. The new bell could barely reach the gate. Students still paused at its sound, listening for the distance it no longer crossed.

Sources

  • A scholarly essay on borrowed objects and communal identity.
  • A transportation history describing seasonal road isolation.
