P04 Worked Example

Study how to synthesize two sources instead of simply placing them next to each other.

Worked Example Source Set

Literary text: The speaker returns to a closed train station and notices ticket windows covered with dust, but the waiting room benches are smooth from years of travelers resting there.

Research source: Scholars often describe transit spaces in literature as symbols of transition, loss, and connection because they hold evidence of people moving through shared public spaces.

Example 1: Identify the Interpretation

The station represents memory and loss because it is closed, but it still carries signs of the people who passed through it.

Example 2: Paraphrase Each Source

The literary text shows a station that is no longer active but still marked by travelers. The research source explains that transit spaces can represent transition, loss, and shared connection.

Example 3: Synthesize the Sources

The closed station supports an interpretation about memory and loss because its dusty windows show abandonment, while its smooth benches show repeated human presence. The research source strengthens this interpretation by explaining that transit spaces often represent both movement and connection.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The station is dusty. Transit spaces are symbols in literature.

Why it is weak: The sentences sit beside each other, but they do not explain how the source ideas connect.

Correct: The dusty station and smooth benches suggest a place shaped by absence and memory, and the research source helps explain why transit spaces can symbolize loss and connection.

Why it is correct: The sentence uses both sources to support one interpretation.
