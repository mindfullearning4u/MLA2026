Synthesizing Sources to Support Text Interpretations

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.RS.01 - Conduct literary research to answer a question, refining scope to align with interpretations of texts and synthesizing information from primary and secondary sources.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.10, MLA.ENG3.W.02, MLA.ENG3.L.01

What You Will Learn

You will learn how to combine evidence from a literary text with information from research sources so the sources work together to support one interpretation.

A synthesis is not a list of source facts. A synthesis explains how sources connect and how they help answer the research question.

Why This Matters

Strong literary research uses more than one source carefully. You show mastery when you can explain what the literary text shows, what the research source adds, and how both sources support the same interpretation.

What You Will Do

  • Read short primary and secondary source excerpts.
  • Paraphrase the main idea of a source accurately.
  • Connect research evidence to a literary interpretation.
  • Practice writing synthesis statements that use standard English conventions.

What Mastery Looks Like

You can answer a literary research question by using a primary source and a secondary source together. Your response explains the connection between the sources instead of dropping evidence into a paragraph without analysis.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection: I can use research sources and literary evidence together to support my interpretation of a text.

Ask TOR for Help: If you cannot tell how two sources connect, ask TOR for help naming the shared idea before you write your synthesis.
