P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Building a Literary Analysis Paragraph

Vocabulary

Claim: A clear interpretation that can be supported with evidence.

Reasoning: The explanation of why the evidence supports the claim.

Evidence: Details from the literary text or source that prove the claim.

Elaboration: Additional explanation that shows deeper understanding of the literary element or meaning.

Literary Passage

The cracked map hung behind the mayor's desk. Its rivers were faded almost white, but one road remained dark from years of fingers tracing the route out of town. When Mara looked at it, she did not see escape. She saw everyone who had once believed leaving was the only way to begin.

Notebook Directions

Step 1: Write one claim about how the map develops meaning in the passage.

Step 2: Copy or paraphrase one piece of evidence from the passage.

Step 3: Write one reasoning sentence that explains why the evidence supports the claim.

Model claim frame: The map develops the idea that...

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Literary analysis claims, reasoning, evidence, and elaboration

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.W.02

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Identify the speaker or writer, audience, purpose, and occasion before judging the argument or rhetoric. These details shape what the choices are meant to do.
  2. Separate claim, evidence, reasoning, and rhetorical technique. A strong analysis names the move and explains how it affects the audience.
  3. Evaluate effectiveness, not just presence. Ask whether the structure, appeal, diction, repetition, or contrast actually helps the author achieve the purpose.
  4. Use precise academic language such as claim, validity, reasoning, appeal, effect, audience, and purpose so your analysis sounds mature and defensible.
