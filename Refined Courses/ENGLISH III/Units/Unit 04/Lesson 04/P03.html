Direct Teaching: Making Reasoning Visible

The C-E-W-E Chain

  1. Claim: precise, interpretive, qualified.
  2. Evidence: relevant textual detail or credible source evidence.
  3. Warrant: why the evidence supports the claim.
  4. Elaboration: significance, pattern, implication, limitation, or counterevidence.

Example — Weak to Strong

Weak: The empty chair symbolizes loss.

Stronger: Because the narrator repeatedly repairs but never sits in the empty chair, it represents grief maintained as a duty rather than merely an absence.

Evidence Is Not Self-Explaining

A quotation shows what the text says. Commentary must identify the relevant wording, pattern, contrast, or structural position and explain the inferential step.

Elaboration Moves

  • Trace a pattern across scenes.
  • Explain a contrast or shift.
  • Connect form to purpose.
  • State an implication.
  • Qualify for counterevidence.
  • Use a source to extend or complicate close reading.

Common Mistakes

  • Plot summary after evidence.
  • “This shows” without reasoning.
  • Evidence chosen for vividness rather than relevance.
  • Overclaiming author intention.
  • Ignoring contradictory details.
  • Using outside research as a substitute for analysis.
