Independent Work: “The Bridge Ledger”

Directions

Read the passage and answer all 18 questions. Develop a qualified claim, select evidence, make warrants explicit, address counterevidence, and elaborate. Verify passage vocabulary and record the source. Submit with Page 2 vocabulary.

The Bridge Ledger

For forty years, Orin recorded every repair to the river bridge in a blue ledger. He listed bolts, boards, storms, and labor hours, but never the names of workers. “A bridge remembers weight, not hands,” he told his daughter Vale.

After a flood removed the eastern span, Vale organized the rebuilding crew. She kept the ledger but added a second column titled “Who Carried It.” Beside each repair she wrote names, injuries, borrowed tools, meals delivered, and arguments resolved. Orin objected that stories made the record imprecise.

When inspectors arrived, Vale gave them both columns. The measurements proved the bridge was safe; the names showed which families had supplied unpaid labor after public funds ran out. The council approved reimbursement. Orin later added one line beneath his oldest entry: “Weight includes what a town asks people to carry.”

Questions 1–6

  1. Infer reimbursement, verify it, and record the source.
  2. State a qualified interpretive claim.
  3. Identify two details supporting it.
  4. Identify one detail that complicates it.
  5. Explain the significance of the two columns.
  6. Analyze Orin’s final statement.

Questions 7–12

  1. Write a warrant connecting the unnamed workers to your claim.
  2. Write a warrant connecting the inspectors’ decision to your claim.
  3. Evaluate the relevance of the ledger details.
  4. Evaluate whether the evidence is sufficient.
  5. Explain how the ending changes the opening belief.
  6. Qualify your claim to address Orin’s concern about precision.

Questions 13–18

  1. Integrate one short quotation with a signal phrase.
  2. Paraphrase another detail accurately.
  3. Write two sentences of elaboration after each.
  4. State one implication for public records.
  5. Create a five-step reasoning chain.
  6. Write a six-sentence analytical paragraph.
