The Unsent Letters

In the museum archive, Ana found six letters her great-aunt had addressed but never mailed. Five ended with careful explanations of why she had left the mountain village for the city. The sixth contained only a date and the sentence, “I have learned that departure can be another form of staying.”

Ana first treated the letters as family history. Then she noticed that each description of the city borrowed language from the village: train smoke became “weather trapped indoors,” apartment windows became “terraces without soil,” and factory whistles became “metal shepherds.” The city was never described on its own terms.

The archive catalog identified the letters as evidence of migration. Ana wondered whether they documented departure, resistance to departure, or an attempt to carry one landscape inside another. She wrote three questions in her notebook, crossed out two that could be answered by dates, and circled the one that required her to reread every metaphor.
