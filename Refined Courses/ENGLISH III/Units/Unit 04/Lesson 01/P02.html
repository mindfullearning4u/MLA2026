P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Set up your notebook for literary research question development.

Notebook Title

Literary Research Questions and Interpretive Focus

Vocabulary

  • Literary research: Investigation that helps answer a question about a literary text.
  • Interpretation: A defensible explanation of what a text means or how it works.
  • Research question: A focused question that guides inquiry.
  • Scope: The size and limits of the question.
  • Refine: To make a question clearer, narrower, and more useful.

Direct Teaching

A literary research question should not ask only for a basic fact. A fact question might be answered in one sentence. A research question should help you investigate an interpretation of the text.

Too broad: What is justice in literature?

Too factual: Who is the speaker in the poem?

Focused and interpretive: How does the speaker's repeated image of locked doors develop the idea that justice requires public access?

Notebook Setup

Create a four-row chart:

  1. Broad topic
  2. Textual detail or problem
  3. Possible interpretation
  4. Focused research question

Leave space under the chart to revise one weak question into a stronger one.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Literary research questions and interpretive focus

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.RS.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Begin with the interpretive question or claim. In English III, research should help explain literature, not simply collect facts about an author or time period.
  2. Separate primary evidence from secondary-source support. Primary evidence comes from the literary text; secondary support comes from commentary, context, or scholarly explanation.
  3. Synthesize instead of stacking quotations. Explain how the source idea changes, deepens, or supports your interpretation of the text.
  4. Check that every source detail connects back to the literary claim. If the connection is not clear, add reasoning or choose stronger evidence.
