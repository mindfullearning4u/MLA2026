P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Set up your notebook for literary research question development.

Notebook Title

Literary Research Questions and Interpretive Focus

Vocabulary

  • Literary research: Investigation that helps answer a question about a literary text.
  • Interpretation: A defensible explanation of what a text means or how it works.
  • Research question: A focused question that guides inquiry.
  • Scope: The size and limits of the question.
  • Refine: To make a question clearer, narrower, and more useful.

Direct Teaching

A literary research question should not ask only for a basic fact. A fact question might be answered in one sentence. A research question should help you investigate an interpretation of the text.

Too broad: What is justice in literature?

Too factual: Who is the speaker in the poem?

Focused and interpretive: How does the speaker's repeated image of locked doors develop the idea that justice requires public access?

Notebook Setup

Create a four-row chart:

  1. Broad topic
  2. Textual detail or problem
  3. Possible interpretation
  4. Focused research question

Leave space under the chart to revise one weak question into a stronger one.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Review the vocabulary and identify the skill being practiced.
  2. Step 2: Read the model or passage carefully before answering.
  3. Step 3: Find the exact evidence, word, sentence, or structural choice that supports your answer.
  4. Step 4: Explain how that evidence connects to meaning, effect, or purpose.
  5. Step 5: Check the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: work one step at a time. Standard path: complete the whole task with evidence and explanation. Accelerated path: compare alternatives or add a second evidence-based explanation.
