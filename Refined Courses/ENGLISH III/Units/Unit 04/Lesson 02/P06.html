Checkpoint: Source Decisions for “Salt Lines”

Directions

Read the poem excerpt and source descriptions. Answer all 12 questions and submit them separately.

Poem Excerpt

Grandmother drew salt lines / across every windowsill, / not to keep storms outside / but to teach the house / where the sea had been.

Years later I sweep them away, / then taste the dust on my hand— / a coast reduced to a room, / a warning reduced to habit, / a habit returning as thirst.

Candidate Sources

  • A coastal-history museum essay with maps and cited oral histories.
  • A peer-reviewed article about domestic ritual and environmental memory in poetry.
  • A travel influencer’s post listing “ten magical salt traditions,” without sources.
  • A recorded interview with a regional resident describing storm rituals, archived by a university.

Mastery Questions

  1. Infer ritual from the source-set context.
  2. Write a focused interpretive question.
  3. Classify each source by function.
  4. Which source offers primary contextual evidence? Explain.
  5. Which source offers secondary literary analysis?
  6. Evaluate the museum essay’s authority and relevance.
  7. Identify a limitation of the recorded interview.
  8. Explain why the influencer post is unsuitable.
  9. Select two sources that answer different subquestions.
  10. Name evidence needed to corroborate one contextual claim.
  11. Explain how the sources could qualify an interpretation of the salt image.
  12. Write a four-sentence source-use plan.
