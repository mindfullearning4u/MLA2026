Independent Work: Source Set for “The Clockmaker’s Daughter”

Directions

Read the literary excerpt and four source descriptions. Answer all 18 questions. Infer passage vocabulary, verify it with a reputable online dictionary or encyclopedia, and record the source. Submit with Page 2 vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Literary Excerpt

When Elara inherited the clock shop, she stopped every clock at 4:17—the minute recorded on her father’s final repair ticket. Customers complained that a shop of silent clocks could not be trusted. Elara replied that accuracy was not the same as obedience.

In the back room she found designs for a public clock that would lose one minute each day, then recover all seven minutes every Sunday at noon. Her father’s note called the delay “a small weekly debt paid in public.” Elara rebuilt the mechanism and installed it above the railway platform. Travelers first mocked its hesitation. Months later, they began arriving early on Sundays to watch the hands leap forward together.

Source Set

  1. A: Digitized municipal records showing factory time regulations and railway schedules; government archive with document images.
  2. B: Peer-reviewed article on clocks, labor discipline, and resistance in industrial fiction; extensive textual citations.
  3. C: Clock company advertisement claiming mechanical precision creates moral character; dated 1910.
  4. D: Anonymous webpage stating the story is “about time healing everything”; no author or citations.
