Lesson Overview: Primary and Secondary Sources in Literary Research

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.W.08 — Locate, evaluate, and use credible primary and secondary sources appropriate to a literary research question.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.01 — Cite relevant textual evidence; MLA.ENG3.R.09 — Analyze relationships among texts and sources; MLA.ENG3.W.07 — Conduct focused research; MLA.ENG3.V.01 — Apply academic and passage-specific vocabulary; MLA.ENG3.L.01 — Apply standard English conventions.

Lesson Focus

Distinguish source types by function, evaluate authority and limitations, and select evidence that genuinely advances a literary interpretation.

What You Will Learn

  • Classify literary texts, manuscripts, letters, reviews, scholarship, databases, and reference works by function.
  • Distinguish primary/secondary from credible/not credible.
  • Evaluate authority, evidence, context, currency, purpose, and relevance.
  • Use sources to support, complicate, or qualify an interpretation.
  • Document source information for later citation.

Why This Matters

A source can be accurate yet irrelevant, useful yet limited, or authoritative for one question but not another. Independent researchers must evaluate both what a source knows and what the analysis needs.
