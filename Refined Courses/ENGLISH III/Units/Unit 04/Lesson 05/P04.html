P04 Worked Example

Study how a writer keeps the literary element at the center of a source-supported paragraph.

Worked Example Passage

The old gate did not open, but roses climbed through its iron bars. Lena stood outside, smelling the flowers she could not reach.

Source note: Gates in literature often symbolize boundaries, while flowers can suggest beauty, growth, or desire.

Example 1: Name the Element

The gate and roses work as symbols. The gate represents a boundary, and the roses represent beauty or desire beyond that boundary.

Example 2: Use Source Support

The source note helps explain why the gate matters as more than a physical object. It supports the idea that Lena is separated from something she wants.

Example 3: Revise for Clarity

Before: The gate and roses show Lena wants something.

After: The locked gate symbolizes a boundary between Lena and the beauty she desires, while the roses growing through the bars make that desire visible but unreachable.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The roses are pretty and the gate is old, so Lena likes gardens.

Why it is weak: This guesses about Lena and does not analyze the symbols.

Correct: The roses and locked gate create a contrast between beauty and restriction, showing Lena's desire for something she cannot reach.

Why it is correct: This explains how the literary elements work together to create meaning.
