P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Conventions That Clarify Analysis

Vocabulary

Sentence boundary: The place where one complete sentence ends and another begins.

Run-on sentence: Two or more complete sentences joined without correct punctuation.

Comma splice: Two complete sentences joined only by a comma.

Fragment: An incomplete sentence that is missing a subject, verb, or complete thought.

Analytical Draft

The author uses the empty station as a symbol it shows public memory because the benches are worn by many travelers. The source explains that transit spaces can represent movement and connection, this supports the interpretation.

Notebook Directions

Step 1: Copy the draft into your notebook.

Step 2: Underline the two sentence-boundary problems.

Step 3: Rewrite the draft as two or three correct sentences.

Step 4: Circle one academic vocabulary word that helps the analysis sound precise.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Standard English conventions in complex analytical writing

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.L.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 11 thinking process explicit so you do not have to guess what the skill requires.

  1. Read the draft as an outside reader. Mark where the claim, reasoning, organization, sentence control, or style becomes unclear.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: first meaning, then organization, then transitions, then sentence precision, then conventions.
  3. Keep the original analytical purpose unless the task asks for a new claim. Revision should strengthen the argument, explanation, or analysis.
  4. Reread the revised version and ask whether the audience can follow the reasoning without extra explanation.
