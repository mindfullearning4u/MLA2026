Literary Passage

For thirty-seven winters, Irena recorded the first night the northern lights appeared above Halden Ridge. She did not claim to predict them. Her ledger contained dates, temperatures, cloud cover, and brief descriptions: “green curtain,” “faint red seam,” “nothing visible, though the magnet trembled.”

When the university announced that a digital observatory would replace her hilltop station, reporters described the new instruments as incapable of fatigue. Irena agreed that this was an advantage. She had once slept through an alarm and missed an entire display. Yet she wondered which instrument would record the silence of the families who climbed the ridge together or the way children lowered their voices when the sky began to move.

On the station’s final night, clouds covered the ridge. The university technician apologized, as if weather had failed to cooperate with the ceremony. Irena opened her oldest ledger and showed him a page marked only “obscured.” Beneath the word, someone had later drawn seven small figures holding hands.

“Your data was edited,” the technician said.

“Expanded,” Irena replied.

At midnight, the new observatory began collecting measurements from beyond the clouds. Irena closed the ledger but did not lock it away. She left it on the public table beside the screen where numbers arrived faster than anyone could read them.

Student Analysis to Edit

The passage contrasts scientific accuracy with human observation, it does not argue that one must replace the other. Irena admits missing a display which makes her unreliable in one sense but her detailed ledgers shows discipline. The universities instruments are described as “incapable of fatigue” this phrase personifies the technology and also emphasizes its advantage. However the new system can measure beyond the clouds, the ledger contains community memory. When the technician says the data was edited Irena answers “Expanded.” Her reply suggest that human additions may deepen a record rather than corrupting it, although the drawing is not scientific data it documents an experience around the observation. By placing the ledger beside the digital screen, both types of record is made available and invites readers to compare them.
