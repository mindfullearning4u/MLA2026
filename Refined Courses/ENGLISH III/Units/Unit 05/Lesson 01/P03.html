Lesson: Diagnose Meaning Before Correcting Form

A Five-Pass Editing Method

  1. Find the sentence core: locate each independent clause and its main subject–verb pair.
  2. Name the relationship: decide whether ideas are equal, dependent, contrasting, causal, illustrative, or qualifying.
  3. Select the structure: use coordination, subordination, a semicolon, a colon, or separate sentences to express that relationship.
  4. Check internal control: test agreement, pronoun reference, modifiers, parallelism, and verb consistency.
  5. Read for meaning and sound: confirm that the revision is grammatical and preserves the intended emphasis.

Example 1 — Boundary and Logic

Faulty: The narrator calls the town peaceful, the repeated locked doors suggest fear.

Revision: Although the narrator calls the town peaceful, the repeated locked doors suggest fear.

Why: Subordination expresses concession and prevents a comma splice.

Example 2 — Evidence Introduction

Faulty: The image supports the central idea, “the lantern remained lit after every window darkened.”

Revision: The image supports the central idea: “the lantern remained lit after every window darkened.”

Why: A colon follows a complete clause and announces directly related evidence.

Example 3 — Parallel Reasoning

Faulty: The committee values speed, keeping costs low, and that the process appears orderly.

Revision: The committee values speed, low costs, and an orderly appearance.

Why: Three coordinated objects now use matching noun phrases.

Example 4 — Pronoun Reference

Faulty: When Lena questions Mara’s interpretation, she becomes defensive.

Revision A: When Lena questions Mara’s interpretation, Mara becomes defensive.

Revision B: When Lena questions Mara’s interpretation, Lena becomes defensive.

Why: Grammar alone cannot determine the intended antecedent; the writer must name it.

Common Mistakes

  • Adding commas wherever speech pauses rather than where grammar requires them.
  • Breaking every long sentence into short ones and losing logical relationships.
  • Correcting grammar while accidentally changing the analytical claim.
  • Assuming a spell-checker can resolve agreement, reference, or meaning.
  • Trusting AI revisions without comparing them to the intended claim and textual evidence.

Digital tools may flag patterns, but the writer remains responsible for meaning, evidence, and final correctness.
