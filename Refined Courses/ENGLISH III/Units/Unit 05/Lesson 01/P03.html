P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Revise conventions in a longer analytical response.

Revision Checklist

1. Complete sentence: Does each sentence have a subject, a verb, and a complete thought?

2. Sentence boundary: Are complete thoughts separated with a period, semicolon, or comma plus coordinating conjunction?

3. Academic word choice: Are words like symbolizes, clarifies, develops, and emphasizes used correctly?

4. Final polish: Are capitalization, spelling, and punctuation correct?

Model Revision

Draft: The author uses the empty station as a symbol it shows public memory.

Revision: The author uses the empty station as a symbol; it shows public memory through the worn benches and stopped clock.

Why it works: The semicolon correctly connects two closely related complete thoughts.

Notebook Task

Write a four-sentence analytical paragraph about a symbol from a previous Unit 4 lesson. Then revise it using the checklist above.

Mark one sentence you revised for punctuation and one sentence you revised for academic vocabulary.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The image is important, it develops memory.

Why it is weak: This is a comma splice because two complete sentences are joined only by a comma.

Correct: The image is important because it develops memory.

Why it is correct: The revision creates one complete sentence with a clear relationship between ideas.

Teachable Explanation

Compare the incorrect and correct versions by checking the exact passage details, the lesson standard, and the meaning that must stay accurate.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Standard English conventions in complex analytical writing

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.L.01

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Read the draft as an outside reader. Mark where the claim, reasoning, organization, sentence control, or style becomes unclear.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: first meaning, then organization, then transitions, then sentence precision, then conventions.
  3. Keep the original analytical purpose unless the task asks for a new claim. Revision should strengthen the argument, explanation, or analysis.
  4. Reread the revised version and ask whether the audience can follow the reasoning without extra explanation.
