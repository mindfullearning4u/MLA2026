Worked Example: Revising a Complex Analytical Paragraph

Passage Being Analyzed

At the memorial meeting, the mayor reads every name quickly so the ceremony can end before the storm. Lina interrupts only once, asking him to repeat the final name. Thunder hides his answer, but the crowd remains seated.

Unrevised Student Paragraph

The scene presents public memory as efficient and also incomplete, the mayor reads the names quickly this makes the ceremony sound like a task. Lina interrupts to ask for one name repeated, which challenges the pace and it shows respect. When thunder hides the answer they remain seated. This detail suggest the community is beginning to listen rather than simply finishing the ceremony, the storm delays them but also creating time for attention.

Pass 1 — Mark Clause Boundaries

The first sentence contains two independent clauses joined only by a comma. The second includes loosely coordinated clauses whose pronoun reference is vague. The fourth contains another comma splice and inconsistent verb form.

Pass 2 — Preserve the Reasoning

The paragraph’s reasoning moves through contrast: the mayor treats remembrance as a task, Lina resists that pace, and the crowd’s stillness suggests a shift toward attention. The revision must preserve this progression rather than merely shorten sentences.

Pass 3 — Sentence-Level Revision

  • Boundary: Replace the first comma with a semicolon or add a subordinating word.
  • Reference: Replace vague which and it with a clear subject.
  • Agreement: Change This detail suggest to This detail suggests.
  • Parallel form: Change delays ... creating to delays ... creates.

Revised Paragraph

The scene presents public memory as both efficient and incomplete. Because the mayor reads the names quickly, the ceremony initially sounds like a task to finish rather than an act of attention. Lina’s request that he repeat the final name challenges that pace and insists that remembrance requires care. Although thunder hides the answer, the crowd remains seated. This detail suggests that the community is beginning to listen rather than simply complete the ceremony: the storm delays the participants, but it also creates time for attention.

Why the Revision Works

The revision corrects boundaries, agreement, reference, and parallelism while strengthening the original line of reasoning. Sentence variety now signals cause, contrast, and conclusion. No correction changes the evidence or invents a new claim.

Alternative Revision Choice

The final colon could be replaced by a semicolon if the writer wants less emphasis: The storm delays the participants; it also creates time for attention. Both versions are correct, but the colon presents the second clause as an explanation of the first.
