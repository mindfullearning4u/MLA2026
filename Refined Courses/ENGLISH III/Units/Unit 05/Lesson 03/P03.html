P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Use context and connotation to analyze tone.

Process

Step 1: Identify the key word.

Step 2: Define it literally.

Step 3: Name its connotation.

Step 4: Connect the connotation to tone.

Model

Sentence: The official dismissed the citizens' questions as chatter.

Analysis: Chatter denotes talk, but it connotes something unimportant or irritating. This word choice creates a dismissive tone toward the citizens.

Notebook Task

Analyze the tone created by these words: persistent, stubborn, frugal, stingy. For each word, write denotation, connotation, and tone effect.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Stubborn and persistent mean exactly the same thing.

Why it is weak: Their denotations are similar, but their connotations differ.

Correct: Persistent has a positive connotation of determination, while stubborn has a negative connotation of refusing to change.

Why it is correct: It separates literal meaning from emotional association.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Context, connotation, denotation, and tone

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.V.03

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Read the full sentence and surrounding sentences before deciding meaning. Advanced vocabulary often depends on context and tone.
  2. Use word parts, etymology, derivations, and related words when the meaning is not obvious from context alone.
  3. Separate denotation from connotation. In Grade 11 texts, a word may carry a historical, rhetorical, or emotional effect beyond its basic definition.
  4. Choose the meaning or word that best fits the author's purpose, audience, and tone.
