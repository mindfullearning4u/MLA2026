Context, Connotation, Denotation, and Tone

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG3.V.03 - Apply knowledge of context clues, figurative language, word relationships, reference materials, and background knowledge to determine the connotative and denotative meaning of words and phrases.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG3.R.09, MLA.ENG3.R.12, MLA.ENG3.V.01

What You Will Learn

You will learn how a word's dictionary meaning, emotional association, and sentence context work together to shape tone in literary and rhetorical texts.

Why This Matters

Writers choose words carefully. A word may have a neutral denotation but a positive or negative connotation that changes the tone of a sentence, speech, or poem.

What You Will Do

  • Separate denotation from connotation.
  • Use context clues to determine meaning.
  • Explain how word choice affects tone.
  • Apply academic vocabulary when discussing tone and meaning.

What Mastery Looks Like

You can explain what a word means literally, what it suggests emotionally, and how that word choice shapes tone.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection: I can use context and word meaning to explain tone.

Ask TOR for Help: If two words have similar definitions but different feelings, ask TOR for help naming each connotation.
