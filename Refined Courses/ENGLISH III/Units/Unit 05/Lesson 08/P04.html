Worked Example: Editing a Community History Article

Draft Passage

The old observatory was closed in 1998, and there were many residents who believed it had become useless. A student committee conducted an investigation of the building. The committee found notebooks showing that volunteer astronomers had recorded unusual storms and taught navigation classes during the 1940s. Because of the fact that these records existed, the students made a recommendation that the observatory should become an archive and public viewing room. “The dome does not only point upward; it points backward,” one student wrote. The proposal was approved quickly. The building now provides an opportunity for people to learn history and to look at the sky.

Step 1 — Preserve Meaning and Evidence

Claim: Research changed the community’s understanding of the observatory and gave it a new purpose. Essential evidence: the notebooks document weather observations and navigation classes; the quotation connects scientific and historical value.

Step 2 — Repair Reasoning and Organization

The draft jumps from discovery to approval. Add a sentence explaining that the notebooks supplied evidence for preservation, and qualify “approved quickly” unless a source confirms the timing.

Step 3 — Refine Language

Replace “conducted an investigation of” with “investigated,” “Because of the fact that” with “Because,” and “made a recommendation that” with “recommended.” Keep the student’s dome metaphor because it gives the article a memorable individual voice.

Step 4 — Verify and Publish

Check the 1998 date and quotation against the archive, attribute the student by name if permission allows, apply the publication style sheet, provide a descriptive heading and image alternative text, and proofread after layout.

Publication-Ready Revision

After the old observatory closed in 1998, many residents assumed it had outlived its purpose. A student committee challenged that belief by investigating the building’s records. Its members discovered notebooks showing that volunteer astronomers had tracked unusual storms and taught navigation during the 1940s. Those records gave the preservation proposal historical evidence, not merely sentimental appeal. The committee recommended converting the observatory into an archive and public viewing room. As one student wrote, “The dome does not only point upward; it points backward.” After officials reviewed the proposal, the building reopened as a place where visitors could study local history and observe the sky.

Revision log: clarified the claim, connected evidence to the proposal, removed nominalizations and redundancy, qualified the approval claim, preserved the purposeful metaphor, and identified items requiring source verification.
