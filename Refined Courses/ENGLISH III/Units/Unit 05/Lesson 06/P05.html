Independent Work: “Reordering the Museum Essay”

Directions

Read the source passage and scrambled analytical draft. Answer all 18 questions, create a reverse outline, diagnose organizational problems, rebuild the line of reasoning, and produce a revised essay plan and synthesis paragraph. Verify the designated passage word using a reputable dictionary or encyclopedia and record the source.

Source Passage

The museum displayed a cracked evacuation bell beside a digital map of the 1952 factory fire. Visitors could select a worker’s name and follow that person’s route out of the building. The bell itself had failed during the fire; workers escaped because supervisors shouted instructions and employees guided one another through smoke. The exhibit label called the bell “a symbol of warning and collective response.”

At the exit, visitors could record what they believed had saved the workers. Most selected “community action.” Some chose “emergency planning.” Few selected “the bell.”

Scrambled Draft Paragraphs

  1. The bell symbolizes institutional failure because it did not ring. The digital map proves that technology can correct history.
  2. Some readers may argue that displaying the bell creates a misleading heroic symbol. This concern is reasonable because the label calls it a symbol of warning.
  3. The exhibit suggests that communities survive emergencies through distributed knowledge rather than one reliable object. The failed bell begins the analysis, but the workers’ routes and visitor responses develop the larger claim.
  4. The digital map allows visitors to follow individual routes. This feature changes the exhibit from a static memorial into an investigation of decisions.
  5. In conclusion, the exhibit is about warning and collective response. The bell, map, and survey are all important.
  6. The exit survey extends the map’s pattern: visitors identify community action and planning more often than the bell.
  7. The counterargument is limited by the exhibit’s design. The cracked bell is not presented as a successful alarm; it becomes evidence of why people’s knowledge mattered.

Part A — Reverse Outline and Diagnosis

  1. State the primary function of each scrambled paragraph A–G.
  2. Identify two paragraphs that make claims before necessary evidence is established.
  3. Explain the unsupported overstatement in Paragraph A.
  4. Which paragraph should serve as the introduction, and what revision does it still need?
  5. Use context to define distributed in Paragraph C; verify it with a reputable source and record the source.

Part B — Rebuild the Line of Reasoning

  1. Choose a governing organizational pattern and justify it.
  2. Place paragraphs A–G in the strongest order; you may split, combine, or delete.
  3. Explain what the bell analysis must establish before the map paragraph.
  4. Explain how the survey evidence extends rather than repeats the map evidence.
  5. Where should the counterargument appear, and why?

Part C — Cohesion and Development

  1. Write a transition from the bell paragraph to the map paragraph.
  2. Write a transition introducing the counterargument.
  3. Revise Paragraph A so its technology claim is proportionate to evidence.
  4. Identify one missing warrant between evidence and central claim.
  5. Explain why Paragraph E is a weak conclusion.

Part D — Synthesis

  1. Create a complete revised outline with a one-sentence function for every paragraph.
  2. Write a 140–200 word synthesis conclusion that does not merely repeat the thesis.
  3. Write a revision rationale explaining three structural changes and how each improves reader understanding.
