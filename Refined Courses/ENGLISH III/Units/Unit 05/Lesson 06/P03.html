P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Reorder a paragraph for coherence.

Scrambled Draft

A. This shows that the narrator's confidence depends on being seen by others.

B. The narrator feels powerful only when the crowd notices him.

C. Near the end of the scene, the narrator says that the applause "lifted him above the street."

D. Without the crowd, his confidence fades, so the moment reveals his insecurity.

E. Before the quote, the narrator has been waiting for public approval.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Find the claim. Sentence B states the main idea.

Step 2: Find context. Sentence E prepares the reader for the evidence.

Step 3: Find evidence. Sentence C gives the quoted detail.

Step 4: Find explanation. Sentence A explains what the evidence shows.

Step 5: End with the closing insight. Sentence D expands the meaning.

Notebook Task

Write the correct order of the sentences in your notebook. Then label each sentence as claim, context, evidence, explanation, or closing insight.

Finally, write one transition you could add before the evidence sentence to make the paragraph smoother.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving a broad answer without identifying the exact evidence, rhetorical move, literary choice, or author purpose.

Correct: Name the specific evidence or choice and explain how it supports meaning, argument, style, or reader effect.

Why this matters: English III mastery requires clear reasoning. The reader must see how your evidence proves the claim.

Teachable Explanation

Compare the incorrect and correct versions by checking the exact passage details, the lesson standard, and the meaning that must stay accurate.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Revision for structure and organization

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG3.W.04

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your reasoning before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Read the draft as an outside reader. Mark where the claim, reasoning, organization, sentence control, or style becomes unclear.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: first meaning, then organization, then transitions, then sentence precision, then conventions.
  3. Keep the original analytical purpose unless the task asks for a new claim. Revision should strengthen the argument, explanation, or analysis.
  4. Reread the revised version and ask whether the audience can follow the reasoning without extra explanation.
