P04 Worked Example

Revise sentence order for structure and organization.

Example 1

Unorganized order: Evidence, claim, explanation.

Problem: The reader sees proof before knowing what the proof is supposed to prove.

Better order: Claim, evidence, explanation.

Example 2

Draft: The quote says the town is silent. The town's silence shows fear. The paragraph is about fear controlling the community.

Revision: The paragraph is about fear controlling the community. The quote describes the town as silent. This silence shows that fear has made people afraid to speak openly.

The revision moves the claim first, then gives evidence, then explains the evidence.

Example 3

Transition problem: The sentence "The author uses imagery" follows a sentence about character motivation with no connection.

Transition revision: "This motivation becomes clearer through the author's imagery."

This transition shows how the next idea connects to the previous idea.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Add "also" before every sentence to make the paragraph organized.

Repeating a transition does not fix organization. The sentences still need a logical order.

Correct: First place the claim, then add context, evidence, explanation, and a closing insight.

This approach improves the structure instead of only adding surface-level transition words.
