Checkpoint: “The Misplaced Counterargument”

Directions

Read the source, draft order, and questions. Submit only these 12 responses in the separate Checkpoint Submission.

Source

A story follows a town that digitizes its handwritten weather journals. The originals remain available, and readers can compare searchable data with marginal notes about crops, illnesses, and community events.

Draft Order

  1. Counterargument: digitization can separate data from material context.
  2. Background on the journals.
  3. Claim: the project expands access while revealing what data categories omit.
  4. Evidence about searchable temperature records.
  5. Evidence about marginal notes.
  6. Conclusion repeating the claim.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Why does the current opening confuse the reader?
  2. Which paragraph should come first, and what function should it perform?
  3. Propose the strongest order for all six parts.
  4. Explain how temperature records and marginal notes relate.
  5. Where should the counterargument appear?
  6. Write a pivot into the counterargument.
  7. Write a response that qualifies rather than dismisses it.
  8. Identify one missing warrant.
  9. Write a transition between the two evidence paragraphs.
  10. Replace the repetitive conclusion with a synthesis conclusion.
  11. Explain the governing organizational pattern.
  12. Write a concise revision rationale naming three structural improvements.
