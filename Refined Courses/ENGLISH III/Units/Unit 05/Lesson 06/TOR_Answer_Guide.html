Revision for Structure and Organization

Independent Work Key

Strong reverse outlines identify C as the introduction but revise it to frame the exhibit and claim; bell analysis should precede map and survey evidence; the counterargument should follow developed evidence and be answered by the exhibit’s explicit presentation of failure. Paragraph A overstates what the map proves and should be qualified. The governing structure should move from failed object to individual routes to visitor synthesis, then counterinterpretation and response. Paragraph E merely repeats. Full work includes a functional outline, relational transitions, a 140–200 word synthesis conclusion, and rationale. Distributed means spread among multiple people or points; accept a reputable verified source.

Checkpoint Key

The counterargument cannot open before readers know the project or claim. A strong order is background, claim, searchable data, marginal notes, counterargument, qualified response/conclusion. The two evidence types show access and material context; the counterargument belongs after both. Strong responses supply a relational transition, missing warrant about categories, and a synthesis conclusion explaining what comparison changes about archival knowledge.

Scoring Guidance

Use the visible rubrics. Require structural reasoning and artifacts, not mere renumbering. Correct and resubmit below 80%.
