Word Relationships, Reference Materials, and Precise Meaning

Independent Work Key

Use these anchor expectations: failure is technical possibility but can imply active collapse; repair implies planned correction not yet funded; danger is urgent but nonspecific. Rina defines the required action first, rejects a technically disputed everyday term, and chooses documented conditions. A civil-engineering glossary fits technical stability; authentic public-safety examples or a corpus fit conventional warning language. Compromise can mean settlement or impairment, making weaken clearer. Strong answers explain how inspection evidence, field definitions, public guidance, audience, and changing conditions jointly control revision. Accept responsible warning signs of no more than 18 words that mandate closure, name the condition without overclaiming, and direct an alternate route.

Checkpoint Key

Mob implies disorder or threat unsupported by the image; gathering implies voluntary assembly contradicted by payroll evidence. The initial factual phrase is supported by shift records; the newspaper report justifies adding only that some remained for a union address. The semicolon distinguishes related but nonidentical events. Proportionate is preferable because claims are scaled to available evidence. Strong responses select appropriate historical or labor references, explain the vividness–accuracy tradeoff, and defend an alternate caption without applying one action to every person.

Scoring Guidance

Use the visible rubrics. Require reference selection, precise evidence, and explicit defense of wording. Correct and resubmit below 80%.
