Worked Example: Choosing the Precise Analytical Verb

Model Passage

The narrator describes the abandoned station as “waiting,” although weeds cover the tracks and the clock has stopped. Later, former passengers gather there to record their memories.

Draft Sentence

The narrator says that the station remains meaningful.

Step 1 — Intended Meaning

The analysis needs a verb showing that imagery indirectly communicates meaning. The narrator does not literally state the claim.

Step 2 — Candidate Words

Says, suggests, implies, indicates, demonstrates, proves, evokes. A thesaurus can generate this list, but each verb makes a different evidence claim.

Step 3 — Reference Check

  • suggests: presents an idea for consideration without making it certain.
  • implies: communicates indirectly.
  • indicates: points toward a conclusion.
  • demonstrates: shows clearly through evidence.
  • proves: establishes conclusively—too strong here.
  • evokes: brings an image or feeling to mind; it needs a different grammatical object.

Step 4 — Context and Collocation

Because “waiting” is personification and the later gathering supports but does not conclusively establish the claim, suggests fits both evidence strength and common analytical usage.

Final Revision

The narrator’s personification of the abandoned station suggests that a place can retain communal meaning after losing its original function.

Alternative and Limitation

Implies is also defensible if the analysis emphasizes indirect communication. Proves is not defensible because literary evidence supports interpretation rather than conclusive proof.
