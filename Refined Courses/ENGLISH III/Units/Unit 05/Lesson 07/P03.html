Lesson: The Publication-Polish Sequence

Five Deliberate Passes

  1. Meaning pass: verify claims, evidence, reasoning, and qualifications.
  2. Paragraph pass: check focus, sequence, cohesion, and emphasis.
  3. Sentence pass: refine clarity, rhythm, variety, reference, and grammatical control.
  4. Word pass: remove redundancy, test diction, and clarify nominalizations.
  5. Publication pass: verify quotations, citations, links, headings, formatting, accessibility, and final proofreading.

Example 1 — Nominalization

Heavy: “The narrator’s recognition of the failure of the system leads to a reconsideration of responsibility.”

Refined: “When the narrator recognizes that the system failed, she reconsiders who is responsible.”

Example 2 — Emphasis

Flat: “The archive preserves records, but its omissions matter too.”

Emphatic: “The archive preserves records. Its silences shape history too.”

The shorter final sentence gives the qualification weight.

Example 3 — Productive Repetition

Not all repetition is redundant. Repeating “The minutes record...” at the start of successive sentences can create a deliberate pattern that exposes what official records omit.

Example 4 — Citation and Format

A polished paragraph can still fail publication review if a quotation is inaccurate, a paraphrase lacks citation, or a heading hierarchy is inconsistent. Source and format checks are intellectual responsibilities, not decoration.

Common Mistakes

  • Deleting every long sentence instead of controlling it.
  • Replacing a distinctive voice with generic academic phrases.
  • Proofreading while major content is still changing.
  • Accepting automated style suggestions that alter meaning.
  • Assuming a correct citation format guarantees a credible source.
