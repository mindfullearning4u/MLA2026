Worked Example: From Revised Draft to Publication Copy

Revised Draft

There is a way in which the repeated presentation of the phrase “temporary closure” has the effect of making the residents’ long experience of exclusion appear to be shorter and less consequential than it actually is. The report is technically accurate, but it is also incomplete in its presentation of lived reality.

Pass 1 — Meaning

The core claim is sound: repeated temporary language minimizes cumulative exclusion. The distinction between factual accuracy and experiential completeness must remain.

Pass 2 — Sentence Structure

The opening expletive “There is a way in which” delays the claim. Nominalizations—presentation, effect, experience—hide actions and agents.

Pass 3 — Precision and Rhythm

Replace abstract phrasing with active verbs, then use a short second sentence to emphasize the qualification.

Publication Copy

By repeatedly calling the shutdown a “temporary closure,” the report minimizes years of exclusion. Its wording is technically accurate but experientially incomplete.

Publication Checks

  • Quotation verified exactly against the source.
  • Citation placed after the quoted phrase according to required style.
  • Experientially retained because it precisely contrasts lived reality with technical description.
  • Heading, font, spacing, and accessibility checked against the publication requirements.

Alternative Style Choice

A writer could retain one longer sentence to create accumulating pressure: “By repeatedly calling the shutdown a ‘temporary closure,’ the report compresses years of exclusion into language that is technically accurate yet experientially incomplete.” Both versions are controlled; the choice depends on desired emphasis and surrounding rhythm.
