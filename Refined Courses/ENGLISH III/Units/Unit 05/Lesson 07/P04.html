P04 Worked Example

Polish style and precision without changing meaning.

Example 1

Draft: The author uses good words to make the mood sad.

Polished: The author uses mournful diction to create a sorrowful mood.

Why it works: The revision replaces vague wording with precise academic vocabulary.

Example 2

Draft: The character is mad and says stuff that shows it.

Polished: The character's sharp accusations reveal her anger and growing distrust.

Why it works: The revision names the kind of language and the emotional effect.

Example 3

Draft: This proves my point, it is very important.

Polished: This evidence supports the claim because it shows the narrator choosing honesty over comfort.

Why it works: The revision fixes the comma splice and explains the evidence clearly.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Make every sentence sound complicated by adding longer words.

Complicated wording can hide meaning. Polish should make writing clearer, not harder to understand.

Correct: Choose precise words that match the task, audience, and meaning.

This improves style while preserving the writer's original purpose.
