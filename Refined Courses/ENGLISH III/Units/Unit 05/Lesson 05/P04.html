P04 Worked Example

Use feedback to revise a sentence for clarity.

Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Draft: The author uses evidence to show the idea.

Feedback: Name the evidence and explain the idea more clearly.

Step 1: The feedback asks for specificity.

Step 2: The unclear words are "evidence" and "idea."

Step 3: Replace vague words with exact details.

Revision: The author uses the repeated image of locked doors to show that the character feels trapped by family expectations.

Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Draft: The source is useful because it talks about the problem.

Feedback: Explain what problem the source addresses and why the evidence is useful.

Revision: The source is useful because it explains how limited transportation prevents some rural students from attending after-school tutoring.

Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Draft: The paragraph is confusing because it has grammar mistakes.

Feedback: Identify the convention problem that affects meaning.

Revision: The paragraph is confusing because several pronouns do not clearly refer to a specific person, so readers cannot tell which character is speaking.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The author makes it clearer because the quote is good.

This revision repeats vague language. It does not tell the reader what is clearer, what the quote shows, or why the quote matters.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct: The author clarifies the character's fear by connecting the quote about "locked windows" to the character's desire to escape the house.

This revision names the evidence, explains the idea, and shows how the evidence supports the analysis.
