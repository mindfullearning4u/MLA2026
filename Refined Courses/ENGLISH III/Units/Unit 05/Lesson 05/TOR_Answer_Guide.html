Feedback and Revision for Clarity

Independent Work Key

Strong diagnoses identify a vague claim, repeated summary, unsupported universal conclusion, and missing reasoning between concrete wall, blue line, brass marker, and communal memory. Priority should begin with claim, then evidence/reasoning, then repetition and conventions. A defensible revision argues that the new wall addresses physical risk while the marker preserves evidence that shaped the community; it should qualify rather than claim that history must never be forgotten. Full responses include a 150–220 word paragraph, a rationale linking at least three changes to feedback, and one remaining limitation. Dedication here means a formal ceremony opening or presenting the structure; accept a reputable verified source.

Checkpoint Key

Draft A is globally unfocused and contains unnecessary general history; its useful element is the subject of changing library technology. Draft B supplies a focused interpretive claim but should acknowledge loss of original function. Strong revisions briefly frame replacement, state that the drawer no longer organizes books, and claim that adaptation gives it a new communal function. Rationales should explain feedback priorities, preparation for evidence, one modified suggestion, and a remaining limitation.

Scoring Guidance

Use the visible rubrics. Score substantive revision and rationale—not mere length or surface editing. Require revision and resubmission below 80%.
