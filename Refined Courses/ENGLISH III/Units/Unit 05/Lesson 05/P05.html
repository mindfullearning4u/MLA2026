Independent Work: “Revision Notes from the Floodwall Essay”

Directions

Read the source passage, draft, and three feedback sets. Answer all 18 questions in complete sentences. Diagnose underlying problems, prioritize revisions, evaluate conflicting advice, and produce a revised paragraph with a clear rationale. For the passage-specific vocabulary item, infer the meaning, verify it with a reputable dictionary or encyclopedia, and record the source.

Source Passage

The river town painted a blue line across storefronts to mark the height of the 1968 flood. Decades later, engineers completed a concrete wall along the bank. During the dedication, the mayor called the wall “the end of our argument with water.” Mara Vale, whose family bakery still carried the blue line, placed a small brass marker on the new wall at the same height. “An argument can end,” she said, “without erasing what taught us to answer.”

Student Draft

The passage is about memory and progress. The blue line is important because it shows the flood. The wall solves the problem and the marker shows the past. The mayor thinks the flood is over but Mara remembers it. This proves that communities should never forget history because history teaches lessons. The concrete wall is strong while the brass marker is small, which is a contrast.

Feedback Set

  • Peer A: “Add more details from the passage.”
  • Peer B: “The paragraph repeats the same idea.”
  • TOR: “Develop a specific claim about how the two markers create meaning; avoid the universal claim that communities should never forget.”

Part A — Diagnose

  1. What does Peer A experience as missing? Identify the underlying problem rather than repeating the comment.
  2. Where does Peer B’s repetition occur?
  3. Why is the TOR’s concern global rather than local?
  4. Identify the unsupported generalization in the draft.
  5. Use context to define dedication as used in the source. Verify it with a reputable source and name it.

Part B — Prioritize and Plan

  1. Rank these concerns—claim, evidence, repetition, conventions—from first to last and justify the order.
  2. Write a more specific interpretive claim.
  3. Select two details that best support the new claim.
  4. Explain how the concrete wall and brass marker function as more than a simple size contrast.
  5. Decide whether the mayor’s metaphor should be quoted. Explain.

Part C — Evaluate Feedback

  1. How can the writer address Peer A without merely making the paragraph longer?
  2. How can one revision address both Peer B and the TOR?
  3. Identify one feedback suggestion that would be incomplete if followed literally.
  4. Generate two possible organizational plans and choose the stronger one.
  5. Explain what evidence would make the phrase “the wall solves the problem” more accurate or more qualified.

Part D — Revise and Explain

  1. Rewrite the complete paragraph in 150–220 words.
  2. Write a revision rationale naming at least three substantive changes and the feedback each addresses.
  3. Evaluate your revision: identify one remaining limitation or question a reader could reasonably raise.
