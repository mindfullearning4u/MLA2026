Lesson: From Reader Comment to Revision Decision

The Six-Step Protocol

  1. Read without defending: identify what the reader experienced.
  2. Locate the evidence: find where the draft created that experience.
  3. Diagnose the cause: name the global or local problem beneath the comment.
  4. Prioritize: revise claim, organization, and evidence before polishing sentences.
  5. Generate options: test more than one possible change.
  6. Evaluate and document: select the option that best serves task, evidence, and audience; state the rationale.

Example 1 — “This Is Confusing”

Weak response: Replace difficult words with simpler words.

Diagnosis: The paragraph introduces a counterclaim before stating its own claim.

Revision: Move the claim first, then frame the counterclaim as a qualification.

Example 2 — “Add More Evidence”

The draft may already contain enough quotations but insufficient explanation. The correct revision may be to analyze how evidence supports the claim rather than adding another quotation.

Example 3 — Conflicting Feedback

One reader asks for more context; another says the introduction is too long. Both may be right if the draft includes broad history but omits the one fact needed to understand the claim. Replace general background with focused context.

Example 4 — AI Feedback

AI may flag repetition or propose organization. Treat suggestions as hypotheses. Compare them with the assignment, verify any factual claims, reject changes that distort evidence or erase your voice, and document major decisions.

Common Mistakes

  • Accepting every suggestion without evaluating purpose.
  • Rejecting feedback because the proposed fix is imperfect.
  • Editing grammar before repairing the central claim.
  • Adding length instead of resolving the reader’s problem.
  • Using vague rationales such as “it sounds better.”
