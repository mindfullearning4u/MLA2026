P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Using Feedback to Revise for Clarity

Vocabulary

Feedback is information a writer uses to improve a draft. Clarity means the reader can understand the idea without confusion. Revision means changing writing to improve meaning, organization, or style. Specificity means using exact details instead of vague language.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read the feedback exactly as written. Do not revise yet. First, identify what the reader could not understand.

Step 2: Decide the feedback type. A clarity comment usually asks you to define a point, explain a connection, add a specific detail, or remove confusing wording.

Step 3: Find the sentence or phrase that caused the problem. Revision works best when you target the exact place where meaning breaks down.

Step 4: Revise without changing the claim. The goal is not to create a new argument. The goal is to make the existing argument easier to follow.

Notebook Task

Copy the draft sentence and feedback below. Then answer the three notebook questions.

Draft sentence: The author makes the character seem different because of what happens.

Feedback: Be more specific. What is different about the character, and what event causes the change?

  1. What part of the sentence is unclear?
  2. What information does the feedback ask the writer to add?
  3. Write one revised sentence that keeps the same basic idea but makes it clearer.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.
