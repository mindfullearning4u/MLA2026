Checkpoint: “The Two Introductions”

Directions

Read the passage, draft introductions, and feedback. Answer all 12 questions and submit them only in the separate Checkpoint Submission.

Source Passage

After the town library replaced its card catalog, custodian Elena kept one drawer beside the computers. Visitors used its blank cards to recommend books to strangers. The obsolete tool no longer organized the collection, but it began organizing conversations.

Draft A

Technology changes many things in society. Libraries have existed for thousands of years and are important. This passage is about a card catalog.

Draft B

The preserved catalog drawer suggests that obsolete tools can gain new communal purposes when people adapt rather than merely discard them.

Feedback

  • Reader 1: Draft A provides context but feels unfocused.
  • Reader 2: Draft B is strong but should acknowledge that the drawer no longer performs its original function.
  • TOR: Build a precise claim and prepare the reader for evidence about both loss and adaptation.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Diagnose the global problem in Draft A.
  2. Identify any useful material in Draft A.
  3. Explain why Draft B better meets reader expectations.
  4. What limitation does Reader 2 identify?
  5. Rank the three feedback concerns by revision priority.
  6. Write a revised introduction that uses the strongest elements of both drafts without unnecessary background.
  7. Explain how your revision prepares for evidence.
  8. Identify one local edit that should occur only after the claim is revised.
  9. Explain why “add more context” could produce a weaker introduction.
  10. State a revision rationale tied to task and audience.
  11. Identify one feedback suggestion you modified rather than followed literally.
  12. Evaluate the revised introduction for one remaining limitation.
