# English III Final Architecture Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit reviewed the full English III curriculum architecture package before lesson production. The audit compared the English III package against the approved English I and English II production frameworks, verified all 24 Florida B.E.S.T. English III benchmark codes and benchmark wording against CPALMS public standards search results, validated MLA standard numbering and domain assignment, checked crosswalk and mapping coverage, audited all 48 lesson mapping rows, and reviewed vertical alignment from English II to English III.

Initial audit status: `PASS`

Final audit status: `PASS`

No architecture-blocking corrections were required after the files were created. The package uses verified Grade 11 benchmark wording and does not duplicate English II. The verified English III progression emphasizes Classical Period speeches and essays, contemporaneous authors, juxtaposition, allegory, literary research, literary analysis writing, complex text analysis, rhetorical oral presentation, audience-experience digital presentation, and online/LAN publication.

## 3. Strengths

- The architecture preserves the approved six-unit, eight-lesson structure.
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together in every unit.
- Lesson 8 contains the Unit Assessment and introduces no new primary standards.
- MLA standard domains remain consistent with English I and English II: Reading, Writing, Language, Vocabulary, Research, Communication.
- Unit 2 protects literary analysis rather than compressing evaluation, themes, juxtaposition, poetry, allegory, contemporaneous authors, and complex narrative writing.
- Unit 3 preserves a clear Classical Period informational/rhetorical sequence.
- Unit 4 maintains a coherent literary research-to-analysis-writing workflow.
- Unit 5 keeps language, vocabulary, style, and revision attached to authentic writing contexts.
- Unit 6 functions as synthesis, oral presentation, digital presentation, and publication rather than first-time instruction.

## 4. Risks

| Unit 6 could become production-heavy if earlier communication practice is weak. | Low | Oral communication, evidence explanation, revision, and digital awareness spiral before Unit 6. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

These are recommendations only and do not block certification.

- During future lesson production, select texts that naturally support Grade 11 requirements: Classical Period speeches and essays, poetry in time-period context, allegory, juxtaposition, and contemporaneous author comparison.
- Maintain a text-bank audit before lesson production so Unit 2 and Unit 3 standards are not forced into weak or mismatched readings.
- Keep Unit 4 products connected: literary research notes, source synthesis, literary analysis, and complex text analysis should remain one coherent workflow.
- During assessment design, ensure `ELA.11.C.5.1` measures improved audience experience rather than slide appearance alone.
- Avoid framing English III as American literature unless future local course requirements add that expectation separately from the verified Grade 11 benchmarks.
