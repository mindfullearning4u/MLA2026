Teacher-Style Explanation

This lesson focuses on Energy and Resource Decision-Making. The standard is MLA.ENV.RES.03; MLA.ENV.RES.02, so the work must stay inside this lesson: resource availability, energy technology, land use, and management tradeoffs.

A strong environmental science answer does more than state an opinion. It reads the required map, graph, table, model, source, or case evidence; names the pattern; explains the environmental relationship; and limits the conclusion to what the evidence can support.

  1. Identify the resource or technology.
  2. Classify it as renewable, nonrenewable, or managed resource.
  3. Compare benefits and environmental costs.
  4. Use the decision matrix or data table to justify a recommendation.

Common Confusion to Avoid

Incorrect: choosing the option that sounds best without comparing costs, benefits, renewal rate, and environmental impact

Correct Thinking

Correct: resource decisions require evidence about availability, renewal, impact, cost, and long-term sustainability
