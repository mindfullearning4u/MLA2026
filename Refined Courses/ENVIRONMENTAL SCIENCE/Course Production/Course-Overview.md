## Course Description

Environmental Science is a mastery-based high-school laboratory science course focused on ecosystems, biodiversity, human impact, sustainability, resource management, environmental systems, population dynamics, pollution, environmental policy, conservation, energy resources, and environmental decision-making.

Students develop environmental science reasoning through evidence-based investigation, monitoring data, ecosystem models, population data, climate and pollution evidence, resource-use analysis, technical communication, and sustainability literacy. Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Earth/Space Science, Marine Science, and Anatomy and Physiology concepts are used only where the Environmental Science course record explicitly requires supporting concepts.

## Six-Unit Sequence

| Unit | Unit Title | Course Role |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Environmental Science Foundations and Scientific Inquiry | Establishes inquiry, lab safety, monitoring, evidence, environmental systems, data, models, and decision-making foundations. |
| Unit 2 | Ecosystems, Energy Flow, and Biodiversity | Develops ecosystem change, biotic/abiotic systems, organism relationships, food webs, energy flow, biodiversity, and extinction. |
| Unit 3 | Population Dynamics and Human Impact | Develops population characteristics, carrying capacity, human population, resource use, environmental health, and environmental quality. |
| Unit 4 | Natural Resources and Resource Management | Develops renewable/nonrenewable resources, energy production, resource availability, land use, forests, wildlife, and sustainable resource decisions. |
| Unit 5 | Pollution, Climate, and Environmental Change | Develops climate change, ocean-climate influence, Florida atmospheric/oceanic/hydrologic systems, pollution, waste, technology, and environmental change. |
| Unit 6 | Sustainability, Environmental Policy, and Future Solutions | Develops environmental monitoring, policy decisions, sustainability, conservation, lifestyle impacts, evidence-based solutions, and future planning. |

## Assessment Structure

Environmental Science uses the approved MLA assessment model: unit pretests, notebook evidence, guided practice, checkpoints, lesson quizzes, and Unit Assessments. Lesson 8 in every unit is titled Putting It All Together, contains the Unit Assessment, synthesizes standards already taught in Lessons 1-7, and introduces no new primary standard.

---

## Lab Expectations

Environmental Science is a laboratory science course. Lab architecture must include scientific inquiry, safe procedures, environmental monitoring, variables, measurement, data tables, graphs, ecosystem models, population models, climate/pollution data, evidence-based interpretation, and technical communication.

---

## Virtual Lab Expectations

Virtual labs may use ecosystem simulations, population models, food-web models, climate data, ocean/atmosphere data, pollution data, resource-use data, GIS-style maps, environmental monitoring data, and policy case data. Virtual labs must require students to observe, collect or interpret data, identify variables, analyze evidence, and communicate conclusions.

---

## Mastery-Based Credit Structure

Students earn credit through demonstrated mastery of Environmental Science standards across unit pretests, lesson evidence, guided practice, checkpoints, lesson quizzes, and unit assessments. Mastery evidence must align to MLA Environmental Science standards and Florida benchmark expectations.
