# Phase 2A-A-2: English II MLA Standard Assignment

## Executive Summary

This report establishes the draft MLA English II standard inventory using the approved MLA English numbering framework:

`MLA.[COURSE].[DOMAIN].[NUMBER]`

For English II, the course identifier is `ENG2`. Standards are numbered within the approved MLA English domains:

- `R` = Reading
- `W` = Writing
- `L` = Language
- `V` = Vocabulary
- `RS` = Research
- `C` = Communication

This inventory preserves the English I production framework as the master template while advancing the grade-level benchmark codes from Grade 9 English I to Grade 10 English II. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, or GIFT files.

Source-control note: CPALMS public standards pages were available as the public source location for Florida B.E.S.T. benchmarks, but the Florida PDF endpoint blocked automated download during this build. The benchmark code progression below follows the Florida B.E.S.T. English I-to-English II grade-code sequence and should receive a final source-of-record wording check before content production is locked.

## English I to English II Comparison Findings

### Standards Unique to English II

| Category | Finding |
|---|---|
| Course-code uniqueness | Every English II standard is uniquely identified by `ENG2` and aligned to Grade 10 benchmark codes. |
| Structural uniqueness | English II does not add new MLA domains beyond English I; rigor increases through text complexity, independence, synthesis, and precision. |
| Literary progression | English II should move beyond introductory literary analysis toward more sustained comparison of craft, perspective, allusion, adaptation, and literary tradition. |
| Informational progression | English II should require stronger evaluation of how structure, rhetoric, support, and argument function across more complex texts and paired sources. |
| Writing progression | English II should expect more independent claim development, source integration, counterclaim handling, elaboration, cohesion, and tone control than English I. |

### Increased Rigor Requirements

| Strand | English I Baseline | English II Rigor Increase |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Explain and analyze key elements, themes, rhetoric, argument, and adaptation. | Sustain analysis across more complex texts, paired texts, and layered author choices. |
| Writing | Produce organized narrative, argument, and expository writing with source support. | Increase independence, precision, counterclaim sophistication, source synthesis, and purposeful tone. |
| Research | Refine questions and evaluate reliable and valid sources. | Use research to support more complex explanatory and argumentative decisions across multiple credible sources. |
| Vocabulary | Use academic vocabulary, etymology, context, figurative language, denotation, and connotation. | Apply vocabulary strategies independently to more nuanced literary, rhetorical, and source-based contexts. |
| Communication | Present with organization, focus, evidence, perspective, and digital clarity. | Present synthesized claims and findings with stronger audience awareness and evidence control. |
