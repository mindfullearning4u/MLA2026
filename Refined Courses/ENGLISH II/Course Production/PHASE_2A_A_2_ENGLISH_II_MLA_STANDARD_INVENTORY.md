# Phase 2A-A-2: English II MLA Standard Assignment

## Executive Summary

This report establishes the draft MLA English II standard inventory using the approved MLA English numbering framework:

`MLA.[COURSE].[DOMAIN].[NUMBER]`

For English II, the course identifier is `ENG2`. Standards are numbered within the approved MLA English domains:

- `R` = Reading
- `W` = Writing
- `L` = Language
- `V` = Vocabulary
- `RS` = Research
- `C` = Communication

This inventory preserves the English I production framework as the master template while advancing the grade-level benchmark codes from Grade 9 English I to Grade 10 English II. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, or GIFT files.

Source-control note: Benchmark codes and wording were verified against the public CPALMS standards search endpoint during the final architecture audit. CPALMS returned benchmark cards for all 24 English II benchmark codes.

## English I to English II Comparison Findings

### Standards Unique to English II

| Category | Finding |
|---|---|
| Course-code uniqueness | Every English II standard is uniquely identified by `ENG2` and aligned to Grade 10 benchmark codes. |
| Structural uniqueness | English II does not add new MLA domains beyond English I; rigor increases through text complexity, independence, synthesis, and precision. |
| Literary progression | English II moves beyond English I narrator-perspective and epic-poetry work into coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, and adaptation analysis. |
| Informational progression | English II adds specific emphasis on historical American speeches and essays, shared-information argument comparison, and author choices in purpose. |
| Writing progression | English II expects formal objective argument tone, narrative pacing for tension/mood/tone, source-based expository voice, and audience-focused revision. |

### Increased Rigor Requirements

| Strand | English I Baseline | English II Rigor Increase |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Explain and analyze key elements, themes, rhetoric, argument, and adaptation. | Analyze comparison, conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, historical American speeches and essays, and shared-information argument moves. |
| Writing | Produce organized narrative, argument, and expository writing with source support. | Increase narrative pacing control, formal objective argument tone, source synthesis, task-appropriate voice, and audience-focused revision. |
| Research | Refine questions and evaluate reliable and valid sources. | Refine research scope and synthesize information from multiple reliable and valid sources. |
| Vocabulary | Use academic vocabulary, etymology, context, figurative language, denotation, and connotation. | Apply vocabulary strategies independently to more nuanced literary, rhetorical, and source-based contexts. |
| Communication | Present with organization, focus, evidence, perspective, and digital clarity. | Present synthesized claims and findings with stronger audience awareness and evidence control. |
