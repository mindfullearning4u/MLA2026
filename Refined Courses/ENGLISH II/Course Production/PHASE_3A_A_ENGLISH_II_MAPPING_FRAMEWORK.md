# Phase 3A-A: English II Mapping Framework

## Executive Summary

This document establishes the English II Mapping Framework using the English I production framework as the master template. It preserves the MLA mastery model, audit phases, naming conventions, documentation format, and course production artifact format while increasing rigor for Grade 10 English Language Arts.

This framework does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, GIFT files, or student-facing instructional content.

## Required Course Architecture

| Requirement | English II Control |
|---|---|
| Course structure | 6 units |
| Unit structure | 8 lessons per unit |
| Lesson 8 | Putting It All Together |
| Lesson 8 assessment | Unit Assessment |
| Lessons 1-7 | Lesson Quiz included |
| Production mode | Structure-only mapping and documentation |
| Content control | No lesson content is created or modified |

## Approved English II Unit Sequence

| Unit | Unit Title | Primary Function |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Foundations of Close Reading, Evidence, and Academic Voice | Establish Grade 10 close reading, paraphrase, vocabulary, evidence, conventions, and academic response habits. |
| Unit 2 | Literary Analysis, Perspective, and Narrative Craft | Advance literary analysis through elements, comparative theme analysis, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, figurative language, poetic ambiguity, adaptation, and narrative craft. |
| Unit 3 | Informational Text, Rhetoric, and Argument Development | Develop informational text analysis through historical American speeches and essays, rhetoric, opposing argument evaluation, and argument writing. |
| Unit 4 | Research, Source Synthesis, and Expository Writing | Connect inquiry, source reliability, note-taking, synthesis, expository writing, and collaborative publication. |
| Unit 5 | Language, Vocabulary, Style, and Revision | Refine language conventions, vocabulary independence, word meaning, style, cohesion, and revision. |
| Unit 6 | Synthesis, Presentation, and Digital Communication | Synthesize reading, writing, research, vocabulary, language, and communication in oral and digital presentation. |

## Vertical Alignment with English I

| Strand | English I Production Baseline | English II Mapping Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Close reading | Paraphrase and evidence foundations support later analysis. | Preserve early support while increasing text complexity and independence. |
| Literary analysis | Literary elements, theme, narrator perspective, epic poetry, figurative language, mood, and adaptation. | Increase rigor through comparative theme analysis, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, and adaptation analysis. |
| Informational text | Structure, central idea support, purpose, rhetoric, and opposing arguments. | Increase rigor through historical American speeches and essays, impact of structure/features, author choices in purpose, and shared-information argument comparison. |
| Research | Question refinement, source reliability, validity, notes, synthesis, expository writing. | Increase source synthesis, research-to-writing integration, and explanation of how evidence supports findings. |
| Argument | Claims, evidence, reasoning, counterclaims, organization, transitions, and tone. | Increase claim precision, counterclaim rebuttal, evidence sufficiency, shared-information analysis, and formal objective tone. |
| Vocabulary | Academic vocabulary, etymology, derivations, context, connotation, denotation. | Increase independence and precision in literary, rhetorical, research, and presentation contexts. |
| Communication | Oral and digital presentation with focus, evidence, perspective, and audience. | Require synthesized findings, credible evidence, clear perspective, and stronger digital communication control. |

## Benchmark Cluster Rules

| Cluster | Primary Unit | Mapping Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational Reading and Academic Response | Unit 1 | Must support every later unit and cannot become remedial-only content. |
| Literary Analysis and Craft | Unit 2 | Must protect comparative theme analysis, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, figurative language, poetic ambiguity, adaptation, and narrative craft from compression. |
| Informational Text and Rhetoric | Unit 3 | Must sequence text structure impact and central ideas in historical American speeches and essays before rhetoric and argument. |
| Argument Analysis and Argument Writing | Unit 3 | Must place argument reading before argument writing. |
| Research and Expository Communication | Unit 4 | Must integrate research, synthesis, expository writing, and collaborative publication as one process. |
| Language, Vocabulary, and Revision | Unit 5 | Must use authentic reading and writing contexts rather than isolated drill. |
| Oral and Digital Communication | Unit 6 | Must synthesize previously taught skills and avoid introducing new major standards in Lesson 8. |

## Lesson Mapping Controls

- Lessons 1-7 may introduce, teach, practice, and assess primary standards.
- Lesson 8 must remain Putting It All Together.
- Lesson 8 must not introduce a new primary standard.
- Most lessons should carry one primary standard.
- Two primary standards are allowed only when the standards are naturally paired.
- Support standards must reinforce instruction without becoming hidden extra lessons.
- Reading, writing, research, vocabulary, language, and communication must spiral across the course.

## Rigor Progression Controls

| Area | Required English II Increase |
|---|---|
| Literary analysis | Move from basic explanation to sustained analysis of comparative themes, conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, and adaptation. |
| Informational text | Move from identifying support to analyzing central ideas and author choices in historical American speeches and essays. |
| Research | Move from gathering valid sources to refining inquiry and synthesizing source findings in explanatory and argumentative contexts. |
| Argumentation | Move from supported claims to more precise reasoning, counterclaim rebuttal, evidence sufficiency, shared-information comparison, and formal objective tone. |
| Vocabulary and language | Move from application to independent precision, connotation control, derivation reasoning, and style refinement. |
| Writing | Move from organized modes to stronger cohesion, elaboration, source integration, and revision independence. |

## Assessment Architecture Controls

| Assessment Artifact | Mapping Requirement |
|---|---|
| Unit Pretest | Must align to unit standards and readiness prerequisites. |
| Notebook Evidence | Must document reading, writing, vocabulary, research, revision, and communication process work. |
| Checkpoint Submission | Must require application of the lesson's primary standard in context. |
| Lesson Quiz | Required for Lessons 1-7. |
| Unit Assessment | Required in Lesson 8 as synthesis of standards already taught. |
