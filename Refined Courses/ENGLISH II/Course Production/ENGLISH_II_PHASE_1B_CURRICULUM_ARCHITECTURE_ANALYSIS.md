# English II Phase 1B: Curriculum Architecture Analysis

## Executive Summary

This document establishes the English II curriculum architecture using the completed English I production framework as the master template. It preserves MLA curriculum methodology, naming conventions, audit phases, documentation format, course production artifact expectations, and mastery-based sequencing.

This is architecture only. It does not create lesson content, assessments, HTML files, quiz files, GIFT files, or LMS pages.

## Approved Unit Architecture

| Unit | Unit Title | Architecture Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Foundations of Close Reading, Evidence, and Academic Voice | Establish Grade 10 paraphrase, vocabulary, evidence, conventions, and academic response readiness. |
| Unit 2 | Literary Analysis, Perspective, and Narrative Craft | Develop literary analysis through elements, comparative themes, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, figurative language, poetic ambiguity, adaptation, and narrative pacing. |
| Unit 3 | Informational Text, Rhetoric, and Argument Development | Develop informational analysis through structure impact, historical American speeches and essays, rhetoric, opposing arguments, shared information, and formal argument writing. |
| Unit 4 | Research, Source Synthesis, and Expository Writing | Connect inquiry, source reliability, scope refinement, synthesis, expository writing with voice, and collaborative multimedia publication. |
| Unit 5 | Language, Vocabulary, Style, and Revision | Refine standard English, word analysis, connotation, denotation, audience-focused revision, style, and precision. |
| Unit 6 | Synthesis, Presentation, and Digital Communication | Synthesize reading, writing, research, language, vocabulary, evidence, findings, reasoning, and digital/oral communication. |

## English I to English II Progression

| Area | English I Foundation | English II Progression |
|---|---|---|
| Literary analysis | Literary elements, theme, narrator perspective, epic poetry, figurative language, mood, and adaptation. | Comparative theme analysis, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, and adaptation analysis. |
| Informational text | Text structure, central idea support, author purpose, rhetoric, and opposing arguments. | Structure impact, central ideas and author purpose in historical American speeches and essays, and shared-information argument comparison. |
| Argument writing | Claims, logical reasoning, credible evidence, counterclaims, organization, elaboration, transitions, and task-appropriate tone. | Formal objective tone, stronger shared-information reasoning, and more independent counterclaim rebuttal. |
| Research | Reliable and valid sources with refined research scope. | Scope refinement plus synthesis of multiple reliable and valid sources. |
| Revision | Clarity and cohesiveness. | Revision to address the needs of a specific audience. |
| Digital communication | Coherent ideas and clear perspective. | Digital presentations that improve understanding of findings, reasoning, and evidence; collaborative writing with multimedia integration. |

## Architecture Controls

- Preserve the MLA mastery model.
- Preserve English I's six-unit, eight-lesson structure.
- Preserve Lesson 8 as Putting It All Together.
- Preserve standard numbering format `MLA.ENG2.[DOMAIN].[NUMBER]`.
- Preserve the approved MLA English domains: Reading, Writing, Language, Vocabulary, Research, Communication.
- Preserve audit phase separation: architecture, inventory, crosswalk, mapping, then future production.
- Do not create student-facing instructional content in architecture documents.
- Do not introduce lesson content, assessments, HTML pages, quiz JSON, lesson JSON, or GIFT files in this phase.
