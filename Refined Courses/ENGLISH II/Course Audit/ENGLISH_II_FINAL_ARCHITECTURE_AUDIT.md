# English II Final Architecture Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit reviewed the full English II curriculum architecture package before lesson production. The audit compared the English II package against the completed English I production framework, verified all 24 Florida B.E.S.T. English II benchmark codes and benchmark wording against CPALMS public standards search results, validated MLA standard numbering and domain assignment, checked crosswalk and mapping coverage, audited all 48 lesson mapping rows, and reviewed vertical alignment from English I to English II.

Initial audit status: `PASS WITH REVISIONS`

Final audit status after corrections: `PASS`

Corrections were required because the initial English II draft incorrectly reused several English I benchmark wordings and did not include a Phase 1B English II curriculum architecture analysis document. Those issues have been corrected in the affected architecture documents.

## 3. Strengths

- The architecture preserves the English I six-unit, eight-lesson structure.
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together in every unit.
- Lesson 8 introduces no new primary standards.
- MLA standard domains remain consistent with English I: Reading, Writing, Language, Vocabulary, Research, Communication.
- Unit 2 protects literary analysis instead of compressing all literary standards into a single generic block.
- Unit 3 preserves the correct sequence: text structure, central ideas, author purpose, rhetoric, opposing arguments, then argument writing.
- Unit 4 maintains a coherent research workflow from inquiry to source synthesis to expository writing and collaborative publication.
- Unit 5 keeps language and revision attached to authentic writing contexts.
- Unit 6 functions as synthesis and communication rather than first-time instruction.

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 2 has high literary-standard density. | Medium | Dedicated lessons protect comparative themes, coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, figurative language, poetic ambiguity, adaptation, and narrative pacing. |
| Unit 3 has high informational/rhetorical/argument density. | Medium | Sequence keeps historical American text analysis before rhetoric and argument writing. |
| Unit 4 may become overloaded if research, synthesis, expository writing, and multimedia publication are treated as unrelated products. | Medium | Mapping treats them as one connected source-based communication process. |
| Unit 5 could become isolated grammar/vocabulary work. | Low | Mapping requires authentic writing and audience-focused revision contexts. |
| Unit 6 could become a new-skills unit if communication is delayed. | Low | Communication spirals from Unit 1 through Unit 5 before final synthesis. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

These are recommendations only and do not block certification.

- During lesson production, select texts that naturally support Grade 10-specific requirements: coming-of-age/conflicting perspectives, poetic ambiguity, historical American speeches and essays, and opposing arguments using shared information.
- Maintain a text-bank audit before production so Unit 2 and Unit 3 standards are not forced into weak or mismatched readings.
- Keep Unit 4 products connected: research notes, synthesis, expository writing, and collaborative multimedia publication should remain one workflow.
- During assessment design, ensure `ELA.10.C.5.1` measures improved understanding of findings, reasoning, and evidence rather than slide appearance alone.
