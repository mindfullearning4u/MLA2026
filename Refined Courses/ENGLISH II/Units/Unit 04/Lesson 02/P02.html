Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Evaluating Source Reliability

Vocabulary

Reliable means the source can be trusted because it uses accurate information and sound evidence.

Credible means the source or author has knowledge, expertise, or authority on the topic.

Valid means the source's evidence actually supports the claim or research need.

Bias means unfair preference for one side that may affect how information is presented.

Loaded language means words chosen to push an emotional reaction instead of fair analysis.

Research Question

How could a school garden support science learning for tenth-grade students during one semester?

Source Cards

Source A: A state university agriculture extension article written by a science education specialist. It explains how school gardens can support observations about plant growth, soil, and ecosystems. It includes classroom examples and was updated in 2025.

Source B: A personal blog post titled "Gardens Are Always Perfect." The author does not list credentials, gives no data, and says, "Only foolish schools avoid gardens."

Source C: A garden supply advertisement that says its tools are the best choice for every school. It describes product prices but does not discuss science learning.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Check relevance. Source A directly connects gardens to science learning, so it fits the research question.

Step 2: Check credibility. Source A is written by a specialist and published by a university extension.

Step 3: Check evidence. Source A includes examples connected to classroom science learning.

Step 4: Check bias. Source B uses loaded language, and Source C is trying to sell products.

Step 5: Choose the source that best answers the question. Source A is strongest for this inquiry.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the research question.
  2. Make a chart with three rows: Source A, Source B, Source C.
  3. For each source, write one note about reliability, credibility, validity, or bias.
  4. Circle the best source for the research question.
  5. Write two sentences explaining your source choice.

Model Example

Best source: Source A

Reason: Source A is most reliable because it is written by a science education specialist, is connected to a university extension, is current, and directly explains science learning in school gardens.
