📘 ENG2 | Unit 04 | Lesson 02

P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Now you will evaluate source validity and bias more closely.

Research Question

How could a pre-order lunch form reduce cafeteria food waste during one semester?

Source Cards

Source D: A school district nutrition report compares tray waste before and after a four-week pre-order lunch pilot. It includes measured waste totals and explains how the data was collected.

Source E: A comment on a social media post says, "Lunch forms are annoying, and nobody should ever use them." The comment gives no evidence.

Source F: A cafeteria menu lists the meals served this week. It is accurate for menu choices but does not discuss waste reduction.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Ask whether the source answers the research question. Source D directly addresses pre-order forms and waste reduction.

Step 2: Ask whether the evidence is valid. Source D uses measured waste totals, so its evidence fits the claim.

Step 3: Look for bias. Source E uses emotional words such as "annoying" and "nobody should ever," but gives no evidence.

Step 4: Remember that a source can be accurate but not useful. Source F may accurately list meals, but it does not answer the waste-reduction question.

Notebook Application

  1. Identify which source is most useful for the research question.
  2. Write one sentence explaining the evidence in that source.
  3. Identify which source shows the most bias.
  4. Write one sentence explaining the loaded language or missing evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Source E is best because it sounds strongly against lunch forms.

Correct: Source D is best because it uses measured waste totals from a pre-order lunch pilot.

Teachable Explanation: Strong emotion is not the same as strong evidence. Reliable research depends on relevant evidence, not just forceful wording.
