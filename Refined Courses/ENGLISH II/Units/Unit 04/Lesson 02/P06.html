P06 — Checkpoint

Checkpoint Submission: Evaluate the source set and answer all 10 questions separately. You must earn at least 80%.

Research question: Which evidence would best help Brookdale decide whether adding shade structures to Central Park could reduce dangerous heat exposure for summer visitors?

Source Set

Source A — City Temperature Survey (2025): Public-works staff measured surface temperatures at marked locations in Central Park on twelve clear summer afternoons. The report includes instruments, times, maps, averages, and limitations.

Source B — ShadePro Company Brochure: A manufacturer says its structures create “perfect safety” and are “the obvious choice for every park.” It gives product specifications but no independent heat-health study.

Source C — University Public-Health Review (2024): Researchers synthesize twenty-eight studies of shade, hydration, and cooling interventions. They explain selection criteria and conclude that shade lowers heat exposure, while warning that design and climate affect results.

Source D — Neighborhood Post: An anonymous writer says Central Park “never feels hot” because the writer visited once on a cloudy morning. No measurements or date are supplied.

Questions

  1. Which source is most directly relevant to Central Park’s current conditions? Explain.
  2. Evaluate Source A’s authority, accuracy, currency, and limitations.
  3. Explain why Source B has a potential financial bias.
  4. Identify two loaded or overstated phrases in Source B.
  5. Evaluate Source C’s credibility and validity for the research question.
  6. Explain one limitation of applying Source C’s general findings to Central Park.
  7. Explain why Source D is weak evidence even though it describes a firsthand visit.
  8. Choose two sources that should be used together and explain how they corroborate or complement each other.
  9. Identify one additional source or type of evidence the city should obtain.
  10. Write a developed source-selection recommendation identifying the strongest and weakest sources and explaining relevance, authority, evidence, currency, purpose, bias, validity, and needed corroboration.
