P05 — Independent Work

Notebook Evidence: Evaluate the source set and answer all 15 questions.

Research question: How might moving North Valley High’s start time from 7:20 a.m. to 8:20 a.m. affect attendance and first-period academic performance during one semester?

Source Set

Source A — State Health Department Brief (2025): Written by adolescent-health researchers. Summarizes sleep recommendations and cites twelve peer-reviewed studies. It discusses statewide patterns but does not report North Valley data.

Source B — North Valley Attendance Records (2023–2025): District records show first-period tardiness and absence rates by month. The records are official primary data, but they were collected before any start-time change and do not measure sleep.

Source C — Peer-Reviewed Multi-School Study (2024): Researchers compare twenty high schools before and after later start times. They explain sampling, controls, limitations, and report modest attendance gains. Participating schools differ from North Valley in transportation arrangements.

Source D — BrightMorning Tutoring Advertisement: The company claims later start times “destroy discipline” and says its early tutoring program is “the only proven answer.” It provides two testimonials but no study, dates, or methods. The company sells before-school tutoring.

Source E — Student Council Survey: Of 410 students who chose to respond, 68 percent preferred a later start and 54 percent predicted they would be less tired. The survey reports wording and totals but does not include nonrespondents or actual attendance outcomes.

Questions

  1. Explain why Source A is credible.
  2. Identify one limitation of Source A’s relevance.
  3. Explain why Source B is authoritative primary evidence for North Valley.
  4. Identify one conclusion Source B cannot validly support by itself.
  5. Evaluate Source C’s authority, accuracy, and validity.
  6. Explain how the transportation difference limits applying Source C to North Valley.
  7. Identify Source D’s purpose and financial interest.
  8. Quote two examples of loaded language or unsupported claims from Source D.
  9. Evaluate the strengths and limitations of Source E.
  10. Explain why preference and predicted tiredness are not the same as measured outcomes.
  11. Rank the five sources from most to least useful for the research question and justify the order.
  12. Select two sources that could corroborate or complement one another and explain how.
  13. Identify one important perspective or type of evidence missing from the set.
  14. Write a source-evaluation paragraph comparing the strongest and weakest sources.
  15. Write a developed recommendation explaining which sources a researcher should use, how each would contribute, what claims should remain limited, and what additional evidence is needed.

Success Check

Use the exact research question. Judge each source by authority, evidence, currency, purpose, bias, relevance, validity, and corroboration—not by whether you agree with it.
