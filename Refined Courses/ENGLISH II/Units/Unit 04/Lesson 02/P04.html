P04 — Worked Example

Follow each model from research question to evidence-based source judgment.

Worked Example 1 — Relevant and Valid

Question: How did a four-week pre-order lunch pilot affect cafeteria food waste?

Source: A district nutrition report identifies the schools, dates, weighing method, weekly waste totals, and limitations.

  1. It is relevant because it studies the same intervention and outcome.
  2. The district nutrition office has institutional authority for cafeteria records.
  3. The documented method makes the data checkable.
  4. The measured before-and-after evidence can validly support a limited conclusion about the pilot.

Judgment: Strong source, although another independent study should corroborate the result before making a broad claim.

Worked Example 2 — Credible but Not Relevant

Question: How might later library hours affect access for students with after-school jobs?

Source: A peer-reviewed article by transportation researchers analyzes late bus routes in three cities.

  1. The authors and review process support credibility.
  2. The article may be accurate and current.
  3. However, it does not examine library hours or library use.

Judgment: Credible for transportation research, but not directly relevant enough to answer this library-access question.

Worked Example 3 — Bias and Weak Evidence

Question: Should the school test a composting program?

Source: A company selling compost bins claims its product is “the only sensible solution” and reports that “schools love it,” but provides no schools, dates, methods, or data.

  1. The sales purpose creates a financial interest.
  2. Loaded language signals advocacy.
  3. The broad claims cannot be checked.
  4. The evidence does not validly support the conclusion.

Judgment: The advertisement may identify a product, but it is weak evidence for deciding whether the program works. Use independent sanitation reports and school records instead.

Final Check

A strong evaluation names the source’s strengths, limitations, purpose, and fit for the exact question. It does not simply label a source “good” or “bad.”
