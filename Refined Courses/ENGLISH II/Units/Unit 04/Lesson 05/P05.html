Independent Work: Integrating Sources About School Transit

Notebook Evidence: Read all three sources and answer the 15 questions. Submit these answers with your Lesson 5 vocabulary and definitions.

Source A — Transit Pilot Report

A semester pilot added one late bus route on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Average ridership reached forty-six students, with the highest use during tutoring and club seasons. Administrators reported fewer requests for emergency rides, but the single route created long travel times for students living farthest from campus.

Source B — Family Survey

Families generally supported the service because it allowed students to participate in academic help and activities. Some caregivers, however, said the route’s unpredictable arrival time complicated evening responsibilities. Respondents favored a tracking notification and two shorter routes if funding permitted.

Source C — Budget Review

The late bus cost less than contracting individual transportation for missed rides. Two routes would improve travel time but increase driver and fuel expenses. The review recommends continuing one route while collecting neighborhood-level ridership data, then adding service only where demand is sustained.

Questions

  1. Write a focused claim supported by the source set.
  2. Choose one Source A detail and integrate it with a signal phrase.
  3. Follow that evidence with a sentence of analysis.
  4. Paraphrase the limitation in Source A and credit it.
  5. Integrate one Source B finding using a transition that signals contrast.
  6. Explain why that transition is accurate.
  7. Blend a short, meaningful quotation of no more than six words from one source into your own sentence.
  8. Write a synthesis sentence connecting Sources B and C.
  9. Choose the best transition between the pilot’s benefits and costs and justify it.
  10. Revise “The late bus is totally amazing and fixes transportation” into academic voice.
  11. Write one measured claim using may, can, or when.
  12. Identify and correct a tone shift in this sentence: “The report recommends more data, and honestly that is kind of annoying.”
  13. Write a three-sentence evidence sandwich using any source.
  14. Combine findings from all three sources in a cohesive 4–5 sentence passage.
  15. Write a 7–9 sentence expository paragraph recommending a next step. Integrate at least two sources, use accurate transitions, maintain consistent academic voice, and explain every piece of evidence.
Quality check: Every quotation or paraphrase has context, credit, and analysis; transitions match meaning; tone and voice remain consistent.
