Checkpoint: Integrating Sources About Device Lending

Checkpoint Submission: Read the new source set and answer all 10 questions independently. A score of 80% is required; revise from TOR feedback if needed.

Source D — Technology Office Summary

A laptop-lending pilot served 118 students during one semester. Most loans supported research projects or homework during device repairs. The office recorded a high return rate, though chargers were frequently misplaced and staff time was needed for inspection and updates.

Source E — Student Interview Findings

Students said borrowed devices prevented missed assignments and reduced stress when home computers failed. Several described the checkout process as “quick and respectful.” Others requested weekend loans and clearer instructions for saving files before returning a device.

Source F — Program Recommendation

The review recommends continuing the program with labeled charger kits, a short digital-use guide, and scheduled maintenance. Weekend expansion should begin as a limited trial because staffing and repair costs are not yet known.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Write a defensible claim reflecting all three sources.
  2. Integrate the number of students served using a source signal and accurate paraphrase.
  3. Write one sentence explaining why that evidence matters.
  4. Blend the phrase “quick and respectful” into your own sentence and credit Source E.
  5. Use a purposeful transition to connect program benefits with a limitation.
  6. Explain why your transition fits the relationship.
  7. Write a synthesis sentence combining Sources D and F.
  8. Revise “The program is perfect and everybody loves it” into precise academic voice.
  9. Create a complete three-sentence evidence sandwich using one source.
  10. Write a 7–9 sentence recommendation integrating at least two sources. Use purposeful transitions, accurate source credit, evidence analysis, and consistent tone and voice.
Before submitting: Check that evidence is introduced and explained, transitions match meaning, and claims do not exceed the sources.
