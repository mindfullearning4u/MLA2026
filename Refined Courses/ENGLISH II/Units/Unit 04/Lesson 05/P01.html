P01 Lesson Overview: Lesson 5: Source Integration, Purposeful Transitions, Tone, and Voice

Lesson Title

Lesson 5: Source Integration, Purposeful Transitions, Tone, and Voice

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG2.W.03 - Write Expository Texts with Voice.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG2.RS.01 - Conduct and Synthesize Reliable Research; MLA.ENG2.W.04 - Revise for Audience Needs; MLA.ENG2.L.01 - Apply Standard English Conventions.

What You Will Learn

  • How to integrate source information smoothly into expository writing.
  • How to choose transitions that show relationships between findings.
  • How to revise tone and voice for a research-based audience.

Why This Matters

Smooth source integration and purposeful transitions help readers follow complex information. Audience-aware tone strengthens credibility in academic and workplace writing.

What You Will Do

  • Read research findings included in the lesson.
  • Practice introducing source evidence and explaining it.
  • Revise sentences for purposeful transitions, academic tone, and clear voice.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Write the lesson vocabulary words and definitions in your notebook.
  2. Complete the Lesson and Worked Example pages.
  3. Complete the Independent Work. Submit your vocabulary and definitions plus the Independent Work in the Notebook Evidence Submission activity.
  4. Complete the Checkpoint and submit those answers separately in the Checkpoint Submission activity.
  5. Complete the lesson quiz or unit assessment when assigned.
  6. If you earn below 80%, review TOR feedback, correct your mistakes, and meet with your TOR when additional help is needed.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your writing introduces source information clearly.
  • Your transitions show cause, contrast, addition, or result accurately.
  • Your revised paragraph uses formal, objective, audience-aware voice.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can write an expository paragraph that uses source evidence smoothly, connects ideas with transitions, and sounds appropriate for an academic audience.
