Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Integrating Source Evidence

Vocabulary

Source integration means placing source information into your own writing smoothly and clearly.

Lead-in is a phrase that introduces where source information came from.

Transition is a word or phrase that shows how ideas connect.

Tone is the attitude created by word choice.

Voice is the overall sound of the writing for its audience and task.

Research Findings

Question: How could pre-order lunch forms reduce cafeteria food waste?

Source A: A district report found that the pre-order pilot reduced unwanted trays because staff prepared meals based on student selections.

Source B: A cafeteria manager interview explained that student reminders helped more students complete the form after the first week.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Start with your own point: Pre-order forms may reduce waste by helping staff prepare only the meals students choose.

Step 2: Add a lead-in: A district report found that...

Step 3: Add the source information in your own sentence.

Step 4: Explain why the evidence matters. The reader should understand how the evidence answers the research question.

Step 5: Use a transition when adding another source, such as "In addition" or "However."

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the research question.
  2. Write one sentence that introduces Source A with a lead-in.
  3. Write one sentence explaining why Source A matters.
  4. Add Source B with a transition.

Model Example

Integrated evidence: A district report found that the pre-order pilot reduced unwanted trays because staff prepared meals from student selections.

Explanation: This finding matters because it shows how the form changed the cafeteria's preparation process, which directly connects to waste reduction.

Transition to Source B: In addition, the cafeteria manager explained that reminders helped more students complete the form.
