Worked Examples

These examples model source integration, transitions, tone, and voice.

Example 1

Source fact: The pilot reduced unwanted trays.

Integrated sentence: A district report found that the pre-order pilot reduced unwanted trays after staff prepared meals from student selections.

Why it works: The sentence names the source type and connects the finding to the process.

Example 2

Relationship: A second source adds related support.

Transition: In addition

Sentence: In addition, a cafeteria manager explained that reminders helped more students complete the form after the first week.

Example 3

Informal: The reminders were a big deal and fixed the form thing.

Academic: The reminders improved the routine by helping more students complete the form consistently.

Why it works: The academic version is precise, formal, and connected to the research finding.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The report said stuff and another source also had stuff.

Correct: A district report found that pre-order forms reduced unwanted trays; in addition, a cafeteria manager explained that reminders helped students use the form consistently.

Why this matters: Source integration should identify findings and show how they connect.
