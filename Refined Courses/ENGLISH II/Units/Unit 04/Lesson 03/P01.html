P01 Lesson Overview: Lesson 3: Note-Taking, Paraphrase, and Source Synthesis

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Note-Taking, Paraphrase, and Source Synthesis

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG2.RS.01 - Conduct and Synthesize Reliable Research.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG2.R.10 - Paraphrase Grade-Level Texts; MLA.ENG2.W.03 - Write Expository Texts with Voice; MLA.ENG2.L.01 - Apply Standard English Conventions.

What You Will Learn

  • How to take notes that preserve the meaning of a source.
  • How to paraphrase source information accurately in your own words.
  • How to synthesize information by combining ideas from more than one reliable source.

Why This Matters

Accurate notes, paraphrasing, and synthesis help you learn from sources without copying or distorting them. These skills support ethical academic and professional communication.

What You Will Do

  • Read source excerpts included in the lesson.
  • Separate useful notes from copied wording.
  • Practice accurate paraphrase and source synthesis.
  • Prepare to use notes in expository writing.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Write the lesson vocabulary words and definitions in your notebook.
  2. Complete the Lesson and Worked Example pages.
  3. Complete the Independent Work. Submit your vocabulary and definitions plus the Independent Work in the Notebook Evidence Submission activity.
  4. Complete the Checkpoint and submit those answers separately in the Checkpoint Submission activity.
  5. Complete the lesson quiz or unit assessment when assigned.
  6. If you earn below 80%, review TOR feedback, correct your mistakes, and meet with your TOR when additional help is needed.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notes identify useful source information without copying full sentences unnecessarily.
  • Your paraphrase keeps the source meaning accurate.
  • Your synthesis combines information from multiple sources to answer one research question.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can gather useful information from reliable sources, restate it accurately, and combine source ideas to answer a research question.
