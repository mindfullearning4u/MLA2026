Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Notes, Paraphrase, and Source Synthesis

Vocabulary

Note-taking means recording important source information in a brief, organized way.

Paraphrase means restating source information accurately in your own words.

Synthesis means combining information from more than one source to explain a larger idea.

Source evidence is information from a source that helps answer a research question.

Expository writing explains or analyzes information using clear organization and an appropriate voice.

Research Question and Sources

Research question: How could a school garden support science learning for tenth-grade students during one semester?

Source A: A state university extension article explains that school gardens let students observe plant growth, soil conditions, and ecosystem relationships over time.

Source B: A biology teacher guide explains that garden journals can help students record observations, compare plant changes, and connect evidence to science vocabulary.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Start with the research question. Only take notes that help answer it.

Step 2: Record short notes, not whole copied paragraphs. A useful note from Source A is: gardens support observations of plant growth, soil, and ecosystems.

Step 3: Paraphrase accurately. Do not change "observe plant growth" into "plants always grow better."

Step 4: Synthesize by connecting sources. Source A explains what students can observe; Source B explains how journals help students record and analyze those observations.

Step 5: Write the synthesis in your own sentence using both sources.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the research question.
  2. Write one useful note from Source A.
  3. Write one useful note from Source B.
  4. Paraphrase one source note accurately.
  5. Write one synthesis sentence that uses both sources.

Model Example

Source A note: Students can observe plant growth, soil, and ecosystem relationships.

Source B note: Garden journals help students record observations and connect them to vocabulary.

Synthesis: Together, the sources show that a school garden can support science learning by giving students real observations to record, compare, and explain with science vocabulary.
