Worked Examples

These examples model notes, paraphrase, and synthesis.

Example 1

Source detail: Garden journals help students record observations over time.

Useful note: Journals support repeated science observations.

Why it works: The note is short, accurate, and connected to the research question.

Example 2

Original: Cafeteria staff prepared meals based on student selections.

Paraphrase: The cafeteria used students' meal choices to decide how much food to prepare.

Why it works: The paraphrase uses different wording but keeps the same meaning.

Example 3

Source A: Gardens let students observe plant growth.

Source B: Journals help students record and compare observations.

Synthesis: A school garden can support science learning when students observe plant growth and use journals to track evidence over time.

Why it works: The sentence combines two sources into one explanation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Source A says gardens are about plants. Source B says journals exist.

Correct: Source A and Source B together show that gardens create observations that students can record and analyze in journals.

Why this matters: Synthesis explains the relationship between sources instead of listing them separately.
