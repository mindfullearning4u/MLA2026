Checkpoint: School-Day Start-Time Research

Checkpoint Submission: Read this new source set and answer all 10 questions independently in complete sentences. A score of 80% or higher is required. If you do not earn 80%, use TOR feedback and revise your work.

Research Question

What should a high school consider before changing its daily start time?

Source D — Transportation Director Memo

Moving the high school start time forty-five minutes later would require changes to the district’s three-tier bus schedule. The district could make the change without purchasing buses, but some elementary routes would also shift. The memo recommends testing revised routes before adopting a permanent schedule and warns that afternoon athletic travel could begin later.

Source E — Student Wellness Survey

In a survey of 612 high school students, 71 percent reported sleeping fewer than eight hours on school nights. Students who worked evening jobs were especially likely to report difficulty waking for school. A majority favored a later start, although some students worried that later dismissal would interfere with employment, athletics, or caring for younger siblings.

Source F — Principal’s Pilot Summary

During a six-week delayed-start pilot, first-period tardiness declined and teachers reported more alert participation. Attendance for the full day changed very little. Families received transportation updates before the pilot, and the school opened an early supervised study space for students whose caregivers could not alter work schedules. The principal recommends a longer trial before drawing conclusions about grades.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Write one traceable note from each source that directly answers the research question.
  2. Which Source D detail limits or qualifies the claim that changing the start time is easy?
  3. Paraphrase the statistic in Source E without copying its sentence structure.
  4. Summarize Source F in one accurate sentence.
  5. Explain one point of agreement between Sources E and F.
  6. Explain how Source D adds a different perspective from Sources E and F.
  7. Identify one claim that the sources do not
  8. Write one synthesis sentence using evidence from two sources and a transition that shows their relationship.
  9. Write a second synthesis sentence using all three sources to identify a condition for a successful schedule change.
  10. Write a 6–8 sentence recommendation to the school board. Integrate at least two paraphrases and one short quotation or precise statistic, credit every source, and explain the relationship among the evidence.
Before submitting: Confirm that every paraphrase changes wording and structure, every source is credited, and your recommendation accounts for both benefits and limitations.
