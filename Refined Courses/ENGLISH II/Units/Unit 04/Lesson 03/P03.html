Notebook Task Part 2

Now you will decide which notes are useful and revise copied wording into accurate paraphrase.

Source Excerpt

Source C: A district nutrition report states that a four-week pre-order lunch pilot reduced the number of unwanted trays because cafeteria staff prepared meals based on student selections. The report also notes that student reminders were needed during the first week because some students forgot to complete the form.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Identify the research question. If the question asks about cafeteria waste, the note about fewer unwanted trays is useful.

Step 2: Keep the source meaning. The report does not say the pre-order form was perfect; it says reminders were needed.

Step 3: Paraphrase both the benefit and limitation.

Step 4: A strong synthesis can explain that a solution may work while still needing support to be successful.

Notebook Application

  1. Write one useful note about the pre-order lunch pilot.
  2. Write one limitation or challenge from the source.
  3. Paraphrase the source in one sentence.
  4. Explain how the note could be used in a research paragraph.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The form was perfect and solved all lunch problems.

Correct: The pre-order form reduced unwanted trays, but students needed reminders during the first week.

Why this matters: A paraphrase must keep the full meaning of the source, including limitations.
