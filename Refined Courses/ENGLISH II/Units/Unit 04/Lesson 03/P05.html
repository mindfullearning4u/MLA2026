Independent Work: Community Cooling Research

Notebook Evidence: Read the three sources and answer all 15 questions in complete sentences. Submit these responses with your Unit 4 Lesson 3 vocabulary and definitions.

Research Question

How can a city reduce dangerous summer heat in neighborhoods with few trees?

Source A — City Heat Office Brief

Temperature mapping conducted during July found that blocks with little shade were as much as 8°F warmer in late afternoon than nearby tree-lined streets. Dark roofs and wide paved lots absorbed heat throughout the day and released it slowly after sunset. The brief recommends planting trees near sidewalks, bus stops, and apartment buildings. It cautions that young trees require watering and several years of growth before they provide full shade.

Source B — Public Works Pilot Report

A two-year pilot applied reflective coating to roofs on three public buildings and resurfaced one schoolyard with lighter material. Roof surface temperatures fell substantially on sunny days, and indoor cooling costs declined at two buildings. The schoolyard felt cooler during afternoon programs, but maintenance crews reported that the coating showed dirt and required periodic cleaning. The report concludes that reflective surfaces can provide quicker relief than newly planted trees but do not supply habitat or improve air quality.

Source C — Neighborhood Association Testimony

Residents support more shade but want the city to involve them in selecting locations. Several speakers explained that earlier tree projects failed when species damaged sidewalks or when watering ended too soon. Others asked for immediate measures at bus stops where older adults wait in direct sun. The association proposes a combined plan: temporary shade structures at the hottest stops, reflective materials on suitable public property, and long-term tree care agreements with neighborhood groups.

Questions

  1. Which details from Source A directly answer the research question? Record two traceable notes.
  2. Which detail from Source A is a limitation, and why must it remain in an accurate note?
  3. Paraphrase the sentence about the 8°F temperature difference. Change both wording and structure.
  4. Summarize Source A in one sentence.
  5. Record two relevant notes from Source B and label each with its source.
  6. Explain why the cleaning concern is relevant rather than a minor unrelated detail.
  7. Paraphrase Source B’s conclusion about speed and additional benefits.
  8. Summarize the central proposal in Source C.
  9. Identify one reason Source C qualifies or complicates Source A’s recommendation.
  10. Explain one point on which Sources A and C agree.
  11. Explain one important contrast between Sources A and B.
  12. Write a synthesis sentence that combines evidence from Sources A and B.
  13. Write a synthesis sentence that combines evidence from all three sources.
  14. Choose one fact worth quoting directly and explain why its exact wording or number matters. Use quotation marks and a source label.
  15. Write a 5–7 sentence research paragraph answering the question. Integrate at least two sources, use a signal phrase or citation for each, and explain how the evidence supports your conclusion.
Quality check: Your notes are traceable, paraphrases preserve meaning without copying structure, and synthesis explains relationships rather than listing sources.
