Checkpoint: Research-to-Publication Synthesis

Checkpoint Submission: Complete all 10 questions independently. Earn at least 80%; revise using TOR feedback if needed.

Source Set: Expanding Weekend Library Service

D — Usage Report: A Saturday pilot averaged 93 visitors, with computers and study rooms in highest demand. Attendance was lowest on holiday weekends.

E — Community Survey: Families valued weekend access for internet, tutoring, and quiet study. Respondents requested predictable schedules and transit information.

F — Budget Review: Targeted Saturdays cost less than weekly expansion. The review recommends a six-month trial, attendance tracking, and schedule adjustments based on demand.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Write a focused research question.
  2. Evaluate the usefulness and limitation of each source.
  3. Write one traceable paraphrase from each source.
  4. Synthesize the three sources in two sentences, showing both agreement and a condition for success.
  5. Write a controlling idea and select a logical organizational pattern.
  6. Create a three-section outline organized by findings.
  7. Write a 7–9 sentence expository recommendation integrating all three sources with accurate transitions and analysis.
  8. Define a target audience and plan one purposeful visual with caption, source label, and alt text.
  9. List five final publication checks covering evidence, organization, language, accessibility, and technical function.
  10. Write a 5–7 sentence rationale explaining why your final communication is credible, coherent, audience-appropriate, and publication-ready.
Before submitting: Check alignment across research question, source use, synthesis, organization, audience, media, and publication quality.
