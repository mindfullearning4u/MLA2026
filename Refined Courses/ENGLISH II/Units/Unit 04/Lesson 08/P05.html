Inquiry Topic: Improving Outdoor Learning

Source A — District Pilot Report

Four schools tested weekly outdoor lessons for one semester. Teachers reported strong engagement during observation, writing, and environmental-science tasks. Scheduling conflicts and weather reduced consistency at two schools. The report recommends clearly defined learning goals, backup indoor plans, and accessible seating rather than simply moving ordinary lessons outside.

Source B — Student Survey

Among 487 respondents, 68% said occasional outdoor learning improved attention. Students valued hands-on activities and a change of setting, but some reported glare, heat, insects, and difficulty hearing instructions. Respondents favored shaded locations, short activity segments, and advance notice about materials or clothing.

Source C — Accessibility and Facilities Review

The review identified two courtyards with accessible paths and nearby restrooms. It recommends stable seating, shade, clear walking routes, amplified instructions when needed, and digital copies of materials. Costs vary by location; the review advises improving one site first and collecting use data before expanding.

Questions

  1. Write a focused, researchable question for this source set.
  2. Evaluate each source’s authority, evidence, relevance, and possible limitation.
  3. Record one traceable note from each source.
  4. Paraphrase the 68% statistic without copying structure.
  5. Identify one limitation that must remain in any recommendation.
  6. Write a synthesis statement showing agreement among the sources.
  7. Write a synthesis statement showing how one source qualifies another.
  8. Develop a controlling idea answering your research question.
  9. Choose and justify an organizational pattern.
  10. Create a four-section outline organized by idea rather than by source.
  11. Write a 7–9 sentence expository paragraph integrating all three sources with purposeful transitions and evidence analysis.
  12. Define a target audience and desired action for a multimedia publication.
  13. Plan one purposeful visual, write its caption and alt text, and identify its source.
  14. Assign four team roles and create a five-item accuracy/accessibility/publication checklist.
  15. Write a 6–8 sentence publication rationale explaining how research question, evidence, organization, voice, media, and accessibility work together.
Quality check: Every stage—from question to publication—remains aligned, accurate, ethical, audience-centered, and accessible.
