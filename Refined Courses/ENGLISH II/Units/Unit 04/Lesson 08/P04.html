Worked Examples

These examples model the full Unit 4 process.

Example 1: Research Question to Evidence

Question: How can student pre-order forms help reduce unwanted lunch trays?

Step 1: Choose evidence that directly answers the question.

Step 2: Use the pilot report because it gives an outcome connected to unwanted trays.

Answer: The pilot report is relevant because it found fewer unwanted trays after staff prepared meals from student selections.

Example 2: Evidence to Expository Explanation

Evidence: Reminders helped more students complete the form after the first week.

Step 1: Explain why the evidence matters.

Step 2: Connect reminders to consistent form completion.

Explanation: This finding matters because the pre-order process works better when students complete the form consistently.

Example 3: Expository Explanation to Publication

Draft Message: Completing the form helps staff prepare meals students are more likely to choose.

Step 1: Decide what the audience needs.

Step 2: Add a clear visual and action step.

Publication Choice: Pair the message with a labeled before-and-after chart and a direct reminder to complete the form when it opens.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Use any source, write a paragraph, and add any picture at the end.

Correct Example:

Use reliable evidence that answers the research question, explain the evidence in an organized paragraph, revise for students, and choose a visual that makes the finding easier to understand.

Teachable Explanation: Unit 4 is a connected process. Research, writing, revision, and publication should work together toward the same audience and purpose.
