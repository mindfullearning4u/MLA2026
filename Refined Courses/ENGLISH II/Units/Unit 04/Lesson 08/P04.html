Worked Examples: From Inquiry to Publication

Worked Example 1: Refine and Evaluate

Broad topic: School food waste.

Focused question: Which combination of meal preordering and student reminders most effectively reduces discarded lunches in a high school?

Source choice: Prefer a recent district waste audit with methods and measurements over an unsigned promotional post claiming a program is “perfect.” The audit supplies verifiable evidence; the post may reveal opinion but cannot establish effectiveness.

Worked Example 2: Notes to Synthesis Paragraph

A: Preordering reduced unwanted trays 28%.

B: Students forgot forms during week one.

C: Reminder messages improved participation.

Model: Meal preordering can reduce waste when schools also support consistent participation. A district pilot recorded 28% fewer unwanted trays, while early missed forms revealed a barrier to implementation. Because reminders improved completion, the sources together suggest that the system’s success depends on both accurate meal counts and clear communication.

Reasoning: The paragraph preserves benefits and limitations, uses transitions by meaning, integrates sources, and explains a condition for success.

Worked Example 3: Adapt and Publish

  • Leadership brief: method, 28% result, limitation, cost, recommendation.
  • Student infographic: simple headline, before/after chart, three action steps, source caption, alt text.
  • Family message: concise explanation of preordering, reminder schedule, and how families can help.

Final audit: verify every number and quotation; maintain consistent tone and headings; credit media; add captions and alt text; test links; preview phone and desktop views.

Common mistake: Reusing identical wording and detail for every audience ignores the purpose of communication.
