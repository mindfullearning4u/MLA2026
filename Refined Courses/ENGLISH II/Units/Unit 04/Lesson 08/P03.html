Putting It All Together — Unit 4 Synthesis

The Research-to-Publication Process

This lesson combines inquiry, source evaluation, note-taking, synthesis, expository writing, source integration, multimedia design, collaboration, and final publication review.

Step 1: Refine the research question

Make the question focused, researchable, significant, and open to evidence rather than a simple yes/no answer.

Step 2: Evaluate sources

Check authority, currency, evidence, purpose, relevance, corroboration, and bias. A useful source can contain a perspective, but its claims must still be supported and appropriately used.

Step 3: Record accurate, traceable notes

Label quotations, paraphrases, and summaries; record source information; preserve limitations; and group notes by idea.

Step 4: Synthesize findings

Explain where sources agree, differ, qualify one another, or reveal cause, effect, problem, solution, or condition.

Step 5: Organize an expository explanation

Choose a structure that matches purpose. Build a controlling idea, logical sections, topic sentences, evidence-analysis pairs, purposeful transitions, and a conclusion.

Step 6: Adapt for audience and media

Select detail, vocabulary, tone, visuals, audio, or interactive elements because they help a specific audience understand and act. Build accessibility into the design.

Step 7: Publish responsibly

Audit claims, citations, organization, language, permissions, accessibility, links, layout, file naming, and final student-facing rendering.

Common Synthesis Mistakes

  • Beginning with a broad topic instead of a focused question.
  • Treating popularity as proof of credibility.
  • Patchwriting or losing source limitations.
  • Listing sources without explaining relationships.
  • Adding media or transitions without purpose.
  • Publishing before checking accuracy, accessibility, and final rendering.
