Worked Examples

Each example shows how a research question becomes more focused and useful.

Example 1

Broad topic: Recycling

Too broad: Is recycling important?

Step 1: Add a place: at school.

Step 2: Add a purpose: reducing cafeteria waste.

Focused question: How could a school recycling program reduce cafeteria waste during one semester?

Example 2

Broad topic: Public transportation

Too narrow: What time does bus 14 stop at Oak Street on Monday?

Step 1: Keep the local focus.

Step 2: Ask a question that needs research and analysis.

Focused question: How could later bus service affect after-school job access for high school students in one neighborhood?

Example 3

Broad topic: School gardens

Opinion question: Why should everyone love school gardens?

Step 1: Remove biased wording.

Step 2: Ask about an effect that evidence could show.

Focused question: What effect could a school garden have on student participation in science observations?

Common Mistake

Incorrect: What about transportation?

Correct: How could later bus service affect after-school job access for high school students in one neighborhood?

Why this matters: The incorrect question is only a vague topic. The correct question names the issue, possible effect, audience, and place.
