Research Brief: Expanding Community Library Access

The Easton Public Library closes at 6:00 p.m. on weekdays. During a three-month survey, 46 percent of high school respondents said the closing time made it difficult to use library computers after sports, jobs, or family responsibilities. Adult respondents most often requested weekend technology workshops and additional quiet study areas.

The library board is considering three possibilities: remaining open until 8:00 p.m. two nights per week, adding Sunday afternoon hours, or creating a small mobile technology lab that could visit schools and community centers. The budget can support only one pilot program for one semester. Staffing estimates, attendance records, survey results, transportation maps, and reports from neighboring libraries are available for research.

The board wants a recommendation that identifies which option could improve access for a clearly defined group. A useful research project must be completed in five weeks. It should not attempt to solve every library-access problem or assume that one option is automatically best.

Questions

  1. State the broad research topic.
  2. Identify the three possible inquiry focuses.
  3. Choose one clearly defined group whose access could be studied.
  4. Write a question that is too broad and explain why it is unmanageable.
  5. Write a biased question and identify the wording that assumes an answer.
  6. Revise the question so it is neutral, open-ended, and focused on one pilot option.
  7. Refine its scope by adding a population, location, time frame, effect, or comparison.
  8. Evaluate the question’s feasibility using the five-week limit and available sources.
  9. Write two relevant subquestions and identify the evidence needed for each.
  10. Present your final research question and write a developed explanation of its inquiry focus, scope, relevance, neutrality, feasibility, and research purpose.
