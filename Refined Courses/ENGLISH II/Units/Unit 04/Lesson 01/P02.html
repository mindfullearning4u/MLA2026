Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Research Questions and Inquiry Focus

Vocabulary

Research topic is the general subject you want to learn about.

Research question is the focused question your research will answer.

Inquiry means the process of asking questions, searching for evidence, and adjusting your thinking based on what you find.

Scope means the size and limits of the research question.

Refine means to make a question clearer, narrower, and more researchable.

Research Scenario

Topic: School gardens

Student goal: The student wants to research whether a school garden could help students learn and support the school community.

Important note: This lesson includes all information you need. You do not need to search outside this page to complete the notebook task.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Start with the broad topic. "School gardens" is a topic, but it is not yet a research question.

Step 2: Ask what you want to learn. A research question begins with words such as how, why, what effect, or to what extent.

Step 3: Limit the scope. Decide the audience, place, time, problem, cause, effect, or solution you will study.

Step 4: Make sure the question needs evidence. A strong research question cannot be answered only by personal opinion.

Step 5: Check whether the question can fit the assignment. If the question would need a whole book to answer, narrow it.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Write the broad topic: school gardens.
  4. Write one research question about school gardens.
  5. Underline the part of the question that limits the scope.

Model Example

Too broad: Are school gardens good?

Better research question: How could a school garden support science learning for ninth- and tenth-grade students during one semester?

Why it is better: The revised question names the topic, the possible effect, the student group, and the time frame.
