Independent Work: Multimedia Water-Conservation Campaign

Notebook Evidence: Read the project brief and source set, then answer all 15 questions. Submit these answers with your Lesson 6 vocabulary and definitions.

Project Brief

Your team must plan a three-part digital campaign encouraging students to reduce unnecessary water use at school. The audience is grades 9–12. The publication will include one infographic, one 60-second video script, and one short announcement.

Source A — Facilities Audit

The audit estimated that leaking fixtures and faucets left running during transitions accounted for substantial avoidable use. Repairs reduced measured consumption, but staff noted that student habits remained important. The audit recommends reporting leaks quickly and turning water off completely.

Source B — Student Survey

Most students knew conservation mattered, yet fewer could name a school-specific action. Students preferred short videos and visual reminders over long email messages. Many said they would report leaks if a simple reporting method were clearly posted.

Source C — Accessibility Review

The review recommends large text, strong color contrast, captions, a transcript for audio, descriptive link wording, and alt text for meaningful images. It cautions that color alone should not communicate categories or urgency.

Questions

  1. State the campaign’s purpose, audience, and desired action.
  2. Select three essential findings and explain why each belongs.
  3. Identify one detail that can be omitted and justify the decision.
  4. Write a student-friendly headline that remains accurate.
  5. Plan the infographic’s visual hierarchy from first element to last.
  6. Choose one chart or visual and explain what it teaches better than prose.
  7. Write useful alt text for that visual.
  8. Draft a 60-second video outline with opening, evidence, action, and closing.
  9. Write a caption or transcript requirement for the video.
  10. Draft the short announcement in 60–80 words.
  11. Explain how tone and vocabulary fit grades 9–12.
  12. Assign four collaborative roles and list one responsibility for each.
  13. Create a three-item shared style guide for voice and design consistency.
  14. Write a five-step publication review checklist covering accuracy, audience, media purpose, accessibility, and technical function.
  15. Explain in 6–8 sentences how the three publication pieces work together without merely repeating one another.
Quality check: Every design choice serves purpose and audience, media teach rather than decorate, source information is accurate, and accessibility is built in.
