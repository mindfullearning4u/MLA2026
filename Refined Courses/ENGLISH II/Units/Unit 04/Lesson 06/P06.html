Checkpoint: Digital Campaign for Library Access

Checkpoint Submission: Complete all 10 questions independently. Earn at least 80%; use TOR feedback to correct and resubmit if needed.

Project Brief and Findings

A library wants a web page and social-media graphic explaining new evening hours to students and families. A pilot recorded a 34% rise in student visits on project nights. Survey comments show that jobs, athletics, and caregiving often prevent earlier visits. The library offers computers, research support, and quiet study space. The web page must work on phones and include accessible headings, contrast, alt text, and descriptive links.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Define the publication’s purpose, two audiences, and desired action.
  2. Explain how the message should differ for students and families.
  3. Write a precise web-page headline and two supporting subheadings.
  4. Integrate the 34% statistic in one audience-appropriate sentence.
  5. Plan the order of four web-page content sections and justify the sequence.
  6. Describe a purposeful social-media visual and write its alt text.
  7. Draft a 40–60 word social-media caption with a clear action step.
  8. Assign four collaborative roles and identify how the team will maintain consistent voice and design.
  9. Identify three accessibility checks and explain why each matters.
  10. Write a 6–8 sentence publication rationale explaining how evidence, organization, media, tone, and accessibility serve the audiences.
Before submitting: Confirm that the message is accurate, audience-centered, readable on a screen, accessible, and technically complete.
