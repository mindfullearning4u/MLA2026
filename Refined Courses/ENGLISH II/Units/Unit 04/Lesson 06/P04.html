Worked Examples

These examples model audience-aware multimedia publication choices.

Example 1

Audience: Students.

Weak wording: Advance meal-selection compliance improves tray-output efficiency.

Better wording: When students complete the lunch form, cafeteria staff can prepare fewer unwanted trays.

Why it works: The revised sentence is accurate and easier for the audience to understand.

Example 2

Weak visual: A random picture of vegetables.

Better visual: A labeled three-step flow chart: complete form, staff prepare selected meals, fewer trays are unwanted.

Why it works: The visual explains the research finding.

Example 3

Team roles: Writer, evidence checker, visual designer, audience reviewer.

Why this works: Each role supports a different part of publication quality.

Result: The team can keep the message accurate, clear, and visually helpful.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The visual can be unrelated as long as it is colorful.

Correct: The visual should help the audience understand the research finding or action step.

Why this matters: Publication design should support meaning and audience understanding.
