Independent Work: Organizing Findings About Public Libraries

Notebook Evidence: Read the source set and answer all 15 questions. Submit these answers with your Lesson 4 vocabulary and definitions.

Source A — Library Access Report

A county review found that evening library visits rose after three branches remained open until 8:00 p.m. Students made up much of the increase, especially during research-project weeks. Computer use also increased because some households lacked reliable broadband. Staffing costs rose modestly, and two branches reported that attendance after 7:30 p.m. was inconsistent.

Source B — Youth Advisory Council Statement

Students described libraries as quiet places for homework, group planning, and internet access. Many could not arrive before the traditional closing time because of sports, jobs, or family responsibilities. The council supported later hours but suggested scheduling them on the busiest weekdays instead of every night. It also recommended posting schedules well in advance.

Source C — Budget Director Analysis

The director compared three options: extending every branch nightly, extending selected branches on high-demand days, or maintaining current hours. Targeted extensions cost less than nightly expansion and allowed the county to adjust staffing according to use. The analysis cautioned that attendance data should be reviewed each semester so hours can change when community needs shift.

Questions

  1. State a controlling idea that explains what the findings collectively show.
  2. Which organizational pattern best fits these findings? Explain your choice.
  3. Group the evidence into two logical body-paragraph subtopics.
  4. Write a topic sentence for the first subtopic.
  5. Select and paraphrase two pieces of evidence for that paragraph.
  6. Write two sentences explaining how that evidence supports the topic sentence.
  7. Write a transition into the second subtopic that accurately signals the relationship.
  8. Write a topic sentence for the second subtopic.
  9. Integrate one finding from Source B and one from Source C in a single cohesive passage.
  10. Explain one limitation that must remain in the discussion.
  11. Revise this informal sentence into academic voice: “Keeping libraries open late is awesome for everybody.”
  12. Write one sentence using a measured qualifier such as may, often, or when.
  13. Write a concluding sentence that returns to the controlling idea without merely repeating it.
  14. Create a brief outline showing the introduction, two body paragraphs, and conclusion.
  15. Using your outline, write a 7–9 sentence expository paragraph that integrates at least two sources, uses purposeful transitions, maintains academic voice, and explains the evidence.
Quality check: Organization follows ideas, transitions name real relationships, claims remain measured, and every piece of evidence receives explanation.
