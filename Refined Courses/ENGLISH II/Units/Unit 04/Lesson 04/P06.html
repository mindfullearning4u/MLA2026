Checkpoint: Explaining a School Garden Proposal

Checkpoint Submission: Read this new source set and answer all 10 questions independently. You must earn at least 80%; revise using TOR feedback if needed.

Source D — Science Department Proposal

Teachers propose converting an unused courtyard into a learning garden. Biology classes could measure plant growth, environmental science students could test soil and water use, and writing classes could develop observation journals. The department notes that instruction would be strongest if teachers coordinated schedules and shared clear learning goals.

Source E — Facilities Review

The courtyard receives sufficient sunlight and has nearby water access. Initial construction is affordable with donated materials, but the garden will require summer watering, tool storage, and regular inspection of walking paths. Facilities staff recommend identifying responsible adults before planting begins.

Source F — Student Survey

Most respondents supported outdoor learning, and many volunteered to help during the school year. Fewer students were available during summer. Students were most interested in experiments, food-growing projects, and using the space for quiet reading. Several requested accessible paths and shaded seating.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Write a precise controlling idea that reflects all three sources.
  2. Select the most effective organizational pattern and justify it.
  3. Group the findings into two body-paragraph subtopics.
  4. Write a cohesive topic sentence for each subtopic.
  5. Integrate one finding from Source D and one from Source F using an accurate transition.
  6. Explain how Source E qualifies or limits the enthusiasm shown in the other sources.
  7. Revise this sentence into measured academic voice: “The garden will totally transform learning forever.”
  8. Write one evidence-analysis pair: a credited paraphrase followed by an explanation of its significance.
  9. Write a concluding sentence appropriate for an objective expository explanation.
  10. Write a 7–9 sentence expository response explaining the proposal’s benefits and conditions for success. Use at least two sources, purposeful transitions, logical organization, and consistent academic voice.
Before submitting: Verify that your structure matches your purpose, evidence is explained, transitions are accurate, and the tone is precise rather than exaggerated.
