Worked Examples

These examples show how structure and voice shape expository writing.

Example 1

Findings: Compost bins reduced trash weight. Signs helped students sort scraps correctly.

Best structure: Cause and effect.

Reason: The paragraph should explain how the bins and signs led to less trash.

Example 2

Informal voice: The compost plan was super great and everyone should do it.

Academic voice: The compost plan reduced cafeteria trash when students had clear directions for sorting food scraps.

Reason: Academic voice is specific, measured, and evidence-based.

Example 3

Findings: School gardens provide observations. Journals help students track and explain observations.

Expository sentence: A school garden can support science learning when students observe plant growth and use journals to record evidence over time.

Reason: The sentence explains the relationship between the findings.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Source A says one thing and Source B says another thing and that's it.

Correct: Source A and Source B together show how observations become stronger learning when students record and compare them.

Why this matters: Expository organization should explain how findings connect.
