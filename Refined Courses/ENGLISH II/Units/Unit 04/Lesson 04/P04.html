Worked Examples: Organizing Findings With Academic Voice

Research Findings

A: A school compost pilot reduced cafeteria trash weight by 31%.

B: Sorting accuracy improved after students saw picture-based signs.

C: Custodians reported contamination when signs were missing or unclear.

Worked Example 1: Cause and Effect

Controlling idea: Clear sorting guidance makes compost programs more effective.

  1. Group the reduced-trash finding with the improved-sorting finding.
  2. Use because to name the cause.
  3. Include the limitation so the claim remains accurate.

Model: The compost pilot reduced cafeteria waste because picture-based signs helped students sort scraps accurately. When guidance was unclear, however, contamination increased, showing that visible directions were necessary to the program’s success.

Worked Example 2: Problem and Solution

Problem: Compost bins alone did not prevent sorting errors. Solution: Picture-based signs clarified where items belonged.

Model: Although the school installed compost bins, unclear sorting created contamination. Adding picture-based signs addressed the problem by giving students an immediate visual guide.

Why it works: Although signals a limitation, and the second sentence explains how the response solves the stated problem.

Worked Example 3: Revise Voice and Cohesion

Weak: The signs were super helpful. Source A says trash went down. Source B says kids sorted better. Compost is obviously awesome.

Revised: Visual guidance strengthened the compost program. The pilot recorded a 31% reduction in trash, while improved sorting followed the addition of picture-based signs. Together, these findings suggest that clear directions help environmental programs produce consistent results.

  1. Replace informal and exaggerated language.
  2. Combine related evidence rather than list sources.
  3. Use while and together to show relationships.
  4. End with a measured explanation supported by the findings.
Common mistake: A transition is useful only when it accurately names the relationship between ideas.
