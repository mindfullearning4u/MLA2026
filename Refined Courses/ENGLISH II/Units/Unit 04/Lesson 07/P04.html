Worked Examples

Each example models how to make source-based communication publication-ready.

Example 1: Evidence Check

Draft: Lunch forms helped.

Step 1: Ask what source finding is missing.

Step 2: Add the specific source result.

Revision: A district pilot report found fewer unwanted trays after staff prepared meals from student pre-order selections.

Example 2: Audience Check

Draft: Pre-order compliance improves operational outcomes.

Step 1: Identify the audience: students.

Step 2: Keep the meaning but make the language easier to use.

Revision: Completing the pre-order form helps staff prepare meals students are more likely to choose.

Example 3: Visual Check

Draft Plan: Add a random cafeteria picture.

Step 1: Ask whether the visual teaches the research finding.

Step 2: Choose a visual that makes the evidence easier to understand.

Revision: Use a before-and-after chart showing unwanted trays before and after the pre-order pilot.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Publish the draft as soon as it has a source and a picture.

Correct Example:

Before publishing, check that the source is accurate, the explanation is clear, the wording fits students, the visual supports the evidence, and the sentences are polished.

Teachable Explanation: A source and a picture are not enough. Publication-ready work must be accurate, explained, audience-aware, visually purposeful, and conventionally correct.
