Worked Examples: Revising Toward Publication

Worked Example 1: Repair Evidence Integration

Draft: Later library hours are best. “Visits increased 37 percent.”

Revision: During a district pilot, student visits increased 37% on evenings with extended hours (Source A). The change suggests that later access can support students whose schedules prevent earlier visits.

Steps: Replace the unsupported superlative, introduce and credit the statistic, then explain its significance.

Worked Example 2: Revise Organization and Voice

Draft order: cost concern → conclusion → background → benefit → another cost.

Published order: background and purpose → documented benefit → operational limitation → evidence-based recommendation.

Voice revision: “The schedule is obviously amazing” becomes “Extended hours may improve access when staffing is matched to demonstrated demand.”

Worked Example 3: Final Publication Audit

Problems found: a chart lacks a source; one link says “click here”; an image has no alt text; headings skip levels; a quotation differs from the source; the file is named “finalfinal2.”

  1. Verify the quotation against the source.
  2. Add the chart’s source and descriptive caption.
  3. Replace “click here” with a descriptive link.
  4. Add useful alt text and repair heading order.
  5. Rename the file clearly and preview it on phone and desktop.
Common mistake: “It looks finished” is not a publication standard. Use a documented review process covering content, evidence, language, accessibility, and function.
