Publication-Ready Source-Based Communication

Direct Teaching

Publication-ready communication is accurate, ethical, coherent, audience-centered, polished, and technically complete.

Step 1: Conduct a content and evidence audit

Confirm that every claim is supported, quotations and paraphrases preserve meaning, citations identify sources, and no evidence is outdated or misrepresented.

Step 2: Strengthen organization and cohesion

Check the controlling idea, paragraph order, headings, topic sentences, transitions, and conclusion. Remove repetition and make every section advance the purpose.

Step 3: Revise for audience, tone, and voice

Use vocabulary and detail appropriate for readers. Maintain a consistent point of view and measured tone. Define unfamiliar terms and replace vague, emotional, or inflated language.

Step 4: Edit for clarity and correctness

  1. Shorten wordy sentences.
  2. Prefer precise verbs and concrete nouns.
  3. Correct fragments, run-ons, agreement, punctuation, and spelling.
  4. Check names, dates, numbers, captions, and source labels.

Step 5: Complete the publication check

Verify layout, headings, contrast, alt text, captions, links, file names, permissions, and mobile readability. Preview the exact student-facing or public version before release.

Common Mistakes

  • Proofreading grammar while leaving unsupported claims.
  • Adding a citation without introducing or explaining evidence.
  • Using inconsistent fonts, headings, tone, or source style.
  • Publishing broken links, unreadable graphics, or missing accessibility text.
  • Assuming the editing view matches the audience’s final view.
