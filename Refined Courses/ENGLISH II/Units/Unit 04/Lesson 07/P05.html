Independent Work: Publication Review of a Community Article

Notebook Evidence: Read the draft and source notes, then answer all 15 questions. Submit these answers with your Lesson 7 vocabulary and definitions.

Source Notes

A: A six-week park program recorded an average of 84 youth participants per week.

B: Families valued free activities but requested earlier schedule announcements.

C: Staff recommended shaded seating, water stations, and accessible paths.

Draft Article

Summer Park Program Was Totally Perfect

The town did a program and lots of kids came. “There were 84 participants.” This proves every family loved everything. Staff also talked about shade and water and stuff. Click here for more. The program should obviously happen every day next year because it was a huge success.

[Insert chart] [Insert photo]

Questions

  1. Identify two unsupported or exaggerated claims.
  2. Revise the headline into accurate, audience-appropriate language.
  3. Correctly introduce, credit, and explain the participation statistic.
  4. Explain why the draft misrepresents Source B.
  5. Write a synthesis sentence connecting Sources B and C.
  6. Replace “shade and water and stuff” with precise language.
  7. Revise the final recommendation into a measured claim.
  8. Propose a logical three-section organization for the article.
  9. Write one purposeful transition between participation results and improvement requests.
  10. Describe the chart that should replace “[Insert chart]” and name its required source label.
  11. Describe a purposeful photo and write useful alt text.
  12. Replace “Click here” with descriptive link wording.
  13. List four copyediting corrections or checks needed before publication.
  14. Create a six-item final publication checklist covering evidence, organization, language, accessibility, links/media, and file naming.
  15. Rewrite the draft as a polished 8–10 sentence article using all three sources, accurate evidence, coherent organization, measured voice, and a defensible conclusion.
Quality check: The revised article is accurate, source-based, cohesive, accessible, audience-appropriate, and ready to publish.
