Should Easton Renovate Its Library or Build a Recreation Pavilion?

Easton has $2.4 million available for one major community project. The public library, built in 1978, has outdated wiring, limited wheelchair access, and only twelve public computers. Annual library visits increased 19 percent over five years, and after-school tutoring now serves about 160 students each week. Renovation would cost an estimated $2.2 million and include accessible entrances, updated technology, study rooms, and energy-efficient systems.

A proposed recreation pavilion would cost $2.3 million and include covered courts, performance space, and restrooms at Central Park. Recreation programs currently have waiting lists totaling 230 residents. Supporters say a pavilion would serve athletics, festivals, and summer programs while attracting visitors. Opponents note that outdoor programs are seasonal and that ongoing staffing and maintenance would cost approximately $95,000 per year, compared with $48,000 in additional annual library operating costs.

In a town survey, 54 percent preferred the library renovation, 39 percent preferred the pavilion, and 7 percent were undecided. Pavilion supporters argue that the survey underrepresented teenagers and young adults. Library supporters respond that the library serves residents of all ages throughout the year. The council must select one project this year.

Questions

  1. State the issue neutrally.
  2. Write a precise claim choosing one project.
  3. Select three facts that directly support your claim.
  4. Organize those facts in the most logical order and explain your choice.
  5. State the strongest counterclaim fairly.
  6. Identify one valid concern within that counterclaim.
  7. Write a rebuttal using evidence rather than personal preference.
  8. Evaluate the relevance of the survey results.
  9. Explain how the annual operating costs affect both sides.
  10. Identify one missing fact that would improve the decision.
  11. Write a transition introducing the counterclaim.
  12. Write a transition moving from counterclaim to rebuttal.
  13. Revise this biased sentence objectively: “Only selfish people would choose the pavilion.”
  14. Outline the order of your introduction, reasons, counterclaim, rebuttal, and conclusion.
  15. Write a formal argument of at least three developed paragraphs using a precise claim, three facts, a fair counterclaim, an evidence-based rebuttal, logical organization, and objective tone.
