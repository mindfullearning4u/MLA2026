P04 Worked Example

Study how each example handles counterclaims, organization, and tone.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Claim: The school should create a quiet study room first.

Counterclaim: Some may argue that debate club should come first because public speaking is valuable.

Rebuttal: However, the study room responds to the larger documented need because 62 percent of surveyed students requested more study space.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Weak tone: People who want festival lights are foolish.

Objective tone: Festival lights could support evening events, but trail repair addresses a safety concern.

Why it works: The revision acknowledges the opposing view without insulting it.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Organized order: Claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, rebuttal, conclusion.

Why this order works: The reader first understands the writer's position, then sees proof, then sees that the writer has considered and answered the opposing view.

Result: The argument sounds complete and credible.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The other side is dumb, so my side wins.

Correct: The opposing view has a reasonable goal, but my claim is better supported because the evidence shows a more urgent safety need.

Why this matters: Formal argument writing must be fair and evidence-based.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Counterclaim, Organization, and Tone Organizer

Use this organizer to integrate a counterclaim while maintaining logical organization and an objective tone.

Analysis Step What to Record Quality Check
1. Present the counterclaim fairly State the opposing position accurately and identify its strongest reason or evidence. The wording is neutral and does not distort the opposing view.
2. Respond with evidence and reasoning Concede a valid point when appropriate, then rebut or qualify the counterclaim. The response uses evidence rather than dismissal or emotional language.
3. Integrate and revise Place the counterclaim where it strengthens the argument and revise transitions and tone. The section flows logically and maintains a formal, objective voice.
