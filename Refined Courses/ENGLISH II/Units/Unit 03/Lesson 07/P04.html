P04 Worked Example

Study how each example handles counterclaims, organization, and tone.

Example 1

Claim: The school should create a quiet study room first.

Counterclaim: Some may argue that debate club should come first because public speaking is valuable.

Rebuttal: However, the study room responds to the larger documented need because 62 percent of surveyed students requested more study space.

Example 2

Weak tone: People who want festival lights are foolish.

Objective tone: Festival lights could support evening events, but trail repair addresses a safety concern.

Why it works: The revision acknowledges the opposing view without insulting it.

Example 3

Organized order: Claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, rebuttal, conclusion.

Why this order works: The reader first understands the writer's position, then sees proof, then sees that the writer has considered and answered the opposing view.

Result: The argument sounds complete and credible.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The other side is dumb, so my side wins.

Correct: The opposing view has a reasonable goal, but my claim is better supported because the evidence shows a more urgent safety need.

Why this matters: Formal argument writing must be fair and evidence-based.
