P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Counterclaims and Objective Tone

Vocabulary

Counterclaim means an opposing claim that disagrees with the writer's position.

Rebuttal means the writer's answer to a counterclaim.

Objective tone means neutral, fair, evidence-based language.

Biased language means wording that unfairly favors or attacks one side.

Organization means the order of ideas in a paragraph or essay.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: State your claim clearly.

Step 2: Give relevant evidence and reasoning.

Step 3: Acknowledge a reasonable counterclaim fairly.

Step 4: Rebut the counterclaim with stronger evidence or reasoning.

Step 5: Keep tone objective by avoiding insults, exaggeration, slang, and emotional shortcuts.

Argument Draft

Prompt: Should the school create a quiet study room before funding a debate club?

Evidence: A survey showed 62 percent of students wanted more study space. Debate can help students organize ideas and build speaking confidence.

Draft: The school should create a quiet study room first because most surveyed students asked for more study space. Some people may argue that debate club should come first because public speaking is valuable. However, the study room would meet the larger documented need because 62 percent of students requested study space.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Label the claim in the draft.
  4. Label the counterclaim.
  5. Label the rebuttal and explain why it is objective.

Model Example

Counterclaim: Some people may argue that debate club should come first because public speaking is valuable.

Rebuttal: However, the study room would meet the larger documented need because 62 percent of students requested study space.

Why it works: The counterclaim is fair, and the rebuttal uses evidence rather than insult.
