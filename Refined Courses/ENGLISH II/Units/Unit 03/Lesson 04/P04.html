P04 Worked Example

Study how each example names a rhetorical choice and explains its effect.

Example 1

Passage: We need roads that carry food. We need roads that carry medicine. We need roads that carry hope.

Step 1: The author repeats "We need roads."

Step 2: The repetition creates emphasis and rhythm.

Step 3: The effect is to make road repair sound urgent and important to many parts of life.

Example 2

Passage: Shall we wait for another flood, or shall we build the wall while the sky is clear?

Step 1: The author uses a rhetorical question.

Step 2: The question pushes the audience toward the obvious answer: act now.

Step 3: The effect is to make delay seem irresponsible.

Example 3

Passage: This is not a favor for one street; it is a safeguard for the entire town.

Step 1: The author uses contrast.

Step 2: The contrast moves from a small benefit to a broad public benefit.

Step 3: The effect is to make the proposal seem fair and necessary for everyone.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author uses repetition because the sentence has words.

Correct: The author repeats "We need roads" to emphasize that roads support food, medicine, and hope.

Why this matters: Analysis must name the choice and explain its effect on the audience.
