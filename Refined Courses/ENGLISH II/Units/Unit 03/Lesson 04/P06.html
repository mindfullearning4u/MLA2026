Open the School Doors After Sunset

Context: A civic advocate urges the school board to open school buildings for evening community programs.

Every afternoon, the final bell sends students home and leaves classrooms dark. Yet outside those locked doors, adults search for language classes, teenagers seek safe study space, and families need reliable internet. Why should a public building stop serving the public at three o’clock?

Last winter, a twelve-week pilot opened one school on Tuesday and Thursday evenings. Attendance averaged 140 residents per night, eighty-six adults completed job applications, and student tutoring participation doubled. These results do not promise perfection, but they prove demand.

Teachers have rightly asked who will supervise and protect the building. The proposal funds trained evening staff and limits programs to designated rooms. Responsibility does not require closing the doors; it requires opening them wisely.

Open the doors to learning. Open the doors to opportunity. Open the doors to neighbors who already support these schools with their trust and taxes.

Questions

  1. Identify audience and purpose.
  2. Analyze the image of dark classrooms.
  3. What effect does the list of community needs create?
  4. Analyze the rhetorical question.
  5. Identify two examples of logos.
  6. Why does the speaker qualify the pilot results?
  7. How does acknowledging teachers’ concerns build ethos?
  8. Explain the contrast between closing and opening wisely.
  9. Analyze repetition in the final paragraph.
  10. Explain how imagery, logos, ethos, contrast, and repetition work together to persuade the board.
