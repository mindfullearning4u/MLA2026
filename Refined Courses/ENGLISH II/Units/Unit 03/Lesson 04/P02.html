P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Rhetorical Choices and Audience Effect

Vocabulary

Rhetorical choice means a language or structure decision an author uses to influence an audience.

Repetition means repeating a word or phrase for emphasis.

Rhetorical question means a question asked to make a point rather than to get an answer.

Appeal means a way of connecting to logic, emotion, credibility, or shared values.

Effect means the impact a choice has on the audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the author's purpose.

Step 2: Find a repeated phrase, question, contrast, emotional word, or appeal.

Step 3: Ask how the choice affects the audience.

Step 4: Connect the effect to purpose.

Step 5: Write the analysis: The author uses [choice] to [effect], which supports the purpose by [explanation].

Read the Passage

Historical Speech Passage: "We Must Keep the Bridge Safe"

We cross this bridge to reach work. We cross this bridge to visit family. We cross this bridge to bring help when help is needed. Shall we wait until the boards fail beneath us, or shall we act while repair is still possible? A safe bridge is not a luxury; it is a promise we make to every person who depends on this road.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Identify the author's purpose.
  4. Find one example of repetition and one rhetorical question.
  5. Explain the effect of each choice.

Model Example

Choice: The phrase "We cross this bridge" is repeated.

Effect: The repetition reminds the audience that many people depend on the bridge.

Purpose connection: This supports the author's purpose of persuading the audience to repair the bridge.
