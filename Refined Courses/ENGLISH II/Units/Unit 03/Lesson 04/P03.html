Read the Passage

Historical Essay Passage: "The Public Well"

A private well may serve one house, but a public well serves the whole road. When the dry months arrive, no family should wonder whether clean water belongs only to those with deeper pockets. A shared well is a practical tool, but it is also a statement: in this town, survival is not a privilege for the fortunate; it is a duty shared by neighbors.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: The author contrasts a private well with a public well.

Step 2: The contrast helps the audience see the difference between individual benefit and shared responsibility.

Step 3: The phrase "deeper pockets" appeals to fairness by suggesting that wealth should not decide access to water.

Step 4: The final sentence appeals to shared values by using words such as duty and neighbors.

Step 5: These choices support the purpose of persuading readers to value the public well.

Notebook Application

  1. Identify one contrast in the passage.
  2. Explain what the contrast helps the audience understand.
  3. Identify one emotional or value-based appeal.
  4. Explain how the appeal supports the author's purpose.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The rhetorical choice is water.

Correct: The author uses contrast between a private well and a public well to show the difference between individual access and community responsibility.

Why this matters: Water is the topic. A rhetorical choice is a decision the writer makes with language or structure.
