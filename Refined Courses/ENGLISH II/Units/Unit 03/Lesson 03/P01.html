Lesson 3: Author Purpose in Historical American Speeches and Essays

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG2.R.07 - Analyze Author Purpose in Historical American Texts.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG2.R.06 - Analyze Central Ideas in Historical American Texts; MLA.ENG2.V.03 - Analyze Meaning, Connotation, and Tone; MLA.ENG2.C.01 - Present Ideas Orally.

What You Will Learn

  • How to identify an author's purpose in a historical American speech or essay.
  • How purpose connects to central idea, audience, word choice, and tone.
  • How to explain why an author includes specific details or calls for action.
  • How to avoid confusing topic with purpose.

What You Will Do

  • Read historical American speech and essay passages included in the lesson.
  • Identify the central idea before naming the purpose.
  • Analyze how details, word choice, and tone reveal purpose.
  • Practice explaining purpose with evidence.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook work names the author's purpose and supports it with evidence.
  • Your responses explain how purpose shapes details and tone.
  • Your checkpoint explains why an author wrote a historical passage.
  • Your assessment answers show comprehension, application, and analysis.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.ENG2.R.07 in student language: I can explain why an author wrote a historical American speech or essay and how the author's choices support that purpose.

Today, your job is to ask, "What is this author trying to make the audience think, feel, or do?" Then you will prove your answer with details from the text.

Need help? Ask your Teacher of Record for help if you can state the topic but cannot explain why the author wrote the passage.
