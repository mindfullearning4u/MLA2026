P03 — Lesson: How Authors Shape Purpose

Direct Teaching

Author purpose is more precise than a label such as inform or persuade. A strong analysis explains what the author wants a particular audience to understand, believe, feel, or do—and how specific choices support that goal.

A Six-Step Purpose Analysis

  1. Identify the rhetorical situation: speaker, audience, historical context, and occasion.
  2. State the central idea: determine the message the author develops.
  3. Name the precise purpose: explain the intended audience response.
  4. Examine diction and tone: identify words that create urgency, hope, concern, authority, or resolve.
  5. Analyze selected details: explain why the author included examples, contrasts, facts, or calls to action.
  6. Connect choice to effect: show how the choices help accomplish the purpose.

Example 1 — Persuade Citizens to Participate

Passage: “The council chamber belongs not to officials alone but to every resident whose road, school, and livelihood are shaped there. Attend, question, and vote, or decisions will be made without your voice.”

Purpose: To persuade local citizens to participate in government.
