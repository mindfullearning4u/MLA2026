Read the Passage

Historical Essay Passage: "The Value of Clean Streets"

A city cannot call itself orderly while its alleys collect broken glass and spoiled food. Clean streets protect health, but they also teach respect for shared spaces. When a merchant sweeps the front walk, a child carries paper to a bin, or a council repairs a drain, each action says that public places belong to everyone. The purpose of street cleaning is not decoration alone; it is a promise that the city will care for the daily life of its people.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: The topic is clean streets.

Step 2: The central idea is that clean streets protect health and show care for shared public life.

Step 3: The author's purpose is to explain and persuade readers that street cleaning is a civic responsibility.

Step 4: Words such as protect, respect, shared, everyone, promise, and care create a serious civic tone.

Step 5: The tone supports the purpose because it makes street cleaning sound like public duty, not a minor chore.

Notebook Application

  1. Write the author's purpose in one sentence.
  2. List three words that support the tone.
  3. Explain how those words help the author achieve the purpose.
  4. Write one sentence you could use in a discussion with your Teacher of Record.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author's purpose is clean streets.

Correct: The author's purpose is to persuade readers that clean streets are a public responsibility because they protect health and show respect for shared spaces.

Why this matters: "Clean streets" is the topic. Purpose explains what the author wants the audience to understand or do about that topic.
