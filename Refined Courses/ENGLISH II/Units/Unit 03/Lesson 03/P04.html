P04 Worked Example

Study how each example identifies purpose through central idea, audience, and word choice.

Example 1

Passage: Citizens, the bridge tax is not a burden without purpose. It is the way we keep farmers, doctors, and families connected across the river.

Step 1: The audience is citizens.

Step 2: The central idea is that the tax supports an important bridge.

Step 3: The purpose is to persuade citizens to support the tax by showing its public value.

Example 2

Passage: Let no child be told that books belong only to those who can buy them. A public school opens a door that poverty should never close.

Step 1: The emotional word choice includes "no child" and "poverty should never close."

Step 2: The tone is urgent and protective.

Step 3: The purpose is to persuade the audience to support public education access.

Example 3

Passage: The town records show that house fires dropped after the new alarm law. The evidence is plain: prevention saves lives.

Step 1: The author uses evidence from town records.

Step 2: The central idea is that prevention improves safety.

Step 3: The purpose is to inform and persuade readers that the alarm law is effective.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The purpose is the bridge.

Correct: The purpose is to persuade citizens to support the bridge tax by showing that the bridge connects the community.

Why this matters: A noun often names the topic. Purpose must explain the author's goal for the audience.
