P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Building a Formal Argument

Vocabulary

Argument means writing that supports a position with evidence and reasoning.

Claim means the writer's position.

Relevant evidence means support that directly connects to the claim.

Reasoning explains how the evidence proves the claim.

Formal tone means serious, respectful, precise language without slang or casual shortcuts.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read the prompt and identify the decision being argued.

Step 2: Write a claim that takes a clear position.

Step 3: Select evidence that directly supports that position.

Step 4: Write reasoning that explains the connection between evidence and claim.

Step 5: Check tone and conventions so the argument sounds formal and credible.

Argument Prompt and Evidence

Prompt: Should the school repair the gym roof before replacing the auditorium sound system?

Evidence 1: The gym roof leaks during heavy rain, and water can damage the floor.

Evidence 2: The auditorium sound system often fails during assemblies and presentations.

Evidence 3: Safety problems can prevent classes, teams, and school events from using a space.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Write a claim answering the prompt.
  4. Choose one piece of evidence.
  5. Write reasoning that explains why the evidence proves the claim.

Model Example

Claim: The school should repair the gym roof first.

Evidence: The roof leaks during heavy rain, and water can damage the floor.

Reasoning: This evidence supports the claim because water damage creates a safety and access problem that could affect classes, teams, and events.
