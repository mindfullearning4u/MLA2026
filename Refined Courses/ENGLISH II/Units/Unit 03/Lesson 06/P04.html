P04 Worked Example

Study how each example builds a formal argument part.

Example 1

Prompt: Should the school add a quiet study room?

Step 1: A precise claim takes a position.

Claim: The school should add a quiet study room because most surveyed students asked for more study space.

Example 2

Claim: The school should add bike racks.

Weak evidence: Bikes are cool.

Strong evidence: The school counted 140 bikes locked to fences last month.

Reason: Strong evidence is specific, measurable, and directly connected to the need.

Example 3

Evidence: The gym roof leaks during heavy rain.

Weak reasoning: That is bad.

Strong reasoning: A leaking roof can damage the floor and create safety risks, so repairing it first protects a space used by classes, teams, and school events.

Why it works: The reasoning explains why the evidence matters.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The school should fix the roof because I think that is best.

Correct: The school should fix the roof first because leaks can damage the floor and create safety risks in a space used by classes and teams.

Why this matters: Formal argument writing must use evidence and reasoning, not personal preference alone.
