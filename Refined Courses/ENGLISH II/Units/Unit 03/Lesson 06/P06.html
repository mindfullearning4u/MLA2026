Should Millbrook Create a Fare-Free Bus Route?

Millbrook’s central bus route costs riders $1.50 per trip and carries about 1,100 riders each weekday. A three-month fare-free trial increased weekday ridership by 26 percent and shortened the average trip by four minutes because drivers no longer handled cash. Eliminating fares permanently would remove $410,000 in annual revenue.

A clean-air grant could replace $250,000 of that revenue for two years, but later funding is uncertain. Downtown employers reported fewer late arrivals during the trial. Two neighborhood associations supported the proposal because many residents do not own cars. A taxpayer group opposed it, arguing that riders should contribute to operating costs and temporary grant money does not ensure long-term affordability.

The transit director proposed a one-year program with quarterly reports on ridership, travel time, operating cost, and access to jobs. The council must decide whether the public benefits justify the financial risk.

Questions

  1. State the issue and write a precise claim.
  2. Identify three facts supporting your claim.
  3. Identify the strongest evidence against your claim.
  4. Evaluate the relevance of the ridership increase.
  5. Explain the limitation created by the two-year grant.
  6. Connect one transportation fact to your claim with clear reasoning.
  7. Connect one community-impact fact to your claim with clear reasoning.
  8. Explain whether the one-year proposal addresses uncertainty sufficiently.
  9. Write a logical conclusion that does not overstate the evidence.
  10. Write a complete formal argument of at least two developed paragraphs using a precise claim, at least three facts, and clear reasoning.
