P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now you will track how details develop a central idea across a historical essay passage.

Read the Passage

Historical Essay Passage: "The Duty of a Free Press"

A free press does more than print daily events. It gives citizens the information they need to judge public choices. If officials act wisely, reports can explain the reasons and results. If officials act poorly, reports can reveal the harm and demand answers. For that reason, a newspaper should not serve only the powerful or the popular. It should serve the public by asking careful questions, checking facts, and making important issues visible.

Deeper Learning: How Details Develop the Idea

Step 1: The topic is the press, or newspapers.

Step 2: The central idea is that a free press serves the public by informing citizens and holding power accountable.

Step 3: The passage develops this idea by explaining what reports do when officials act wisely and what reports do when officials act poorly.

Step 4: The final sentence refines the idea by listing the responsibilities of the press: asking questions, checking facts, and making issues visible.

Step 5: A strong response explains how the details work together, not just which detail sounds important.

Notebook Application

  1. Write the central idea of "The Duty of a Free Press."
  2. Choose one detail about wise officials and one detail about poor officials.
  3. Explain how those details develop the central idea.
  4. Write one sentence explaining why the final sentence is important.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The central idea is newspapers.

Correct: The central idea is that a free press serves the public by informing citizens, checking facts, and holding leaders accountable.

Why this matters: "Newspapers" is only a topic. A central idea must make a complete point about the topic.
