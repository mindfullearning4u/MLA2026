P01 Lesson Overview: Lesson 8: Putting It All Together - Unit 3 Synthesis

Lesson Title

Lesson 8: Putting It All Together - Unit 3 Synthesis

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.ENG2.R.05, MLA.ENG2.R.06, MLA.ENG2.R.07, MLA.ENG2.R.08, MLA.ENG2.R.12, MLA.ENG2.W.02.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG2.R.10, MLA.ENG2.V.01, MLA.ENG2.L.01, MLA.ENG2.C.01.

What You Will Learn

  • How Unit 3 informational reading and argument writing skills work together.
  • How structure, central idea, purpose, rhetoric, and opposing arguments shape a text.
  • How to use reading evidence to build a formal argument response.

Why This Matters

Synthesis prepares you to transfer informational reading and argument-writing skills to unfamiliar texts and real decisions—the independent thinking expected beyond this course.

What You Will Do

  • Review Unit 3 skills step by step.
  • Read the included informational passage and argument prompt.
  • Practice identifying structure, central idea, purpose, rhetorical choices, and counterclaims.
  • Prepare for and complete the Unit 3 Assessment.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Write the lesson vocabulary words and definitions in your notebook.
  2. Complete the Lesson and Worked Example pages.
  3. Complete the Independent Work. Submit your vocabulary and definitions plus the Independent Work in the Notebook Evidence Submission activity.
  4. Complete the Checkpoint and submit those answers separately in the Checkpoint Submission activity.
  5. Complete the lesson quiz or unit assessment when assigned.
  6. If you earn below 80%, review TOR feedback, correct your mistakes, and meet with your TOR when additional help is needed.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook identifies the Unit 3 skill required by each question.
  • Your checkpoint combines reading analysis with formal argument writing.
  • Your Unit Assessment shows mastery of Unit 3 standards without needing outside materials.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can analyze how informational texts are built, explain what they mean and why they were written, evaluate arguments, and write a formal argument using evidence and objective tone.
