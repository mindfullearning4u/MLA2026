Worked Examples: Unit 3 Synthesis

Use these examples to see how the Unit 3 standards work together.

Example 1: Identify Text Structure

Question: The passage describes two possible library improvements and then explains why one should come first. What structure is being used?

Step 1: Notice the two options: evening hours and display screens.

Step 2: Notice that the passage compares reasons for both options.

Step 3: Notice that the author recommends one option.

Answer: The passage uses comparison and argument structure because it compares two options and supports one recommendation.

Example 2: Explain Purpose and Rhetorical Choice

Question: Why does the author include the sentence, "A library serves the community best when people can actually enter the building and use its resources"?

Step 1: Identify the author's purpose: persuade readers to support evening hours.

Step 2: Identify the rhetorical choice: the sentence appeals to practical community access.

Step 3: Explain the effect: it makes evening hours seem more urgent than display screens.

Answer: The sentence supports the author's persuasive purpose by emphasizing access as the library's main responsibility.

Example 3: Build an Argument Response

Question: Write a response that includes a claim, evidence, counterclaim, and rebuttal.

Step 1: Claim: The library should extend evening hours first.

Step 2: Evidence: Sign-in logs show frequent requests for later access.

Step 3: Counterclaim: Display screens could advertise events.

Step 4: Rebuttal: Events already have steady participation, while later access is a documented need.

Model response: The library should extend evening hours first because sign-in logs show frequent requests for later access. Some residents may argue that display screens should come first because they could advertise events. However, event participation is already steady, while evening hours would help residents who cannot visit before 5:00 p.m.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author just likes evening hours more than screens.

Why this is incorrect: This guess does not use evidence from the passage and does not explain structure, purpose, or reasoning.

Correct: The author supports evening hours by comparing the two options and showing that later access is a more documented need than event advertising.

Why this is correct: This answer uses the passage's evidence and explains how the author's organization supports the argument.
