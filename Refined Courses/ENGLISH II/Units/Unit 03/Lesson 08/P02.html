Notebook Title: Unit 3 Synthesis: Informational Text and Argument

Vocabulary

Text structure is the way an author organizes information, such as cause and effect, problem and solution, comparison, or sequence.

Central idea is the main point the author develops across the text.

Author purpose is the reason the author writes, such as to inform, persuade, explain, or call readers to action.

Rhetorical choice is a deliberate writing move, such as repetition, contrast, direct address, or loaded language.

Counterclaim is an opposing argument. Rebuttal is the response that explains why the claim is stronger.

Included Passage

Community Library Proposal

The community library is considering two improvements for the coming year: extending evening hours or purchasing new display screens for the lobby. Supporters of evening hours explain that many students and adults cannot visit the library before 5:00 p.m. because of school, work, or family responsibilities. If the library stayed open later two nights each week, more residents could use computers, study tables, and research materials.

Supporters of display screens argue that the screens would help advertise library events. They believe more residents would attend workshops, reading nights, and community meetings if announcements were easier to see. However, attendance records show that many programs already have steady participation, while sign-in logs show frequent requests for later access. For that reason, the library board should extend evening hours first. A library serves the community best when people can actually enter the building and use its resources.

Teaching: How to Read This Passage Step by Step

Step 1: Identify the topic. The topic is a library improvement decision.

Step 2: Find the structure. The passage presents two options, compares reasons for each option, and then recommends one option. This is comparison plus argument structure.

Step 3: Identify the central idea. The central idea is that extending evening hours should come before buying display screens because access to library resources is the more documented need.

Step 4: Identify purpose. The author is trying to persuade readers that the library should prioritize evening access.

Step 5: Notice the counterclaim. The display-screen argument is included so the author can answer it fairly.

Notebook Directions

Model answer: Some people believe display screens should come first because the screens could advertise library events.
