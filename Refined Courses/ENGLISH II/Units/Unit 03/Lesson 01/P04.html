P04 Worked Example

Study how each answer moves from naming a structure to explaining its impact.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Passage: First, the volunteer team sorted donated books by grade level. Next, they labeled boxes for classrooms. Finally, they delivered the boxes to teachers before the first bell.

Step 1: The signal words are first, next, and finally.

Step 2: The structure is sequence.

Step 3: The sequence structure helps the reader follow the order of tasks and understand how the book delivery was completed efficiently.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Passage: The river trail flooded after heavy rain, so the parks department closed the path. Because the closure blocked the safest route to school, workers built a temporary gravel walkway.

Step 1: The words after, so, and because show relationships between events.

Step 2: The structure is cause and effect.

Step 3: The structure helps the reader see why the temporary walkway was necessary: flooding caused the closure, and the closure created a safety problem.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Passage: The club faced a problem: students wanted tutoring, but many could not stay after school. The solution was a rotating lunch tutoring table staffed by volunteers from the honor society.

Step 1: The passage directly names a problem and a solution.

Step 2: The structure is problem and solution.

Step 3: The structure helps the reader understand both the need and the response. It makes the lunch tutoring table seem practical because it solves the schedule problem.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The passage has a structure because it has sentences.

Correct: The passage uses problem and solution because it explains that students need tutoring but cannot stay after school, then presents lunch tutoring as the solution.

Why this matters: Every passage has sentences, but analysis must identify how the writer organizes ideas and what that organization helps the reader understand.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.
