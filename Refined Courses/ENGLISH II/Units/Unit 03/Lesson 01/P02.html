P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: How Text Structure Shapes Meaning

Vocabulary

Text structure means the way information is organized in a text.

Cause and effect explains why something happened and what happened as a result.

Problem and solution presents a difficulty and explains one or more ways to respond to it.

Text features are tools such as headings, lists, dates, captions, and signal words that help readers follow information.

Impact means the effect a structure or feature has on the reader's understanding.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read for the topic first. Before naming a structure, ask, "What is this text mostly about?"

Step 2: Look for signal words. Words such as because, as a result, first, however, unlike, problem, and solution can point to structure.

Step 3: Identify the structure. Decide whether the writer is explaining causes, comparing ideas, listing steps, describing details, or presenting a problem and solution.

Step 4: Identify any text features. Headings, numbered lists, dates, and bold words tell readers how to group information.

Step 5: Explain the impact. A strong answer says what the structure helps the reader understand, not just what the structure is called.

Read the Passage

Passage: "Why the Town Opened a Cooling Center"

During the first week of July, the town library recorded temperatures above ninety-five degrees for four days in a row. Older residents, outdoor workers, and families without reliable air conditioning were at risk of heat illness. Because several neighborhoods lost power during the hottest afternoon, city leaders opened the gym at Eastside School as a cooling center. The gym had backup power, water stations, folding cots, and volunteers who could help visitors contact relatives. As a result, more than two hundred residents had a safe place to rest until evening temperatures dropped.

Text feature: The title gives the reader a why question before the passage begins.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the title: How Text Structure Shapes Meaning.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Write the topic of the passage in one sentence.
  4. Name the main text structure.
  5. Explain how the title helps the reader focus before reading.

Model Example

Structure: Cause and effect

Evidence: The passage says the town had high temperatures, power loss, and residents at risk of heat illness.

Impact: The cause-and-effect structure helps the reader understand that the cooling center was not random. It was a direct response to dangerous heat and power problems.
