P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Now you will explain how a second structure changes what the reader notices.

Read the Passage

Passage: "Two Ways to Reduce Lunch Waste"

The cafeteria studied two ways to reduce food waste. The first plan was a share table, where students could leave unopened milk, fruit, or packaged snacks for others. This plan helped because food that might have been thrown away could still be eaten. The second plan was a pre-order lunch form. Students selected meals each morning, so the kitchen prepared fewer unwanted trays. Unlike the share table, the pre-order form reduced waste before lunch began. Together, the two plans showed that waste can be reduced both after food is served and before food is prepared.

Deeper Learning: From Structure to Impact

Step 1: Notice the passage compares two plans: a share table and a pre-order lunch form.

Step 2: Notice the signal word Unlike. That word tells you the writer is showing a difference.

Step 3: Name the structure: compare and contrast.

Step 4: Explain the impact: the structure helps the reader understand that both plans reduce waste, but they work at different points in the lunch process.

Step 5: Use evidence. A complete answer should mention both the share table and the pre-order form.

Notebook Application

  1. Write the structure used in "Two Ways to Reduce Lunch Waste."
  2. Write one signal word or phrase that helped you identify the structure.
  3. Explain how the structure helps the reader understand the difference between the two plans.
  4. Use one detail about the share table and one detail about the pre-order form.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The passage is compare and contrast because it talks about lunch waste.

Correct: The passage uses compare and contrast to show that the share table reduces waste after food is served, while the pre-order form reduces waste before food is prepared.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect answer names a topic but does not explain the structure's effect. The correct answer connects both parts of the comparison to what the reader learns.
