P05 — Independent Work

Notebook Evidence: Read both arguments and answer all 15 questions.

Should Harbor Avenue Become Bus-Only During Rush Hour?

Shared information: Buses carry 42% of rush-hour travelers on Harbor Avenue but occupy 8% of vehicles. A six-week trial shortened average bus trips by nine minutes. Average car trips increased by four minutes. Retail sales along the avenue changed by less than 1%. Emergency vehicles retained access.

Argument A — Keep the Bus-Only Plan

The trial moved the greatest number of people more efficiently. A nine-minute improvement benefits thousands of riders, including workers and students without flexible schedules. Although drivers lost four minutes, the street’s limited space should serve the largest number of travelers. Stable retail sales also weaken the claim that fewer cars will harm businesses. The city should make the plan permanent and review traffic on nearby streets every three months.

Argument B — End the Bus-Only Plan

City streets should remain available to all taxpayers, not reserve lanes for one kind of vehicle. The trial increased car travel time, which may push traffic onto residential streets. Six weeks is too short to measure effects on deliveries, customer habits, or future congestion. Because the retail change was small, it proves neither benefit nor harm. The city should restore mixed traffic while conducting a longer study.

Questions

  1. State Argument A’s claim.
  2. State Argument B’s claim.
  3. Identify three facts both authors use.
  4. How does A interpret the bus and car travel-time data?
  5. How does B interpret the same data?
  6. Explain A’s assumption about limited street space.
  7. Explain B’s assumption about taxpayer access.
  8. Which argument uses retail data more cautiously? Explain.
  9. What additional evidence would test the residential-traffic concern?
  10. Evaluate the relevance of the six-week trial length.
  11. Does A acknowledge a limitation? Explain its proposed response.
  12. Does B offer a workable alternative? Explain.
  13. Identify one strength and one weakness in each argument.
  14. Which argument is currently better supported? Defend your judgment with shared information and reasoning.
  15. Write a balanced evaluation explaining how the two authors use the same evidence to reach different conclusions.
