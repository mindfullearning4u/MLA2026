P04 Worked Example

Study how each example evaluates claim, evidence, and reasoning.

Example 1

Issue: Whether to extend library hours.

Claim: The library should stay open later.

Evidence: Sixty percent of evening computer users are students completing assignments.

Reasoning: The evidence is relevant because later hours would directly support students who need computer access.

Example 2

Issue: Whether to add more bike racks.

Weak evidence: My friend likes bikes.

Better evidence: The school counted 140 bikes locked to fences last month.

Reasoning: The better evidence is measurable and directly connected to the need for bike racks.

Example 3

Shared information: A park has broken lights and a damaged basketball court.

Argument A: Fix the lights first because safety affects all evening visitors.

Argument B: Fix the court first because athletes use it after school.

Evaluation: Argument A may be stronger if the goal is broad public safety because the evidence affects all evening visitors.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The argument I agree with is automatically the strongest.

Correct: The strongest argument is the one with relevant evidence and reasoning that clearly support the claim.

Why this matters: Academic analysis requires evidence-based evaluation, not personal preference.
