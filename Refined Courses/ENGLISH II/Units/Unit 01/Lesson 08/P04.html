Worked Example: A Complete Evidence-Based Response

Passage

Westfield High opened a peer tutoring center after teachers noticed that many students needed help outside regular class hours. During the first semester, 184 students attended at least once, and 63 percent returned for additional sessions. Surveys showed that students valued flexible scheduling, although several requested more science tutors. The school recruited four science-club volunteers for the next semester.

Task

Evaluate whether the tutoring center responded effectively to student needs.

Step 1: Focused Claim

The tutoring center responded effectively to student needs while identifying an area for improvement.

Step 2: Accurate Paraphrase

The school created after-hours academic support, and many participants used it more than once.

Step 3: Relevant Evidence

The strongest evidence is that 63 percent of participating students returned for additional sessions. The request for more science tutors identifies a specific limitation.

Step 4: Reasoning

Repeat attendance indicates that students found the service useful. Recruiting science volunteers demonstrates that the school used feedback to improve the program.

Step 5: Complete Model

The peer tutoring center responded effectively to student needs while continuing to improve. During its first semester, 63 percent of participating students returned for additional sessions, which indicates that the service provided useful academic support. Student feedback also revealed a shortage of science tutors, and the school addressed that limitation by recruiting four science-club volunteers. Together, the attendance data and the school’s response demonstrate an evidence-based effort to meet student needs.

Step 6: Final Check

  • Claim answers the task.
  • Paraphrase is accurate.
  • Evidence is relevant.
  • Reasoning explains significance.
  • Academic vocabulary and conventions are correct.
