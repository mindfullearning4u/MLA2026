Unit 1 Synthesis: Putting It All Together

Direct Teaching

Synthesis means combining the Unit 1 skills into one complete response. You will close-read, paraphrase, select evidence, explain reasoning, use academic vocabulary and voice, and edit conventions.

Step 1: Annotate the text

Identify the central idea, important relationships, unfamiliar terms, and details that could support a response.

Step 2: Restate the task

Determine exactly what you must explain, analyze, or evaluate. Use the task language to keep your claim focused.

Step 3: Form an accurate claim

Your claim must answer the task and remain within what the text supports.

Step 4: Paraphrase and select evidence

Restate necessary context in your own words, then choose the most relevant detail or data. Preserve all facts and relationships.

Step 5: Explain the reasoning

Show how the evidence proves the claim. Use academic terms such as demonstrates, indicates, relevant, significant, and therefore.

Step 6: Revise the complete response

Check academic voice, sentence clarity, precise pronoun references, capitalization, punctuation, and boundaries.

Synthesis frame: The text demonstrates ___ . The author explains ___ . The detail ___ supports this claim because ___ . Therefore, ___ .

Common Mistakes

  • Summarizing without answering the task.
  • Copying instead of paraphrasing.
  • Listing evidence without reasoning.
  • Using academic terms incorrectly.
  • Editing grammar while accidentally changing meaning.
