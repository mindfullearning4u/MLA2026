The Mobile Library Project

Residents in the northern part of Carter County lived more than twenty miles from the nearest public library. Limited public transportation made regular visits difficult, especially for children, older adults, and residents without cars.

The county library proposed a mobile branch that would visit six communities each week. The vehicle would carry books, provide internet access, and allow residents to request materials from the main collection. The first-year cost was estimated at $185,000, including the vehicle, technology, fuel, and staff time.

Before requesting funding, librarians ran a three-month pilot using a borrowed van. The pilot recorded 1,420 visits, 2,870 borrowed items, and 196 new library memberships. A visitor survey found that 84 percent wanted weekly service to continue. However, the borrowed van had no wheelchair lift and could support only two computer stations.

The county commission approved funding for an accessible vehicle with four computer stations. It required quarterly reports on visits, circulation, internet use, costs, and community feedback. Commissioners explained that the pilot demonstrated substantial demand, while the reporting requirement would help them evaluate whether the permanent program remained effective and financially responsible.

Questions

  1. State the central problem.
  2. Paraphrase the proposed solution accurately.
  3. List the services the mobile branch would provide.
  4. What was the purpose of the pilot?
  5. Select two numerical details demonstrating demand.
  6. What two limitations did the pilot van have?
  7. How did the permanent plan address those limitations?
  8. Write an objective claim evaluating the commission’s decision.
  9. Select the strongest evidence for that claim.
  10. Explain why the evidence is relevant.
  11. Use pilot, accessible, and evaluate accurately.
  12. Replace vague language: “The van was a really good idea because tons of people used it.”
  13. Explain why quarterly reports strengthen the decision.
  14. Identify one detail less relevant to a claim about accessibility.
  15. Write a four-sentence response with claim, evidence, and reasoning.
  16. Revise Question 15 for academic voice and precise pronoun references.
  17. Check conventions and describe one correction.
  18. Write a final five-sentence synthesis integrating accurate paraphrase, two evidence details, reasoning, academic vocabulary, and a conclusion.
