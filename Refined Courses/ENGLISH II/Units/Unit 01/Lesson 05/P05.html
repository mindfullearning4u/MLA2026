The Student Welcome Desk

At the beginning of the school year, new students often arrived at the main office with the same questions. They wanted to know where to pick up schedules, how to find clubs, and whom to ask about bus routes. Office staff answered each question, but long lines formed before first period.

A group of student ambassadors proposed a welcome desk near the front entrance. They planned to staff it for twenty minutes each morning during the first two weeks. The ambassadors created a campus map, a list of club sponsors, and a chart of common transportation questions. They also practiced greeting students and explaining when a question needed an adult’s help.

During the first morning, the desk became crowded. Some ambassadors gave directions at the same time, visitors could not tell whom to listen to. The group paused and revised its routine. One ambassador greeted each visitor, another found information, and a third recorded questions that the guide did not answer.

By Friday, the line at the main office was shorter. New students reported that the welcome desk made the building feel less confusing. The ambassadors noticed one remaining problem: the printed campus map did not show a recently moved counseling office. They corrected the map and asked the office to review it before the next school year.
