Academic Vocabulary in Speaking and Writing

Direct Teaching

Academic vocabulary communicates precise relationships among ideas. Strong speakers and writers choose a term because its meaning fits the evidence—not because it merely sounds formal.

Step 1: Identify the purpose

Decide whether you need to compare, analyze, clarify, support, infer, or evaluate. Your purpose controls the vocabulary you choose.

Step 2: Match the term to the evidence

If details prove a claim, call them evidence. If you explain how parts work together, you analyze. If you show similarities and differences, you compare.

Vague: The second plan is better because it has good things.
Precise: The second proposal is more effective because specific survey evidence supports its recommendation.

Step 3: Build a clear written sentence

Name the idea, use the academic term accurately, and connect it to a specific detail: The data support the proposal because 72 percent of students requested a later library closing time.

Step 4: Adapt the sentence for speaking

Keep the precise vocabulary but use a natural signal phrase and shorter phrasing: My evidence is the survey result: 72 percent of students requested later hours.

Step 5: Explain the term when needed

Do not overload a listener. Briefly define an unfamiliar term through context: The result is significant, meaning it is large enough to affect the decision.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a sophisticated word with the wrong meaning.
  • Repeating vocabulary without explaining the evidence.
  • Using vague pronouns such as it or they without clear references.
  • Reading a dense written sentence aloud without adapting it for listeners.

Precision Check

  1. Does the term match my purpose?
  2. Does the evidence support the sentence?
  3. Would a listener understand the term in context?
  4. Can I replace any vague word with a more exact one?
