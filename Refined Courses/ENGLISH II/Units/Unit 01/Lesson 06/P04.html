Worked Example: Precise Vocabulary

Passage

The student council considered two ways to reduce cafeteria waste. A poster campaign cost less, but a reusable-container program was expected to prevent more trash. In a survey of 240 students, 68 percent said they would use reusable containers if washing stations were available. The council recommended a one-month container trial.

Example 1: Choose the Best Term

  1. Purpose: explain why the recommendation is reasonable.
  2. Evidence: 68 percent would participate.
  3. Best terms: evidence, supports, recommendation.
  4. Model: The survey evidence supports the council’s recommendation for a reusable-container trial.

Example 2: Add Analysis

  1. Go beyond repeating the number.
  2. Explain its significance.
  3. Model: The 68-percent response is significant because broad participation could make the trial effective.

Example 3: Adapt for Speaking

  1. Keep the key academic terms.
  2. Shorten the sentence and add a listener cue.
  3. Oral model: The strongest evidence is the survey. It shows that most students would participate, so the trial has a reasonable chance of success.
Common mistake: “The plan is awesome because lots of people like it.” This exaggerates and uses vague language.
