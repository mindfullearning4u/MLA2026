The Safer Crosswalk Proposal

Families near Harbor Middle School asked the city to improve a busy crosswalk. During morning arrival, cars often turned while students were crossing. A neighborhood group documented 37 unsafe turns in five school days and surveyed 92 families.

Seventy-six percent of respondents supported adding flashing warning lights. Others preferred a crossing guard, while a small group believed current signs were sufficient. The group’s proposal recommended flashing lights and a painted stop line because both could operate throughout the day.

At the council meeting, student speaker Lina Perez explained that the observations provided direct evidence of risk. She clarified that the survey measured community opinion, not the actual number of unsafe turns. Her precise distinction strengthened the presentation. The council approved a traffic study before deciding whether to fund the improvements.
