P04 Worked Example

Study each example carefully. The goal is to understand how a reader moves from close reading to accurate paraphrase.

Worked Example 1: Find the Main Idea

Passage: During the first week of practice, the robotics team tested three different wheel designs. The team discovered that the largest wheels moved quickly on smooth floors but struggled on rough surfaces.

Step 1: Identify the topic. The passage is about a robotics team testing wheel designs.

Step 2: Identify the main idea. The team learned that wheel size affected how the robot moved on different surfaces.

Step 3: Keep the important contrast: large wheels were fast on smooth floors but did not work as well on rough surfaces.

Worked Example 2: Build the Paraphrase

Original Passage: During the first week of practice, the robotics team tested three different wheel designs. The team discovered that the largest wheels moved quickly on smooth floors but struggled on rough surfaces.

Step 1: Start with the main idea in your own words.

The robotics team learned that different wheels worked better in different conditions.

Step 2: Add the key details without copying the original structure.

Strong paraphrase: In the team's first practice week, students compared three wheel designs and found that the biggest wheels were fast on smooth floors but less useful on rough ground.

Worked Example 3: Check Accuracy

Question: Did the paraphrase keep the author's meaning?

Check 1: It includes the team and the testing. Yes.

Check 2: It includes three wheel designs. Yes.

Check 3: It keeps the contrast between smooth floors and rough ground. Yes.

Conclusion: The paraphrase is accurate because it keeps the meaning and key details while using new wording.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The team tried wheels, and the small wheels were best everywhere.

Why it is wrong: This changes the meaning. The original passage says the largest wheels were fast on smooth floors but struggled on rough surfaces. It does not say small wheels were best everywhere.

Correct: The team tested three wheel designs and learned that the largest wheels worked well on smooth floors but had trouble on rough surfaces.

Why it is correct: The wording changes, but the original meaning stays accurate.
