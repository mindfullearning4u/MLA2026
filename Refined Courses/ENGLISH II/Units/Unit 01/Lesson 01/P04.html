Worked Example: From Close Reading to an Accurate Paraphrase

Model Passage

For years, the center of Bellwood contained an unused parking lot that collected heat and rainwater. Residents proposed replacing it with a public garden. At first, some business owners worried that fewer parking spaces would discourage customers. The planning committee studied traffic patterns and found that nearby lots were rarely full. Volunteers then designed raised beds, shaded benches, and a channel that directed storm water toward native plants. Within a year, the garden supplied produce to a neighborhood pantry and gave residents a cooler place to gather.

Step 1 — Read for the Gist

The passage explains how a community changed an unused lot into a garden after investigating and addressing concerns.

Step 2 — Identify the Central Idea

Central idea: Careful research and community cooperation transformed a neglected space into a useful public resource.

This statement includes both the process and the result without listing every detail.

Step 3 — Select Essential Evidence

  • The committee studied traffic patterns and found nearby parking was sufficient.
  • Volunteers designed features that addressed heat, rainwater, food access, and gathering space.

These details explain how the plan solved problems and benefited the community.

Step 4 — Make and Support an Inference

Inference: The project succeeded because supporters responded to objections with evidence instead of ignoring them.

Support: The traffic study directly answered the business owners’ parking concern.

Steps 5–7 — Build the Paraphrase

Meaning notes: unused hot lot; parking concern; research showed capacity; garden solved several community needs.

Draft: Bellwood residents wanted to turn an empty downtown lot into a garden, but local merchants feared losing parking. After a study showed that other lots had enough space, volunteers created a garden that managed rainwater, reduced heat, provided food, and offered a meeting place.

Accuracy check: The draft preserves every essential idea, changes wording and structure, and adds no unsupported claim.

Second Quick Model

Source: Although the museum extended its hours, attendance increased only after it offered free evening programs.

Accurate paraphrase: Staying open later did not attract more visitors by itself; participation rose when the museum added no-cost nighttime events.

Why it works: The contrast and cause remain accurate, but the wording and structure are new.
