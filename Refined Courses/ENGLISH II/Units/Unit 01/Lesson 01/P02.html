P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Close Reading Before Paraphrasing

Vocabulary for Your Notebook

  • Close reading: reading carefully enough to notice meaning, details, and word choices.
  • Main idea: the most important point the passage communicates.
  • Key detail: an important fact, example, or idea that supports the main idea.
  • Paraphrase: a restatement of the same meaning in your own words and sentence structure.

Step-by-Step Teaching: Read Before You Restate

Before you write a paraphrase, you need to understand the passage. Do not start by replacing one word at a time. That usually creates a weak paraphrase because the sentence still follows the original too closely.

Use this sequence:

  1. Read the passage once to understand the topic.
  2. Read it again and ask, "What is the author mainly saying?"
  3. Underline or list two key details that must stay in your paraphrase.
  4. Cover the passage for a moment and explain the meaning out loud or silently.
  5. Write the meaning in your own words. Then check that you did not add, remove, or change the author's point.

Passage for Your Notebook

Passage: When the community garden opened, several students visited after school to water the seedlings. At first, the garden looked like a few rows of dirt, but the students knew the small plants needed steady care before vegetables would appear.

Important: This passage is included here so you do not need to look anywhere else.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, write the title Close Reading Before Paraphrasing. Then complete these four parts.

  1. Write the topic of the passage in one short phrase.
  2. Write the main idea in one complete sentence.
  3. List two key details that must stay in an accurate paraphrase.
  4. Write one sentence explaining why students could not expect vegetables right away.

Model Start

Topic: Students caring for a new community garden.

Why this works: The topic is short. It does not try to explain every detail yet. It simply names what the passage is about.
