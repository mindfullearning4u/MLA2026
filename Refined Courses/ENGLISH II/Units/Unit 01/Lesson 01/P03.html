P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Notebook Title: Turning Understanding into a Paraphrase

Direct Teaching: What Makes a Paraphrase Accurate?

An accurate paraphrase has three parts working together.

  1. Same meaning: Your version keeps the author's idea.
  2. Your wording: Your version does not copy the author's sentence.
  3. Important details: Your version keeps details that are needed for the meaning.

Passage for Part 2

Passage: The school library stayed open late on Thursdays so students could work on group projects. The extra hour helped students share resources, ask questions, and finish tasks that were difficult to complete during the regular school day.

Remember: You do not need another source. Use this passage for the notebook task.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, complete each step in order. Do not skip to the final paraphrase.

  1. Write the main idea of the passage in your own words.
  2. List two details that explain why the late library hour helped students.
  3. Write a two-sentence paraphrase of the whole passage.
  4. Check your paraphrase by answering: Did I keep the meaning? Did I use my own wording? Did I keep the important details?

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The library stayed open late so students could work on group projects and finish tasks.

Why this is weak: This is too close to the original. It copies the structure and several exact phrases without showing independent understanding.

Correct: On Thursdays, students had an extra hour in the library to collaborate and complete project work that was hard to finish during normal school hours.

Why this is stronger: The meaning stays the same, but the sentence structure and wording are changed.

Need help? Ask your Teacher of Record for help if your paraphrase keeps sounding exactly like the original sentence.
