Direct Teaching: Close Reading and Accurate Paraphrasing

What Close Reading Does

Close reading means examining a text carefully enough to explain what it says, how it develops meaning, and which details support your thinking. Accurate paraphrasing restates the author’s meaning in genuinely new wording and sentence structure.

Step-by-Step Close Reading Process

  1. Preview: identify the text type, title, and context.
  2. Read for the gist: state what happens or what the passage explains.
  3. Reread and annotate: mark repeated ideas, shifts, unfamiliar words, and striking details.
  4. Identify the central idea: express the most important message in one sentence.
  5. Select essential details: choose details that directly develop that idea.
  6. Make an inference: combine a textual clue with logical reasoning.
  7. Explain evidence: state how the selected detail supports your claim.
  8. Check accuracy: confirm that your explanation matches the text and adds no unsupported idea.

How to Paraphrase Accurately

  1. Read the complete sentence or paragraph until you understand it.
  2. Look away and state the meaning aloud in simple words.
  3. Keep the main idea and essential details.
  4. Change both wording and sentence structure.
  5. Compare your version with the source and correct copied phrasing or changed meaning.

Source: Heavy rain flooded the trail, so park workers closed it until the ground became safe.

Accurate paraphrase: Park staff temporarily blocked access to the path because the storm left it dangerously waterlogged.

A Second Teaching Example

Text: Lena kept the cracked compass because it reminded her that her grandfather had trusted her to finish the journey.

Inference: The compass matters emotionally, not practically.

Evidence explanation: Its physical damage makes it unreliable as a tool, but Lena keeps it because it represents her grandfather’s trust.

Common Mistake

Too close: Workers closed the trail because heavy rain flooded it until it was safe.

This copies the source structure and most of its wording. A strong paraphrase changes the expression while preserving the meaning.
