Worked Example: Choosing Precise Academic Language

Model Passage

Students at Monroe High investigated why fewer classmates were using the school library. Their survey revealed that many students could not visit during the regular school day. The team analyzed attendance records, compared several scheduling options, and recommended opening the library one evening each week. After a six-week trial, evening attendance increased steadily, so the principal concluded that the extended schedule addressed an unmet need.

Step 1 — Identify Important Academic Words

investigated = examined a question carefully
revealed = made information known
analyzed = examined information closely
recommended = formally suggested
concluded = reached a judgment after reviewing evidence

Step 2 — Use Context to Confirm Meaning

The team looked at attendance records and scheduling choices before making a suggestion. Those actions show that analyzed means more than “looked at”; it means examined information to understand it. The phrase “after a six-week trial” shows that the principal’s conclusion was based on results.

Step 3 — Revise a Vague Response

Vague response: The students did some stuff and had a good idea for the library.

Problems: did, stuff, good idea do not name the investigation, evidence, or proposal.

Precise revision: The students analyzed survey and attendance data, then recommended evening library hours to address limited student access.

Step 4 — Add Evidence and Reasoning

Complete academic response: The students’ investigation demonstrates that evidence can guide a practical school decision. Survey results revealed that daytime scheduling limited access, and the six-week trial showed steadily increasing evening attendance. Therefore, the principal reasonably concluded that extended hours met a genuine need.

Step 5 — Check the Academic Voice

  • Clear claim: evidence guided the decision.
  • Precise verbs: demonstrates, revealed, concluded.
  • Specific evidence: survey findings and attendance growth.
  • Logical connection: therefore links the results to the conclusion.
  • Appropriate tone: formal, accurate, and free of slang or exaggeration.

Second Worked Example

Source: The two neighborhoods had similar populations, but the area with shaded bus stops reported more summer riders.

Weak: Shade made buses way better.

Academic revision: The comparison indicates that shaded stops may encourage summer ridership because the neighborhood offering shade reported greater use despite a similar population.

Why it works: The word indicates avoids overstating cause, and the sentence includes the relevant comparison.
