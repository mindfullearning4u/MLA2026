Direct Teaching: Paraphrase with Relevant Textual Evidence

The Three-Part Skill

A strong response does three things: (1) states the source meaning accurately in new words, (2) selects evidence directly connected to that meaning, and (3) explains the connection.

Step 1 — Identify the Assigned Focus

Read the question first. Decide whether you must explain a central idea, character decision, cause, comparison, or other specific point. Evidence is relevant only when it helps answer that focus.

Step 2 — Determine the Source Meaning

  1. Read the complete passage.
  2. State the main idea of the relevant section.
  3. Separate essential ideas from examples and background details.
  4. Check that your statement adds no opinion or unsupported conclusion.

Step 3 — Paraphrase Accurately

  1. Look away from the source and explain the meaning aloud.
  2. Write the idea with new wording and sentence structure.
  3. Keep necessary names, facts, relationships, and sequence.
  4. Compare the paraphrase with the source for accuracy.

Source: Because the bridge inspection found weakened supports, officials limited heavy traffic until repairs were complete.

Paraphrase: Officials temporarily restricted large vehicles after inspectors discovered that the bridge supports needed repair.

Step 4 — Select Relevant Evidence

Ask: “If this detail disappeared, would my reader still understand why the paraphrase is accurate?” Choose details that directly confirm the idea.

Relevant: inspectors found weakened supports; heavy traffic was limited.

Not relevant to the cause: the bridge crossed a wide river. This may be true, but it does not explain the restriction.

Step 5 — Explain the Connection

Use reasoning language such as This detail supports the paraphrase because… or Together, these details show…

Model: The inspection finding supports the paraphrase because it identifies the safety problem that caused officials to restrict heavy vehicles.

Two Quick Evidence Tests

Question: Why did Maya bring an umbrella?

Relevant detail: “Dark clouds gathered and the forecast predicted afternoon storms.”

Irrelevant detail: “Maya’s umbrella had a yellow handle.”

Question: How did the team solve the delay?

Relevant detail: They divided the work and created a shared schedule. A detail about their uniform color would not support the solution.

Common Mistakes

  • Copying the source with only one or two changed words.
  • Selecting a dramatic detail that does not answer the question.
  • Dropping an essential cause, result, comparison, or limitation.
  • Quoting evidence without explaining how it proves the paraphrase.
