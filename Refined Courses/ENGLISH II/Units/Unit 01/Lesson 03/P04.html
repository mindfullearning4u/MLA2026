Worked Example: Paraphrasing and Proving the Meaning

Model Passage

For several summers, Mill Creek became too warm for native trout during long dry periods. A conservation group proposed planting trees along the exposed banks. Some residents questioned whether trees could affect water temperature. The group compared shaded and unshaded sections and found that shaded water remained several degrees cooler on hot afternoons. Volunteers then planted native trees in the most exposed areas and protected the young trunks from wildlife. Five years later, the new shade had reduced peak temperatures, although the group continued monitoring because rainfall and water depth also affected the creek.

Step 1 — Read the Question

Question: How did the conservation group respond to the creek’s high summer temperatures?

The response must explain the group’s actions and the evidence guiding them.

Step 2 — Determine the Meaning

The group tested whether shade affected temperature, used the results to choose where to plant trees, and continued monitoring because other variables also mattered.

Step 3 — Draft the Paraphrase

Accurate paraphrase: After comparisons showed that shaded portions of Mill Creek stayed cooler, the conservation group planted native trees along exposed banks. Later measurements showed lower peak temperatures, but the group kept collecting data because rainfall and water depth could also influence results.

The wording and structure are new, while the actions, findings, and limitation remain intact.

Step 4 — Select Evidence

  • Evidence 1: shaded water remained several degrees cooler.
  • Evidence 2: trees were planted in the most exposed areas.
  • Evidence 3: monitoring continued because rainfall and water depth also affected the creek.

Each detail directly confirms an important part of the paraphrase.

Step 5 — Explain the Connection

The comparison between shaded and unshaded sections supports the paraphrase because it provides the evidence that led to tree planting. Continued monitoring supports the final sentence because the passage explicitly identifies other factors that could affect temperature.

Completed Evidence-Based Response

The conservation group used evidence to address Mill Creek’s high summer temperatures. After finding that shaded sections remained cooler, volunteers planted native trees along exposed banks. The comparison is relevant because it explains why shade became the chosen strategy. The group continued monitoring because rainfall and water depth could also influence the creek, showing that the project’s results were promising but not caused by shade alone.

Second Quick Model

Source: The museum moved popular exhibits closer to the entrance after visitor surveys showed that guests with limited time often missed them.

Paraphrase: Survey results led the museum to relocate frequently requested displays so short-term visitors could reach them more easily.

Relevant evidence: surveys showed time-limited guests missed popular exhibits. A detail about the building’s age would be irrelevant.
