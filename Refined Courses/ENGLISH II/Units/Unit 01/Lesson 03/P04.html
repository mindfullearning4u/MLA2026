P04 Worked Example

This example shows how to build a paraphrase and support it with relevant evidence.

Example 1: Read for Meaning

Passage: The media center created a quiet checkout line for students who had already chosen books. This allowed students who were ready to leave to check out quickly while others continued browsing.

Think aloud: The main idea is not just that there was a line. The important idea is that the new line made checkout faster for students who were ready.

Paraphrase: The media center made book checkout more efficient by giving ready students a separate line.

Example 2: Choose Relevant Evidence

Possible evidence A: "students who had already chosen books"

Possible evidence B: "others continued browsing"

Stronger evidence: Evidence A is stronger for the paraphrase because it explains who used the checkout line and why it helped.

Evidence sentence: The passage says the line was for "students who had already chosen books," which supports the idea that it helped ready students leave faster.

Example 3: Explain the Connection

Paraphrase: The media center made book checkout more efficient by giving ready students a separate line.

Evidence: The line was for "students who had already chosen books."

Explanation: This evidence supports the paraphrase because students who had already chosen books did not need to wait behind students who were still deciding.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The evidence is "media center."

This is a phrase from the passage, but it does not prove the paraphrase. It only names the place.

Correct: The evidence is "students who had already chosen books."

This detail is correct because it supports the idea that the separate line helped students who were ready to check out.
