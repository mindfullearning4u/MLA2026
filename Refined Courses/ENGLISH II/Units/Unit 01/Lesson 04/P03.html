Direct Teaching: Evidence-Based Written Responses

Response Structure

Claim → Evidence → Reasoning → Closing. A claim answers the question. Evidence supplies a relevant quotation or paraphrased detail. Reasoning explains how the evidence proves the claim. The closing reinforces the answer.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Identify the exact task.
  2. Write a direct one-sentence claim.
  3. Select the strongest relevant evidence.
  4. Introduce and quote or paraphrase it accurately.
  5. Explain why it supports the claim.
  6. Remove unrelated sentences.
  7. Revise for clarity, audience, citation, and conventions.

Model

Text: Ridership increased after covered bicycle racks were installed.

Claim: Covered racks encouraged bicycle use.

Evidence: More students rode after installation.

Reasoning: The timing supports the claim because students gained protected storage.

Evidence Integration

Weak: “Ridership increased.”

Stronger: The report states that ridership increased after installation, indicating that protected storage addressed a practical barrier.

Common Mistakes

  • Evidence without a claim.
  • Summary instead of analysis.
  • Repeating evidence as reasoning.
  • Irrelevant details.
  • Excessive copying or missing citation.
