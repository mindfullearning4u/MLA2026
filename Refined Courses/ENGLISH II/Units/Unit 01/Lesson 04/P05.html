Independent Work: “The Easton Community Garden”

Directions

Read the passage and answer all 18 questions. Submit these responses with Page 2 vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Passage

The vacant lot beside Easton Apartments collected litter and storm water. Residents proposed a garden, but the board questioned whether enough people would maintain it. A survey produced eighty-four volunteer pledges.

Organizers created individual beds and pantry plots. Rain channels directed water into planted areas. During the first season, volunteers logged work hours and harvest weight. The garden produced more than nine hundred pounds of vegetables, including three hundred for the pantry.

Several beds dried out during a hot week, and evening work sessions had low attendance. Organizers installed an irrigation timer and moved sessions to Saturday mornings. Participation increased.

At review, the board examined volunteer logs, harvest records, water bills, and comments. Water use rose modestly, but drainage complaints decreased. The board renewed the agreement and funded a tool shed.

The project did not solve every neighborhood need, but it transformed an unused lot into a managed space that produced food, reduced standing water, and brought residents together.
