The Riverside Trail Decision

Riverside’s walking trail connected three neighborhoods to the town center, but heavy rain frequently washed gravel into drainage channels. Residents complained about puddles, erosion, and sections that became inaccessible to wheelchairs and bicycles.

The parks department considered repaving the entire trail or repairing only the most damaged areas. Complete repaving would cost $420,000 and close the trail for four months. Targeted repairs would cost $165,000 and require several two-week closures, but engineers warned that untreated sections might need additional work later.

To understand community priorities, the department surveyed 612 residents. Fifty-four percent preferred complete repaving, 33 percent supported targeted repairs, and 13 percent had no preference. Accessibility advocates favored complete repaving because it would create a consistent surface. Several nearby businesses supported targeted repairs because long closure could reduce customer traffic.

The town council approved complete repaving but divided construction into three stages so part of the trail would remain open. The council explained that this compromise addressed accessibility and long-term maintenance while reducing disruption. It also required monthly public updates on costs and construction progress.
