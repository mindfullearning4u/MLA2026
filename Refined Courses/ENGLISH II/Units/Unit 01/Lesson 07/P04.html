Worked Example: Building an Integrated Response

Passage

A high school replaced disposable water bottles at athletic events with refill stations. During the first month, custodians collected 38 percent less plastic waste near the stadium. Students reported that the stations were convenient, but some visitors had difficulty finding them. The school added directional signs before the next event.

Example 1: Claim and Paraphrase

  1. Central idea: refill stations reduced waste but needed clearer directions.
  2. Accurate paraphrase: The refill program lowered stadium plastic waste, although the school had to improve station visibility.
  3. Check: the wording is new and both important ideas remain accurate.

Example 2: Evidence and Reasoning

  1. Claim: the program was effective.
  2. Best evidence: plastic waste decreased 38 percent.
  3. Reasoning: The 38-percent decrease demonstrates that the stations changed disposal patterns, which supports the claim that the program was effective.

Example 3: Academic Voice and Revision

Weak: The stations were awesome, but people couldn’t find the things.

  1. Replace casual language with precise terms.
  2. Clarify the pronoun reference.
  3. Preserve the contrast.

Revision: The refill stations significantly reduced plastic waste, but the school needed clearer signs so visitors could locate them.

Complete Model

The refill-station program effectively reduced plastic waste at athletic events. During the first month, custodians collected 38 percent less plastic near the stadium. This evidence demonstrates that visitors used the stations, while the school’s decision to add signs addressed the remaining access problem.
