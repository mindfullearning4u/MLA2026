Integrated Close Reading, Evidence, and Academic Voice

Direct Teaching

An effective response combines several skills: understand the text, state an accurate idea, paraphrase without copying, select relevant evidence, explain the connection, and revise for precise academic voice.

Step 1: Read for the central idea

Ask what the passage mainly explains or argues. Separate the author’s point from interesting but minor details.

Step 2: Paraphrase accurately

Restate the idea in new words and sentence structure. Keep names, numbers, and relationships accurate. Do not add an opinion.

Step 3: Select relevant evidence

Choose the detail that most directly proves your claim. More evidence is not automatically better; relevance matters.

Step 4: Explain the connection

Use reasoning words such as demonstrates, indicates, supports, significant, and therefore to show why the evidence matters.

Step 5: Use academic voice

Replace vague or casual wording with precise terms. Maintain an objective tone and avoid unsupported claims such as “everyone knows.”

Step 6: Revise conventions and clarity

Check complete sentences, boundaries, punctuation, capitalization, pronoun references, and logical transitions.

Weak: The library did a good thing because people liked it.
Strong: The attendance increase supports the library’s decision because it demonstrates that extended hours addressed a documented community need.

Common Mistakes

  • Copying instead of paraphrasing.
  • Giving evidence without explaining it.
  • Using impressive vocabulary inaccurately.
  • Changing facts while revising.
  • Using vague pronouns or casual phrases.

Final Response Formula

Accurate claim + relevant evidence + reasoning + precise academic voice + correct conventions.
