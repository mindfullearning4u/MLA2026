P03 — Lesson: Cohesion, Transitions, and Audience-Aware Sentence Control

Cohesive writing guides readers from one idea to the next through logical order, purposeful transitions, clear references, and varied sentence structures.

Five-Step Cohesion Check

  1. Identify the paragraph's controlling idea.
  2. Arrange details in a logical pattern: time, cause/effect, comparison, or importance.
  3. Select transitions that express the real relationship.
  4. Check pronouns and repeated key terms for clear reference.
  5. Vary sentence openings and lengths while preserving clarity for the audience.

Example 1: Addition versus contrast

“The garden provides fresh produce. In addition, it gives students research experience.” Addition is accurate. If the second idea challenged the first, however would be appropriate instead.

Example 2: Cause and effect

“Heavy rain saturated the field. Therefore, the game was moved indoors.” Therefore makes the causal relationship explicit; meanwhile would incorrectly signal simultaneous events.

Example 3: Sentence control and reference

Weak: “The committee reviewed the plan, and they discussed costs, and they voted.” Revised: “After reviewing the plan and discussing costs, committee members voted.” The revision removes repetition and clarifies who acted.

Common Mistakes

  • Adding a transition that does not match the relationship.
  • Beginning every sentence the same way.
  • Using pronouns with unclear antecedents.
  • Combining so many ideas that the sentence becomes difficult to follow.
