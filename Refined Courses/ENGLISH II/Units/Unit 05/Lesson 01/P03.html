P03 — Lesson: Convention Choices and Audience

Now you will connect convention corrections to audience and purpose.

Why Conventions Matter

A convention error is not only a grammar problem. It can change how readers understand the message. If a sentence is incomplete, readers may not know what happened. If two ideas are run together, readers may not see the relationship between the ideas.

In authentic writing, your goal is not to sound perfect for no reason. Your goal is to make meaning clear for a real audience.

Step-by-Step Application

  1. Identify the audience. In the library draft, the audience may be school leaders.
  2. Identify the purpose. The writer wants to persuade school leaders to keep the library open.
  3. Choose conventions that make the reason clear.
  4. Use precise academic vocabulary when it helps the audience understand the issue.
  5. Revise sentence by sentence so each idea is complete and connected.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

When the library closes early. Students often complete assignments in loud hallways.

Correct Example:

When the library closes early, students often complete assignments in loud hallways.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect example separates a dependent clause from the sentence it belongs to, creating a fragment. The correct example joins the dependent clause to the complete thought, so the reader understands the cause-and-effect relationship.
