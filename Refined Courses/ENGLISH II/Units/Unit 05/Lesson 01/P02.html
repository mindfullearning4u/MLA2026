Notebook Task Part 1: Conventions in Context

Notebook Title: Unit 5 Lesson 1 - Standard English Conventions in Authentic Writing

Vocabulary

  • Convention: an accepted rule for grammar, punctuation, capitalization, or usage.
  • Fragment: an incomplete sentence that is missing a subject, verb, or complete thought.
  • Run-on: two or more complete thoughts joined incorrectly.
  • Authentic writing: writing meant for a real purpose, audience, or situation.
  • Revision: changing writing to improve meaning, clarity, organization, or audience fit.

Authentic Draft

The library should stay open after school because many students need a quiet place to work. Some students ride late buses, they need a safe place to study. When the library closes early. Students often complete assignments in loud hallways.

Step-by-Step Teaching

  1. Read for meaning first. The writer wants the library to stay open after school.
  2. Find sentence boundaries. Each complete sentence needs a subject, a verb, and a complete thought.
  3. Check for run-ons. "Some students ride late buses, they need a safe place to study" joins two complete thoughts with only a comma.
  4. Check for fragments. "When the library closes early" begins a thought but does not finish it.
  5. Revise so conventions make the message easier to understand.

Notebook Directions

Copy the draft into your notebook. Label one run-on and one fragment. Then write a corrected version of each sentence.

Model Example: Run-on correction - Some students ride late buses, so they need a safe place to study.
