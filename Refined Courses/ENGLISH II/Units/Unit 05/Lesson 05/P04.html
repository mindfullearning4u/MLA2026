P04 Worked Example

Each example shows how feedback becomes revision.

Example 1: Vague Feedback

Feedback: Make this clearer.

Step 1: Ask what is unclear.

Step 2: Find vague words such as "it" or "good."

Revision: Replace vague wording with a specific reason for the audience.

Example 2: Audience Feedback

Feedback: This sounds too casual for school leaders.

Step 1: Identify casual wording.

Step 2: Replace it with respectful academic language.

Revision: "It would be good" becomes "Extended hours would support students who need a supervised study space."

Example 3: Evidence Feedback

Feedback: Add support for your claim.

Step 1: Keep the claim.

Step 2: Add a reason or evidence that helps the reader understand the claim.

Revision: "Students need it" becomes "Students who ride late buses need a supervised place to complete assignments while they wait."

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Delete the whole paragraph whenever someone gives feedback.

Correct Example:

Use feedback to improve the part of the paragraph that needs clearer evidence, tone, wording, or conventions.

Teachable Explanation: Revision is not starting over automatically. Strong revision protects the main idea while improving how well the writing serves the audience.
