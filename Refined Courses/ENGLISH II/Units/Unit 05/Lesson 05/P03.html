P03 — Lesson: Feedback and Revision for Audience Needs

Strong revision is purposeful: writers interpret feedback, decide what the audience needs, and make changes that improve meaning—not merely surface appearance.

Revision Cycle

  1. Identify the audience, purpose, and required outcome.
  2. Sort feedback by focus: ideas/evidence, organization, audience/tone, wording, or conventions.
  3. Prioritize changes that most improve understanding.
  4. Revise the passage—not just the isolated sentence.
  5. Compare before and after versions and explain the effect.
  6. Proofread only after meaning and organization are strong.

Example 1: Add evidence

Draft: “The library should stay open later because it would help.” Feedback: Show who benefits and how. Revision: “Keeping the library open until late buses depart would give students a supervised place to complete assignments.”

Example 2: Adjust tone

Draft to principal: “You need to fix the confusing schedule.” Feedback: Use a respectful, solution-focused tone. Revision: “A clearer schedule with consistent labels would help students locate meeting times.”

Example 3: Improve organization

If feedback says the recommendation appears before the problem is clear, move the problem and evidence first, then present the recommendation. Reordering can improve logic without adding new facts.

Common Mistakes

  • Accepting every suggestion without evaluating it.
  • Correcting punctuation while ignoring weak reasoning.
  • Adding words without improving clarity.
  • Changing tone so much that the original purpose disappears.
