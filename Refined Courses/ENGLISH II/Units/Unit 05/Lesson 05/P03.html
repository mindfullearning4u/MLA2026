Notebook Task Part 2: Applying Feedback

Now you will turn useful feedback into a stronger revision.

Revised Model

The library should remain open until late buses depart because many students need a supervised place to complete assignments after school. Extended hours would support students who cannot leave campus immediately and would reduce the need to work in loud hallways.

Step-by-Step Explanation

  1. The revision keeps the same claim: the library should stay open later.
  2. It adds a reason that fits the audience: students need a supervised study place.
  3. It replaces vague wording with precise academic language.
  4. It uses complete sentences and correct punctuation.
  5. It explains why the change matters to students and school leaders.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

The feedback says be better, so I added: The library is really, really good.

Correct Example:

The feedback asks for a clearer reason, so I added: Extended hours would give students a supervised place to complete assignments after school.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect revision reacts to vague feedback without improving the audience's understanding. The correct revision names the feedback goal and makes a specific improvement.
