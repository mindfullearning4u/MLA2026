P05 — Independent Work

Read the draft, audience information, and feedback. Answer all 12 questions in complete sentences in your notebook.

Draft: A Better Welcome Night

Audience: families considering attending the school's fall welcome night. Purpose: explain the event and encourage attendance.

Welcome night is next Thursday and it will be good. There are lots of things, and everybody should come. Teachers will talk in rooms. Clubs have tables. The schedule is kind of confusing, but people can ask somebody. Food is available, and the event ends at eight. You will learn stuff about the school.

Peer feedback:

  • Families need the exact time, location, and order of events.
  • Explain what visitors will gain instead of saying “good” and “stuff.”
  • The sentence about the confusing schedule may reduce confidence; offer a solution.
  • Use a welcoming but professional tone.
  • Group related information so readers can scan it quickly.

Available facts: The event runs 5:30–8:00 p.m. in the main building. At 5:30, families check in and receive a color-coded schedule. Teacher sessions begin at 6:00. The club fair and refreshments open at 6:45. Student ambassadors guide visitors throughout the evening.

Questions

  1. State the draft's audience and purpose.
  2. Identify three vague or ineffective phrases.
  3. Sort each feedback item as ideas/evidence, organization, audience/tone, wording, or conventions.
  4. Which revision should be prioritized first, and why?
  5. Write a precise opening sentence using the available facts.
  6. Revise the sentence about the confusing schedule so it offers a solution.
  7. Replace “learn stuff” with audience-relevant information.
  8. Explain how the color-coded schedule and ambassadors address family needs.
  9. Propose an effective order for the information.
  10. Rewrite one sentence in a welcoming, professional tone.
  11. Produce a polished paragraph of at least five sentences.
  12. Explain three revision choices and how each improves the message for its audience.
