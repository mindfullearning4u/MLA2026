P04 — Worked Example

Follow the model as an editor improves a vague paragraph without changing its facts.

Draft

The storm was bad. Rain went across the field, and the river became big. Officials told residents to leave quickly.

Model 1: bad → severe

  1. Dictionary: severe means very serious or intense.
  2. Relationship: bad is broad; severe identifies degree.
  3. Context: flooding and evacuation support high intensity.
  4. Revision: “The storm was severe.”

Model 2: went → swept

  1. A thesaurus offers moved, flowed, traveled, swept.
  2. Swept suggests forceful movement across a surface.
  3. The storm context supports force, so “Rain swept across the field” is precise.

Model 3: big and quickly

Swollen precisely describes a river enlarged beyond its usual level. Immediately is more exact than quickly when officials require action without delay.

Revised paragraph: The storm was severe. Rain swept across the field, and the river became swollen. Officials told residents to leave immediately.

Quality Check

Each replacement is grammatically correct, supported by context, and more exact without exaggerating the facts.
