A Proposal for a Student Writing Center

Audience: principal and school improvement committee. Purpose: recommend a peer writing center.

Draft: Our school should make a writing center because students has lots of problems with writing and it would be really awesome. Some students don’t know good grammar, other students have ideas but they cant organize it. The center could help kids with essays, applications, and other stuff. We did a little trial program and people liked it. Due to the fact that writing is important for everything, the committee should definitely give us a room and money. This will fix writing at our school.

Verified facts: A four-week pilot served 32 students; 7 trained peer tutors held two sessions each week; 81% of participants completed a revised draft; surveys showed organization and sentence clarity were the most common needs; the proposal requests one classroom after school and $450 for training materials.

Questions

  1. State the audience and purpose.
  2. Identify and correct two convention errors.
  3. Identify three vague, informal, or exaggerated phrases.
  4. Use context to explain what pilot means in the verified facts.
  5. Choose a precise replacement for “problems with writing” and justify it.
  6. Select the two strongest facts for the recommendation and explain why.
  7. Revise one sentence to improve cohesion with an accurate transition.
  8. Rewrite “Due to the fact that” concisely.
  9. Revise the request so its tone fits school leaders.
  10. Write a supportable conclusion without claiming the center will “fix writing.”
  11. Produce a polished 8–10 sentence proposal using only verified facts.
  12. Explain four revision decisions: conventions, vocabulary/meaning, organization/cohesion, and audience/style.
