P03 — Lesson: Putting Unit 5 Skills Together

A final revision is not a hunt for random errors. It is a planned sequence that improves correctness, meaning, organization, style, and audience fit.

The Six-Pass Synthesis

  1. Conventions: correct agreement, pronouns, fragments, run-ons, punctuation, and usage.
  2. Meaning: use context, roots, connotations, and precise vocabulary.
  3. Organization: check focus, logical order, transitions, and cohesion.
  4. Evidence and explanation: make every claim specific and supported.
  5. Style and audience: adjust tone, formality, sentence variety, and concision.
  6. Final proof: read once for meaning and once sentence by sentence.

Example 1 — Conventions and Precision

Before: The volunteers was really good at helping kids.

After: The volunteers were effective tutors for elementary students.

Why: The revision corrects agreement and replaces vague, informal wording.

Example 2 — Cohesion and Evidence

Before: Attendance improved. We sent reminders. The program mattered.

After: After organizers began sending reminders, weekly attendance increased from 61% to 84%, demonstrating that consistent communication strengthened participation.

Why: A transition shows cause and effect, while data replaces an unsupported claim.

Example 3 — Audience and Style

Before: Our project was awesome, so you should totally fund it.

After: Funding would allow the program to serve 20 additional students while maintaining its small-group tutoring model.

Why: The tone fits grant reviewers, and the request states a measurable outcome.

Common Mistakes

  • Proofreading grammar before fixing unclear ideas.
  • Replacing simple words with complicated but inaccurate ones.
  • Adding transitions that do not match the relationship between ideas.
  • Calling a sentence better without naming the skill and effect.
