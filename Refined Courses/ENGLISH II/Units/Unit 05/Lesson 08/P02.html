Notebook Task Part 1: Unit 5 Revision Checklist

Notebook Title: Unit 5 Lesson 8 - Language, Vocabulary, Style, and Revision Synthesis

Vocabulary Review

Convention: a grammar, usage, punctuation, or capitalization rule that helps readers understand writing.

Etymology: the history or origin of a word.

Derivation: a related word formed from a root or base word.

Connotation: the feeling connected to a word.

Denotation: the dictionary meaning of a word.

Revision: changing writing to improve meaning, organization, word choice, audience fit, and clarity.

Polish: final improvement of style, precision, sentence control, and conventions.

Synthesis Draft for This Lesson

Audience: school leaders

Purpose: explain why a supervised after-school study space would help students

Draft: The school should keep the library open longer. It helps kids who have to wait. The hallways are loud, and students cannot focus good. This change would be awesome because it gives people a place for work.

Step-by-Step Synthesis Process

Step 1: Check conventions. Correct errors such as focus good to focus well.

Step 2: Check word precision. Replace kids, people, and place for work with more exact language.

Step 3: Check tone and connotation. Replace awesome because it is too casual for school leaders.

Step 4: Check sentence flow. Use transitions that show cause, result, addition, or contrast accurately.

Step 5: Check audience fit. Ask whether a school leader would understand the specific need and the reason for the request.

Notebook Directions: Copy the five-step checklist and apply it to the synthesis draft.

Model Example

Original: This change would be awesome because it gives people a place for work.

Revised: This change would support students by providing a supervised academic workspace after school.

Why this works: The revision improves tone, precision, audience fit, and clarity while keeping the same basic meaning.
