P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Convention and Clarity

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Original: Students cannot focus good.

Thinking: The sentence uses an adjective where an adverb is needed.

Revision: Students cannot focus well.

Reason: The convention correction makes the sentence clear and polished.

Worked Example 2: Vocabulary and Tone

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Original: This change would be awesome.

Thinking: Awesome is positive, but its casual connotation does not fit school leaders.

Revision: This change would support student academic needs.

Reason: Support is formal, precise, and audience-appropriate.

Worked Example 3: Full Synthesis Revision

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Original: It helps kids who have to wait.

Thinking: It is unclear, kids is informal, and the reason needs more detail.

Revision: Extended library hours would help students who wait for late buses by giving them a quiet place to complete assignments.

Reason: The revision improves clarity, precision, audience fit, and explanation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Fix only commas and leave vague words, informal tone, and unclear pronouns in the draft.

Correct Example:

Revise conventions, word choice, tone, sentence flow, and audience fit before submitting the final draft.

Teachable Explanation: Unit 5 synthesis requires more than one skill. A polished draft uses conventions, vocabulary knowledge, precise meaning, and audience-focused revision together.
