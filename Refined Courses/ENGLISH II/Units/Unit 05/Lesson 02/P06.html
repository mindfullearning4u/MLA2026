A Garden Built from Word Roots

The science club planned a biodiversity garden beside the school. Before planting, the members studied the vocabulary in their guide. They recognized bio, the Greek root meaning “life,” in biology, biography, and biodiversity. Yet the guide made clear that the words were not interchangeable. Biology is the study of life, a biography is a written account of a life, and biodiversity describes the variety of living things in an environment.

The garden would include a hydroponic station. The root hydr means “water,” and the club inferred that hydroponic plants grow with water playing a central role. Context confirmed the inference: the next sentence explained that the plants would receive dissolved nutrients without soil. Students also discussed photosynthesis. They connected photo, meaning “light,” with photograph and photocopy, but the suffix and context showed that photosynthesis names a process rather than an image.

During construction, the club used a thermometer to monitor the greenhouse. Students recognized therm, meaning “heat,” in thermal and thermostat. They also used a microscope to inspect leaves. The root scope relates to seeing or viewing, appearing in telescope and periscope. By building word families, the students could approach unfamiliar terms without guessing blindly.

At the dedication, the principal praised the club's conservation work. One student first assumed conservation simply meant “saving.” After tracing serv, meaning “keep or protect,” and rereading the passage, she refined the definition: conservation is the careful protection and management of natural resources. Her revision demonstrated the club's main discovery—word history can suggest a meaning, but careful readers confirm that meaning with structure and context.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. State the passage's central idea about determining word meaning.
  2. Explain the shared root meaning in biology, biography, and biodiversity.
  3. How does the passage distinguish those three bio-family words?
  4. Use both the root and context to define hydroponic.
  5. Why would defining photosynthesis as “a picture made with light” be inaccurate?
  6. Build a three-word family for therm and explain the shared idea.
  7. Compare microscope and telescope. What is shared, and what changes?
  8. Break conservation into a base/root and suffix when possible, then explain its contextual meaning.
  9. What mistake did the student make when she first defined conservation? How did she correct it?
  10. Write a concise paragraph explaining why etymology, derivation, word families, and context should be used together.
