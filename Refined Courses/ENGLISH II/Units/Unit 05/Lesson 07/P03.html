P03 — Lesson: Style, Precision, and Audience-Focused Polish

Polishing is purposeful revision. Every change should make the writing clearer, more exact, more appropriate for its audience, or easier to read.

A Five-Step Polish

  1. Confirm audience and purpose. Decide what readers need and what response you want.
  2. Replace vague, inflated, or informal words. Choose language that names the exact idea.
  3. Adjust tone and formality. Match the situation without sounding artificial.
  4. Improve sentence flow. Combine choppy ideas, remove repetition, and vary sentence openings.
  5. Proofread last. Correct grammar, usage, punctuation, and spelling after ideas are clear.

Example 1 — Precision

Before: The tutoring program helped many people.

After: The tutoring program served 48 students and increased weekly homework completion.

Why it works: The revision replaces vague praise with measurable evidence.

Example 2 — Audience and Tone

Before: Our schedule was awesome and stopped everyone from getting confused.

After: A consistent meeting schedule reduced conflicts and helped volunteers arrive prepared.

Why it works: The wording is professional, specific, and suitable for school leaders.

Example 3 — Concision and Flow

Before: Due to the fact that the room was small in size, we made the decision to divide into two separate groups.

After: Because the room was small, we divided into two groups.

Why it works: The revision removes unnecessary words without losing meaning.

Common Mistakes

  • Changing words only to make them sound longer or more complicated.
  • Using casual praise such as awesome instead of evidence.
  • Proofreading commas while leaving the main idea vague.
  • Ignoring the reader’s knowledge, needs, or expectations.
