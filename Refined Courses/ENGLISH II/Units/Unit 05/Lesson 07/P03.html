P03 Notebook Task - Part 2: Making Polish Decisions

Polishing requires a reason for each change. A strong writer can say, "I changed this word because it is more precise," or "I corrected this sentence because the audience needs clear academic grammar." Do not revise randomly. Each change should improve audience fit, precision, style, or correctness.

Step 1: Reread the sentence and ask what the audience needs to understand.

Step 2: Decide whether the problem is vague wording, informal tone, weak sentence flow, or a convention error.

Step 3: Make one targeted revision.

Step 4: Explain why the revision is stronger.

Notebook Directions: Rewrite this sentence and explain your revision: "It is a good thing because kids need it after school."

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

The library should stay open because it is awesome and kids need a cool spot.

Correct Example:

The library should remain open later because students who wait for late buses need a supervised academic workspace.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect example uses casual words that do not fit school leaders. The correct example keeps the same idea but uses formal, precise language and identifies the student need.

Use this second pass to strengthen your thinking before you move to practice. The goal is not just to get an answer; the goal is to explain why the answer is supported by the text, source, sentence, or communication situation.

  1. Reread the embedded passage, source, scenario, sentence, or prompt on this page and mark the most useful details.
  2. Separate evidence from opinion. Evidence is stated or clearly implied by the text; opinion is a reaction that still needs proof.
  3. Write the reasoning step that connects the evidence to the skill. Use because, therefore, this shows, or this matters because to make the connection visible.
  4. Revise vague words into precise academic language that matches the standard and lesson vocabulary.
