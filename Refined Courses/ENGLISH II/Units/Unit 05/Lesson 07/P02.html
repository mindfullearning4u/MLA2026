Notebook Task Part 1: Polishing for a Specific Audience

Notebook Title: Unit 5 Lesson 7 - Style, Precision, and Audience-Focused Polish

Vocabulary

Style is the way writing sounds because of sentence structure, word choice, and tone.

Precision means choosing words that say exactly what you mean.

Audience-focused polish means making final revisions so the writing fits the reader's expectations.

Connotation is the feeling or association a word creates beyond its dictionary meaning.

Derivation is a word formed from another word or root, such as revise, revision, and revisable.

Student Draft for This Lesson

Audience: school leaders

Purpose: persuade school leaders to support a study space after school

Draft: The library should stay open later because students need a good place to do work. It is a helpful thing for people who wait for late buses. Also, it would be nice because the hallways are loud and students cannot focus good.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the audience. The audience is school leaders, so the writing should be respectful, formal, and specific.

Step 2: Find vague words. Words such as good, helpful thing, people, and nice do not tell the reader enough.

Step 3: Replace vague words with precise words. Good place can become supervised study space. People can become students who wait for late buses.

Step 4: Check tone. The phrase it would be nice sounds casual. A stronger version is this change would support students.

Step 5: Correct conventions. The phrase students cannot focus good should become students cannot focus well.

Notebook Directions: In your notebook, copy the draft, underline three vague or informal phrases, and write one precise replacement for each phrase.

Model Example

Original: It is a helpful thing for people who wait for late buses.

Polished: Extended library hours would provide students who wait for late buses with a supervised academic space.

Why this works: The polished sentence names the action, identifies the students, and uses precise academic wording that fits school leaders as the audience.
