Worked Example

Example 1: Replacing Vague Words

Original: Students need a good place to work.

Thinking: Good place is vague. A school leader needs to know what kind of place and why it matters.

Revision: Students need a supervised academic space to complete assignments after school.

Reason: The revision is more precise and fits the audience.

Example 2: Using Word Families

Original: The plan is useful.

Thinking: Useful is general. The word support belongs to a word family that fits school improvement: support, supportive, supports.

Revision: The plan supports students who need a quiet place to study after school.

Reason: The verb supports is precise and shows the action clearly.

Example 3: Correcting Conventions

Original: Students cannot focus good in loud hallways.

Thinking: Good is an adjective, but the sentence needs an adverb to describe how students focus.

Revision: Students cannot focus well in loud hallways.

Reason: Correct conventions make the sentence more polished and credible.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Use bigger words even when they do not fit the sentence.

Correct Example:

Use precise words that fit the meaning, audience, and tone of the sentence.

Teachable Explanation: Polished writing is not about sounding complicated. A strong word is the word that communicates the exact meaning clearly to the intended audience.
