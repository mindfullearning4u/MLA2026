P05 — Independent Work: Polish a Scholarship Response

Read the draft and source facts. Answer all 12 questions. Submit these answers with your vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Building a Neighborhood Reading Program

Audience: scholarship committee. Purpose: demonstrate leadership and growth.

Draft: Last spring I did a really good reading thing for kids at our community center. I got some volunteers, and we met on Saturdays. It was kind of hard because people forgot to come, but I fixed it. We used books in English and Spanish, which was awesome for everybody. The children got better, and I learned a lot about being a leader. Due to the fact that the program worked well, I think the committee should know that I can make a difference.

Verified facts: The writer recruited 8 volunteers; 24 children participated; text reminders increased weekly attendance to 85%; volunteers used bilingual books; children completed reading logs; the writer scheduled teams and adjusted plans after reviewing attendance.

Questions

  1. State the audience and purpose.
  2. Identify two vague phrases and explain why each is weak.
  3. Identify two informal or exaggerated phrases.
  4. Choose two verified facts that best demonstrate leadership and explain why.
  5. Revise the opening sentence for precision and audience fit.
  6. Replace “I fixed it” with a precise explanation.
  7. Revise the sentence about bilingual books using a professional tone.
  8. Rewrite the final sentence concisely.
  9. Combine or reorder two ideas to improve flow.
  10. Write a stronger conclusion that shows growth.
  11. Produce a polished 6–8 sentence scholarship paragraph using only the verified facts.
  12. Explain three revision decisions: one for precision, one for audience, and one for style or sentence flow.
