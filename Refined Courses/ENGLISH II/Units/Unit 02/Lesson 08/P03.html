P03 — Lesson: Unit 2 Synthesis

Synthesis Process

  1. Establish literal events, characters, setting, conflict, and point of view.
  2. Trace theme through choices, consequences, and change.
  3. Compare perspectives and explain a coming-of-age realization when present.
  4. Analyze figurative language, symbolism, ambiguity, mood, and tone.
  5. Connect pacing and structure to tension and meaning.
  6. Recognize source-text patterns when a work adapts a myth or earlier story.
  7. Write a unified claim that explains how several techniques work together; support it with precise evidence.

The Synthesis Difference

Separate observations become synthesis when they support one larger interpretation. Instead of writing “the author uses a symbol, a mood shift, and short sentences,” explain how the symbol, shift, and pacing together reveal the character’s movement from imitation to independence.

Common Mistakes

Avoid listing devices, summarizing the plot, forcing every skill into one answer, or using evidence without explanation. Select the techniques that most strongly support your claim.

Additional Synthesis Examples

Example 1 — Character, Symbol, and Theme

A character repairs a cracked compass after rejecting a mentor’s advice. The compass can symbolize guidance; repairing it can demonstrate maturation and develop a theme about learning from inherited wisdom.

Example 2 — Figurative Language and Pacing

“The seconds crawled toward the final bell.” Personification creates impatience, while the expanded attention to seconds slows pacing and increases tension.

Example 3 — Adaptation and Perspective

A modern quest story gives the traditional monster a voice. The changed perspective complicates the source text’s hero-versus-villain structure and may produce a theme about judgment.

Full Analysis Routine

  1. Identify the central conflict and perspectives.
  2. Trace character change.
  3. Analyze an important symbol or image.
  4. Explain figurative language and mood.
  5. Evaluate pacing or structure.
  6. Connect craft choices to theme.
  7. Use multiple pieces of evidence.
  8. Explain relationships among the techniques instead of listing them.
