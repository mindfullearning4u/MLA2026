P04 Worked Example

Study how each example compares source, adaptation, and effect.

Example 1: Preserved Element

Question: What does the adaptation preserve?

Step 1: In the source, a messenger shares fire.

Step 2: In the adaptation, Lina shares access to a digital archive.

Modeled response: The adaptation preserves the idea of one person sharing a restricted resource with a larger community.

Example 2: Transformed Element

Question: What does the adaptation transform?

Step 1: The source uses fire as a powerful resource.

Step 2: The adaptation uses digital information as the powerful resource.

Modeled response: The adaptation transforms fire into access to information, making the conflict about knowledge in a modern school setting.

Example 3: Effect of the Adaptation

Question: Why does the transformation matter?

Step 1: The older source focuses on survival and communal warmth.

Step 2: The modern adaptation focuses on learning and shared access.

Modeled response: The adaptation makes the myth's question about who should control powerful resources relevant to students by connecting it to research access.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The source and adaptation are the same because both people break a rule.

Correct example:

Both characters break a rule to share a restricted resource, but the adaptation changes the resource from fire to digital information so the conflict fits a modern school setting.

Why this matters: Adaptation analysis must explain both similarity and meaningful change.
