P04 — Worked Example

Source: “The Thread Through Night”

A young guide enters a stone maze to rescue villagers taken by a beast. An elder gives her a spool of red thread. She ties one end at the entrance, defeats the beast, and follows the thread home. The people praise her courage, but she credits the elder’s wisdom.

Adaptation: “Signal in the Tunnel”

During a subway blackout, trainee engineer Aria enters a closed tunnel to reach trapped passengers. Her retired supervisor gives her an old radio transmitter that leaves a repeating signal. Aria clears debris, leads riders out by following the signal, and publicly credits the supervisor whose methods the agency had dismissed.

Step-by-Step Analysis

  1. Preserved: descent into danger, guidance tool, rescue, return.
  2. Changed: maze becomes subway; thread becomes radio signal; beast becomes infrastructure failure.
  3. Shared theme: courage succeeds through guidance and memory.
  4. New purpose: the adaptation makes the pattern relevant to technology and institutional respect for experience.
  5. Effect: replacing a monster with a system failure shifts attention from individual combat to public responsibility.

Modeled response: Both texts present courage as a combination of action and inherited wisdom. The radio signal modernizes the thread while preserving its guiding function. By making the danger a subway failure, the adaptation emphasizes collaboration and responsible infrastructure rather than heroic combat.

Second Worked Example: A Modern Persephone Pattern

In a contemporary story, Ava spends half the year in her coastal hometown and half with her mother in a distant city. Each move changes her friendships and sense of identity. Unlike the ancient seasonal myth, Ava negotiates the schedule herself at the end.

  1. Source: The Persephone pattern of divided worlds and recurring departure.
  2. Preserved: Separation, return, and seasonal imagery.
  3. Changed: Ava gains agency rather than remaining the object of others’ decisions.
  4. New effect: The adaptation explores adolescence, shared custody, and self-determination.
  5. Theme comparison: Both address separation, but the adaptation emphasizes creating belonging across two homes.

Modeled response: By preserving the cycle of departure and return while giving Ava control over the final arrangement, the author transforms an ancient pattern about power into a modern coming-of-age statement about agency.
