Source: “The Jar of the South Wind”

Captain Leona’s crew had crossed the gray sea for nine days when an island keeper offered them shelter. Before they departed, he gave Leona a sealed bronze jar. “The south wind sleeps inside,” he warned. “Open it only when the northern cliffs appear. Used too soon, it will command the sea instead of serving the ship.” Leona promised to wait, and the crew stored the jar beneath the mast.

On the third calm day, however, Leona feared that their food would run out. Ignoring the keeper’s warning, she opened the jar to hurry the voyage. The released wind struck the sails like a giant hand, shattered a steering oar, and drove the ship far from its course. Leona first blamed the weather, but when the crew became frightened, she admitted what she had done. That night she studied the stars her grandmother had taught her to read. By following their pattern and rationing the remaining food, she guided the damaged ship home. The crew forgave her only after she accepted responsibility and taught every sailor how to read the sky.

Adaptation: “The Unverified Alert”

Student journalist Maya received an anonymous message claiming that unsafe chemicals had leaked near the school garden. Her editor reminded her to confirm the claim with two reliable sources, but Maya worried another news site would publish first. She posted the warning immediately and added a dramatic headline: “Toxic Spill Threatens Campus.” Families rushed to pick up students, clubs canceled events, and the principal closed the garden while officials investigated.

The investigation found no spill. Maya traced the message to a mislabeled public spreadsheet about a routine cleaning product delivery. At first she blamed the confusing data, but then she admitted that she had ignored the verification rule. She interviewed the grounds manager, corrected the article, and published a clear explanation of how the error occurred. Using the same data tools that had misled her, Maya created a source-checking guide for the student newsroom. Trust returned slowly, but her classmates began using the guide before publishing urgent claims.

Questions

  1. Identify the shared plot pattern.
  2. Compare the protagonists’ initial errors.
  3. What does the storm jar become in the adaptation?
  4. What do the stars become?
  5. State the shared theme.
  6. How does the modern context change the stakes?
  7. Why might the adapter choose journalism?
  8. Explain one preserved symbol or function.
  9. Evaluate one important change.
  10. Write a paragraph analyzing how the adaptation reshapes the source for a modern audience.
