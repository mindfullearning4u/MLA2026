📘 ENG2 | Unit 02 | Lesson 05

P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Now you will explain how one poem can support more than one reasonable interpretation.

Poem

A blue door waits at the end of the alley,
paint peeling like old maps in the rain.
No house holds it. No key turns near it.
Still, someone has polished the brass knob clean.
I stand with my notebook under my coat,
listening to water write on the stones.
The door does not open. The door does not close.
It only shines where my hand might go.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: One interpretation is that the door represents opportunity. The polished knob and the speaker's possible hand suggest a future choice.

Step 2: Another interpretation is that the door represents uncertainty. It has no house and no key, so the speaker cannot know where it leads.

Step 3: Both interpretations are reasonable because the poem gives evidence for both possibility and uncertainty.

Step 4: Strong analysis explains the tension between meanings instead of forcing the poem to mean only one thing.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write Interpretation 1: the door as opportunity.
  2. Add two pieces of evidence that support that interpretation.
  3. Write Interpretation 2: the door as uncertainty.
  4. Add two pieces of evidence that support that interpretation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The poem is confusing, so it can mean anything.

Correct example:

The poem is ambiguous because the door can suggest opportunity through the polished knob, but it can also suggest uncertainty because no house holds it and no key turns near it.

Why this matters: Ambiguity is not random. A strong interpretation must be supported by specific words and images from the poem.
