P04 Worked Example

Study how each example explains ambiguity with evidence instead of guessing.

Example 1: Literal Meaning

Question: What is the literal situation in the poem?

Step 1: Identify what appears in the poem: a blue door, rain, stones, a brass knob, and a speaker with a notebook.

Step 2: State what happens: the speaker stands near the door but does not enter.

Modeled response: Literally, the poem shows a speaker standing in the rain near a blue door that is not attached to a house.

Example 2: One Interpretation

Question: How can the door represent opportunity?

Step 1: Use evidence: "the brass knob clean" and "where my hand might go."

Step 2: Explain the evidence: the clean knob and possible hand suggest the speaker could choose to reach toward the door.

Modeled response: The door can represent opportunity because the polished knob and the speaker's possible hand suggest a choice waiting to be made.

Example 3: Another Interpretation

Question: How can the door represent uncertainty?

Step 1: Use evidence: "No house holds it" and "No key turns near it."

Step 2: Explain the evidence: the door is disconnected from a clear destination, so the speaker cannot know where it leads.

Modeled response: The door can represent uncertainty because it stands without a house or key, making its purpose unclear.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The door means opportunity because doors always mean opportunity.

Correct example:

The door may mean opportunity because the polished knob and the speaker's possible hand suggest a choice the speaker could make.

Why this matters: The incorrect example relies on a general symbol rule. The correct example uses details from this poem.
