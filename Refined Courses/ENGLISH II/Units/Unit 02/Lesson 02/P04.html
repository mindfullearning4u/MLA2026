Worked Example: Comparing Theme Development

Text A

After losing the election for team captain, Nia avoided practice. When a younger runner asked for help, Nia reluctantly shared her training plan. Guiding the runner reminded her that leadership was an action, not a title. She returned to practice and continued mentoring.

Text B

Owen inherited his father’s workshop but knew little about repairs. Embarrassed, he kept the door locked. When a neighbor’s chair broke, Owen admitted his uncertainty and asked an experienced carpenter for guidance. Months later, he reopened the workshop as a shared community space.

Example 1: Themes

Text A: Genuine leadership is demonstrated through service rather than position.

Text B: Growth begins when people admit limitations and accept help.

Example 2: Shared Idea

Both texts suggest that identity depends more on purposeful action than on status or appearance.

Example 3: Different Development

  1. Text A uses disappointment and mentoring to redefine leadership.
  2. Text B uses embarrassment and collaboration to redefine competence.
  3. Nia helps another person first; Owen first allows another person to help him.
  4. Both endings transform a private struggle into community contribution.

Complete Comparative Model

Both texts show that meaningful identity develops through action rather than a title or possession. Nia discovers that she can lead by mentoring even without being captain, whereas Owen becomes capable by admitting uncertainty and learning from another person. The authors use opposite forms of help—giving and receiving—to develop a shared theme about humility and contribution.
