P04 Worked Example

Study how each example builds a comparative theme response from both passages.

Worked Example 1: Topic to Theme

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Question: What shared theme fits both passages?

Step 1: Identify the topic. Both passages involve helping someone else.

Step 2: Turn the topic into a complete idea. The theme must say what the text suggests about helping.

Step 3: Check both passages. Jonas helps Mina by accepting her row, and Talia helps Andre by giving him the blazer.

Modeled response: A shared theme is that helping others often requires giving up something we wanted to protect.

Worked Example 2: Theme Development in One Passage

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Question: How does Passage A develop the theme?

Step 1: Notice Jonas's starting point. He wants the garden to look perfect.

Step 2: Notice his choice. He decides not to pull Mina's uneven seeds.

Step 3: Notice the result. The crooked row grows taller than the others.

Modeled response: Passage A develops the theme by showing Jonas give up control over the garden and learn that supporting Mina matters more than making every row look perfect.

Worked Example 3: Comparative Theme Development

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Question: How do the passages develop the theme in similar and different ways?

Step 1: State the similarity. Both characters help another person by giving something up.

Step 2: State the difference. Jonas gives up control over a project, while Talia gives up something physical and personal.

Step 3: Explain what the comparison reveals. The theme applies in different situations because sacrifice can be emotional or material.

Modeled response: Both passages show that helping others requires sacrifice, but they develop the theme differently: Jonas gives up his idea of perfection, while Talia gives up her spare blazer so Andre can feel ready.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

Jonas plants seeds and Talia has a uniform. They are different.

Correct example:

The passages use different situations to develop the same theme: Jonas helps Mina by accepting an imperfect garden row, and Talia helps Andre by giving him the spare blazer.

Why this matters: The incorrect example compares surface events only. The correct example compares how the events develop the shared theme.
