Independent Work

Directions: Read both stories and answer all 18 questions. Submit these answers with your vocabulary and definitions in Notebook Evidence Submission.

Text A: The Seed Box

When Alma’s family moved into an apartment, she carried a wooden box of seeds from her grandmother’s farm. There was no yard, and the balcony received little sunlight. Alma kept the box closed because planting seemed pointless.

One afternoon, she noticed an abandoned courtyard behind the building. The soil was hard and scattered with stones, but an elderly neighbor offered tools. Together they cleared one narrow strip. Several seeds failed, yet a row of beans eventually climbed the fence.

By summer, other residents added herbs and flowers. Alma mailed her grandmother a photograph. On the back she wrote, “The farm did not follow me, but what it taught me did.”

Text B: The Borrowed Song

Micah refused to play his grandfather’s violin after joining a modern music group. He feared the old instrument would make him seem out of place. Before the group’s first performance, their keyboard stopped working.

Micah brought the violin only as a backup. During rehearsal, he played a melody his grandfather had taught him, and the drummer built a rhythm beneath it. The unexpected combination became the strongest part of their performance.

Afterward, Micah did not abandon modern music or imitate his grandfather’s style exactly. He began writing songs that placed the violin beside electronic sounds. He realized tradition could become material for something new.
