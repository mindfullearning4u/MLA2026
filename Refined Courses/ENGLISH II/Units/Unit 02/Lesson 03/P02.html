P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Coming-of-Age and Conflicting Perspectives

Vocabulary

Coming-of-age experience means a moment when a young character gains maturity, responsibility, or a new understanding.

Perspective means a character's way of seeing a situation.

Conflicting perspectives means two or more viewpoints that disagree because the characters value different things.

Responsibility means a duty or choice a character must take seriously.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the young character and the decision or problem the character faces.

Step 2: Identify at least two perspectives. Ask, "Who sees this situation differently?"

Step 3: Look for evidence of what each person values. One character may value safety, while another values independence.

Step 4: Find the moment when the young character's understanding changes.

Step 5: Explain how the author shows growth through actions, thoughts, dialogue, or choices.

Read the Passage

Passage: "The Bridge at Maple Creek"

Elena had waited all summer to ride her bike alone across the narrow bridge at Maple Creek. To her, the bridge meant freedom: the market, the park, and the library were only ten minutes away if she could cross it herself. Her father saw the bridge differently. He saw slick boards after rain, cars that rounded the curve too quickly, and a creek that rose without warning. "Wait until I can go with you," he said. Elena folded her arms and looked away, certain he still saw her as a child. Later that afternoon, she found her younger brother Mateo pushing his scooter toward the same bridge. He grinned and said he wanted to prove he was brave too. Elena opened her mouth to tell him to hurry, but the wind shook the bridge rails and a truck rushed past the curve. She heard her father's warning in a new way. "Not today," Elena told Mateo, turning his scooter around. "We can practice in the parking lot first." When her father saw them returning, Elena expected a lecture. Instead, she said, "I thought you were just saying no. I did not understand that you were asking me to notice what could happen." Her father nodded, and for the first time, Elena felt that being trusted might begin with learning when to wait.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title and vocabulary.
  2. Make a chart with three columns: Character, Perspective, and Evidence.
  3. Add Elena's perspective at the beginning of the passage.
  4. Add her father's perspective.
  5. Write one sentence explaining why the perspectives conflict.

Model Example

Elena's beginning perspective: The bridge represents freedom because it would let her reach places by herself.

Father's perspective: The bridge represents risk because he notices slick boards, fast cars, and rising water.

Conflict: Elena focuses on independence, while her father focuses on safety.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two possible interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.
