P04 — Worked Example

Passage: “The Last Rehearsal”

Jonah had earned the lead solo and believed every rehearsal should protect his performance. When Maya, a new musician, missed an entrance, he asked the director to remove her from the final concert. “The audience deserves perfection,” Jonah insisted. The director answered, “The audience also deserves to hear what a group can become when its members listen to one another.” Jonah dismissed the comment. During the final rehearsal, however, his own string snapped. Maya immediately adjusted her part, filling the silence until he could rejoin. After rehearsal, Jonah found her packing quietly. “I thought mistakes made the group weaker,” he said. “You showed me that how we respond matters more.” At the concert, Jonah asked the director to introduce the ensemble together rather than announcing his solo first.

Step-by-Step Analysis

  1. Beginning perspective: Jonah believes excellence means protecting individual perfection.
  2. Conflicting perspective: The director believes a strong ensemble grows through listening and mutual support.
  3. Turning point: Jonah’s string breaks and Maya protects the performance by adapting.
  4. Changed understanding: Jonah realizes mistakes do not define a group; its response does.
  5. Evidence of maturation: He gives up special recognition and asks that the ensemble be introduced together.
  6. Author’s craft: Dialogue states the conflict, the broken string tests both perspectives, and Jonah’s final action proves the change.

Modeled Response

Jonah’s coming-of-age experience develops through a conflict between individual perfection and shared responsibility. At first, he wants Maya removed because he believes her mistake threatens his performance. The director challenges that view by arguing that musicians become stronger when they listen to one another. When Jonah’s string breaks, Maya’s quick adjustment prevents his mistake from ruining the rehearsal. This consequence changes his perspective. His request to introduce the ensemble together shows maturation because he now values cooperation over personal recognition.

Why This Response Works

It identifies both perspectives, cites the pressure point, compares Jonah’s beginning and ending choices, and explains how the author uses dialogue and action to reveal growth.

Second Worked Example: “The Community Garden Vote”

Leah argues that the vacant lot should become a sports court because teenagers need an active place to gather. Her younger brother Mateo supports a community garden because several neighbors cannot easily reach a grocery store. Leah initially dismisses gardening as an activity for adults. During a heat wave, she helps Mateo deliver vegetables from a distant garden to an elderly neighbor and realizes that access to fresh food is also a youth concern. At the final meeting, Leah proposes a smaller court beside raised garden beds and volunteers to organize teen workdays.

  1. Initial perspective: Leah sees community need only through recreation.
  2. Competing perspective: Mateo emphasizes food access and neighborhood health.
  3. Pressure point: Delivering vegetables makes the abstract issue personal.
  4. Changed understanding: Leah recognizes that responsible leadership weighs needs beyond her own.
  5. Proof of maturation: Her compromise and volunteer plan convert insight into action.
  6. Craft: Contrast, consequence, and the final proposal reveal growth without making either original perspective worthless.

Modeled Analysis

Leah’s coming-of-age experience develops when a conflict about public space forces her to broaden her definition of community responsibility. At first, she values the lot mainly as a place for teenagers to exercise. Mateo’s concern about food access introduces a competing perspective. The heat-wave delivery becomes the turning point because Leah sees how the garden affects a neighbor’s daily life. Her final proposal combines recreation with food access, proving that she has matured from defending one group’s preference to balancing several community needs.
