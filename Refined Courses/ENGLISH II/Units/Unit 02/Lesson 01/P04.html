Worked Example 1: Character and Conflict

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Question: What conflict does Mara face?

Step 1: Look for what the character wants or needs to do. Mara needs to speak at a town meeting about saving the library.

Step 2: Look for what makes that difficult. Her notes shake, and she wants to sound certain even though she is afraid.

Step 3: Explain the conflict. Mara faces an internal conflict between fear and courage.

Modeled response: Mara faces an internal conflict because she wants to speak confidently for the library, but her shaking notes show that she feels afraid.

Worked Example 2: Setting and Mood

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Question: How does the setting shape the mood?

Step 1: Notice the setting details. The storage room smells of dust and cardboard, and the lantern creates warm circles of light.

Step 2: Decide what feeling those details create. The room feels quiet, private, and reflective.

Step 3: Connect the mood to Mara's situation. The quiet setting gives Mara space to face her fear before speaking publicly.

Modeled response: The quiet storage room creates a reflective mood because Mara can practice alone while the lantern's warm light makes the moment feel serious but hopeful.

Worked Example 3: Symbol and Layered Meaning

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Question: How does the lantern add layered meaning?

Step 1: Identify the literal role of the lantern. It lights the storage room.

Step 2: Identify the remembered role of the lantern. Mara's grandfather used it to guide neighbors during storms.

Step 3: Connect the symbol to Mara. Mara is preparing to guide others by speaking for the library.

Modeled response: The lantern adds layered meaning because it is both a real source of light and a symbol of guidance, reminding Mara that speaking up can help her community.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The setting is a storage room, and Mara is there.

Correct example:

The storage room creates a quiet mood that shows Mara preparing privately before she must speak publicly at the town meeting.

Why this matters: The incorrect example names the setting but stops there. The correct example explains how the setting affects mood and connects to Mara's conflict.
