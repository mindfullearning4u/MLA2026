Worked Example: From Plot to Layered Meaning

Passage

Every evening, Lena’s grandfather wound the hallway clock even though it had not kept accurate time for years. After he moved away, Lena stopped winding it. Weeks later, while preparing to leave for college, she found his note inside the clock: “Time does not wait for certainty.” Lena wound the clock, listened to its uneven ticking, and carried her suitcase outside.

Example 1: Literal Elements

  1. Character: Lena.
  2. Conflict: she is uncertain about leaving for college.
  3. Plot change: the note prompts her to act.
  4. Setting/object: the old hallway clock connects the present decision to her grandfather.

Example 2: Symbol and Evidence

  1. Repeated object: the clock.
  2. Literal role: it measures time imperfectly.
  3. Symbolic possibility: time continues even when a person feels uncertain.
  4. Evidence: the note and Lena carrying her suitcase after winding the clock.

Example 3: Theme and Layered Meaning

Theme: People sometimes must move forward before they feel completely ready.

Analysis: The clock’s uneven ticking reflects Lena’s uncertainty, while her decision to wind it and leave shows that imperfect confidence does not prevent action. The story’s literal departure therefore also represents emotional growth.

Common mistake: “The clock symbolizes college.” This is unsupported. The evidence more strongly connects the clock with time, uncertainty, memory, and forward movement.
