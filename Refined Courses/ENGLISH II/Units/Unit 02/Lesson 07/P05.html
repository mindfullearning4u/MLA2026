Directions

Read “The Last Elevator.” Answer Questions 1–12, then complete the revision task.

The Last Elevator

At 8:57, Caleb remembered the portfolio on the twenty-third floor. The scholarship office closed at nine. He pushed into the lobby as the final elevator doors began to meet. “Hold it!” A hand appeared between them.

The elevator rose. Floor numbers blinked: 7, 11, 15. The stranger beside him held a folder identical to Caleb’s. At floor 18, the lights failed. The car stopped so gently that silence arrived before fear. Caleb pressed the alarm. Nothing. From the ceiling came a faint scrape, followed by three careful knocks.

The stranger opened her folder. Inside was not a portfolio but a building map. “Maintenance inspection,” she whispered. “There is a service ladder above us.” The knocks came again—faster. Caleb looked at the clock: 8:59. He could wait, climb, or miss the deadline. He placed his portfolio inside his jacket and reached for the ceiling panel.

Questions and Revision

  1. Where does the pace accelerate?
  2. Where does it slow?
  3. How does the deadline create tension?
  4. Analyze the floor-number sentence.
  5. Why is the silence emphasized?
  6. What effect do the knocks create?
  7. How is information delayed?
  8. Describe the tone.
  9. Identify two strong verbs.
  10. How does paragraph length affect pace?
  11. What decision ends the passage?
  12. Explain how pace reveals Caleb’s character.
  13. Rewrite the final paragraph in 150–200 words. Slow the decision, then accelerate the action. Use sensory detail, internal thought, sentence variety, and consistent tone.
  14. Write 3–4 sentences explaining three pacing choices in your revision.
