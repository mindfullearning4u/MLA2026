P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Controlling Narrative Pace

Vocabulary

Pacing means the speed at which a story moment feels to the reader.

Tension means a feeling of suspense, pressure, or uncertainty.

Mood means the feeling created for the reader.

Tone means the writer's attitude toward the subject or moment.

Scene means a focused part of a narrative with a clear moment, place, and action.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Decide what the reader should feel: tension, calm, dread, excitement, relief, or hope.

Step 2: Slow down important moments with sensory detail, short actions, and character thoughts.

Step 3: Speed up less important action with concise summary.

Step 4: Use sentence length on purpose. Short sentences can create urgency. Longer sentences can stretch reflection.

Step 5: Revise by asking whether each sentence controls the reader's feeling.

Read the Passage

Passage: "The Hallway Clock"

Jalen reached the end of the hallway just as the final bell rang. The classroom door stood twenty steps away. Inside his backpack, the presentation cards bent against his folder. He had practiced all week, but his hands still felt cold. Ten steps. The clock above the door clicked once, loud as a lock. Five steps. Ms. Rivera looked up from her desk. Jalen stopped with his hand on the knob. He could still turn around. Instead, he breathed in, opened the door, and said, "I'm ready."

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the title and vocabulary.
  2. Copy two short sentences that slow the moment down.
  3. Identify one sensory detail.
  4. Explain how the pacing creates tension.

Model Example

Sentence: "Ten steps."

Effect: The short sentence slows the walk into measured pieces, which makes the reader feel Jalen's nervous attention to every step.
