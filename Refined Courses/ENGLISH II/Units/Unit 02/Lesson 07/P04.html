P04 — Worked Example

Draft Passage

Rina walked into the empty greenhouse to retrieve her notebook. She heard a noise, found the notebook, and left.

Revision

The greenhouse door resisted, then sighed open. Rina stepped between rows of black leaves. Her notebook waited on the far table. Halfway there, glass clicked behind her.

She stopped.

A vine trembled although the air was still. Rina reached the table. Ten steps. Five. Her fingers closed around the notebook just as a shadow crossed the frosted door. She did not look back. She ran.

Step-by-Step Explanation

  1. The opening slows entry with precise movement and personification.
  2. The distant notebook establishes a goal.
  3. The unexplained click delays certainty.
  4. The one-sentence paragraph creates a pause.
  5. “Ten steps. Five.” shortens rhythm and accelerates action.
  6. The shadow withholds identity, sustaining suspense.
  7. The final short sentences produce a fast escape.

Effect: Pace shifts from cautious to suspended to rapid while the uneasy tone remains consistent.

Second Worked Example: “The Final Envelope”

Rosa crossed the office in six steps. At the desk, she slid one finger beneath the envelope flap, stopped, and reread her name. The clock clicked. A chair scraped in the hallway. She lifted the paper only far enough to see the first word: Congratulations.

  1. Slow point: The opening action is divided into small physical movements.
  2. Delayed information: The result remains hidden until the final word.
  3. Sound details: The clock and scraping chair heighten awareness of waiting.
  4. Mood: Anxious anticipation.
  5. Tone: Controlled and serious rather than excited.
  6. Effect: Slow pacing makes the result feel personally important.

Modeled response: The writer slows the moment through precise actions and interrupted movement. By delaying the letter’s contents while emphasizing ordinary sounds, the passage places the reader inside Rosa’s anxious waiting.
