P04 Worked Example

Study how narrative choices control pace and feeling.

Worked Example 1: Short Sentences

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Question: How do "Ten steps. Five steps." affect pacing?

Step 1: The short sentences break the walk into small units.

Step 2: This slows the moment because the reader notices each movement.

Modeled response: The short sentences slow the pace and create tension by making each step toward the door feel important.

Worked Example 2: Sensory Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Question: How does "the clock above the door clicked once, loud as a lock" create mood?

Step 1: The sound detail makes the hallway feel quiet and tense.

Step 2: Comparing the click to a lock makes the moment feel final.

Modeled response: The clock detail creates a tense mood because the sound seems to pressure Jalen into deciding whether to enter.

Worked Example 3: Showing Growth Through Action

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Question: Why is "I'm ready" effective?

Step 1: Jalen could turn around, but he chooses to open the door.

Step 2: The line shows courage without needing to say "Jalen became brave."

Modeled response: The dialogue is effective because it shows Jalen acting despite fear, which gives the scene a determined tone.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

I added more words, so the pacing is better.

Correct example:

I slowed the important moment by adding sensory detail, short sentences, and Jalen's thought before he opens the door.

Why this matters: Strong pacing is purposeful. More words only help if they control tension, mood, or tone.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.
