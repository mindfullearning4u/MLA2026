P04 — Worked Example

Passage: “Before the Doors Opened”

The auditorium crouched in darkness while Laila waited behind the curtain. Empty seats rose like silent judges. Her prepared speech rattled in her mind, a box of loose buttons she could not sort. Then the custodian opened the lobby doors. Morning light spilled across the aisle, and voices drifted in—warm, ordinary, imperfect. Laila breathed. The stage was no longer a cliff; it was a bridge.

Step-by-Step Explanation

  1. “Auditorium crouched”: personification makes the room seem threatening.
  2. “Seats…like silent judges”: simile creates an anxious, scrutinized mood.
  3. “Box of loose buttons”: metaphor communicates scattered thoughts.
  4. Light spilled / warm voices: imagery shifts the mood toward reassurance.
  5. Cliff to bridge: paired metaphors show Laila reinterpreting the stage from danger to connection.

Modeled response: The author shifts the mood from anxiety to cautious confidence. At first, “silent judges” makes the audience space feel threatening. After warm voices enter, the stage changes from “a cliff” to “a bridge,” showing that Laila now sees speaking as connection rather than danger.

Second Worked Example: “Storm Glass”

The narrator observes, “Clouds bruised the afternoon sky, and the windows held their breath.”

  1. Device 1: “Bruised” is metaphorical diction suggesting injury and darkness.
  2. Device 2: The windows are personified as holding their breath.
  3. Mood: Suspenseful and threatening.
  4. Effect: The language makes the approaching storm feel like a dangerous force rather than ordinary weather.
  5. Interpretation: The external storm mirrors the narrator’s fear of an approaching confrontation.

Modeled response: The author combines metaphor and personification to create suspense. Describing the clouds as “bruised” gives the sky an injured, ominous appearance, while windows that “held their breath” suggest that even the house anticipates danger. These choices transform weather into a reflection of the narrator’s anxiety.
