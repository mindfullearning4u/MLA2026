After the Bell

The final bell cracked through the building, and students poured into the late-afternoon sunlight. Mina remained beside the empty display case where her sculpture should have stood. Its clean glass shelves seemed to stare back at her. The rejection email was a stone in her pocket, heavy even though it weighed almost nothing. She had spent six weeks shaping the clay figure, smoothing its hands, and carving a tiny doorway into its chest.

Outside, laughter flashed like coins, bright and unreachable. Lockers slammed, shoes squeaked, and voices swelled toward the exits. Each cheerful sound made the hallway feel colder. Mina pulled the email from her pocket and reread the first sentence: “Your work was not selected for the district exhibition.” The words marched across the screen like a row of locked gates. She folded the paper until the message disappeared.

As she reached her locker, she noticed Mr. Ortiz, the evening custodian, studying the original sketch taped beside it. He leaned closer to the drawn doorway. “This makes me remember my father’s workshop,” he said. “He always left the door open when someone needed help.” His words opened a small window in the wall of her disappointment. For the first time, Mina wondered whether the sculpture’s value depended entirely on a judge’s decision.

She unfolded the email again. Beneath the rejection, a second paragraph invited students to revise their pieces and submit them to the community arts showcase. Evening light pooled across the floor, turning the empty display case gold. Mina photographed her sketch, gathered her tools, and began listing changes she wanted to make. The hallway no longer felt like a tunnel closing behind her. It had become a path she had not noticed before.
