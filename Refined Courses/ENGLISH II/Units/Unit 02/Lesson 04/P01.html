P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 4: Figurative Language, Mood, and Interpretive Effect

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG2.R.09 - Analyze Figurative Language and Mood.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG2.R.03 - Analyze Coming-of-Age Perspectives; MLA.ENG2.V.03 - Determine Word Meaning; MLA.ENG2.C.01 - Present Ideas Orally.

What You Will Learn

  • How figurative language helps create mood in a literary text.
  • How word choice changes the feeling of a scene.
  • How to explain interpretive effect, not just identify a figure of speech.
  • How mood can support a character's experience or conflict.

What You Will Do

  • Read a literary passage included directly in the lesson.
  • Identify examples of figurative language and important word choices.
  • Explain the mood created by those language choices.
  • Write analysis that connects language, mood, and meaning.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook work identifies figurative language accurately.
  • Your responses explain how language creates mood.
  • Your checkpoint connects a specific phrase to a larger interpretation of the passage.
  • Your assessment answers show analysis, not just labeling.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.ENG2.R.09 in student language: I can explain how figurative language creates a feeling or mood in a text.

Today, your job is to go beyond naming a metaphor or simile. You will explain how the language makes the scene feel and why that feeling matters.

Need help? Ask your Teacher of Record for help if you can find figurative language but cannot explain the mood or effect it creates.
