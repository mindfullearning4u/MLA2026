P04 Worked Example: Rehearsing and Revising Before Submission

Study how each example moves from a problem to a clear revision.

Worked Example 1: Rehearse the Oral Explanation

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Problem: The speaker note says, "This is good for students."

Modeled thinking: The note is too general. It does not explain the evidence or the proposal.

Revision: This proposal would help students who remain after dismissal by giving them a supervised place to complete academic work.

Reasoning: The revision names the audience need and explains the benefit clearly.

Worked Example 2: Revise the Digital Slide

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Problem: The slide title says, "Data," and the chart has no label.

Modeled thinking: The audience should not have to guess what the numbers represent.

Revision: Title the slide "Survey Evidence: Students Remain After School" and label the chart "68 of 120 surveyed students stay after dismissal."

Reasoning: The revision makes the evidence visible, specific, and connected to the purpose.

Worked Example 3: Use Feedback Before Publishing

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Feedback: Your tone is too casual for school leaders.

Modeled thinking: I should revise casual words, not change the whole claim.

Revision: Replace "This idea is awesome" with "This proposal would support students' academic focus after school."

Reasoning: The revision keeps the meaning but makes the tone more formal and precise.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I rehearsed once, noticed the note was vague, and submitted it anyway.

Correct: I rehearsed once, noticed the note was vague, revised it to explain the evidence, and checked that the slide matched the note.

Why this matters: Rehearsal is not just practice. It is a quality check that should lead to revision before publication.
