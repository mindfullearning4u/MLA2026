Notebook Task Part 2: Apply Feedback Precisely

Now you will use feedback to make a specific revision. Feedback is useful only when it leads to a clear improvement.

Step-by-Step Application

Step 1: Identify the feedback. Example: The presentation needs clearer explanation of why the survey result matters.

Step 2: Find the exact part to revise. In this case, revise the speaker note, not the whole presentation.

Step 3: Make the revision specific. Add the number, explain its meaning, and connect it to the proposal.

Step 4: Check that the slide still supports the note. If the note explains survey evidence, the slide should show the survey result clearly.

Notebook Directions: Write one feedback statement, one revision target, and one revised speaker note for the presentation draft.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Feedback said to explain the number, so I added a brighter background and a bigger title.

Correct: Feedback said to explain the number, so I revised the speaker note to explain that 68 of 120 students is more than half of the surveyed group.

Why this matters: The incorrect response changes appearance but does not address the feedback. The correct response targets the exact weakness: the audience needs to understand what the evidence means.
