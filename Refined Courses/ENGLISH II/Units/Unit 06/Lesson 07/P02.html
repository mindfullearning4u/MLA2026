P02 Notebook Task - Part 1: Rehearsal Checklist

Notebook Title: Unit 6 Lesson 7 - Final Presentation Rehearsal and Feedback

Vocabulary

Rehearsal: a practice run used to test whether a presentation is clear, organized, and ready.

Feedback: specific information that helps a speaker improve the work.

Publication readiness: the point when work is clear, accurate, formal, complete, and ready to share.

Alignment: the match between slide content, speaker notes, evidence, and purpose.

Delivery: how a speaker uses words, pacing, and explanation to communicate a message.

Presentation Draft for This Lesson

Purpose: persuade school leaders to support a supervised after-school study space.

Audience: school leaders who need clear evidence and a respectful explanation.

Slide: Survey Result: 68 of 120 students stay after school at least twice a week.

Speaker Note: This shows many students need a supervised place to work after school.

Feedback Received: Explain why the number matters, use a formal tone, and make sure the final slide clearly labels the survey result.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read the slide without speaking. Ask: Can the audience understand the main evidence by looking at the slide?

Step 2: Read the speaker note aloud. Ask: Does the note explain the evidence instead of repeating it?

Step 3: Check alignment. The slide should show the key evidence, and the speaker note should explain why the evidence matters.

Step 4: Check tone and conventions. Formal language and correct grammar help the audience trust the presentation.

Notebook Directions: Copy the four-step checklist. Then write one sentence explaining which step would most improve the draft presentation.

Model Example

Before rehearsal: This number is important because students need a place.

After rehearsal: Because 68 of 120 surveyed students remain on campus after dismissal, the proposal responds to a documented need for a supervised academic workspace.

Why this works: The revision names the evidence, explains the importance, keeps a formal tone, and connects the evidence to the proposal.
