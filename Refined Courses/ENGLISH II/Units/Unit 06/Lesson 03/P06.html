Draft Presentation: A Student Technology Help Desk

Technology matters in school. Some students are very good with computers, and the robotics club recently won a regional award. During the first quarter, the media specialist recorded 143 student requests for help with passwords, file uploads, document formatting, and presentation software. Forty-one requests occurred during the final two days before major projects were due. A student technology help desk could operate in the media center during two lunch periods each week. Trained volunteers would follow a written support guide and refer account-security problems to staff. Some students also enjoy video games after school. The help desk would give students timely assistance while allowing the media specialist to focus on complex problems. The school should test the program for one quarter and track the number and type of requests, wait times, resolved problems, and referrals.

Audience: Principal, media specialist, teachers, and student representatives.

Purpose: Recommend a limited student technology help-desk trial.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. State the draft’s central perspective in one precise sentence.
  2. Identify two details that distract from that perspective and explain why.
  3. Identify the strongest evidence of student need.
  4. Explain how the proposed schedule supports coherent focus.
  5. What reasonable concern does the referral rule address?
  6. Organize the useful details into two main points.
  7. Write a transition between those points.
  8. Rewrite the first two sentences as a focused opening for the stated audience.
  9. Write one body paragraph that uses evidence and reasoning without drifting from the perspective.
  10. Write a closing that recommends the trial and explains how success should be measured.
