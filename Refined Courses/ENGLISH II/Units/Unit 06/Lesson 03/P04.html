P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Focused Perspective

Weak perspective: The library is useful for many reasons.

Thinking: This is too broad. The listener does not know what the presentation will prove.

Focused perspective: Extended library hours would help students who remain on campus study safely after school.

Example 2: Relevant Detail

Possible detail: Students wait in noisy hallways after dismissal.

Thinking: This detail supports the need for a quiet study location.

Decision: Keep this detail.

Example 3: Distracting Detail

Possible detail: Some students enjoy mystery novels.

Thinking: This may be true, but it does not support the need for after-school study space.

Decision: Remove this detail from the presentation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Add every detail you know so the presentation sounds full.

Correct Example:

Choose only details that support the focused perspective.

Teachable Explanation: More details do not automatically make a presentation stronger. Focused details help the audience understand and remember the speaker's point.
