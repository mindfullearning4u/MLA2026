P04 — Worked Example: Build and Maintain a Clear Perspective

Model Presentation Passage

Franklin High should test extended library hours on Tuesdays and Thursdays because students need a safe, focused place to complete academic work after dismissal. Library records show that all twelve computers were occupied during eighteen of twenty lunch periods, and staff recorded ninety-six requests for research help after school. These facts establish a clear need for more access. The plan is also practical: existing activity buses leave at 5:45 p.m., and their routes currently average only 61 percent capacity. Although staffing would cost about $1,100 for an eight-week trial, the school could track attendance, computer use, tutoring requests, and behavior before deciding whether to continue. Therefore, a limited trial offers a responsible way to meet student needs while protecting school resources.

Step-by-Step Analysis

  1. Identify the perspective: The school should test extended library hours because students need supervised academic space.
  2. Trace the focus: Each sentence develops either student need or practical feasibility.
  3. Check the evidence: Computer use, research requests, bus capacity, and cost are measurable and relevant.
  4. Notice the transition: “The plan is also practical” moves from need to feasibility without changing the perspective.
  5. Address a concern: The cost is acknowledged and answered with a limited, measurable trial.
  6. Evaluate the closing: It returns to the perspective and explains why the proposal is responsible.

Worked Example 2 — Relevance Test

Detail: The library displays student artwork near the entrance.

Question: Does it prove student need, safety, feasibility, or academic benefit?

Decision: Remove it. It is interesting but does not advance the perspective.

Worked Example 3 — Repair Coherence

Weak sequence: cost → student hobbies → computer demand → bus schedule.

Revised sequence: student need → academic evidence → transportation feasibility → staffing concern → measured trial.

The revised order lets the audience understand why the proposal is needed before considering how it can work.

Focus Organizer

Section Purpose Focus Check
Opening State the perspective Is the position precise?
Body Develop reasons with evidence Does every detail advance the position?
Closing Return to the perspective Does the ending show why it matters?
