P03 — Lesson: Presenting a Clear Perspective with Coherent Focus

Direct Teaching

A clear perspective tells the audience the speaker’s position or lens. Coherent focus means every claim, fact, example, transition, and explanation helps develop that perspective. Strong speakers do not merely stay on the same broad topic; they choose details that advance one controlling idea.

A Five-Step Focus Process

  1. Write the perspective: state the position in one precise sentence.
  2. Name the audience and purpose: decide what listeners need to understand or do.
  3. Test relevance: ask how each detail supports the perspective.
  4. Sequence the support: arrange ideas so each point develops the previous one.
  5. Check coherence: use transitions and remove distracting or contradictory details.

Example 1 — Remove an Irrelevant Detail

Perspective: The library should remain open after school because students need supervised academic space.

Relevant: Ninety-six students requested after-school research help.

Irrelevant: The speaker’s favorite novels are displayed near the entrance.

The first detail proves student need; the second does not advance the perspective.

Example 2 — Repair a Shift in Focus

Unfocused: The courtyard needs repairs. School lunches should offer more choices. Students also need quiet space.

Coherent: Repairing the courtyard would create a safe, supervised space for quiet lunch and reading.

The revision combines related ideas under one perspective and removes the unrelated lunch-menu claim.

Example 3 — Use a Coherent Sequence

Point 1: Establish the need with attendance or survey evidence.

Point 2: Explain a feasible solution with cost and staffing evidence.

Point 3: Address a concern and return to the perspective.

Transition: “Once the need is clear, the next question is whether the plan is practical.”

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing a broad topic with a precise perspective.
  • Including interesting facts that do not support the controlling idea.
  • Repeating the position without developing it with evidence and reasoning.
  • Shifting tone, audience, or purpose halfway through the presentation.
