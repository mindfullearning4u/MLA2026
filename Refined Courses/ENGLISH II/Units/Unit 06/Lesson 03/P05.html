P05 — Independent Work: Revise for Perspective and Coherence

Directions: Read the complete draft and source notes. Answer all 12 questions in complete sentences. Submit this work with your vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Draft Presentation: Phones During Study Hall

Phones are everywhere, and people have many opinions about them. Some students listen to music while they work. The school cafeteria recently changed its lunch schedule. During study hall, notifications can interrupt reading and writing. In a student survey, 68 percent of 240 respondents said they check a phone at least once during a thirty-minute study period. However, some students use phones to access a dictionary, timer, calculator, or teacher-approved research link. Teachers recorded fewer unfinished assignments in two classes that used a “phones face down unless authorized” routine for four weeks. Therefore, phones are useful, but they can also be distracting. Schools should do something reasonable.

Source Note A: The survey was voluntary and represents 240 of the school’s 780 students.

Source Note B: The classroom observation covered two classes for four weeks; it suggests a useful pattern but does not prove the routine will work everywhere.

Audience: Students, teachers, and school administrators.

Purpose: Recommend a fair study-hall phone policy.

Questions

  1. Identify the draft’s implied perspective.
  2. Explain why the opening is too broad.
  3. Identify the sentence that is unrelated to the presentation’s focus.
  4. Write a precise perspective statement suited to the audience and purpose.
  5. Which survey detail is relevant, and what limitation must the speaker acknowledge?
  6. How does Source Note B support the perspective without proving too much?
  7. Group the useful details into two coherent main points.
  8. Write a transition between those two points.
  9. Revise “Schools should do something reasonable” into a specific recommendation.
  10. Write a revised opening that states the perspective and previews the main points.
  11. Write a focused body paragraph using evidence, reasoning, and a qualification.
  12. Write a closing that reinforces the same perspective without merely repeating it.
Success Check: Every detail in your revision must support one clear, audience-appropriate perspective.
