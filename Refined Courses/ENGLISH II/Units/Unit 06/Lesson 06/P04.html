P04 — Worked Example: Coordinating Spoken and Digital Communication

Model Situation: A student will present to school leaders about creating a supervised after-school waiting area. Read the complete source notes and follow the three worked examples showing how the slide, spoken explanation, timing, tone, and contingency plan work together.

Model Source Notes

During six afternoons, a student committee counted between 41 and 73 students remaining on campus after dismissal. In a voluntary survey of 120 students, 68 said they stayed after school at least twice per week, and 49 said they usually waited in hallways or outdoor areas. The survey did not include every student and did not ask why students stayed. The assistant principal stated that any waiting area would require adult supervision, a sign-in process, and a six-week pilot before a permanent decision.

Worked Example 1 — Assign Each Mode a Role

Slide title: “A Consistent Group Remains After Dismissal”
Slide display: 68 of 120 surveyed students stay at least twice weekly; 49 report waiting in informal areas.
Speaker note: “The voluntary survey represents only part of the school, but it identifies a substantial group that regularly remains on campus. The observation range of 41 to 73 students across six afternoons supports the need to study a supervised option.”

Why it works: The slide shows concise evidence. The speaker interprets, qualifies, and connects the evidence to the claim instead of reading the slide.

Worked Example 2 — Coordinate Timing, Tone, and Audience Focus

  1. Display the evidence slide and pause so the audience can read the title.
  2. Point to the two key values while explaining their meaning.
  3. Use formal language: “The evidence supports a pilot,” not “Obviously, everyone needs this.”
  4. Transition: “The evidence identifies the need; the next slide explains how the school can test a safe response.”

Worked Example 3 — Build an Accessible Proposal and Contingency Plan

Proposal slide: Six-week supervised pilot; library annex; student sign-in; participation and safety review.
Accessibility: Large type, high contrast, short parallel phrases, verbal description of the slide, and no color-only meaning.
Contingency: If the display fails, provide a printed one-page handout and continue using the prepared explanation.

Integration Quality Checklist

  • The spoken and digital parts support one claim.
  • The slide shows what the audience needs to see.
  • The speaker explains meaning, limits, and relevance.
  • Timing and transitions tell the audience where to focus.
  • Tone and conventions fit school leaders.
  • Visuals are readable and accessible.
  • A technical rehearsal and contingency plan are prepared.
Common mistake: Making the speaker notes and slide identical. Integrated communication is complementary: the digital element shows essential evidence while the speaker supplies explanation and reasoning.
