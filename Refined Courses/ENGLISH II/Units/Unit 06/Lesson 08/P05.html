P05 — Independent Work: Design a Complete Evidence-Based Presentation

Directions: Read the complete source packet. Then answer all 12 questions in order. Your responses must show how audience, purpose, claim, evidence, reasoning, digital design, organization, language, and delivery work together.

Presentation Situation

Your student advisory committee will present to teachers and school leaders. The committee must recommend one practical way to make academic feedback more useful to students. Your presentation may use only the information in the source packet below.

Source A — Student Survey Summary

A voluntary survey was completed by 160 students. Ninety-eight students said comments such as “explain your reasoning” or “use stronger evidence” helped them revise. Forty-seven students said they sometimes received a score without understanding what to improve. Eighty-four students wanted a brief opportunity to ask questions after receiving major-assignment feedback. Because participation was voluntary, the results describe the respondents and may not represent the entire school.

Source B — Teacher Interview

Ms. Alvarez explains that useful feedback should be specific, timely, and connected to the learning goal. She asks students to identify one successful choice and one revision priority before meeting with her. She warns that conferences require time and should not replace written comments. In her classes, students who submit a revision plan usually make more focused changes than students who simply reread the score.

Source C — Pilot Program Report

For six weeks, two English classes tested a “feedback response” routine. After receiving comments, students wrote: (1) the learning goal, (2) one comment they understood, (3) one question, and (4) one planned revision. Of 54 participating students, 43 completed the routine at least four times. Teachers reported that student questions became more specific. The report did not compare grades with classes outside the pilot, so it cannot prove that the routine caused higher achievement.

Questions

  1. Identify the presentation’s specific audience and purpose.
  2. Write a focused, arguable claim recommending one practical feedback improvement.
  3. Select the strongest detail from Source A and explain why it supports your claim.
  4. Select one useful detail from Source B and explain how it strengthens or qualifies your reasoning.
  5. Use Source C accurately. What can the pilot report support, and what can it not prove?
  6. Write a two-sentence synthesis that connects evidence from at least two sources.
  7. Identify one reasonable concern the audience might raise and write a response supported by the packet.
  8. Plan four presentation sections in a logical order and state the purpose of each.
  9. Design one slide: provide its title, exact concise text, and description of a useful visual.
  10. Write speaker notes for that slide that explain the evidence without repeating the slide text.
  11. Write a formal transition into the conclusion and a clear audience action.
  12. Create a rehearsal checklist containing at least five delivery or technology checks, then explain which check is most important for this situation.
Mastery Check: Every answer should remain consistent with the same audience, purpose, and claim. Cite the source letter whenever you use evidence, qualify limitations honestly, and use complete sentences.
