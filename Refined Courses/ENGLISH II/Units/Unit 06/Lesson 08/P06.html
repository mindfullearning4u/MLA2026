Presentation Situation

You will present to the principal, parent council, and student representatives. Your purpose is to recommend whether the school should pilot a weekly device-free lunch period designed to encourage conversation and reduce distractions.

Source A — Lunch Observation

During four lunch periods, a student committee observed 210 students. About 61% used a phone for at least part of lunch. Observers also recorded students talking, reading, completing homework, or playing table games. The observations show common behaviors during those four days, but they do not reveal why students used devices or how students would respond to a policy change.

Source B — Student Comments

In an anonymous form, some students said phones help them contact family members, check transportation, or take a needed break. Other students said constant notifications make face-to-face conversation difficult. Several suggested a voluntary device-free zone instead of a schoolwide rule. Because the form was optional, the comments represent perspectives rather than a complete measure of student opinion.

Source C — Administrative Requirements

The principal will consider a six-week pilot if the recommendation explains the purpose, protects emergency communication, identifies a location and supervision plan, and includes a way to evaluate results. Any proposal must avoid presenting uncertain evidence as fact.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. State the audience, purpose, and communication situation in one complete response.
  2. Write a precise claim recommending a specific pilot approach.
  3. Select one detail from Source A and explain both its value and its limitation.
  4. Use one perspective from Source B to strengthen or complicate your recommendation.
  5. Explain how Source C should affect the content and organization of the presentation.
  6. Write a three-sentence synthesis using at least two sources and clear reasoning.
  7. Plan one digital slide: give its title, exact text, visual, and source label.
  8. Write speaker notes that explain the slide, qualify the evidence, and connect it to the claim.
  9. Outline the opening, two body sections, response to a concern, and conclusion/action.
  10. Identify two presentation revisions you would make after rehearsal and explain how each improves audience understanding or delivery.
