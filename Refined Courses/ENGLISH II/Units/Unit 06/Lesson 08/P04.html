Worked Example: Unit 6 Synthesis

Each example shows how a Unit 6 skill fits into the final presentation process.

Example 1: Match Purpose and Audience

Situation: The audience is school leaders, and the purpose is to explain a proposal.

Modeled thinking: I need formal wording, specific evidence, and a respectful explanation.

Strong choice: "The survey evidence suggests that a supervised study space would support students who remain on campus after dismissal."

Reasoning: The sentence fits the audience and explains the proposal without casual language.

Example 2: Combine Slide and Speaker Note

Slide: 68 of 120 surveyed students stay after school at least twice a week.

Speaker note: This matters because more than half of the surveyed students may need a supervised academic place to work after dismissal.

Modeled thinking: The slide provides the data. The note explains the meaning.

Reasoning: The audience receives both the evidence and the interpretation.

Example 3: Prepare for Final Submission

Checklist: audience, purpose, evidence, slide clarity, speaker-note explanation, formal tone, conventions, and feedback response.

Modeled thinking: A presentation is ready only when every part supports the same message.

Final check: The claim is clear, the evidence is labeled, the note explains the evidence, and the language is correct.

Reasoning: This confirms that the oral and digital communication work together.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: My slides look finished, so I do not need to check the speaker notes.

Correct: My slides look finished, and I still need to check that the speaker notes explain the evidence clearly and correctly.

Why this matters: Unit 6 mastery requires integrated communication. The digital and oral parts must both be ready.
