P04 — Worked Example: From Evidence to a Complete Presentation

Model Situation
A student is preparing a four-minute presentation for the principal and student council. The purpose is to recommend a supervised after-school study space. Read the model source notes, slide plan, and speaker notes. Then follow the analysis showing how the pieces work together.

Model Source Notes

The school library closes at 3:15 p.m. Bus riders often wait until 4:30 p.m. A voluntary survey of 120 students found that 68 remain on campus after school at least twice per week; 49 of those students reported that they usually complete schoolwork while waiting. The assistant principal reported that any new study space would require adult supervision and a clear sign-in procedure. Because the survey was voluntary and represented only part of the school, its results suggest a need but do not prove that every student wants the proposal.

Worked Example 1 — Build the Claim

  1. Identify the audience: school decision-makers.
  2. Identify the purpose: recommend a realistic solution.
  3. Draft the claim: “The school should pilot a supervised after-school study space for students waiting for transportation.”
  4. Check alignment: The claim is formal, focused, and possible for the audience to act upon.

Worked Example 2 — Select, Qualify, and Explain Evidence

Slide text: “68 of 120 surveyed students remain after school at least twice weekly.”
Speaker note: “This voluntary survey represents only part of the school, so it cannot describe every student. However, it shows that a substantial group regularly waits on campus and may benefit from a supervised place to work.”
  1. The number is accurate and labeled.
  2. The qualification acknowledges the sample limitation.
  3. The reasoning explains why the evidence matters to the claim.

Worked Example 3 — Organize the Full Message

SectionContentPurpose
OpeningDescribe students waiting after school and state the claim.Establish relevance.
EvidencePresent the survey and explain its limit.Support the need credibly.
ConcernAddress supervision and sign-in requirements.Show feasibility.
ConclusionRequest a six-week pilot and evaluation.Give a clear action.

Final Rehearsal Revision

Before: “A lot of students need this, and it would be really good.”
After: “A six-week supervised pilot would allow the school to measure participation, safety, and academic use before making a permanent decision.”
Why stronger: The revision is precise, formal, measurable, and suited to decision-makers.
Common mistake: Treating the slide, speech, and evidence as separate products. They must work together: the slide shows, the speaker explains, and both advance the same claim.
