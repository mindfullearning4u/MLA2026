P03 — Lesson: Digital Presentation of Findings, Reasoning, and Evidence

Direct Teaching

A digital presentation should make the speaker’s reasoning easier to understand. Every heading, chart, image, label, citation, and line of text must serve the audience and clarify a finding, its evidence, or the reasoning that connects them.

Five-Step Slide Design Process

  1. State one purpose per slide.
  2. Select only the evidence the audience must see.
  3. Choose the right form: chart for comparison, timeline for sequence, image for concrete context, or concise text for a claim.
  4. Explain the evidence orally; do not place the entire speech on the slide.
  5. Check accessibility: readable contrast, large text, clear labels, source credit, and meaningful alternative text.

Example 1 — Comparison

Finding: Most surveyed students would use extended library hours. Use a labeled bar chart showing 226 yes, 58 unsure, and 28 no—not a decorative stock photo.

Example 2 — Reasoning

Place the key statistic on the slide, then say: “Because nearly three-fourths of respondents indicated likely use, a limited trial has enough interest to justify measurement.”

Example 3 — Source Transparency

Label the chart “Voluntary student survey, n=312.” This preserves credibility by showing the source and limitation.

Common Mistakes

  • Full paragraphs in small text.
  • Charts without labels or units.
  • Decorative visuals unrelated to the finding.
  • Reading the slide instead of explaining the reasoning.
  • Missing source credits or inaccessible color contrast.
