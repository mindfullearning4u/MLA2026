Worked Example 1: Slide Heading

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Weak heading: Survey

Thinking: This heading names the source type but not the point.

Strong heading: Many Students Remain on Campus After Dismissal

Worked Example 2: Visual Feature

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Evidence: 68 students remain on campus; 49 wait in informal areas.

Thinking: A simple bar chart can help the audience compare the numbers quickly.

Best feature: A labeled two-bar chart with the numbers clearly shown.

Worked Example 3: Reasoning Slide

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Weak slide text: This matters.

Thinking: The audience needs to know why it matters.

Strong slide text: Supervised study space turns waiting time into safe academic time.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Choose a chart because charts always look impressive, even if the chart does not match the evidence.

Correct Example:

Choose a visual feature because it matches the evidence and helps the audience understand the point.

Teachable Explanation: Digital features must serve meaning. A chart is useful only when it makes the evidence easier to understand.
