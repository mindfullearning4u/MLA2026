P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Notebook Focus: Turning source information into an oral outline

Deeper Learning

An oral outline helps the speaker avoid jumping from idea to idea. Listeners need a clear path. That path should begin with the topic, move to the claim, support the claim with evidence, explain the evidence with reasoning, and end with a clear closing idea.

Strong sequence: Opening statement, claim, evidence from Source A, evidence from Source B, reasoning, closing statement.

Notebook Task: Turn the source set from P02 into a five-part oral outline.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Students wait after school. The handbook says rules. So we need a room.

Correct Example:

Because many students remain on campus after dismissal, the school should provide a supervised study space that follows handbook expectations and supports academic work.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect example lists ideas without explaining the connection. The correct example connects the evidence to a clear claim and explains why the claim matters.
