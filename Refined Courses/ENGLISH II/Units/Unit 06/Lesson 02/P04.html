P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Choosing Credible Evidence

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Question: Which source detail best supports a study-space proposal?

Thinking: A survey count is more useful than a vague personal opinion because it gives specific information.

Best evidence: Source A reports that 49 surveyed students usually wait in hallways, outside the office, or near the bus loop.

Worked Example 2: Writing Reasoning

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Evidence: Students should wait in supervised locations.

Thinking: Reasoning must explain why this evidence supports the claim.

Reasoning: A supervised study space would help the school meet this expectation while giving students a productive place to work.

Worked Example 3: Organizing an Oral Outline

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Opening: Many students remain on campus after dismissal.

Claim: The school should create a supervised after-school study space.

Evidence: Survey results and handbook expectations both show the need for a supervised location.

Closing: A study space would make waiting time safer, clearer, and more academically useful.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Read every source sentence out loud with no explanation.

Correct Example:

Select the strongest evidence and explain how it supports the claim.

Teachable Explanation: Oral communication is not a source dump. A speaker must choose, organize, and explain evidence so listeners understand the point.

Oral Organization and Evidence Organizer

Use this organizer to arrange oral information logically and support it with credible evidence.

Analysis Step What to Record Quality Check
1. Focus the message Write the controlling idea and the two or three points the audience must remember. Every point directly supports the purpose.
2. Sequence and connect ideas Choose a logical pattern and plan spoken transitions, signposts, and previews. Listeners can follow the organization without seeing a written outline.
3. Integrate credible evidence Match each point with relevant evidence and briefly explain source authority and significance. Evidence is accurate, sufficient, and explained rather than merely mentioned.
