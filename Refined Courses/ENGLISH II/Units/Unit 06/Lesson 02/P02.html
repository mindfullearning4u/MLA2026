P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Organizing Oral Information with Credible Evidence

Vocabulary

Claim is the main point the speaker wants the audience to accept or understand.

Credible evidence is trustworthy information that supports a claim.

Reasoning explains how the evidence supports the claim.

Oral outline is an organized speaking plan with a beginning, middle, and end.

Central idea is the most important idea developed by a source or speaker.

Read the Source Set

Source A: Student Survey Summary

In a survey of 120 students, 68 students reported that they remain on campus at least one day each week after regular dismissal. Of those students, 49 reported that they usually wait in hallways, outside the office, or near the bus loop.

Source B: School Handbook Excerpt

The school handbook states that students who remain on campus after dismissal should wait in supervised locations and should not block hallways, office entrances, or outdoor walkways.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: State a clear claim. The claim should be one sentence.

Step 2: Choose credible evidence. Evidence should come from a source that can be trusted.

Step 3: Explain reasoning. Do not expect the audience to connect the evidence for you.

Step 4: Put the information in a logical order: opening, claim, evidence, reasoning, closing.

Step 5: Keep speaking notes short enough to use while presenting.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Write one claim based on the source set.
  4. Choose one piece of credible evidence.
  5. Write one reasoning sentence that explains why the evidence matters.

Model Example

Claim: The school should create a supervised after-school study space.

Evidence: Source A reports that 49 surveyed students usually wait in hallways, outside the office, or near the bus loop.

Reasoning: This evidence shows that many students already remain on campus, so a supervised study space would organize that waiting time more safely and productively.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or passage detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two possible interpretations, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.
