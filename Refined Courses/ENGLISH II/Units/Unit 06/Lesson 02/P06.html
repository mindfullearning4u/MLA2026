P06 — Checkpoint: Organize Evidence for an Oral Recommendation

Directions: Read the new source set and answer all 10 questions. Submit these responses separately as your Checkpoint Submission.

Source Set: Extending Library Hours

The school library currently closes at 3:30 p.m. A proposal would keep it open until 5:30 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays for an eight-week trial. Students could use computers, research databases, tutoring tables, and quiet study space.

Source A — Library Records: During the last four weeks, all twelve public computers were in use during 18 of 20 lunch periods. The librarian also recorded 96 requests for after-school research help, although the library was closed when most students were available.

Source B — Transportation Report: Existing activity buses leave at 5:45 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays and currently average 61 percent capacity. The transportation coordinator says library participants could use these routes without adding a new bus.

Source C — Staffing Memo: One certified staff member must supervise the library. Reassigning an existing rotating duty would cost approximately $1,100 for the eight-week trial. The memo recommends tracking attendance, computer use, behavior incidents, and tutoring requests.

Source D — Social Post: “The library should stay open because every good school does it.” No evidence or source is provided.

Checkpoint Questions

  1. Write the central recommendation for an oral presentation to school leaders.
  2. Identify the two most credible sources and explain why each is trustworthy.
  3. Explain why Source D should not be used as primary evidence.
  4. Choose either problem–solution or cause–effect organization and justify your choice.
  5. Group the evidence into two main points.
  6. Write an opening that previews those two points.
  7. Quote or paraphrase one precise fact and explain its significance.
  8. Write a transition between your two main points.
  9. Address one reasonable concern about cost, staffing, or transportation with evidence.
  10. Write a closing that restates the recommendation and proposes how the trial should be evaluated.
