P03 — Lesson: Oral Communication Purpose, Audience, and Perspective

Effective speakers make deliberate choices. The central message may remain stable, but evidence, tone, vocabulary, organization, and delivery must fit the audience and purpose.

A Five-Step Speaking Plan

  1. Name the purpose: inform, explain, persuade, or motivate.
  2. Analyze the audience: identify knowledge, concerns, values, and needed context.
  3. State the perspective: make the speaker’s position or lens clear.
  4. Select and organize evidence: use relevant details in a logical sequence.
  5. Choose delivery: adjust tone, vocabulary, pace, emphasis, and formality.

Example 1 — Inform Students

Message: A supervised study room will open after school.

Choice: Emphasize location, hours, homework support, and transportation timing in clear, direct language.

Example 2 — Persuade School Leaders

Message: Approve the supervised study room.

Choice: Emphasize safety, staffing, documented student need, cost, and measurable academic benefits in a respectful formal tone.

Example 3 — Address Families

Message: Explain how the study room supports students.

Choice: Clarify supervision, pickup procedures, available help, and how families receive updates.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the same wording for every audience.
  • Giving opinions without relevant evidence.
  • Hiding the speaker’s purpose or perspective.
  • Using a casual or demanding tone with decision-makers.
