P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Purpose, Audience, and Perspective in Oral Communication

Vocabulary

Purpose means the reason you are speaking, such as to explain, persuade, inform, or reflect.

Audience means the listeners who need to understand your message.

Perspective means the point of view or position from which you speak.

Oral communication means sharing ideas out loud in a clear, organized way.

Speaking notes are brief reminders that help a speaker stay organized without reading an entire script.

Read the Source Passage

Passage: "A Clear Voice in a School Meeting"

When students speak at a school meeting, their words can help adults understand what daily school life feels like. A strong speaker does not simply say, "This is what I want." A strong speaker explains the purpose, considers what the audience needs to know, and presents a perspective with respect. For example, a student asking for more after-school study space should explain who needs the space, why the need matters, and how the request connects to student learning.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the purpose. Ask, "Why am I speaking?"

Step 2: Identify the audience. Ask, "Who is listening, and what do they need to know?"

Step 3: Identify the perspective. Ask, "What position or point of view am I presenting?"

Step 4: Choose details that match the audience. A student audience may need examples from daily life. School leaders may need clear reasons and respectful wording.

Step 5: Use complete, clear speaking notes. Notes should guide your explanation, not distract from it.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the notebook title.
  2. Copy the vocabulary words and meanings.
  3. Write the purpose of the speaker in the passage.
  4. Identify the audience in the passage.
  5. Explain the perspective the student speaker should present.

Model Example

Purpose: To explain why after-school study space matters.

Audience: School leaders at a meeting.

Perspective: A student perspective focused on learning needs.

Speaking note: Students who wait for transportation need a supervised place to study because hallways are distracting.
