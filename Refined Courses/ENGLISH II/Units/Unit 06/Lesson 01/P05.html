P05 — Independent Work: Plan an Audience-Focused Oral Message

Read the situation, draft, and verified facts. Answer all 12 questions. Submit these responses with your vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Weekly Progress Updates

Situation: You will speak for three minutes to the student advisory council about whether students should receive a brief digital progress update every Friday.

Audience: students, teachers, and one assistant principal. Possible purpose: persuade the council to recommend a six-week pilot.

Draft opening: Everybody knows grades are confusing, and the school should just send updates because it would be way better. Some students will probably complain, but they need to be more responsible. I think this is obvious.

Verified facts: In a survey of 210 students, 68% said they sometimes learn about missing work too late; teachers already update the gradebook weekly; a pilot would send a short summary, not a new grade; students could opt out of notifications; the council will evaluate the pilot after six weeks.

Questions

  1. State the speaker, occasion, audience, and purpose.
  2. Describe two different audience concerns.
  3. State a clear perspective that avoids exaggeration.
  4. Identify two phrases in the draft that could alienate listeners.
  5. Select the two strongest facts for this audience and explain why.
  6. Choose one fact that needs explanation before listeners can understand it.
  7. Revise the opening sentence to establish purpose respectfully.
  8. Write an audience connection that includes students and teachers.
  9. Organize three main speaking points in a logical order.
  10. Recommend an appropriate tone, pace, and emphasis.
  11. Write a polished 6–8 sentence opening using only verified facts.
  12. Explain three choices involving purpose, audience, and perspective.
