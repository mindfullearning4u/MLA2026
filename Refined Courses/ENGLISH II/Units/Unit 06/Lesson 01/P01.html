P01 Lesson Overview: Lesson 1: Oral Communication Purpose, Audience, and Perspective

Lesson Title

Lesson 1: Oral Communication Purpose, Audience, and Perspective

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.ENG2.C.01 - Present information clearly for a specific purpose, audience, and perspective.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG2.R.10 - Read Grade-Level Texts Accurately; MLA.ENG2.V.01 - Use Academic Vocabulary; MLA.ENG2.L.01 - Apply Standard English Conventions.

What You Will Learn

  • How purpose, audience, and perspective shape an oral explanation.
  • How to choose details that help listeners understand your point.
  • How academic vocabulary and standard English make speaking clearer and more credible.

Why This Matters

Purpose, audience, and perspective determine whether spoken communication succeeds. These choices matter in interviews, meetings, presentations, advocacy, and everyday conversations.

What You Will Do

  • Study a short source passage about student communication.
  • Plan a short oral explanation for a specific audience.
  • Revise speaking notes so they match purpose, audience, and perspective.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Write the lesson vocabulary words and definitions in your notebook.
  2. Complete the Lesson and Worked Example pages.
  3. Complete the Independent Work. Submit your vocabulary and definitions plus the Independent Work in the Notebook Evidence Submission activity.
  4. Complete the Checkpoint and submit those answers separately in the Checkpoint Submission activity.
  5. Complete the lesson quiz or unit assessment when assigned.
  6. If you earn below 80%, review TOR feedback, correct your mistakes, and meet with your TOR when additional help is needed.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook identifies purpose, audience, and perspective.
  • Your practice and quiz answers select audience-appropriate oral communication choices.
  • Your checkpoint explains how you would speak clearly to a specific audience.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can plan what to say by thinking about why I am speaking, who is listening, and what perspective I need to communicate.
