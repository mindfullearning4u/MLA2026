P03 — Lesson: Digital Presentation Clarity and Evidence Understanding

Direct Teaching

Clear digital presentations reduce the audience’s mental load. Speakers must use visual hierarchy, concise wording, accurate evidence, readable labels, and spoken explanation so viewers know what to notice and why it matters.

Five-Step Clarity Check

  1. State one message per slide.
  2. Place the most important evidence where the eye goes first.
  3. Use a heading that states the finding, not merely the topic.
  4. Explain the evidence orally and show its source.
  5. Test readability, contrast, labels, alternative text, and accuracy.

Example 1 — Heading

Weak: “Survey Results.” Strong: “Most Respondents Want Two Weekly Study-Space Days.”

Example 2 — Evidence

Show “214 of 360 respondents” beside a labeled bar; do not write “almost everyone.”

Example 3 — Reasoning

Say why the number matters: it supports a limited trial, while the voluntary sample requires caution.

Common Mistakes

  • Topic-only headings
  • Tiny text and decorative clutter
  • Unlabeled charts
  • Claims stronger than the evidence
