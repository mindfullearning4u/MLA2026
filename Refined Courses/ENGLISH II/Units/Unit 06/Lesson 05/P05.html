P05 — Independent Work: Clarify Evidence for a Digital Presentation

Directions: Read the complete source packet about reducing wasted electricity at school. Answer all 12 questions in order. Your work must demonstrate accurate evidence selection, clear digital design, source qualification, reasoning, and audience awareness.

Presentation Situation

You are preparing a three-minute presentation for the principal and facilities committee. Your purpose is to recommend one practical, measurable step to reduce unnecessary electricity use.

Source A — Classroom Observation

During ten after-school checks, a student team visited 24 classrooms. Lights were left on in 11 empty rooms at least once, and interactive displays were left powered on in 7 empty rooms at least once. The checks occurred on five Tuesdays and five Thursdays during one month. The team did not measure the exact electricity used by each device.

Source B — Facilities Manager Interview

The facilities manager explained that heating and cooling use more school electricity than classroom lights. However, turning off lights and displays is an immediate behavior students and teachers can control. She recommended a four-week reminder pilot and a follow-up observation using the same procedure before purchasing new equipment.

Source C — Student Proposal Draft

The draft proposes a small reminder card near classroom doors and a rotating student “last-out check.” It claims the plan will “cut the school’s electricity bill in half,” but no source in the packet supports that number. The draft’s strongest measurable goal is to reduce the number of empty rooms with lights or displays left on during the follow-up checks.

Independent Work Questions

  1. Identify the audience, purpose, and most reasonable presentation claim.
  2. Select the strongest evidence detail from Source A and explain its relevance.
  3. Explain one limitation of Source A that the presentation should acknowledge.
  4. Use Source B to explain why the proposal can still matter even though lighting is not the school’s largest electricity use.
  5. Identify and correct the unsupported claim in Source C.
  6. Write a two-sentence synthesis connecting Sources A and B.
  7. Create a clear slide title that states the main takeaway rather than merely naming the topic.
  8. Choose the best visual form for Source A—table, bar graph, pie chart, decorative photo, or paragraph—and justify your choice.
  9. Write the exact labels and source credit that should appear with the visual.
  10. Write three speaker-note sentences that interpret the evidence, qualify its limit, and connect it to the recommendation.
  11. Explain two ways the slide should be revised for clarity and accessibility.
  12. Write a concise conclusion that restates the claim and requests a measurable next step.

Lesson-Specific Success Checklist

  • I answered all 12 questions in order.
  • My claim fits the principal and facilities committee.
  • Every number, label, and source credit is accurate.
  • I identified what the evidence proves and what it does not prove.
  • My slide title communicates a takeaway.
  • My visual choice makes comparison easy and is not decorative.
  • My speaker notes interpret rather than repeat the slide.
  • My conclusion requests a practical, measurable action.
  • I used complete sentences and standard English.
