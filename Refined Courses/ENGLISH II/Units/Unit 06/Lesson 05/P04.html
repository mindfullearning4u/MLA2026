P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Revising a Heading

Weak heading: Data

Thinking: The audience does not know what the data proves.

Revised heading: Survey Data Shows a Need for Supervised Waiting Space

Example 2: Revising Evidence Display

Weak display: A sentence with several numbers crowded together.

Thinking: The audience may miss the important numbers.

Revised display: Two labeled bullets: 68 students remain on campus; 49 wait in informal areas.

Example 3: Revising Closing

Weak closing: The end.

Thinking: The closing should reinforce the message.

Revised closing: Clear evidence supports a supervised study space that helps students wait safely and productively.

Common Mistake

Incorrect Example:

Make the slide prettier but leave the evidence vague and unreadable.

Correct Example:

Revise the slide so the evidence is readable, labeled, and clearly connected to the claim.

Teachable Explanation: Digital clarity is not decoration. A strong presentation design helps the audience understand evidence and reasoning quickly.
