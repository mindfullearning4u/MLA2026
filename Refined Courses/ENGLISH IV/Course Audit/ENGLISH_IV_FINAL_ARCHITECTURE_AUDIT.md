# English IV Final Architecture Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit reviewed the full English IV curriculum architecture package before lesson production. The audit compared the English IV package against the approved English I, English II, and English III production frameworks, verified all 24 Florida B.E.S.T. English IV benchmark codes and benchmark wording against CPALMS public standards search results, validated MLA standard numbering and domain assignment, checked crosswalk and mapping coverage, audited all 48 lesson mapping rows, and reviewed vertical alignment from English III to English IV.

Initial audit status: `PASS`

Final audit status: `PASS`

No architecture-blocking corrections were required after the files were created. The package uses verified Grade 12 benchmark wording and does not duplicate English III. The verified English IV progression emphasizes advanced evaluation, functional significance, multiple themes, major poets in historical context, text/support effectiveness, related argument evaluation, rhetorical choices across multiple texts, topical-issue research, in-depth argument writing, in-depth complex text analysis, foreign phrases, digital presentation design/evaluation, and multimedia text creation/publishing/sharing.

## 3. Strengths

- The architecture preserves the approved six-unit, eight-lesson structure.
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together in every unit.
- Lesson 8 contains the Unit Assessment and introduces no new primary standards.
- MLA standard domains remain consistent with English I-III: Reading, Writing, Language, Vocabulary, Research, Communication.
- Unit 2 protects advanced literary evaluation rather than compressing all literary standards into one general block.
- Unit 3 sequences text/support effectiveness before purpose, related arguments, and rhetoric across multiple texts.
- Unit 4 maintains a coherent topical research-to-argument-and-analysis workflow.
- Unit 5 keeps language, vocabulary, style, and revision attached to authentic writing contexts.
- Unit 6 functions as synthesis, oral presentation, digital evaluation, and multimedia publication rather than first-time instruction.

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 2 has high literary-standard density. | Medium | Dedicated lessons protect functional significance, multiple themes, character perspective, major poets, figurative language, classic influence, and narrative synthesis. |
| Unit 3 has high evaluation density. | Medium | Sequence places structure/support effectiveness before purpose, related arguments, and rhetoric across texts. |
| Unit 4 may become overloaded if topical research, argument, and in-depth analysis are treated as unrelated products. | Medium | Mapping treats them as one connected research and writing process. |
| Unit 5 could become isolated grammar/vocabulary work. | Low | Mapping requires authentic literary, rhetorical, research, argument, analysis, and publication contexts. |
| Unit 6 could become production-heavy if earlier communication practice is weak. | Low | Oral communication, evidence explanation, revision, and digital awareness spiral before Unit 6. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

These are recommendations only and do not block certification.

- During future lesson production, select texts that naturally support Grade 12 requirements: major poets in historical context, classic influence on contemporary world texts, text effectiveness, related argument evaluation, and rhetorical choices across multiple texts.
- Maintain a text-bank audit before lesson production so Unit 2 and Unit 3 standards are not forced into weak or mismatched readings.
- Keep Unit 4 products connected: topical research notes, source synthesis, in-depth argument, and in-depth complex text analysis should remain one coherent workflow.
- During assessment design, ensure `ELA.12.C.5.1` measures design and evaluation of digital presentation effectiveness rather than visual polish alone.
- Avoid assigning English IV a specific literary tradition unless future local course requirements add that expectation separately from the verified Grade 12 benchmarks.
