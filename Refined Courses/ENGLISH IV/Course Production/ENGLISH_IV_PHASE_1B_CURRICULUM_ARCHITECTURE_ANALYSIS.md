# English IV Phase 1B: Curriculum Architecture Analysis

## Executive Summary

This document establishes the English IV curriculum architecture using the approved English I, English II, and English III production frameworks as the master template. It preserves MLA curriculum methodology, naming conventions, audit phases, documentation format, course production artifact expectations, and mastery-based sequencing.

This is architecture only. It does not create lesson content, assessments, HTML files, quiz files, GIFT files, lesson JSON files, or LMS pages.

## Approved Unit Architecture

| Unit | Unit Title | Architecture Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Foundations of Grade 12 Close Reading, Evidence, and Academic Voice | Establish Grade 12 paraphrase, academic vocabulary, evidence, conventions, and advanced analytical response readiness. |
| Unit 2 | Advanced Literary Evaluation, Perspective, Poetry, and Narrative Synthesis | Develop evaluation of literary elements, theme development, character perspective, major poets in historical context, figurative language, classic influence, and complex narrative writing. |
| Unit 3 | Advanced Informational Text, Rhetoric, and Argument Evaluation | Develop evaluation of structures, support, author purpose, multiple related arguments, rhetorical choices across texts, and author effectiveness. |
| Unit 4 | Topical Research, Argument, and In-Depth Text Analysis | Connect topical-issue research, source synthesis, argument writing, and in-depth analysis of complex texts. |
| Unit 5 | Language, Vocabulary, Style, and Revision | Refine standard English, academic vocabulary, etymology, derivations, foreign phrases, word meaning, purpose, clarity, structure, and style. |
| Unit 6 | Synthesis, Presentation, Digital Evaluation, and Multimedia Publication | Synthesize reading, research, argument, analysis, revision, oral presentation, digital presentation design/evaluation, and multimedia sharing. |

## English III to English IV Progression

| Area | English III Foundation | English IV Progression |
|---|---|---|
| Literary analysis | Literary evaluation, universal themes across time/place, juxtaposition, poetry in time-period context, allegory, contemporaneous authors. | Functional significance of literary elements, two or more themes, character perspective including conflicting perspectives, major poets in historical context, and classic influence on contemporary world texts. |
| Informational text | Classical Period speeches and essays, multiple arguments, reasoning, validity, rhetoric evaluation. | Evaluation of text effectiveness, support effectiveness, purpose, multiple related arguments, and rhetorical choices across multiple texts. |
| Writing | Literary analysis, complex text analysis, complex narratives with multiple perspectives. | In-depth argument writing, in-depth complex text analysis, and complex narratives with multiple perspectives and universal themes. |
| Research | Literary research with primary and secondary sources. | Topical-issue research using a variety of sources. |
| Communication | Rhetorical oral presentation, audience-experience digital presentation, multimedia online/LAN publication. | Oral presentation with rhetorical devices, digital presentation design/evaluation for effectiveness, and multimedia text creation/publishing/sharing across digital formats. |

## Architecture Controls

- Preserve the MLA mastery model.
- Preserve the approved six-unit, eight-lesson course structure.
- Preserve Lesson 8 as Putting It All Together.
- Preserve standard numbering format `MLA.ENG4.[DOMAIN].[NUMBER]`.
- Preserve the approved MLA English domains: Reading, Writing, Language, Vocabulary, Research, Communication.
- Preserve audit phase separation: architecture, inventory, crosswalk, mapping, then future production.
- Do not create student-facing instructional content in architecture documents.
- Do not introduce lesson content, assessments, HTML pages, quiz JSON, lesson JSON, or GIFT files in this phase.
- Store final audit documents in the sibling `Course Audit` folder.
