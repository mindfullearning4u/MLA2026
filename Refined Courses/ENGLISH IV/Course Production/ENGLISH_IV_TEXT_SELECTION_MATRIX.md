# English IV Text Selection Matrix

## Executive Summary

This document identifies required text and product types needed for future English IV lesson production. It supports the approved English IV architecture, verified Florida B.E.S.T. Grade 12 benchmarks, and MLA English IV standard mapping.

This is a planning and production-control document only. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, GIFT files, quiz JSON files, lesson JSON files, LMS pages, or student-facing instructional content.

## Selection Controls

| Control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Standards basis | Florida B.E.S.T. Grade 12 English Language Arts benchmarks |
| Course structure | 6 units, 8 lessons per unit |
| Lesson 8 | Putting It All Together with Unit Assessment |
| Text role | Support future standards-aligned lesson production |
| Content boundary | No lesson content, activities, assessments, prompts, questions, or student instructions |

## Unit 2 Text Requirements

Unit 2: Advanced Literary Evaluation, Perspective, Poetry, and Narrative Synthesis

| Required Text Type | Standards Supported | Selection Purpose | Production Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major poets in historical context | MLA.ENG4.R.04 | Support evaluation of works of major poets in historical context. | Texts must allow meaningful connection between poetic work and historical context. |
| Contemporary world texts | MLA.ENG4.R.11 | Support analysis of classic literature's influence on contemporary world texts. | Texts must be suitable for tracing influence, not merely sharing a broad topic. |
| Classic literature influence texts | MLA.ENG4.R.11 | Provide source/influence relationships between classic literature and later world texts. | Pairings must support influence analysis without requiring unsupported literary-history claims. |
| Multi-theme literary works | MLA.ENG4.R.02, MLA.ENG4.W.01 | Support analysis of two or more themes and narrative writing with universal themes. | Texts must contain multiple trackable themes developed across the work. |
| Texts with character perspective and conflicting perspectives | MLA.ENG4.R.03, MLA.ENG4.W.01 | Support evaluation of character perspective, including conflicting perspectives. | Texts must contain perspective development, not isolated disagreement only. |
| Texts with strong figurative language for evaluation | MLA.ENG4.R.09, MLA.ENG4.V.03 | Support evaluation of an author's use of figurative language. | Texts must contain figurative language that affects meaning, tone, style, or interpretation. |
| Literary texts with functional-significance evidence | MLA.ENG4.R.01 | Support evaluation of key elements and explanation of functional significance. | Texts must contain identifiable elements that shape interpretation. |

## Unit 3 Text Requirements

Unit 3: Advanced Informational Text, Rhetoric, and Argument Evaluation

| Required Text Type | Standards Supported | Selection Purpose | Production Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational texts with clear structure and features | MLA.ENG4.R.05 | Support evaluation of structures and features. | Texts must have structure/features that can be critiqued for effectiveness. |
| Texts where author support could be evaluated for effectiveness | MLA.ENG4.R.06 | Support evaluation of central idea development and support effectiveness. | Texts must contain enough support to evaluate quality and possible improvement. |
| Texts with clear author purpose | MLA.ENG4.R.07 | Support evaluation of choices in establishing and achieving purpose. | Texts must make author purpose traceable through choices, structure, evidence, or rhetoric. |
| Multiple related arguments | MLA.ENG4.R.08 | Support comparison of arguments in related texts. | Text sets must share a topic or issue while developing distinct claims or reasoning. |
| Related texts using the same information differently | MLA.ENG4.R.08 | Support analysis of same-information use across related arguments. | Text sets must include overlapping information used for different argumentative ends. |
| Rhetorical comparison texts | MLA.ENG4.R.12 | Support evaluation of rhetorical choices across multiple texts. | Texts must contain comparable rhetorical choices, strategies, or appeals. |
| Texts with varying effectiveness for critique | MLA.ENG4.R.05, MLA.ENG4.R.06, MLA.ENG4.R.07, MLA.ENG4.R.12 | Support critique of effectiveness in structure, support, purpose, and rhetoric. | Include stronger and weaker examples where future instruction requires evaluation. |

## Unit 4 Text Requirements

Unit 4: Topical Research, Argument, and In-Depth Text Analysis

| Required Text Type | Standards Supported | Selection Purpose | Production Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current topical issues | MLA.ENG4.RS.01, MLA.ENG4.W.02 | Support topical-issue research and in-depth argument writing. | Issues must be researchable through varied credible sources and appropriate for Grade 12 analysis. |
| Varied source sets | MLA.ENG4.RS.01 | Support synthesis from a variety of sources. | Source sets should include varied formats, perspectives, or evidence types where appropriate. |
| Research source collections | MLA.ENG4.RS.01, MLA.ENG4.W.02, MLA.ENG4.W.03 | Support research-to-writing workflow. | Collections must support synthesis, not isolated summary. |
| Argument exemplars | MLA.ENG4.W.02, MLA.ENG4.R.08 | Support future modeling of in-depth argument writing. | Exemplars must show claims, valid reasoning, credible evidence, elaboration, and subject understanding. |
| In-depth analysis exemplars | MLA.ENG4.W.03 | Support future modeling of complex text analysis. | Exemplars must show logical organization, appropriate tone/voice, and thorough understanding. |
| Complex texts suitable for Grade 12 analysis | MLA.ENG4.W.03, MLA.ENG4.R.05, MLA.ENG4.R.06, MLA.ENG4.R.12 | Support in-depth analysis of complex texts. | Texts must be complex enough to justify Grade 12 analytical expectations. |

## Unit 6 Text/Product Requirements

Unit 6: Synthesis, Presentation, Digital Evaluation, and Multimedia Publication

| Required Text/Product Type | Standards Supported | Selection Purpose | Production Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oral presentation models | MLA.ENG4.C.01 | Support future analysis of logical organization, coherent focus, credible evidence, and rhetorical devices. | Models must show presentation structure and rhetorical choices. |
| Digital presentation examples | MLA.ENG4.C.02 | Support design and evaluation of digital presentations for effectiveness. | Examples should vary in effectiveness so students can evaluate design choices in future production. |
| Multimedia publication models | MLA.ENG4.C.03 | Support future multimedia creation, publishing, and sharing. | Models must demonstrate multimedia text features and publication format. |
| Examples suitable for evaluating effectiveness | MLA.ENG4.C.02, MLA.ENG4.C.03 | Support evaluation of digital and multimedia communication. | Examples must contain identifiable strengths and weaknesses for effectiveness review. |
| Examples of publishable multimedia texts | MLA.ENG4.C.03 | Support future publication expectations across digital formats. | Examples must be appropriate for publication-quality discussion without becoming templates for required student content. |

## Cross-Unit Text Selection Controls

| Control Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Grade level | Texts and products must support Grade 12 reading, writing, research, and communication rigor. |
| Alignment | Every selected text/product should connect clearly to at least one approved MLA English IV standard. |
| Complexity | Texts must support evaluation, synthesis, and in-depth analysis rather than only identification or summary. |
| Variety | Text sets should include literary, informational, rhetorical, research, digital, and multimedia materials where required by unit standards. |
| Accessibility | Future production should scaffold complex texts without simplifying or replacing benchmark-level expectations. |
| Neutrality | Topical issue materials should support academic analysis and source evaluation, not unsupported persuasion. |
