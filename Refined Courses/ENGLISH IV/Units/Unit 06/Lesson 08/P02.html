Notebook Title

Unit 6 Communication Synthesis

Vocabulary

  • Synthesis: combining multiple skills or ideas into one connected product.
  • Audience-ready: prepared for the needs, expectations, and access of the intended audience.
  • Credible communication: communication supported by trustworthy evidence, source credit, and accurate reasoning.
  • Digital effectiveness: how well digital choices help the audience understand the message.
  • Publication readiness: the final check before sharing a multimedia product publicly.

Embedded Scenario

Embedded Unit 6 Synthesis Scenario: A student is preparing a final digital presentation and multimedia publication about expanding access to career certification programs. The project must include a focused oral explanation, effective rhetorical choices, a research-based claim, digital slides that are clear and accessible, a multimedia publication plan, source credits, and a final sharing format appropriate for classmates, families, and community partners.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Start with the claim. A final communication project needs one focused message.

Step 2: Connect oral explanation to evidence. The speaker must explain why the evidence matters.

Step 3: Design slides that support the message. The digital presentation should guide attention, not overload the audience.

Step 4: Prepare multimedia publication. Written, visual, audio, and source-credit elements must work together.

Step 5: Revise for audience readiness. The final product should be credible, accessible, clear, and professional.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create a synthesis plan with four sections: oral explanation, digital slides, multimedia publication, and final revision checks.

Model Example

Oral explanation: State the claim and explain two credible pieces of evidence.

Digital slides: Use clear titles, readable visuals, and limited text.

Publication: Include credits, access checks, and a shareable format.
