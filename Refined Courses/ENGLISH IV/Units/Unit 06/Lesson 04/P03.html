P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now evaluate design choices based on function. A design choice is strong when it helps the audience follow, remember, or evaluate the message.

Embedded Scenario

Embedded Design Scenario: A student team is preparing a digital presentation about community access to career certification programs. Their first draft contains twelve text-heavy slides, three unrelated photos, a chart without a title, and bullet points copied directly from a source. Their revised goal is to create a presentation that helps an audience understand the problem, compare two solutions, and remember the strongest evidence. The team must decide which visuals, text, layout choices, and speaker notes will make the presentation clear, credible, and effective.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Decide what each slide should do. A slide might define the problem, compare solutions, display evidence, or prompt audience action.

Step 2: Use limited text. A slide should highlight key ideas while the speaker explains details aloud.

Step 3: Choose visuals that perform a job. A visual should clarify data, show relationships, or make a comparison easier to understand.

Step 4: Revise for audience access. Strong contrast, readable font size, clear labels, and simple organization help the audience process information quickly.

Notebook Directions

Plan one revised slide. Write the slide title, three short bullet points, one visual you would include, and one sentence explaining why the design supports the audience.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: Add more photos and brighter colors so the presentation looks exciting.

Correct example: Replace unrelated photos with one labeled chart that compares access barriers and supports the presentation claim.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version treats design as decoration. The correct version evaluates whether the design choice helps the audience understand evidence and reasoning.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Digital presentation design for effectiveness

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.C.02

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your reasoning is specific, defensible, and mature enough for Grade 12 mastery.

  1. Identify the audience, purpose, mode, and evidence before designing the presentation or publication.
  2. Organize the message for real-time understanding. Viewers or listeners should not need hidden explanation to follow the reasoning.
  3. Use digital, visual, rhetorical, or multimedia choices only when they improve understanding, credibility, emphasis, or audience engagement.
  4. Evaluate the final product for accuracy, accessibility, polish, and effectiveness before sharing or submitting it.
