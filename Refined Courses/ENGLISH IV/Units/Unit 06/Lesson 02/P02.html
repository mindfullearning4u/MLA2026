Notebook Title

Rhetorical Devices in Oral Presentation

Vocabulary

  • Rhetorical device: a language choice used to affect audience understanding or response.
  • Repetition: repeating a word or phrase to emphasize an idea.
  • Parallel structure: using similar grammatical patterns to make ideas balanced and memorable.
  • Analogy: a comparison that explains one idea through another.
  • Rhetorical question: a question asked to make a point rather than to receive an answer.

Step 1: Rhetoric Must Serve the Message

Rhetorical devices should not be added only to sound dramatic. A device is effective when it helps the audience understand, remember, or evaluate the message. If a device distracts from evidence or exaggerates the claim, it weakens the presentation.

Step 2: Connect Rhetoric to Evidence

A strong speaker may use repetition to emphasize a finding, parallel structure to organize several reasons, or analogy to make a complex idea understandable. The device must still connect to valid evidence and reasoning.

Embedded Presentation Excerpt

Excerpt: We need evidence that is current, evidence that is credible, and evidence that our audience can understand.

Teacher-like thinking: The speaker uses repetition and parallel structure. Repeating evidence that is emphasizes three qualities of strong evidence and helps the audience remember the point.

Notebook Directions

  1. Identify the rhetorical device in the excerpt.
  2. Explain how the device helps the listener.
  3. Write one sentence using parallel structure about credible communication.
