P04 Worked Example

Embedded Scenario

Embedded Publication Scenario: A senior team has created a multimedia public-service announcement about preparing for postsecondary training. The product includes a one-minute narration, three slides, an infographic, a brief research summary, and source credits. Before sharing it with classmates and a local career center, the team must decide whether the message is focused, whether the visuals and narration work together, whether the evidence is credited, whether the digital format is accessible, and whether the final product sounds professional enough for a public audience.

Example 1: Evaluate Focus

Question: What should the team check first?

Modeled thinking: A public-service announcement needs one clear purpose. If the message is scattered, the audience will not know what action or understanding matters most.

Answer: Check that every element supports preparing for postsecondary training.

Example 2: Evaluate Coherence

Question: How should narration and visuals work together?

Modeled thinking: Narration should explain or extend visuals, not simply repeat slide text or introduce unrelated ideas.

Answer: The narration should guide viewers through the infographic and slides so the message feels unified.

Example 3: Evaluate Publication Readiness

Question: What must be checked before sharing with a career center?

Modeled thinking: A public audience requires professional language, source credits, accurate evidence, readable design, and an accessible format.

Answer: The team should verify accuracy, credits, accessibility, format, and professional voice before publishing.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: The most important revision is adding more animations so viewers stay interested.

Correct example: The most important revision is clarifying source credits and aligning narration with visuals so the audience can trust and understand the message.

Teachable explanation: Animation can decorate a product, but credibility and coherence are stronger publication-readiness priorities.
