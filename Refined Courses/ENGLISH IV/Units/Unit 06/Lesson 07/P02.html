P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Publication-Ready Multimedia Review

Vocabulary

  • Publication-ready: revised, accurate, credited, accessible, and appropriate for sharing.
  • Multimedia coherence: the way words, visuals, audio, and evidence work together toward one purpose.
  • Public audience: viewers or listeners beyond the original classroom writer.
  • Professional voice: clear, respectful, accurate language suitable for academic or workplace settings.
  • Revision priority: the most important change needed before publication.

Embedded Scenario

Embedded Publication Scenario: A senior team has created a multimedia public-service announcement about preparing for postsecondary training. The product includes a one-minute narration, three slides, an infographic, a brief research summary, and source credits. Before sharing it with classmates and a local career center, the team must decide whether the message is focused, whether the visuals and narration work together, whether the evidence is credited, whether the digital format is accessible, and whether the final product sounds professional enough for a public audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the purpose. The public-service announcement should help viewers prepare for postsecondary training.

Step 2: Check focus. Every slide, visual, narration section, and source should support that purpose.

Step 3: Check coherence across modes. The narration should explain the visuals; the infographic should clarify the research; the slides should not compete with the audio.

Step 4: Check credibility. The research summary and source credits must make the evidence trustworthy and traceable.

Step 5: Check publication fit. A product shared with a career center needs professional voice, accessible design, and a format that opens easily.

Notebook Directions

Create a publication-readiness checklist with five categories: focus, coherence, credibility, accessibility, and professional voice. Write one question for each category.

Model Example

Category: Credibility.

Checklist question: Are all research claims supported by source credits that viewers can recognize?

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this skill feels new, begin by naming the topic, defining the key vocabulary in your own words, and finding one sentence or detail that clearly connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete each step in order: identify the skill, locate evidence, explain the connection, and check that your response answers the prompt directly.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by adding a more precise word choice, a second relevant detail, or a clearer explanation of why the evidence matters.
