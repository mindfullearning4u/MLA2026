P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Presenting Research With Claim, Evidence, and Significance

Vocabulary

  • Topical research: focused investigation of a specific issue or question.
  • Presentation claim: the main point the speaker wants the audience to understand or accept.
  • Credible evidence: support that is trustworthy, relevant, and appropriate for the claim.
  • Significance: why the evidence matters beyond being a fact.
  • Research limitation: a boundary or weakness that affects how strongly evidence can be used.

Embedded Passage

Embedded Research Brief: A regional college success office studied whether a senior capstone showcase should include student research presentations. Survey data from 420 students showed that 72 percent felt more prepared for college interviews after practicing formal presentations. Faculty interviews reported that students who explained their research aloud were more likely to clarify their claim, select stronger evidence, and revise vague reasoning. The report recommended a short presentation format: a focused claim, two credible pieces of evidence, a brief explanation of research limits, and a closing statement that connects the findings to a real audience need.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Begin with the research question or topic. A presentation cannot include every detail from research, so the speaker must choose one focus.

Step 2: Turn the topic into a claim. A topic names the subject; a claim says what the research shows about that subject.

Step 3: Select evidence that directly supports the claim. In the research brief, the survey data and faculty interviews are stronger than a general statement that presentations are useful.

Step 4: Explain significance. A Grade 12 presentation should not stop after naming evidence. It must explain how the evidence proves, complicates, or limits the claim.

Step 5: Plan the oral order: claim first, evidence second, explanation third, limitation fourth, conclusion last.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, write one focused claim based on the research brief. Then list two pieces of credible evidence and explain what each piece helps the audience understand.

Model Example

Claim: Senior capstone presentations can strengthen college readiness because they require students to clarify and explain their research for an audience.

Evidence: Seventy-two percent of surveyed students felt more prepared for college interviews after formal presentation practice.

Explanation: This evidence matters because college and workforce settings often require students to explain ideas aloud, not only submit written work.
