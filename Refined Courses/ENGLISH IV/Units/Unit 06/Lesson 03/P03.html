ENGLISH IV | Unit 06 | Lesson 03

P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from selecting evidence to shaping an oral explanation. Your goal is to make the audience understand the logic of your research, not simply hear a list of findings.

Embedded Passage

Embedded Research Brief: A regional college success office studied whether a senior capstone showcase should include student research presentations. Survey data from 420 students showed that 72 percent felt more prepared for college interviews after practicing formal presentations. Faculty interviews reported that students who explained their research aloud were more likely to clarify their claim, select stronger evidence, and revise vague reasoning. The report recommended a short presentation format: a focused claim, two credible pieces of evidence, a brief explanation of research limits, and a closing statement that connects the findings to a real audience need.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Identify the audience need. A college-readiness audience needs to know whether presentations build transferable skills.

Step 2: Choose an order that makes reasoning visible. Start with the claim, then move to the strongest evidence, then explain what the evidence proves.

Step 3: Acknowledge limits briefly. If the research uses one regional office, say that the findings are useful but may need confirmation in other settings.

Step 4: Use transition language. Words such as therefore, however, and as a result help listeners track the reasoning.

Notebook Directions

Write a four-sentence oral explanation: sentence 1 states the claim, sentence 2 presents evidence, sentence 3 explains significance, and sentence 4 acknowledges a limitation or audience connection.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: The report says presentations are good and students like them.

Correct example: The report suggests capstone presentations support college readiness because 72 percent of surveyed students felt better prepared for interviews after formal presentation practice.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version is vague and does not identify credible evidence. The correct version names the claim, includes specific data, and connects the evidence to a readiness outcome.
