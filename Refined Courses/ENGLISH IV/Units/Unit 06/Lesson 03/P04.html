ENGLISH IV | Unit 06 | Lesson 03

P04 Worked Example

Embedded Passage

Embedded Research Brief: A regional college success office studied whether a senior capstone showcase should include student research presentations. Survey data from 420 students showed that 72 percent felt more prepared for college interviews after practicing formal presentations. Faculty interviews reported that students who explained their research aloud were more likely to clarify their claim, select stronger evidence, and revise vague reasoning. The report recommended a short presentation format: a focused claim, two credible pieces of evidence, a brief explanation of research limits, and a closing statement that connects the findings to a real audience need.

Example 1: Build the Claim

Question: What focused claim could guide the presentation?

Modeled thinking: I need a claim that is narrower than capstone presentations are helpful. The brief emphasizes college interviews, research clarity, and evidence selection.

Answer: Capstone research presentations strengthen college readiness by requiring students to explain evidence clearly for an audience.

Example 2: Select Evidence

Question: Which evidence should the speaker use first?

Modeled thinking: The survey statistic is concrete and easy for listeners to understand. It directly supports readiness.

Answer: Use the 72 percent survey result first because it is specific, credible, and directly connected to presentation practice.

Example 3: Explain Significance

Question: How should the speaker explain why the evidence matters?

Modeled thinking: I should not repeat the statistic. I should explain what the statistic reveals about student preparation.

Answer: The statistic matters because students are practicing the same kind of clear oral explanation expected in interviews, college seminars, and workplace meetings.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: A lot of students probably improved, so presentations should be required.

Correct example: Because 72 percent of surveyed students reported stronger interview readiness, the presentation format appears to build a practical communication skill linked to college and career settings.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect response guesses and overclaims. The correct response uses the actual evidence, explains its function, and avoids claiming more than the research proves.
