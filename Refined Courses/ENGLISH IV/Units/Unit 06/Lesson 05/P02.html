P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Evaluating Digital Presentation Effectiveness

Vocabulary

  • Effectiveness: how well a presentation achieves its purpose for its audience.
  • Accessibility: how easily audience members can read, hear, and understand the message.
  • Credibility: the trustworthiness of information, sources, and presentation choices.
  • Audience fit: how well language, examples, and design match audience needs.
  • Evaluation criteria: standards used to judge quality.

Embedded Presentation Review

Embedded Digital Presentation Review: A public health internship team created a digital presentation about expanding telehealth appointment access for rural patients. The presentation includes a title slide, a chart comparing appointment wait times before and after a pilot program, three source citations, and a final recommendation. However, several slides contain dense paragraphs, one chart lacks axis labels, the color contrast makes two slides difficult to read, and the speaker uses technical terms such as asynchronous intake and care continuity without defining them. The team wants to evaluate whether the presentation is effective for a mixed audience of patients, clinic staff, and county leaders.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the presentation purpose. The team wants to explain telehealth access and support a recommendation.

Step 2: Identify the audience. Patients, clinic staff, and county leaders will not all share the same background knowledge.

Step 3: Evaluate clarity. Dense paragraphs and undefined technical terms may make the message difficult to follow.

Step 4: Evaluate credibility. Source citations and a wait-time chart support credibility, but the chart needs labels so the audience can interpret it.

Step 5: Evaluate accessibility. Low color contrast and technical language create barriers for some viewers.

Notebook Directions

Create a two-column chart. In the left column, list three presentation weaknesses. In the right column, explain how each weakness affects the audience.

Model Example

Weakness: The chart lacks axis labels.

Audience impact: Viewers may see that the chart contains data but not understand what the numbers measure or why the pilot program matters.
