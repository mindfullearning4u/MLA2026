P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from identifying weaknesses to making evidence-based evaluation judgments. A strong evaluation explains both the problem and the reason it matters.

Embedded Presentation Review

Embedded Digital Presentation Review: A public health internship team created a digital presentation about expanding telehealth appointment access for rural patients. The presentation includes a title slide, a chart comparing appointment wait times before and after a pilot program, three source citations, and a final recommendation. However, several slides contain dense paragraphs, one chart lacks axis labels, the color contrast makes two slides difficult to read, and the speaker uses technical terms such as asynchronous intake and care continuity without defining them. The team wants to evaluate whether the presentation is effective for a mixed audience of patients, clinic staff, and county leaders.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Separate preference from evaluation. Saying a slide looks boring is a preference. Saying low contrast makes text hard to read is an evaluation.

Step 2: Connect each judgment to a criterion. Use clarity, credibility, accessibility, organization, or audience fit.

Step 3: Recommend a revision. An evaluation should help the presenter improve the product.

Step 4: Use precise academic vocabulary. Terms such as accessible, credible, coherent, and audience-appropriate make the evaluation more professional.

Notebook Directions

Write one evaluation paragraph explaining whether the telehealth presentation is currently effective. Include at least two criteria and one recommended revision.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: The slides are bad because they do not look very good.

Correct example: The presentation is only partly effective because the source citations support credibility, but dense paragraphs, undefined technical terms, and low color contrast reduce clarity and accessibility for a mixed audience.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version gives a vague opinion. The correct version evaluates specific features and explains their effect on audience understanding.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Evaluating digital presentations for effectiveness

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.C.02

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your reasoning is specific, defensible, and mature enough for Grade 12 mastery.

  1. Identify the audience, purpose, mode, and evidence before designing the presentation or publication.
  2. Organize the message for real-time understanding. Viewers or listeners should not need hidden explanation to follow the reasoning.
  3. Use digital, visual, rhetorical, or multimedia choices only when they improve understanding, credibility, emphasis, or audience engagement.
  4. Evaluate the final product for accuracy, accessibility, polish, and effectiveness before sharing or submitting it.
