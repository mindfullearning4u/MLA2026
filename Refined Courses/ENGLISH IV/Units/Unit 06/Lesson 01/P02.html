P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Organizing Evidence for Oral Communication

Vocabulary

  • Focus: the main point a speaker wants the listener to understand.
  • Credible evidence: information from a trustworthy source that supports a point.
  • Paraphrase: restating source information accurately in new wording.
  • Oral organization: the order used to guide listeners through ideas.
  • Transition: language that shows how one spoken idea connects to the next.

Step 1: Start With One Focus

An oral response needs a clear focus because listeners cannot reread your words the way they can reread a paragraph. Begin by stating the main point in one sentence. A strong focus is specific, not general.

Weak focus: Public transportation is important.

Stronger focus: Reliable public transportation supports employment because workers need consistent access to job sites.

Step 2: Use Evidence Listeners Can Trust

Credible evidence should come from a source that has authority, accuracy, and relevance. In oral communication, introduce the evidence briefly before explaining it.

Example: A city transportation report found that workers with unreliable transit missed more shifts. This evidence supports the focus because it connects transportation access to employment stability.

Embedded Source Note

Source note: A local workforce report states that employees with unreliable transportation are more likely to miss scheduled shifts and lose work hours.

Accurate paraphrase: Workers who cannot depend on transportation may lose work time because they cannot arrive consistently.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write one focused speaking claim about transportation and employment.
  2. Paraphrase the embedded source note accurately.
  3. Explain how the evidence supports the claim.
