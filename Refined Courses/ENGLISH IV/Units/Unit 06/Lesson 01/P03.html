P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Learning: Organize for the Listener

Listeners need signals. A strong oral response usually follows this order: focus, evidence, explanation, conclusion. This order lets the listener know what you are arguing, what supports it, why it matters, and what final point to remember.

Model Oral Response

Focus: Reliable transportation supports employment stability.

Evidence: A local workforce report states that employees with unreliable transportation are more likely to miss scheduled shifts.

Explanation: This evidence matters because missed shifts can reduce income and make employment less secure.

Conclusion: For that reason, transportation access is a workforce issue, not only a travel issue.

Notebook Application

  1. Label the focus, evidence, explanation, and conclusion in the model.
  2. Write one transition that could introduce the evidence.
  3. Write one transition that could introduce the conclusion.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Start speaking with evidence before the listener knows the main point.

Correct: State the focus first, then present evidence and explain why it matters.

Teachable explanation: Evidence is easier to understand when listeners know what point the evidence supports.
