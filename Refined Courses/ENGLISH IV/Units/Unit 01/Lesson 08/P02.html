Embedded Passage

Passage: Independent Learning Habits

Independent learning does not mean working without support. Effective independent learners use resources deliberately, ask focused questions, evaluate feedback, and revise their work. A student who receives feedback but does not examine it carefully may repeat the same error, while a student who studies the feedback can identify the pattern behind the mistake. Strong independent learning therefore depends on both personal responsibility and thoughtful use of available support. In college and workplace settings, this habit matters because people are expected to improve work from evidence, feedback, and reflection.

Detailed Teaching

Step 1: Identify the qualified claim. Independent learning does not mean working without support.

Step 2: Paraphrase the claim accurately. Independent learners still use resources, questions, feedback, and revision.

Step 3: Select evidence about feedback and repeated errors.

Step 4: Explain the evidence with academic vocabulary such as evaluate, revise, pattern, and reflection.

Notebook Directions and Model

  1. Write the notebook title and vocabulary.
  2. Paraphrase the qualified claim.
  3. Select one evidence detail.
  4. Explain the evidence's function.

Model: The passage argues that independent learning includes using support thoughtfully. The example about studying feedback supports this claim because it shows that improvement depends on evaluating mistakes, not simply receiving help.
