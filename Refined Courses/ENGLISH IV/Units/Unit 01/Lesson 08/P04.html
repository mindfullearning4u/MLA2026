Embedded Passage

Passage: Independent Learning Habits

Independent learning does not mean working without support. Effective independent learners use resources deliberately, ask focused questions, evaluate feedback, and revise their work. A student who receives feedback but does not examine it carefully may repeat the same error, while a student who studies the feedback can identify the pattern behind the mistake. Strong independent learning therefore depends on both personal responsibility and thoughtful use of available support. In college and workplace settings, this habit matters because people are expected to improve work from evidence, feedback, and reflection.

Example 1: Qualified Claim

Claim: The passage defines independent learning as responsible use of support, not isolation.

Modeled thinking: This claim preserves the passage's first sentence and avoids the mistake of saying independent learners work alone.

Reasoning: It accurately frames the passage's central distinction.

Example 2: Evidence Explanation

Paraphrased evidence: A student who studies feedback can identify the pattern behind a mistake.

Rationale: This evidence supports the claim because it shows that support becomes useful when the learner evaluates it and uses it to improve.

Reasoning: The explanation connects evidence to the passage's larger point.

Example 3: Synthesis Response

Response: The passage defines independent learning as responsible use of support rather than isolation. Effective learners ask focused questions, evaluate feedback, and revise their work. The feedback example is important because it shows that improvement comes from recognizing patterns in mistakes and using support thoughtfully. This habit prepares students for college and workplace settings, where people must improve work from evidence, feedback, and reflection.

Reasoning: The response combines paraphrase, evidence, academic vocabulary, conventions, and readiness connection.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: Feedback automatically improves work.

Correct example: Feedback improves work when the learner examines it carefully and uses it to revise.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect example removes the learner's responsibility. The correct example preserves the passage's cause-and-effect logic.
