Passage: Precision in Civic Conversation

When people discuss a public issue, precision matters. A speaker who describes a policy as beneficial is making a broad positive judgment, but a speaker who calls it equitable is making a more specific claim about fairness. Likewise, describing evidence as relevant means it connects to the issue, while describing it as credible means it can be trusted. Academic vocabulary allows writers and speakers to make exact distinctions. Without precise words, an argument may sound confident while leaving the audience unsure about what is being claimed, how evidence supports it, or why the conclusion should be accepted.

Instructions

Complete Parts A, B, and C in your notebook. Your responses must use academic vocabulary accurately and follow standard English conventions.

Part A: Vocabulary Distinctions

Explain the difference between beneficial and equitable. Then explain the difference between relevant and credible.

Part B: Precise Application

Write four original sentences: one using beneficial, one using equitable, one using relevant, and one using credible. Each sentence must show the word's specific meaning.

Part C: Paraphrase and Evaluate

Paraphrase the final sentence of the passage. Then evaluate how precise vocabulary helps an audience understand a claim, evidence, and conclusion.
