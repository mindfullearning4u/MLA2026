Checkpoint Task

Use this embedded passage: Careful reading is not the same as slow reading. A careful reader notices how each sentence changes or refines the idea before it. When a writer qualifies a claim with words such as however, although, or specifically, the reader must preserve that qualification in a paraphrase. A strong paraphrase does more than replace words with synonyms; it restates the full meaning, including relationships among ideas, limits on claims, and the author's emphasis. In college and workplace settings, inaccurate paraphrase can distort a source, weaken an argument, or create confusion about what a document actually says.

Submit: one accurate paragraph paraphrase and a four-sentence explanation of how your paraphrase preserves the passage's meaning, qualification, relationships, and emphasis.

Mastery Criteria

  • The paraphrase restates the full passage accurately in your own wording and structure.
  • The explanation identifies at least one qualification and one relationship among ideas.
  • The response uses academic vocabulary correctly.
  • The response follows standard English grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and spelling.
  • The response does not introduce future-lesson standards or unrelated analysis tasks.
