Embedded Passage

Passage: On Careful Reading

Careful reading is not the same as slow reading. A careful reader notices how each sentence changes or refines the idea before it. When a writer qualifies a claim with words such as however, although, or specifically, the reader must preserve that qualification in a paraphrase. A strong paraphrase does more than replace words with synonyms; it restates the full meaning, including relationships among ideas, limits on claims, and the author's emphasis. In college and workplace settings, inaccurate paraphrase can distort a source, weaken an argument, or create confusion about what a document actually says.

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from sentence-level restatement to whole-passage paraphrase. A whole-passage paraphrase is not a summary. A summary shortens the main idea. A paraphrase restates the full meaning of the selected passage or section in your own words.

Step 1: Review your meaning-unit chart from Part 1.

Step 2: Combine the meaning units into a coherent paragraph.

Step 3: Check whether your paragraph keeps the passage's major relationships: contrast, refinement, qualification, and consequence.

Step 4: Revise for standard English conventions so the reader can follow your thinking without confusion.

Deeper Learning: Evaluate the Accuracy

At Grade 12, you should evaluate whether a paraphrase is effective. Ask these questions:

  • Does it preserve the author's meaning without adding a new opinion?
  • Does it preserve qualifications such as "not the same as" and "does more than"?
  • Does it preserve the consequences of inaccurate paraphrase?
  • Does it use original sentence structure instead of copying the source?

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: Careful reading just means reading slowly and changing the writer's words into synonyms so the paragraph sounds different.

Correct example: Careful reading means tracking how each sentence develops the writer's idea so a paraphrase can restate the full meaning, including limits, relationships, and emphasis.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect example distorts the passage because it says careful reading "just means reading slowly," while the passage explicitly says careful reading is not the same as slow reading. It also reduces paraphrase to synonym replacement. The correct example preserves the passage's central distinction and explains the function of careful reading.

Notebook Task

Write one full-paragraph paraphrase of the embedded passage. Then write three sentences explaining why your paraphrase is accurate. Use the words preserve, qualification, and distort correctly in your explanation.
