Embedded Passage

Passage: On Careful Reading

Careful reading is not the same as slow reading. A careful reader notices how each sentence changes or refines the idea before it. When a writer qualifies a claim with words such as however, although, or specifically, the reader must preserve that qualification in a paraphrase. A strong paraphrase does more than replace words with synonyms; it restates the full meaning, including relationships among ideas, limits on claims, and the author's emphasis. In college and workplace settings, inaccurate paraphrase can distort a source, weaken an argument, or create confusion about what a document actually says.

Instructions

Complete Parts A, B, and C in your notebook. Use complete sentences and standard English conventions. Your work should show how you moved from close reading to accurate paraphrase.

Part A: Meaning Units

Divide the passage into four or five meaning units. For each unit, write one sentence explaining its role in the passage.

Part B: Full Paraphrase

Write one full-paragraph paraphrase of the passage. Do not copy the original sentence order. Preserve contrast, qualification, emphasis, and consequences.

Part C: Accuracy Check

Write a short evaluation of your paraphrase. Explain one place where you preserved a qualification and one place where you preserved the author's emphasis.
