P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Paraphrasing Relevant Evidence

Vocabulary

  • Relevant: directly connected to the claim or issue.
  • Evidence: information from a source that supports or clarifies a claim.
  • Paraphrase: an accurate restatement in your own words.
  • Credible: trustworthy and believable.
  • Rationale: the reason that explains why evidence matters.

Embedded Passage

Passage: Transit Access and Student Participation

A community college review found that students with reliable evening transportation were more likely to attend tutoring sessions, library workshops, and study groups after work. The report did not claim that transportation alone caused higher grades. Instead, it concluded that dependable transit expanded students' access to academic support services. The strongest evidence came from attendance records showing that late-afternoon workshop participation increased after the college added a shuttle route to nearby bus stops. The review recommended continuing the shuttle program while also studying other barriers, including childcare, course scheduling, and internet access.

Detailed Teaching

Step 1: Identify the claim you need to support. Example claim: Reliable transportation can increase student access to academic support.

Step 2: Choose evidence that directly connects to that claim. The workshop attendance detail is relevant because it shows increased use of support services after the shuttle route was added.

Step 3: Paraphrase the evidence accurately. Do not exaggerate the report into saying transportation alone caused higher grades.

Step 4: Explain the rationale. The evidence matters because it links transportation access to participation in support services.

Notebook Directions and Model

  1. Write the notebook title and vocabulary.
  2. Write the claim from the teaching section.
  3. Copy one relevant piece of evidence from the passage.
  4. Paraphrase the evidence in your own words.
  5. Explain why the evidence is relevant.

Model paraphrase: After the shuttle route was added, more students attended late-afternoon workshops, which suggests that transportation access helped students use academic support services.
