Embedded Passage

Passage: Transit Access and Student Participation

A community college review found that students with reliable evening transportation were more likely to attend tutoring sessions, library workshops, and study groups after work. The report did not claim that transportation alone caused higher grades. Instead, it concluded that dependable transit expanded students' access to academic support services. The strongest evidence came from attendance records showing that late-afternoon workshop participation increased after the college added a shuttle route to nearby bus stops. The review recommended continuing the shuttle program while also studying other barriers, including childcare, course scheduling, and internet access.

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from selecting evidence to explaining evidence. A Grade 12 response should not drop in evidence and expect the reader to make the connection. You must state the relevance clearly.

Step 1: State the claim.

Step 2: Paraphrase the evidence.

Step 3: Explain how the evidence supports the claim.

Step 4: Prepare one sentence you could say aloud to explain your reasoning logically.

Deeper Learning

Relevant evidence must be both connected and accurately represented. If evidence is connected but exaggerated, the paraphrase becomes misleading. If a paraphrase is accurate but unrelated to the claim, it is not useful evidence. Strong academic reasoning requires both accuracy and relevance.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: The shuttle caused students to earn higher grades.

Correct example: The shuttle appears to have increased access to support services because workshop attendance rose after the route was added.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect example overstates the source by claiming the shuttle caused higher grades. The passage explicitly says the report did not make that claim. The correct example preserves the actual conclusion and connects the evidence to access.

Notebook Task

Write a claim, a paraphrased evidence sentence, and a two-sentence explanation of relevance. Then write one sentence you could say aloud to present the evidence logically.
