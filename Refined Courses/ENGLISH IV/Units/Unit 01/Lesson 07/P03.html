Embedded Passage

Passage: Responsible Technology Use

Digital tools can expand learning when students use them with purpose. A note-taking application can help a student organize source ideas, and a shared document can make peer feedback more efficient. However, technology does not automatically improve learning. If students copy information without paraphrasing, accept suggestions without evaluating them, or use digital tools to avoid careful thinking, the tool weakens rather than strengthens academic work. Responsible technology use requires students to preserve source meaning, evaluate support, and communicate conclusions clearly.

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from written explanation to oral explanation. A brief oral explanation needs a clear order: claim, paraphrased evidence, and rationale. The words should be precise enough for Grade 12 work but direct enough for a listener to follow.

Step 1: State the claim in one sentence.

Step 2: Paraphrase one piece of evidence.

Step 3: Explain the evidence's function.

Step 4: Revise for academic voice and standard conventions.

Deeper Learning

Integrated response means each part does a job. Paraphrase shows understanding. Evidence supports the claim. Vocabulary makes meaning precise. Conventions keep the reasoning credible. Oral organization helps the listener follow the connection.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: Technology always improves learning because apps are efficient.

Correct example: Technology can improve learning when students use tools purposefully and continue to evaluate, paraphrase, and communicate clearly.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect example ignores the passage's qualification. The correct example preserves the condition that technology helps only when used responsibly.
