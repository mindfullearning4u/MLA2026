Passage: Clarity in Professional Communication

In professional communication, conventions are not decorative rules; they protect meaning. A misplaced comma can blur the relationship between ideas, and a sentence fragment can make a recommendation sound unfinished. Clear conventions also help readers evaluate a writer's reasoning. When grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and spelling are controlled, the audience can focus on the claim, evidence, and explanation instead of struggling to decode the sentence. Strong writers revise conventions because accurate language supports credible thinking.

Instructions

Complete Parts A, B, and C in your notebook. Correct each sentence and explain why the correction matters.

Part A

Paraphrase the main idea of the passage in one clear sentence.

Part B

Correct three convention errors: one fragment, one agreement error, and one comma error.

Part C

Explain how each correction improves clarity, credibility, or meaning.
