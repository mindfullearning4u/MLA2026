Embedded Passage

Passage: Clarity in Professional Communication

In professional communication, conventions are not decorative rules; they protect meaning. A misplaced comma can blur the relationship between ideas, and a sentence fragment can make a recommendation sound unfinished. Clear conventions also help readers evaluate a writer's reasoning. When grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and spelling are controlled, the audience can focus on the claim, evidence, and explanation instead of struggling to decode the sentence. Strong writers revise conventions because accurate language supports credible thinking.

Continued Notebook Application

Now apply conventions to an analytical sentence. First decide what the sentence means. Then revise the convention error. Finally, explain why the correction improves clarity.

Step 1: Identify the complete thought.

Step 2: Check sentence boundaries and punctuation.

Step 3: Check agreement and capitalization.

Step 4: Explain how the correction helps the reader follow the reasoning.

Deeper Learning

At Grade 12, conventions are part of credibility. A reader may understand your general idea, but repeated errors can weaken trust in your reasoning. Correct conventions make your claim, evidence, and explanation easier to evaluate.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: When conventions are controlled, the audience can focus on reasoning, instead of decoding errors.

Correct example: When conventions are controlled, the audience can focus on reasoning instead of decoding errors.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect sentence places an unnecessary comma before "instead of." The correction keeps the phrase attached to the idea it completes, which improves flow and clarity.
