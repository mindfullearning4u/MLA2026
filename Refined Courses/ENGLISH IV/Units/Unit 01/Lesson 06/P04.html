Embedded Passage

Passage: Community Feedback and Decision Making

Before changing the schedule for a public study center, the planning team gathered feedback from students, tutors, and local employers. The comments did not produce complete consensus, but they revealed a shared concern: evening access mattered for students who worked during the day. The team recommended extending hours twice a week and reviewing attendance data after three months. This recommendation was credible because it responded to stakeholder concerns, used a limited trial period, and included a plan for evaluating results.

Example 1: Written Explanation

Modeled thinking: I need vocabulary that accurately describes the groups affected by the decision.

Response: The recommendation is credible because it responds to stakeholder concerns from students, tutors, and employers.

Reasoning: Stakeholder is precise because these groups are affected by the study center schedule.

Example 2: Spoken Explanation

Modeled thinking: For speech, I should keep the sentence direct but still academic.

Oral version: The plan is credible because it responds to stakeholder concerns and uses attendance data to check whether the change works.

Reasoning: The oral version is clear, focused, and easy to follow.

Example 3: Paraphrase with Vocabulary

Original idea: The comments did not produce complete consensus, but they revealed a shared concern.

Paraphrase: Although the feedback did not show full agreement, it did show that several groups were concerned about evening access.

Reasoning: The paraphrase preserves the limitation on consensus and explains the shared concern.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: The recommendation was consensus because attendance data would be checked.

Correct example: The recommendation was credible because it included a plan to evaluate attendance data.

Teachable explanation: Consensus means agreement. Credible means trustworthy. The correct sentence uses the word that matches the evidence.
