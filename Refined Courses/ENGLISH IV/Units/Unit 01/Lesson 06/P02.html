ENGLISH IV | Unit 01 | Lesson 06

P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Academic Vocabulary for Speaking and Writing

Vocabulary

  • Stakeholder: a person or group affected by a decision.
  • Consensus: general agreement among people or groups.
  • Recommendation: a suggested course of action.
  • Rationale: the reason that explains a choice or recommendation.
  • Coherent: logically organized and easy to follow.
  • Credible: trustworthy and believable.

Embedded Passage

Passage: Community Feedback and Decision Making

Before changing the schedule for a public study center, the planning team gathered feedback from students, tutors, and local employers. The comments did not produce complete consensus, but they revealed a shared concern: evening access mattered for students who worked during the day. The team recommended extending hours twice a week and reviewing attendance data after three months. This recommendation was credible because it responded to stakeholder concerns, used a limited trial period, and included a plan for evaluating results.

Detailed Teaching

Step 1: Identify key academic words in the passage: stakeholder, consensus, recommendation, credible, and evaluating.

Step 2: Match each word to its function. Stakeholder names affected groups. Consensus describes agreement. Recommendation names the proposed action. Credible evaluates trustworthiness.

Step 3: Use the words in a written explanation before using them in speech.

Step 4: Prepare an oral version that is coherent: claim first, evidence second, rationale third.

Notebook Directions and Model

  1. Write the notebook title and vocabulary.
  2. Paraphrase the passage's recommendation.
  3. Write one sentence explaining why the recommendation is credible.
  4. Convert your sentence into a brief spoken explanation.

Model: The team recommended testing extended evening hours twice a week and reviewing attendance after three months. This recommendation is credible because it addresses stakeholder concerns while using data to evaluate whether the change works.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Academic vocabulary in speaking and writing

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.V.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 12 thinking process explicit so you can evaluate, explain, and apply the skill independently.

  1. Read the word or phrase in context before deciding meaning. Advanced texts often use familiar words in specialized or layered ways.
  2. Use etymology, derivations, foreign phrases, and reference materials when context alone is not enough.
  3. Separate denotation from connotation and tone. A precise word may shape the reader's attitude as much as it communicates a definition.
  4. Choose the meaning or wording that best fits purpose, audience, genre, and rhetorical effect.
