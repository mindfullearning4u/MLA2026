P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Embedded Passage

Passage: Libraries as Learning Spaces

A modern library is no longer only a storage place for printed books. In many communities, the library functions as a learning center where people attend workshops, use technology, meet with tutors, and access quiet study space. This expanded role does not make books less important; instead, it shows that reading, research, and community learning now support one another. A strong library program balances access to texts with access to guidance, tools, and collaborative spaces. When a library removes barriers to information and support, it strengthens independent learning.

Continued Notebook Application

Now revise an analytical response. Revision is not only fixing commas. Revision means improving purpose, clarity, structure, and style so the reader can follow your analysis.

Step 1: Check the purpose. Does the response explain a claim?

Step 2: Check clarity. Can a reader identify the paraphrased source idea?

Step 3: Check structure. Does the response move from claim to evidence to reasoning?

Step 4: Check style. Does the response use academic vocabulary without sounding vague or inflated?

Deeper Learning

Grade 12 analytical writing should evaluate the function of a source idea. Do not simply say what the passage says. Explain what that idea does in the passage. For example, the library passage shifts the reader from an older view of libraries as book storage to a broader view of libraries as learning support systems.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example: Libraries are good because books and computers are there.

Correct example: The passage presents libraries as learning centers because they combine access to texts with guidance, technology, and collaborative study spaces.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect example is vague and does not analyze the passage. The correct example paraphrases the source idea accurately and explains the function of the library's expanded role.
