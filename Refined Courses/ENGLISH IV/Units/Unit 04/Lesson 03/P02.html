P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Synthesizing Research Notes into a Claim

Vocabulary

  • Synthesis: combining information from multiple sources to explain a larger idea.
  • Paraphrase: restating source information accurately in your own words.
  • Pattern: a repeated idea or relationship across sources.
  • Tension: a meaningful difference between sources that needs explanation.
  • Claim: a position or interpretation that can be supported with evidence.

Teach the Concept

Research synthesis is not the same as a source list. A source list says, "Source A says this. Source B says this. Source C says this." Synthesis asks a more advanced question: "What do these sources show when I read them together?"

To synthesize, move through four steps. First, understand each source accurately. Second, paraphrase each source without changing its meaning. Third, group sources by what they have in common or where they disagree. Fourth, write a sentence that explains the relationship among the sources.

In Grade 12 writing, synthesis matters because college and workplace readers expect you to do more than collect information. They expect you to interpret information, notice patterns, and use evidence to support a focused argument.

Embedded Source Notes

Research question: How do flexible workplace schedules affect employee productivity and communication?

Source Note A: A workplace survey found that employees with flexible schedules reported fewer interruptions during deep-work tasks.

Source Note B: A management report warned that teams using flexible schedules sometimes had slower response times when expectations for communication were unclear.

Source Note C: A productivity study found that flexible schedules were most effective when teams used shared calendars and planned collaboration windows.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Step 1: Identify the issue. The issue is not whether flexible schedules are simply good or bad. The issue is how they affect productivity and communication.

Step 2: Paraphrase each source. Source A suggests flexible schedules can reduce interruptions. Source B warns that communication can slow down. Source C explains that planning tools help make flexibility more effective.

Step 3: Find the relationship. These sources create a balanced pattern: flexibility may improve focused work, but it requires deliberate communication systems.

Step 4: Write a synthesis statement. Model: The sources suggest that flexible schedules can improve productivity when they reduce interruptions, but they work best when teams also establish clear communication routines.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, copy the research question and write three labeled sections: Source A, Source B, and Source C. Paraphrase each source note in one sentence. Then write one synthesis statement that explains what the sources show together.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Synthesizing research for argument and analysis

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.RS.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 12 thinking process explicit so you can evaluate, explain, and apply the skill independently.

  1. Begin by defining the topical issue, research question, or analytical claim. Grade 12 research must stay focused enough to support in-depth reasoning.
  2. Separate facts, perspectives, claims, and source judgments. Do not treat every source as equally useful or equally credible.
  3. Synthesize by explaining relationships among sources: agreement, disagreement, emphasis, limitation, or implication.
  4. Use evidence to build a defensible conclusion, then explain why the conclusion matters for the topic, argument, or analysis.
