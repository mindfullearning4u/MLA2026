ENGLISH IV | Unit 04 | Lesson 03

P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identify the Evidence Pattern

Source notes: One source says community gardens increase access to fresh produce. Another source says gardens require volunteers and water access. A third source says neighborhoods with garden programs often report stronger community participation.

Modeled thinking: I should not write three separate sentences and stop. I need to ask what the notes show together. Two notes show benefits, and one note explains a condition needed for success.

Reasoning: The pattern is conditional benefit: gardens may help communities, but they need resources and organization.

Example 2: Write the Synthesis

Model synthesis: The sources suggest that community gardens can improve food access and neighborhood connection, but their success depends on steady volunteer support and basic resources.

Modeled thinking: This sentence combines all three source notes. It does not exaggerate. It also uses the transition "but" to show the tension between benefit and challenge.

Reasoning: A synthesis should be accurate, balanced, and useful for a future claim.

Example 3: Move from Synthesis to Claim

Model claim: Cities should support community gardens when they can also provide water access, volunteer coordination, and long-term maintenance planning.

Modeled thinking: The claim does not simply say "gardens are good." It uses the synthesis to make a specific argument with conditions.

Reasoning: Strong Grade 12 argument writing grows out of research. The claim reflects both the benefits and the limits found in the sources.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Community gardens are always successful because they help people.

Correct: Community gardens can benefit neighborhoods when cities plan for water access, volunteer coordination, and maintenance.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect statement overstates the evidence and ignores the source that identifies practical limits. The correct version makes an arguable claim that remains faithful to the research synthesis.
