ENGLISH IV | Unit 04 | Lesson 03

P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Synthesizing Research for Argument and Analysis

This lesson teaches you how to move from separate research notes to a clear synthesis that can support an in-depth argument or analysis.

Standards Covered

  • Primary: MLA.ENG4.RS.01 - Conduct Topical Research
  • Support: MLA.ENG4.R.10 - Paraphrase Accurately
  • Support: MLA.ENG4.W.02 - Write In-Depth Arguments with Evidence
  • Support: MLA.ENG4.L.01 - Use Standard English Conventions

What You Will Learn

  • How synthesis differs from listing sources one at a time.
  • How to group source information by shared ideas, tensions, and evidence patterns.
  • How to paraphrase research accurately before using it in a claim.
  • How sentence structure and conventions help readers follow complex evidence.

What You Will Do

  • Read short source notes about a topical issue.
  • Sort evidence into categories that reveal a larger pattern.
  • Write a synthesis statement that connects more than one source.
  • Use the synthesis to prepare for argument writing.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Identify source relationships instead of treating sources as isolated facts.
  • Paraphrase research information without changing the meaning.
  • Write a synthesis statement that could support a reasoned claim.
  • Complete Guided Practice, the checkpoint, and the Lesson Quiz.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

You are learning how research becomes reasoning. Instead of dropping facts into a paragraph, you will connect sources so the evidence helps explain an issue and support a strong academic claim.
