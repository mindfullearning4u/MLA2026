P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Evaluate the Claim

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Weak claim: Schools should do something about career preparation.

Improved claim: Schools should offer short career-readiness workshops for seniors because targeted sessions can help students practice interviews, workplace email, and application language before graduation.

Modeled thinking: The improved claim is specific. It names the action, the audience, and the reason. It can be supported with evidence.

Example 2: Connect Evidence to Reasoning

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Evidence: A counselor report found that students who completed mock interviews were more likely to revise resumes and prepare specific examples for interview questions.

Reasoning: This evidence supports career-readiness workshops because it shows that structured practice leads students to improve the exact materials and communication skills they need beyond high school.

Modeled thinking: The reasoning explains why the evidence matters. It does not simply repeat the evidence.

Example 3: Check Validity

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Question: Does the evidence prove that every student will get a job?

Answer: No. The evidence supports a more limited claim: workshops can improve preparation. A valid argument should not promise results the evidence cannot prove.

Modeled thinking: I need to keep the claim aligned with the evidence. This makes the argument more credible.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Career workshops guarantee that students will succeed after graduation.

Correct: Career workshops can strengthen seniors' preparation by giving them structured practice with interviews, resumes, and workplace communication.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect claim overpromises. The correct claim stays within what the evidence can reasonably support.
