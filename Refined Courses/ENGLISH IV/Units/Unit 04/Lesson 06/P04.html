Example 2: Tone

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Evidence: "She did not call the meeting a failure. Not yet."

Modeled thinking: The short sentence "Not yet" creates a tone of restraint and possibility.

Reasoning: The tone prevents the scene from becoming simply hopeless. It suggests that Maya is evaluating the setback carefully.

Example 3: Voice

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Evidence: "A failed vote, she thought, was sometimes only the first draft of a better argument."

Modeled thinking: Maya's voice connects civic action to revision. She sounds persistent and analytical.

Reasoning: This voice gives the passage a mature perspective on failure as part of a thinking process.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Maya is in a room after a vote, and the room has chairs and lights.

Correct: The physical details of the emptied room create a restrained tone, while Maya's final thought shows a voice that treats failure as revision.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect response identifies details but does not analyze function. The correct response explains how those details shape tone and voice.
