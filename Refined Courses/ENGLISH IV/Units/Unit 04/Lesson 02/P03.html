P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now you will evaluate how sources work together. A source can be useful even if it does not answer the whole research question by itself.

Embedded Sources

Research Question: How should schools evaluate student use of AI writing tools?

Source A: Teacher Survey Most teachers surveyed said AI tools can help students brainstorm, but they were concerned about essays submitted without student revision.

Source B: Student Handbook Draft The draft allows AI brainstorming but requires students to document when they use digital writing support.

Source C: Opinion Column The column calls AI writing "a shortcut that hides real thinking" and argues for strict limits on student use.

Deeper Learning

Source A and Source B are more balanced because they distinguish support from dishonesty. Source C uses stronger negative diction, especially "shortcut" and "hides real thinking." That wording reveals concern, but it may not fully address responsible uses such as brainstorming.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Step 1: Identify each source's contribution.

Step 2: Notice agreement: all sources recognize that AI affects student writing.

Step 3: Notice disagreement: sources differ on whether controlled AI support should be allowed.

Step 4: Synthesize: schools may need policies that distinguish acceptable support from replacement of student thinking.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Source C is the only useful source because it has the strongest opinion.

Correct: Source C is useful for understanding a strict-limit perspective, but Sources A and B add evidence about practical policy distinctions.

Why this matters: Strong research does not choose the loudest source. It compares what each source contributes and what each source leaves out.
