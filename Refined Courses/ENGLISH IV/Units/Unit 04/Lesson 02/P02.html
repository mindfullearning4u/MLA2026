P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Comparing Sources for Research

In your notebook, you will track what each source contributes to a research question.

Vocabulary

  • Source variety: Using different kinds of sources, such as reports, articles, interviews, data, or expert commentary.
  • Synthesis: Combining information from more than one source to build a fuller understanding.
  • Perspective: A source's point of view or angle on an issue.
  • Evidence gap: Information still missing after reviewing a source.
  • Connotation: The emotional or cultural meaning a word carries beyond its literal definition.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the research question. The question decides which sources are useful.

Step 2: Identify each source type. A data report may show patterns, while an interview may show lived experience.

Step 3: Identify each source's claim or main contribution. Ask what new information the source adds.

Step 4: Compare sources. Look for agreement, disagreement, emphasis, and missing information.

Step 5: Synthesize. Write a statement that explains what the sources together suggest.

Embedded Sources

Research Question: How do evening library hours affect adult learners who work during the day?

Source A: Library Attendance Report Evening computer lab attendance increased by 28 percent during a six-week pilot. The largest increase occurred between 6:00 p.m. and 7:30 p.m.

Source B: Adult Learner Interview One adult learner said evening access made it possible to finish job training modules after work because daytime appointments were impossible.

Source C: Budget Memo The pilot required one additional staff member for two evenings each week and used existing computer lab space.

Model Example

Source A shows a measurable increase in attendance. Source B explains why evening access matters to at least one adult learner. Source C adds feasibility information. Together, the sources suggest that evening library hours may support adult learners and can be tested with limited staffing.

Notebook Directions

Create a chart with three columns: source, contribution, and limitation. Then write one synthesis sentence using all three sources.
