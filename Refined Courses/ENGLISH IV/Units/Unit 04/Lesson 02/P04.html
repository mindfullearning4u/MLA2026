P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identify Source Contribution

Source: A data report shows evening lab use increased during a pilot program.

Contribution: The source provides measurable evidence of demand.

Reasoning: Data helps show whether a need is repeated, not just isolated.

Example 2: Identify Source Limitation

Source: One interview explains why an adult learner needs evening access.

Limitation: One interview does not prove how many people share the same need.

Reasoning: The interview is useful for human impact, but it should be paired with broader evidence.

Example 3: Synthesize Sources

Synthesis: The data report shows demand for evening access, while the interview explains why that access matters to adult learners. Together, the sources suggest that evening library hours may address both measurable demand and real scheduling barriers.

Reasoning: This synthesis combines sources instead of summarizing them separately.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Source A says attendance increased. Source B says one learner needed evening access.

Correct: Source A shows measurable demand, while Source B explains the human reason behind that demand; together they support the value of evening access.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version lists sources. The correct version synthesizes by explaining the relationship between sources.
