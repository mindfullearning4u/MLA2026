P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

From Topic to Research Question

In your notebook, you will practice turning a general issue into a question that can guide research.

Vocabulary

  • Topical issue: A current problem or question people discuss, study, or debate.
  • Research question: A focused question that can be answered with evidence from sources.
  • Inquiry focus: The specific direction your research will take.
  • Scope: The size or boundaries of a research question.
  • Paraphrase: A restatement of source information in your own words that keeps the original meaning accurate.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Start with a topical issue. A topic such as public transportation, school safety, or artificial intelligence is only a starting point.

Step 2: Identify a problem or tension. Ask what people disagree about or what decision needs evidence.

Step 3: Narrow the focus. Limit the topic by place, group, cause, effect, policy, or solution.

Step 4: Write a researchable question. A researchable question cannot be answered with only yes, no, or personal opinion.

Step 5: Test the question. Ask whether sources could provide evidence to answer it.

Embedded Source Note

Source Note: A city report states that bus delays increased most often during afternoon transfer periods at three major stations. The report recommends studying schedule spacing, driver availability, and rider demand before changing routes.

Model Example

Too broad: Why is public transportation important?

Focused and researchable: How do afternoon transfer delays at major bus stations affect workers who rely on connecting routes?

Why it works: The second question names a specific issue, group, and evidence focus.

Notebook Directions

Write the topical issue, the problem or tension, two possible research questions, and one accurate paraphrase of the source note.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Topical issue research questions and inquiry focus

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.RS.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 12 thinking process explicit so you can evaluate, explain, and apply the skill independently.

  1. Begin by defining the topical issue, research question, or analytical claim. Grade 12 research must stay focused enough to support in-depth reasoning.
  2. Separate facts, perspectives, claims, and source judgments. Do not treat every source as equally useful or equally credible.
  3. Synthesize by explaining relationships among sources: agreement, disagreement, emphasis, limitation, or implication.
  4. Use evidence to build a defensible conclusion, then explain why the conclusion matters for the topic, argument, or analysis.
