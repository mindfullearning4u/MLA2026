P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now you will evaluate research questions. Strong research questions are focused enough to investigate but open enough to require evidence and reasoning.

Embedded Issue Brief

Issue Brief: Several high schools are considering whether to limit student phone use during instructional time. Supporters argue that phones distract students and reduce participation. Opponents argue that students need phones for family communication, work responsibilities, and digital access. Some schools have tested phone lockers, while others have used classroom-level phone policies.

Deeper Learning

A research question should not decide the answer before research begins. "Why are phone lockers the best policy?" is biased because it assumes one solution is best. "How do phone locker policies affect participation and student access during instructional time?" is stronger because it invites evidence from more than one side.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Step 1: Identify the issue: student phone use during class.

Step 2: Identify tension: distraction versus access and communication.

Step 3: Narrow the focus: phone lockers, classroom policies, participation, access, or family communication.

Step 4: Write a balanced research question that does not assume an answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Why should all schools ban phones because phones are bad?

Correct: How do phone restriction policies affect student participation and student access during instructional time?

Why this matters: The incorrect question is biased and already assumes the answer. The correct question is focused, researchable, and open to evidence.
