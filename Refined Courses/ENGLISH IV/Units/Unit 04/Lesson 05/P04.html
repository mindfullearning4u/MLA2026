ENGLISH IV | Unit 04 | Lesson 05

P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Shallow Elaboration

Claim: Schools should offer financial literacy modules for seniors.

Evidence: Many seniors could define credit but struggled to compare interest rates and repayment timelines.

Shallow explanation: This is important because financial literacy is important.

Modeled thinking: This explanation is weak because it repeats the idea of importance without explaining the evidence.

Example 2: Developed Elaboration

Improved explanation: The evidence shows that students may know financial vocabulary without being ready to evaluate actual financial choices. Comparing interest rates and repayment timelines requires applied reasoning, not memorization. A required module would give seniors practice with decisions they may soon face independently.

Modeled thinking: This version explains context, significance, and consequence. It shows thorough subject understanding.

Example 3: Revision for Structure

Original order: Students need help. The survey showed a gap. Schools should require modules.

Revised order: Schools should require a short financial literacy module for seniors. A district survey showed that many seniors could define credit but struggled to compare interest rates, fees, and repayment timelines. This gap matters because students may soon make independent financial decisions that require comparing real costs, not just knowing terms.

Reasoning: The revised order moves from claim to evidence to elaboration, so the reader can follow the argument.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The evidence is good and proves my claim because it says students need financial literacy.

Correct: The evidence supports the claim because it reveals a practical gap between knowing financial terms and using those terms to evaluate real repayment costs.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect response uses vague praise. The correct response explains the exact function of the evidence.
