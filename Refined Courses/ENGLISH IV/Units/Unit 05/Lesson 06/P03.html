Deeper Learning: Transitions Show Logic

Transitions are not decorations. They tell the reader how one idea relates to another. However signals contrast. For example signals evidence. Therefore signals a conclusion. If the transition does not match the relationship, the paragraph becomes confusing.

Embedded Passage

Draft: The author uses historical evidence to support the claim. However, the evidence shows the pattern has continued across several decades.

Problem: However signals contrast, but the second sentence supports the first sentence. The transition creates the wrong relationship.

Revision: The author uses historical evidence to support the claim. In fact, the evidence shows the pattern has continued across several decades.

Notebook Application

  1. Explain why however is inaccurate in the draft.
  2. Explain why in fact works better.
  3. Write another transition that could fit the revised sentence.
  4. Explain what relationship your transition shows.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author uses evidence. However, the evidence supports the claim.

Correct: The author uses evidence. Specifically, the evidence supports the claim by showing a repeated pattern.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect transition signals contrast when the ideas actually connect. The correct transition signals explanation and makes the paragraph more coherent.
