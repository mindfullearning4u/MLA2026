P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Main Idea First

Draft Order: Evidence appears first, then the paragraph announces the claim.

Revision Strategy: Move the main idea to the beginning so the reader knows how to interpret the evidence.

Reasoning: Evidence is easier to understand when the reader knows the point it supports.

Example 2: Evidence Before Explanation

Draft Order: The paragraph explains what the evidence means before presenting the evidence.

Revision Strategy: Present the evidence first, then explain how it supports the claim.

Reasoning: Readers need to see the evidence before they can evaluate the explanation.

Example 3: Transition Precision

Draft: The study includes a large sample. However, the results are more reliable.

Revision: The study includes a large sample. Therefore, the results are more reliable.

Reasoning: The second sentence is a result, not a contrast. Therefore signals the correct relationship.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Add a transition word anywhere to make the paragraph organized.

Correct: Choose a transition that accurately shows the relationship between ideas.

Teachable explanation: Organization depends on logic, not just transition words. A transition that signals the wrong relationship makes writing less clear.
