P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Revision for Structure and Organization

Vocabulary

  • Structure: the way ideas are arranged in writing.
  • Organization: the logical order and grouping of ideas.
  • Transition: a word, phrase, or sentence that shows how ideas connect.
  • Coherence: the quality of ideas fitting together clearly.
  • Topic sentence: a sentence that introduces the main idea of a paragraph.

Step 1: Identify the Paragraph Purpose

Before revising organization, decide what the paragraph is supposed to do. A paragraph might define a problem, compare two ideas, defend a claim, analyze a text, or explain a cause-and-effect relationship.

The best order depends on the purpose. A paragraph defending a claim usually moves from claim, to evidence, to explanation. A paragraph explaining a process usually moves in sequence.

Step 2: Check the Order of Ideas

Read each sentence and ask what job it does. Does it introduce, support, explain, contrast, or conclude? If a sentence explains evidence before the evidence appears, the reader may become lost. If a transition says therefore but the previous sentence does not give a reason, the organization is weak.

Embedded Paragraph

Disorganized Draft: This evidence shows the policy could help families. The report found that childcare costs prevent many parents from working full time. Affordable childcare is essential to workforce stability. Therefore, the policy deserves serious consideration.

Improved Organization: Affordable childcare is essential to workforce stability. The report found that childcare costs prevent many parents from working full time. This evidence shows the policy could help families remain employed. Therefore, the policy deserves serious consideration.

Teacher-like thinking: The improved version starts with the main idea, gives evidence, explains the evidence, and then draws a conclusion.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the disorganized draft and improved organization.
  2. Label each sentence as main idea, evidence, explanation, or conclusion.
  3. Explain why the improved order is easier to follow.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Revision for structure and organization

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.W.04

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 12 thinking process explicit so you can evaluate, explain, and apply the skill independently.

  1. Read the draft for purpose first. Decide what the writing is supposed to prove, explain, evaluate, or communicate.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: reasoning, organization, evidence, transitions, sentence control, style, and conventions.
  3. Keep the original purpose while strengthening clarity and authority. Revision should make the thinking easier to follow, not replace it with a different message.
  4. Check whether the final version sounds polished, precise, audience-aware, and ready for academic or workplace communication.
