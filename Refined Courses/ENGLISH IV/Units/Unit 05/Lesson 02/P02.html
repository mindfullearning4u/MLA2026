P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Using Word History to Build Meaning

Vocabulary

  • Etymology: the history and origin of a word.
  • Derivation: the way a word is formed from another word or root.
  • Root: the base meaning part of a word.
  • Foreign phrase: a phrase borrowed from another language and used in English.
  • Context: the surrounding words and ideas that help determine meaning.

Teach the Concept

Many academic English words come from Latin and Greek roots. Knowing common roots helps you make reasonable meaning decisions even when a word is new. For example, the root cred relates to belief. That root appears in credible, credibility, incredible, and credulous.

Derivations help you see word families. If you know that scribe means write, then describe, transcript, inscription, and prescribe all become easier to analyze.

Foreign phrases also appear in academic and professional English. A phrase such as status quo means the existing state of things. A phrase such as ad hoc means created for a specific purpose or situation.

Embedded Sentence Set

Sentence 1: The committee questioned the credibility of the report because several claims lacked evidence.

Sentence 2: The team formed an ad hoc group to solve the scheduling problem before the deadline.

Sentence 3: The proposal challenged the status quo by asking the school to revise a long-standing policy.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Step 1: Identify the word or phrase. In Sentence 1, the key word is credibility.

Step 2: Use word history. The root cred relates to belief, so credibility concerns whether something can be believed or trusted.

Step 3: Use context. The report lacked evidence, so the committee questioned whether it was trustworthy.

Step 4: Paraphrase accurately. The committee doubted whether the report could be trusted because some claims were unsupported.

Notebook Directions

Choose two words or phrases from the sentence set. For each one, write the meaning, the clue you used, and an accurate paraphrase of the sentence.
