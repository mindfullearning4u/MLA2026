Notebook Title

Unit 5 Synthesis: Language, Vocabulary, Style, and Revision

Vocabulary Review

  • Convention: a standard language rule that helps readers understand meaning.
  • Etymology: the history or origin of a word.
  • Connotation: the feeling or association connected to a word.
  • Revision: improving writing for purpose, clarity, organization, or style.
  • Polish: final preparation for an intended reader.

Synthesis Step 1: Read for Meaning

Before revising, determine what the sentence or paragraph means. Check vocabulary, context, connotation, and sentence structure. If you misread the meaning, your revision may accidentally change the writer's purpose.

Synthesis Step 2: Revise for the Reader

After you understand the meaning, revise for the reader. Ask: Is the sentence conventional? Is the word choice precise? Is the order logical? Is the style appropriate for the audience? Does the final version preserve the original meaning?

Embedded Writing Sample

Draft: The source is good it talks about costs and it shows useful stuff.

Integrated Revision: The source is useful because it provides cost data that helps readers evaluate the proposal's financial impact.

Teacher-like thinking: The revision fixes the sentence boundary, replaces vague language, clarifies purpose, and preserves the original meaning.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the draft and integrated revision.
  2. Identify one convention improvement.
  3. Identify one vocabulary improvement.
  4. Explain how the revision improves purpose and clarity.
