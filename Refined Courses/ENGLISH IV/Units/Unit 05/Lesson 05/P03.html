P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Learning: Feedback Becomes an Action

Strong revision turns feedback into an action. Feedback such as be clearer is not an action yet. An action would be: define the claim, name the evidence, explain the connection, or replace vague wording with precise language.

Before revising, write the action you will take. This prevents random edits that do not solve the real problem.

Embedded Writing Sample

Original: The poem's tone is important because the speaker feels things strongly.

Feedback: Identify the tone and connect it to the speaker's word choice.

Revision: The poem's restrained tone reveals the speaker's grief more powerfully because the careful word choice suggests emotion being controlled rather than openly expressed.

Step-by-step explanation: The revision names the tone, identifies the effect, and explains how word choice creates meaning.

Notebook Application

  1. Write the action needed to respond to the feedback.
  2. Identify the vague phrase in the original sentence.
  3. Explain why the revision is clearer.
  4. Write one additional revision that improves clarity without changing the meaning.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The poem's tone is very important and the speaker has strong feelings.

Correct: The poem's restrained tone reveals controlled grief because the speaker's careful word choice keeps emotion beneath the surface.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect revision repeats the same vague idea. The correct revision names the tone, explains the feeling, and connects the effect to word choice.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Feedback and revision for purpose and clarity

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.W.04

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your reasoning is specific, defensible, and mature enough for Grade 12 mastery.

  1. Read the draft for purpose first. Decide what the writing is supposed to prove, explain, evaluate, or communicate.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: reasoning, organization, evidence, transitions, sentence control, style, and conventions.
  3. Keep the original purpose while strengthening clarity and authority. Revision should make the thinking easier to follow, not replace it with a different message.
  4. Check whether the final version sounds polished, precise, audience-aware, and ready for academic or workplace communication.
