P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Revision for Purpose and Clarity

Vocabulary

  • Revision: improving writing by changing ideas, organization, clarity, or language.
  • Purpose: what the writing is trying to accomplish.
  • Clarity: how easy it is for the reader to understand the meaning.
  • Feedback: information that helps the writer improve the work.
  • Precision: exactness in word choice, reasoning, and explanation.

Step 1: Read Feedback for the Real Problem

Feedback often points to a problem beneath the surface. If feedback says, clarify your claim, the issue may not be grammar. The real problem may be that the reader cannot tell what position or interpretation you are defending.

Ask three questions: What is confusing? What should the reader understand? What change would make that understanding easier?

Step 2: Revise Purpose Before Polish

Do not begin revision by changing a few words at random. First, decide whether the sentence or paragraph accomplishes its purpose. In an argument, the purpose may be to defend a claim. In a complex text analysis, the purpose may be to explain how an author's choice creates meaning.

Once the purpose is clear, then revise wording and conventions.

Embedded Writing Sample

Original: The article is effective because it talks about evidence and the author uses examples.

Feedback: Clarify the purpose of the sentence. What kind of effectiveness are you evaluating?

Revision: The article is effective because its evidence links the proposed policy to measurable public benefits, which makes the author's recommendation more credible.

Teacher-like thinking: The revision does not simply sound better. It explains what the evidence does and why that matters.

Notebook Directions

Copy the original, feedback, and revision. Then answer:

  1. What was unclear in the original sentence?
  2. How did the revision clarify purpose?
  3. Which words in the revision make the reasoning more precise?

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this skill feels new, begin by naming the topic, defining the key vocabulary in your own words, and finding one sentence or detail that clearly connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete each step in order: identify the skill, locate evidence, explain the connection, and check that your response answers the prompt directly.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by adding a more precise word choice, a second relevant detail, or a clearer explanation of why the evidence matters.
