Example 1: Revising a Claim

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Original: The author makes a good argument about technology.

Feedback: Clarify the claim and the specific issue.

Revision: The author makes a convincing argument that workplace technology should increase efficiency without reducing employee privacy.

Reasoning: The revision names the topic, the position, and the tension being evaluated.

Example 2: Revising Evidence Explanation

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Original: This quote proves the point because it is strong.

Feedback: Explain how the evidence supports the point.

Revision: This evidence supports the point because it shows that the policy's benefits are measurable, not merely promised.

Reasoning: The revision explains the connection between the evidence and the claim.

Example 3: Revising for Conventions and Clarity

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Original: The writer uses data it makes the claim credible.

Feedback: Correct the sentence boundary and clarify the relationship.

Revision: The writer uses data, which makes the claim more credible.

Reasoning: The revision fixes the run-on sentence and shows the cause-effect relationship clearly.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author makes a good point, and it is clear and good.

Correct: The author makes a credible point because the evidence connects the policy to specific public benefits.

Teachable explanation: Repeating positive words does not improve clarity. The correct revision names what makes the point credible.
