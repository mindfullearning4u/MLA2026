P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Connotation

Sentence: The editorial called the reform necessary, but the opposition called it intrusive.

Modeled thinking: Both words refer to the same reform. Necessary suggests the reform is justified and needed. Intrusive suggests it crosses a boundary. The connotation changes the tone from practical approval to criticism.

Example 2: Tone

Sentence: The narrator describes the mansion as imposing rather than impressive.

Modeled thinking: Impressive usually suggests admiration. Imposing can suggest power, pressure, or intimidation. The writer's choice creates a tense tone instead of simple admiration.

Example 3: Figurative Meaning

Sentence: The candidate said the budget was a compass, not a cage.

Modeled thinking: The sentence uses two metaphors. A compass suggests guidance and direction. A cage suggests restriction. The contrast argues that the budget should guide decisions without trapping future choices.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Imposing and impressive mean the same thing, so either word would create the same tone.

Correct: The words overlap in denotation, but imposing suggests pressure or intimidation, while impressive suggests admiration.

Teachable explanation: Advanced vocabulary analysis requires attention to nuance. Words can share a basic meaning while producing different emotional and rhetorical effects.
