P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Deeper Learning: Precision in Paraphrase

Precise vocabulary matters when you paraphrase. A paraphrase should not simply replace words with rough synonyms. It must preserve the exact relationship among ideas. If the original says a change is gradual, a paraphrase that says it is minor may change the meaning because a gradual change can become major over time.

Embedded Passage

Passage: The court issued a provisional order while it reviewed the full record. The decision could be revised after additional evidence was submitted.

Reference-style entry: provisional: arranged for the present time but likely to change later; temporary; conditional.

Step-by-step explanation: The phrase could be revised confirms that provisional means temporary or conditional. The word does not mean careless or unimportant. It means the order is current but not final.

Notebook Application

  1. Define provisional using the context and reference entry.
  2. Identify one context clue that supports your definition.
  3. Write an accurate paraphrase of the passage.
  4. Explain why the word temporary is closer than careless.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Provisional means weak, so the court's order does not matter.

Correct: Provisional means temporary or conditional. The order matters now, but it may change after more evidence is reviewed.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer adds a judgment that the passage does not support. The correct answer uses the reference entry and the sentence clue could be revised.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Word relationships, reference materials, and precise meaning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.V.03

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your reasoning is specific, defensible, and mature enough for Grade 12 mastery.

  1. Read the word or phrase in context before deciding meaning. Advanced texts often use familiar words in specialized or layered ways.
  2. Use etymology, derivations, foreign phrases, and reference materials when context alone is not enough.
  3. Separate denotation from connotation and tone. A precise word may shape the reader's attitude as much as it communicates a definition.
  4. Choose the meaning or wording that best fits purpose, audience, genre, and rhetorical effect.
