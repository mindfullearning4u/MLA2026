ENGLISH IV | Unit 05 | Lesson 01

P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Comma and Coordinating Conjunction

Original: The report identified several delays, it did not explain their causes.

Revision: The report identified several delays, but it did not explain their causes.

Reasoning: The word "but" shows contrast and fixes the comma splice.

Example 2: Semicolon

Original: The evidence is relevant, the reasoning is incomplete.

Revision: The evidence is relevant; the reasoning is incomplete.

Reasoning: A semicolon can join two complete, closely related sentences without a coordinating conjunction.

Example 3: Colon for Explanation

Original: The paragraph needs one major revision, the evidence must be explained.

Revision: The paragraph needs one major revision: the evidence must be explained.

Reasoning: The colon introduces the specific explanation of the revision needed.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The evidence is relevant; because it directly supports the claim.

Correct: The evidence is relevant because it directly supports the claim.

Teachable explanation: A semicolon must join two complete sentences. "Because it directly supports the claim" is a dependent clause, so the semicolon is incorrect.
