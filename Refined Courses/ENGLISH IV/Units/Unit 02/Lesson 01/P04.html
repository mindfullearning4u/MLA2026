P04 Worked Example

Embedded Passage

Mara returned to the harbor after sunset, carrying the lantern her grandfather had left on the kitchen table. The town had installed electric lights along the pier, but she still lit the old lantern before walking to the water. Its flame shook in the wind, small but stubborn, while the new lamps shone with a clean, steady glare. At the edge of the dock, Mara held the lantern toward the dark bay. She knew no ship needed its light anymore. Still, the glow warmed her hands, and for the first time that week, the silence beside her felt less like absence and more like a place where memory could stand.

Example 1: Symbol

Question: What is the functional significance of the lantern?

Step 1: Identify the element: the lantern.

Step 2: Paraphrase the evidence: Mara lights the lantern even though ships no longer need it.

Step 3: Explain function: the lantern shows that memory can still have emotional purpose after practical purpose is gone.

Example 2: Contrast

Question: How does the contrast between the lantern and electric lights add meaning?

Step 1: The new lights are steady and practical.

Step 2: The lantern is old, small, and unstable.

Step 3: The contrast shows that usefulness is not the only kind of value. The lantern matters because it carries emotional history.

Example 3: Setting and Mood

Question: What does the harbor setting do?

Step 1: The harbor is quiet, dark, and connected to the past.

Step 2: Mara stands at the edge of the dock after sunset.

Step 3: The setting creates a reflective mood and gives Mara a physical place to confront loss and memory.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The setting is a harbor, and harbors have water.

Correct: The harbor setting functions as a threshold between past and present because Mara stands at the edge of the dock holding an old family object in a changed modern place.

Feedback: The incorrect response identifies a literal fact. The correct response explains how the setting shapes interpretation.
