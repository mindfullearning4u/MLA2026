P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Evaluating Figurative Language

Vocabulary

  • Figurative language: language that means more than its literal words.
  • Metaphor: a direct comparison that describes one thing as another.
  • Personification: giving human qualities to something nonhuman.
  • Connotation: the emotional or suggested meaning of a word.

Embedded Passage: "After the Storm"

By morning, the city wore a coat of broken branches. Rainwater whispered along the curb, carrying leaves like small, exhausted boats. In the park, the oldest oak lifted one split limb toward the pale sky, not in defeat but in accusation. People stepped around the puddles quietly, as if the storm had taken the street's voice and left everyone listening for what it would say next.

Detailed Teaching: Step by Step

Step 1: Identify the figurative phrase. "The city wore a coat of broken branches" is a metaphor.

Step 2: Explain literal meaning. The storm left broken branches across the city.

Step 3: Explain figurative effect. The city seems like a person covered by damage, which makes the destruction feel intimate and visible.

Step 4: Evaluate effectiveness. The metaphor is effective because it turns storm damage into an image of burden and aftermath, not just debris.

Notebook Directions

Create a chart with three columns: Figurative Phrase, Literal Meaning, and Effect on Interpretation. Add at least three phrases from the passage.

Model Example

Phrase: rainwater whispered

Literal meaning: water moved quietly along the curb.

Effect: The personification makes the scene feel hushed and watchful after the storm.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this skill feels new, begin by naming the topic, defining the key vocabulary in your own words, and finding one sentence or detail that clearly connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete each step in order: identify the skill, locate evidence, explain the connection, and check that your response answers the prompt directly.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by adding a more precise word choice, a second relevant detail, or a clearer explanation of why the evidence matters.
