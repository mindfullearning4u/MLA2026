ENGLISH IV | Unit 02 | Lesson 05

P04 Worked Example

Embedded Passage

By morning, the city wore a coat of broken branches. Rainwater whispered along the curb, carrying leaves like small, exhausted boats. In the park, the oldest oak lifted one split limb toward the pale sky, not in defeat but in accusation. People stepped around the puddles quietly, as if the storm had taken the street's voice and left everyone listening for what it would say next.

Example 1: Metaphor

Phrase: the city wore a coat of broken branches.

Meaning: branches covered the city after the storm.

Effect: The metaphor makes the damage feel like a heavy covering the city must carry.

Example 2: Simile

Phrase: leaves like small, exhausted boats.

Meaning: leaves float in water after the storm.

Effect: The simile makes the leaves seem worn down, extending the mood of exhaustion.

Example 3: Personification

Phrase: the storm had taken the street's voice.

Meaning: the street is unusually quiet after the storm.

Effect: The personification makes the silence feel unnatural and meaningful.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The leaves are boats.

Correct: The leaves are compared to exhausted boats, which makes the aftermath of the storm feel drained and weary.

Feedback: Figurative language compares or suggests; it does not usually make a literal claim.
