P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Tracking Character Perspective

Vocabulary

  • Perspective: a character's way of seeing, interpreting, or responding to a situation.
  • Conflicting perspectives: different viewpoints that create tension or disagreement.
  • Development: the way a character's view changes, deepens, or becomes clearer.
  • Evaluate: judge the effect or significance of a perspective.

Embedded Passage: "The Orchard Decision"

Nadia pressed her palm against the old orchard gate while her brother Tomas unfolded the developer's letter. The trees behind them were bent and hollow in places, but in spring they still filled the road with white blossoms. "The offer pays every debt," Tomas said. "It gives us a future." Nadia looked past him to the rows their grandmother had planted after arriving with two suitcases and no English. "It gives us money," she answered. "That is not the same thing." Tomas folded the letter carefully, as if neatness could make the decision merciful. "Grandmother wanted us safe," he said. Nadia opened the gate and stepped inside. "She also wanted us rooted."

Detailed Teaching: Step by Step

Step 1: Identify the situation. Nadia and Tomas must decide whether to sell the orchard.

Step 2: Identify each perspective. Tomas sees the sale as safety and financial relief. Nadia sees the orchard as family history and belonging.

Step 3: Find evidence. Tomas says the offer pays every debt and gives them a future. Nadia says money is not the same as a future and connects the orchard to their grandmother.

Step 4: Evaluate conflict. The conflict matters because both characters are partly right. Tomas values survival; Nadia values identity. The passage becomes more complex because it does not make either perspective simple or foolish.

Notebook Directions

Create a chart with four columns: Character, Perspective, Evidence, and Why It Matters. Complete one row for Nadia and one row for Tomas.

Model Example

Character: Tomas

Perspective: Selling the orchard is practical because it would pay debts and create security.

Why it matters: Tomas's perspective prevents the conflict from becoming only sentimental. He forces readers to consider the real pressure of survival.
