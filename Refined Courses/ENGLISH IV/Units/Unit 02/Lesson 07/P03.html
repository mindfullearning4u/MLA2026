ENGLISH IV | Unit 02 | Lesson 07

P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now you will move from planning to drafting. A complex narrative should not explain the theme like an essay. Instead, it should let the reader infer the theme from what characters notice, say, choose, and revise in their thinking.

Step 1: Choose a point of view. Decide whether the reader will see the conflict through one character or more than one character.

Step 2: Select a meaningful moment. Do not write an entire life story. Choose one scene where the conflict becomes visible.

Step 3: Use concrete details. Show the room, object, gesture, silence, or line of dialogue that reveals perspective.

Step 4: Show change or recognition. A complex narrative usually includes a shift in understanding, even if the problem is not fully solved.

Step 5: Revise for clarity. Remove details that do not help perspective, conflict, or theme.

Embedded Drafting Passage

Draft passage: Mara stood in the hallway with the real estate folder pressed against her coat. Behind her, Daniel opened the kitchen cabinet and lifted the chipped blue mug their mother had used every morning. "It's just a house," Mara said, but her voice sounded too sharp in the empty room. Daniel did not answer. He turned the mug slowly, as if the answer might be printed on the bottom.

This passage reveals perspective through action and dialogue. Mara wants distance from pain. Daniel wants contact with memory. The chipped mug becomes a concrete detail that carries emotional meaning.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy one sentence from the drafting passage that reveals Mara's perspective.
  2. Copy one detail that reveals Daniel's perspective.
  3. Explain how the mug functions as more than an object.
  4. Write two original sentences that could continue the scene and reveal a universal theme.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The story is about a house, and the theme is that houses are important.

Correct: The house becomes a source of conflict because Mara and Daniel attach different meanings to it; this develops a universal theme about how grief can shape memory in different ways.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect response stays at the surface level. It names the object but does not explain perspective or theme. The correct response explains the function of the object, connects it to conflicting perspectives, and identifies a broader human insight.
