P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Build a Narrative Premise

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Prompt: Write a premise for a narrative about a universal theme.

Step 1: Choose a human concern: loyalty.

Step 2: Create a situation that pressures that concern: A student must decide whether to reveal that a friend used someone else's work.

Step 3: Add perspective: The narrator values loyalty but also values honesty.

Step 4: State the theme direction: Real loyalty may require telling the truth, even when truth creates conflict.

Modeled thinking: I am not starting with a moral statement alone. I am building a situation where a character must discover the meaning of the theme through conflict.

Example 2: Add Multiple Perspectives

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Premise: A student must decide whether to report a friend's academic dishonesty.

Perspective A: The narrator believes reporting the friend will feel like betrayal.

Perspective B: The teacher believes silence protects unfairness.

Perspective C: The friend believes pressure excuses the mistake.

Step-by-step reasoning: Multiple perspectives make the narrative complex because no character sees the whole issue in the same way. The writer can reveal conflict through dialogue, hesitation, and consequences instead of simply announcing the lesson.

Example 3: Revise for Theme

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Weak draft: I told the teacher because cheating is wrong.

Revision: I paused outside the classroom with my hand on the cold metal handle, realizing that protecting my friend from consequences might also protect the lie that was already hurting both of us.

Step 1: Add a concrete detail: the cold metal handle.

Step 2: Add internal conflict: protecting a friend versus protecting a lie.

Step 3: Let the theme emerge: honesty can be painful but necessary.

Modeled thinking: The revision is stronger because it dramatizes the theme through action and reflection.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The theme is honesty. The character is honest. Then the story ends.

Correct: The narrative should place the character in a situation where honesty conflicts with another value, such as loyalty, fear, or belonging, so the theme develops through pressure and choice.

Teachable explanation: Naming a theme is not the same as developing it. A complex narrative creates a situation where the theme becomes visible through conflict, perspective, and consequence.
