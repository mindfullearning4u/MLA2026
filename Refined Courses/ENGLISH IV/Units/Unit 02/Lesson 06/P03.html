P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Embedded Texts

Classic context: In Homer's The Odyssey, Odysseus spends years trying to return home after war. The epic uses a journey pattern involving obstacles, temptation, disguise, recognition, and the question of what home means after long absence.

Contemporary passage: After three years working overseas, Amara landed before dawn in the city where she had grown up. She expected the streets to recognize her, but new towers had risen where the market used to lean into the river. At her mother's apartment, the lock had been changed for safety, and Amara stood in the hallway with a suitcase and a passport full of stamps. She almost laughed at the old story she had loved as a child, the one where the wanderer returned and the house knew him. Her mother opened the door, studied her face, and said, "You have brought the distance with you." Only then did Amara understand that coming home was not the end of the journey but the last test of it.

Deeper Learning

Influence is not the same as copying. A contemporary text can borrow a classic pattern and change its meaning. In the classic pattern, homecoming often tests whether the hero can reclaim a place. In Amara's story, homecoming tests whether she can accept that home and self have both changed.

Use this frame: The contemporary text is influenced by ___ because ___. It transforms the classic pattern by ___.

Notebook Application

Write three sentences explaining one classic influence and one transformation in the contemporary passage. Include accurate paraphrase of both the classic context and the modern passage.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The texts are the same because both are about someone going home.

Correct: The contemporary passage is influenced by the classic homecoming pattern, but it transforms the journey into a modern test of changed identity and belonging.

Why this matters: Influence analysis must explain both similarity and transformation.
