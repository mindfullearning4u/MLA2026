P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Before the Unit Assessment, practice deciding which skill a question requires. If a question asks what an object does in the passage, think functional significance. If it asks how a larger idea grows, think theme development. If it asks how a character understands a situation, think perspective. If it asks how an older pattern appears in a newer text, think influence.

Step 1: Read the question stem first.

Step 2: Identify the skill being tested.

Step 3: Return to the passage for evidence.

Step 4: Choose the answer that explains effect, not just identification.

Step 5: Eliminate answers that are true but too shallow.

Embedded Practice Passage

Passage: "The Unsent Letter"

Oren folded the letter for the third time, though there was no envelope on the desk. Outside, the city celebrated the treaty with bells and lanterns, but his room remained dark except for the square of moonlight on the floor. He had written, I was brave, then crossed it out. He had written, I survived, then crossed that out too. At last he wrote, I am still learning what courage cost.

Notebook Directions

  1. Identify Oren's perspective on courage.
  2. Explain how the crossed-out sentences develop theme.
  3. Explain the contrast between the celebrating city and the dark room.
  4. Write one assessment-style answer that explains function, not just meaning.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The crossed-out words show that Oren writes a letter.

Correct: The crossed-out words show Oren's changing perspective; he rejects simple claims about bravery and survival before recognizing that courage has lasting emotional costs.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer identifies an action but does not explain function. The correct answer explains how the action develops perspective and theme.
