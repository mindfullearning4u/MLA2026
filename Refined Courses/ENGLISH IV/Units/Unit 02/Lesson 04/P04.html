ENGLISH IV | Unit 02 | Lesson 04

P04 Worked Example

Embedded Passage

Context: Emily Dickinson was a 19th-century American poet known for compressed language, unconventional punctuation, and poems that explore abstract ideas through concrete images from nature, faith, death, and inner life.

Excerpt: "Hope" is the thing with feathers -
That perches in the soul -
And sings the tune without the words -
And never stops - at all -

Example 1: Metaphor

Step 1: Identify the choice: hope is compared to a feathered thing.

Step 2: Explain meaning: hope becomes alive, light, and persistent.

Step 3: Add context: Dickinson often uses concrete images to explore inner experience, so the metaphor is central rather than decorative.

Example 2: Theme

Step 1: Identify the theme: hope persists inwardly.

Step 2: Use evidence: the song never stops.

Step 3: Evaluate: the poem develops hope as quiet endurance rather than loud confidence.

Example 3: Historical Context

Step 1: Identify context: Dickinson is a 19th-century poet known for compressed and unconventional style.

Step 2: Connect context to form: the short lines and dashes make the thought feel concentrated.

Step 3: Evaluate: the form supports the poem's intense focus on inward resilience.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The bird is important because Dickinson liked nature.

Correct: The bird metaphor matters because Dickinson uses a concrete natural image to make hope feel inward, alive, and persistent.

Feedback: The incorrect response makes a broad assumption. The correct response connects poetic choice, meaning, and context.
