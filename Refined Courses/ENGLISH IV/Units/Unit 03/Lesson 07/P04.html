P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Evaluate Structure

Question: How does the structure strengthen the cooling-center passage?

Modeled thinking: The passage begins with a problem, gives evidence, offers a solution, and acknowledges long-term needs.

Reasoning: This structure is effective because it guides decision-makers from need to action without pretending the short-term solution fixes every problem.

Example 2: Evaluate Evidence and Reasoning

Question: Why is the heat-index evidence useful?

Modeled thinking: The heat index and emergency-call details show that the problem is documented and urgent.

Reasoning: The evidence supports the claim because it connects the proposed cooling centers to a real public safety need.

Example 3: Evaluate Rhetoric

Question: How does cautious diction affect the passage?

Modeled thinking: Words such as "temporary," "targeted," and "longer-term" show that the author is not overstating the plan.

Reasoning: This rhetoric is effective because it builds credibility with readers who may worry about cost or scope.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The passage is strong because it has facts.

Correct: The passage is strong because its facts are relevant, its problem-solution structure is clear, and its cautious rhetoric fits a policy audience.

Teachable explanation: A complete evaluation explains how specific features work together. Naming facts without explaining relevance is too shallow for Grade 12 analysis.
