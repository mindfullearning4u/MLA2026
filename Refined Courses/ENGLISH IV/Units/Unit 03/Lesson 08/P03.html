Embedded Passage

Passage: Emergency Text Alerts

The county should modernize its emergency alert system by allowing residents to receive text notifications in multiple languages. Recent storms showed that warnings do not help if residents do not receive or understand them quickly. Text alerts can reach people who are away from television or radio, and translated messages can reduce confusion during evacuation decisions. The county should begin with the five most commonly requested languages and review usage after the next storm season.

Deeper Learning

A strong synthesis response makes a judgment. For example: "The passage is effective for county leaders because it connects a public safety problem to a limited, measurable solution." That sentence is stronger than "The passage is about emergency alerts" because it evaluates purpose, audience, and effectiveness.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Step 1: The claim is that the county should modernize emergency alerts.

Step 2: The support connects warnings to speed, access, and understanding.

Step 3: The rhetoric frames the issue as practical public safety.

Step 4: The limited rollout makes the proposal more manageable.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The passage is good because alerts are important.

Correct: The passage is effective because it links emergency alerts to specific access problems and proposes a limited multilingual rollout that county leaders could measure.

Why this matters: The incorrect example gives an opinion about the topic. The correct example evaluates how the argument works.
