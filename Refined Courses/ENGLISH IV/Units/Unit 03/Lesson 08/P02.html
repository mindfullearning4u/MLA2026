Notebook Title

Unit 3 Synthesis Review

This notebook task prepares you to connect the main Unit 3 reading skills.

Vocabulary

  • Text structure: The way information is organized.
  • Central idea: The main point developed across a text.
  • Author purpose: The reason the author wrote the text.
  • Related argument: An argument connected to another argument by topic, claim, or evidence.
  • Rhetorical choice: A deliberate choice used to influence the audience.

Step-by-Step Synthesis Process

Step 1: Read for the central idea and claim.

Step 2: Identify structure and how it guides the reader.

Step 3: Evaluate whether the support is relevant and sufficient.

Step 4: Identify the author's purpose and intended audience.

Step 5: Evaluate rhetorical choices and explain their effect.

Embedded Passage

Passage: Community Internet Access

The city should create evening internet access hours at neighborhood centers. Many job applications, training modules, medical forms, and college documents now require reliable internet access. Residents without dependable service at home often complete these tasks on phones or delay them entirely. The city already owns buildings with secure wireless networks, so extending evening access two nights each week would use existing resources before creating a larger program. A three-month pilot would help officials measure demand and adjust staffing responsibly.

Model Example

The passage uses a problem-solution structure to argue for evening internet access. Its evidence connects internet access to employment, health care, and education tasks. The cautious rhetoric, especially "pilot" and "measure demand," makes the plan seem practical for city officials.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, identify the central idea, structure, evidence, purpose, audience, and one rhetorical choice from the passage. Write one sentence for each category.
