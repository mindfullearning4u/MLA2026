P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identify the Rhetorical Choice

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Text: "No one should have to stand in the dark and wonder whether the bus stop is safe."

Step 1: The sentence uses direct, universal language: "No one should have to."

Step 2: It frames lighting as a safety and dignity issue, not only a technical concern.

Step 3: The effect is emotional and inclusive because the wording invites readers to imagine themselves or people they know in the situation.

Example 2: Connect Choice to Purpose

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Text: "City transit records show that most rider complaints occur after 8:00 p.m."

Step 1: The author uses documented records.

Step 2: This choice supports the purpose of persuading decision-makers who need evidence before spending money.

Step 3: The choice is effective because it turns a general concern into a measurable problem with a clear planning focus.

Example 3: Compare Effectiveness Across Texts

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Question: Which text is more effective for a city budget committee?

Step 1: Identify the audience. A budget committee needs evidence, priorities, and implementation details.

Step 2: Text A provides records, complaint times, and a targeted plan.

Step 3: Text B creates urgency, but it offers less detail about implementation.

Conclusion: Text A is more effective for a budget committee because its rhetorical choices match the audience's need for practical evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Text A uses facts, so it is automatically more effective than Text B.

Correct: Text A is more effective for a planning audience because its facts support a targeted solution. Text B may be more effective for a public audience because its inclusive diction builds urgency and shared responsibility.

Teachable explanation: A rhetorical choice is effective when it fits the purpose and audience. Facts are not automatically stronger than emotional appeals. The best evaluation explains the situation in which each choice works.
