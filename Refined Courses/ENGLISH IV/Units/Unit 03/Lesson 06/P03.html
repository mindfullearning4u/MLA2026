ENGLISH IV | Unit 03 | Lesson 06

P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now you will move from identifying rhetorical choices to judging their effectiveness. Effectiveness depends on purpose and audience. A dramatic tone may work well in a public appeal, but it may weaken a formal policy memo if it replaces evidence.

Embedded Texts

Text A: Transit Safety Report

City transit records show that most rider complaints occur after 8:00 p.m. at stops with limited lighting. The transportation department should prioritize lighting upgrades at the ten highest-complaint stops before expanding service hours. This targeted plan responds to documented safety concerns without delaying the larger transit improvement schedule.

Text B: Community Speech

No one should have to stand in the dark and wonder whether the bus stop is safe. Parents, workers, and students deserve a transit system that respects their daily lives. Better lighting is not merely a technical upgrade. It is a promise that public transportation belongs to everyone, including the people who ride after sunset.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Step 1: Both texts support better lighting at bus stops.

Step 2: Text A uses a practical, evidence-based frame. It names records, complaint times, and a targeted plan.

Step 3: Text B uses inclusive and emotional diction, including "parents, workers, and students" and "belongs to everyone."

Step 4: Text A is more effective for budget or planning audiences because it shows feasibility. Text B is more effective for community audiences because it builds shared concern.

Step 5: A strong evaluation explains both effectiveness and limitation. Text B creates urgency, but Text A gives decision-makers a clearer implementation path.

Notebook Directions

Write one paragraph comparing the rhetorical choices in Text A and Text B. Include one choice from each text, explain the effect of each choice, and judge which text is more effective for its likely audience.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Text B is better because it sounds stronger and cares more about people.

Correct: Text B may be more effective for a community audience because its inclusive diction connects the safety issue to parents, workers, and students. Text A may be more effective for city planners because its complaint data and targeted plan show a practical path to action.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect response makes a personal preference judgment. The correct response evaluates rhetorical effectiveness by connecting diction, audience, purpose, and evidence.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Rhetorical choices across multiple texts

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.R.12

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your reasoning is specific, defensible, and mature enough for Grade 12 mastery.

  1. Identify the claim, audience, purpose, and context before evaluating effectiveness. A choice is effective only in relation to what the writer is trying to accomplish.
  2. Separate evidence from reasoning. Evidence shows what the author uses; reasoning explains how the author connects evidence to the claim.
  3. Evaluate validity by asking whether the reasoning is logical, the evidence is relevant, and the conclusion follows from the support.
  4. Compare effects across texts when needed. Grade 12 analysis should explain how different authors use similar information or rhetorical tools for different ends.
