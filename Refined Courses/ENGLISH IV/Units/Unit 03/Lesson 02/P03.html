P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Effective support usually has three qualities: it is relevant, specific, and explained. A weak detail may sound interesting but not actually develop the central idea. A strong detail helps the reader understand the central idea more fully.

Step 1: Separate the central idea from minor details.

Step 2: Ask whether each detail directly supports the central idea.

Step 3: Identify whether the author explains the importance of the detail.

Step 4: Decide what type of support would improve the text: data, expert testimony, example, comparison, or explanation.

Step 5: Explain your recommendation clearly.

Embedded Passage for Evaluation

Passage: "Public Reading Programs"

Public reading programs can help communities build stronger literacy habits. Libraries often host summer reading challenges, family reading nights, and book discussion groups. These programs can make reading feel social instead of isolated. One library director said the programs were "popular with many families." The passage would be stronger if it included participation numbers, reading-growth data, or examples of how families changed their reading routines after attending.

Notebook Directions

  1. State the central idea.
  2. Identify the weakest piece of support.
  3. Explain why that support is limited.
  4. Recommend one stronger type of support.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The quote is strong support because someone said it.

Correct: The quote is limited support because "popular with many families" is vague; participation data or specific examples would show the program's effect more clearly.

Teachable explanation: A source statement is not automatically strong. It must be specific enough and clearly connected to the central idea.
