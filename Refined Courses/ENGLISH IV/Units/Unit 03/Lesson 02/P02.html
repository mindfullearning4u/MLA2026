P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Evaluating Central Idea Support

Vocabulary

  • Central idea: The main point an informational text develops.
  • Support: Evidence, examples, explanation, data, or reasoning that develops the central idea.
  • Relevant: Directly connected to the central idea.
  • Sufficient: Enough to make the point convincing or clear.
  • Elaboration: Explanation that shows how evidence supports the central idea.

Detailed Teaching: What Makes Support Effective?

Support is effective when it clearly develops the central idea. A detail can be true but still weak if it is not relevant, specific, or explained. Evaluation means judging the quality of support, not just counting details.

Step 1: State the central idea in your own words.

Step 2: Identify the support the author uses.

Step 3: Test relevance. Ask: Does this support directly connect to the central idea?

Step 4: Test sufficiency. Ask: Is there enough support for the reader to understand or accept the idea?

Step 5: Test elaboration. Ask: Does the author explain how the support proves the idea?

Embedded Passage

Passage: "Community Repair Cafes"

Community repair cafes can reduce waste while helping residents learn practical skills. At these events, volunteers help people repair small appliances, clothing, bicycles, and electronics instead of throwing them away. One neighborhood repair cafe reported that residents fixed 68 percent of the items they brought during a six-month period. Participants also said they were more likely to try simple repairs at home after watching volunteers explain each step. Repair cafes do not solve every waste problem, but they can make reuse more visible, social, and practical.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the notebook title.
  2. Copy and define the five vocabulary terms.
  3. State the central idea of the passage in one sentence.
  4. List three pieces of support from the passage.
  5. Evaluate which support is strongest and explain why.

Model Example

The central idea is that repair cafes reduce waste and teach practical repair skills. The 68 percent repair statistic is strong support because it gives specific evidence that the events actually lead to repaired items, not just good intentions.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this skill feels new, begin by naming the topic, defining the key vocabulary in your own words, and finding one sentence or detail that clearly connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete each step in order: identify the skill, locate evidence, explain the connection, and check that your response answers the prompt directly.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by adding a more precise word choice, a second relevant detail, or a clearer explanation of why the evidence matters.
