P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identify Structure

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Passage focus: Digital appointment systems.

Step 1: The passage presents a new system.

Step 2: It explains benefits such as scheduling flexibility and reduced wait times.

Step 3: It explains limitations such as internet access and comfort with forms.

Step 4: It recommends a flexible solution.

Modeled thinking: This is a problem-solution structure with benefits and limitations. The organization helps the reader understand why a balanced recommendation is necessary.

Example 2: Evaluate a Feature

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Possible feature: A two-column chart labeled "Benefits" and "Limitations."

Step 1: The passage compares positive and negative effects.

Step 2: A chart would visually separate those effects.

Step 3: This would help readers scan the tradeoffs quickly.

Modeled answer: A two-column chart would make the passage more effective because it would clarify the contrast between efficiency benefits and access concerns.

Example 3: Recommend an Improvement

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Weak recommendation: The passage should be better.

Stronger recommendation: The passage should add a heading before the limitations section so readers can clearly distinguish the problems digital systems may create from the benefits they provide.

Step 1: Name the feature: heading.

Step 2: Name where it goes: before limitations.

Step 3: Explain its effect: clearer distinction between benefits and problems.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Add a picture because pictures are always good.

Correct: Add a chart comparing benefits and limitations because the passage asks readers to weigh both sides of digital scheduling.

Teachable explanation: A feature is effective only if it serves the text's purpose. The correct answer links the feature to the information readers need to compare.
