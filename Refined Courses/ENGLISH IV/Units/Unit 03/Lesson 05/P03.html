P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

When comparing arguments, do not simply summarize both. A comparison must explain how the arguments differ and evaluate the effect of those differences.

Step 1: Identify the shared issue.

Step 2: Identify the different claims.

Step 3: Compare evidence quality.

Step 4: Compare reasoning.

Step 5: Decide which argument is more convincing for a specific reason.

Embedded Paired Passages for Evaluation

Passage A: Cities should create more car-free downtown blocks because pedestrian areas can increase foot traffic for small businesses. One downtown district reported higher weekend sales after temporary car-free streets were tested.

Passage B: Cities should be cautious about car-free downtown blocks because delivery drivers, older residents, and people with disabilities may need vehicle access. Any plan should include accessible drop-off zones and delivery windows.

Notebook Directions

  1. Identify the shared issue.
  2. Explain how the claims differ.
  3. Identify one strong piece of evidence from each passage.
  4. Write one sentence judging which passage has more complete reasoning.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Passage A and Passage B are both about streets, so they are the same argument.

Correct: Both passages address car-free downtown blocks, but Passage A emphasizes business benefits while Passage B emphasizes access concerns and implementation limits.

Teachable explanation: Same topic does not mean same argument. You must compare claims, evidence, reasoning, and purpose.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Same information, rhetoric, and argument comparison

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.R.08

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your reasoning is specific, defensible, and mature enough for Grade 12 mastery.

  1. Identify the claim, audience, purpose, and context before evaluating effectiveness. A choice is effective only in relation to what the writer is trying to accomplish.
  2. Separate evidence from reasoning. Evidence shows what the author uses; reasoning explains how the author connects evidence to the claim.
  3. Evaluate validity by asking whether the reasoning is logical, the evidence is relevant, and the conclusion follows from the support.
  4. Compare effects across texts when needed. Grade 12 analysis should explain how different authors use similar information or rhetorical tools for different ends.
