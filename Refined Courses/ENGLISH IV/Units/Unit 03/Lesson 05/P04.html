ENGLISH IV | Unit 03 | Lesson 05

P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Compare Claims

Issue: Car-free downtown blocks.

Passage A claim: Cities should create more car-free blocks.

Passage B claim: Cities should be cautious and include access protections.

Modeled thinking: The claims are related but not identical. Passage A promotes expansion. Passage B limits or qualifies the policy.

Example 2: Compare Evidence

Passage A evidence: Weekend sales increased after a temporary test.

Passage B evidence: Delivery drivers, older residents, and people with disabilities may need access.

Modeled answer: Passage A uses measurable business evidence, while Passage B uses stakeholder-based reasoning. Passage B would be stronger with specific access data or examples from cities that used drop-off zones successfully.

Example 3: Judge Effectiveness

Question: Which argument is more complete?

Step 1: Passage A gives evidence for benefits.

Step 2: Passage B addresses limitations and implementation.

Step 3: A complete argument should consider both benefits and access concerns.

Modeled answer: Passage B may be more complete because it does not reject the policy entirely but explains conditions needed for fair implementation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Passage A is better because it sounds more positive.

Correct: Effectiveness depends on evidence and reasoning, not positivity. Passage A has measurable evidence, while Passage B has a stronger discussion of limitations and implementation.

Teachable explanation: Do not judge arguments by agreement or tone alone. Evaluate how well each argument supports its claim.
