ENGLISH IV | Unit 03 | Lesson 05

P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Comparing Related Arguments and Uses of Information

Vocabulary

  • Related arguments: Arguments that address the same issue or information but may reach different conclusions.
  • Claim: The position an argument asks readers to accept.
  • Reasoning: The explanation that connects evidence to a claim.
  • Rhetoric: Strategic language choices used to affect readers.
  • Comparative evaluation: A judgment about how two texts differ in effectiveness.

Detailed Teaching: How to Compare Related Arguments

Two arguments may discuss the same issue but use information differently. One may emphasize benefits. Another may emphasize risks. Your task is to compare how each author uses evidence and reasoning.

Step 1: Identify the issue both arguments address.

Step 2: State each claim separately.

Step 3: Identify evidence each author uses.

Step 4: Explain each author's reasoning.

Step 5: Judge which argument uses information more effectively and why.

Embedded Paired Passages

Passage A: Schools should allow phone lockers during class because students report fewer distractions when phones are out of reach. A pilot program found that teachers redirected students less often during lessons after lockers were introduced.

Passage B: Schools should avoid mandatory phone lockers because families may need to contact students quickly during urgent situations. A better policy would teach responsible use while allowing supervised access when necessary.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the notebook title and vocabulary.
  2. Identify the shared issue.
  3. State the claim in Passage A.
  4. State the claim in Passage B.
  5. Explain which argument would need more evidence to be fully convincing.

Model Example

Both passages address student phone access in school. Passage A argues for phone lockers by emphasizing reduced distraction. Passage B argues against mandatory lockers by emphasizing emergency communication and responsible use. Passage A has stronger direct evidence because it cites a pilot program, while Passage B would need evidence about how often urgent contact is necessary during class.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Same information, rhetoric, and argument comparison

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG4.R.08

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It makes the Grade 12 thinking process explicit so you can evaluate, explain, and apply the skill independently.

  1. Identify the claim, audience, purpose, and context before evaluating effectiveness. A choice is effective only in relation to what the writer is trying to accomplish.
  2. Separate evidence from reasoning. Evidence shows what the author uses; reasoning explains how the author connects evidence to the claim.
  3. Evaluate validity by asking whether the reasoning is logical, the evidence is relevant, and the conclusion follows from the support.
  4. Compare effects across texts when needed. Grade 12 analysis should explain how different authors use similar information or rhetorical tools for different ends.
