P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identify Purpose

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Question: What is the author's purpose in "Before the Storm"?

Step 1: The topic is emergency alerts.

Step 2: The author identifies a problem: general warnings may not help all people.

Step 3: The author recommends plain-language guides.

Modeled answer: The author's purpose is to persuade local agencies to make emergency guidance more specific and accessible before severe weather occurs.

Example 2: Evaluate an Author Choice

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Choice: The author lists a family in a mobile home, a college student in a dorm, and an older adult who cannot drive.

Step 1: The list shows different needs.

Step 2: It makes the problem concrete.

Step 3: It supports the call for specific guidance.

Modeled answer: The list is effective because it shows that one general warning may not fit different living situations, which strengthens the author's argument for clearer guidance.

Example 3: Explain Tone

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Tone: Practical and reassuring.

Evidence: "Clear preparation does not create panic. It gives people time to make safer choices."

Step 1: The author anticipates fear about panic.

Step 2: The author reframes preparation as safety.

Modeled answer: The calm tone is effective because it makes the recommendation sound reasonable rather than alarmist.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author uses examples because examples are required.

Correct: The author uses examples to show that different people need different emergency guidance, which supports the purpose of persuading agencies to provide clearer instructions.

Teachable explanation: Do not name a choice without explaining its function. The correct answer connects the choice to the author's purpose.
