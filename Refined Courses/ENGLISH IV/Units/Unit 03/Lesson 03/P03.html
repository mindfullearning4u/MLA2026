P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Author purpose is often more precise than one word. "To inform" may be true, but it may not be enough. Ask what the author wants readers to understand or do after reading.

Step 1: Name the broad purpose.

Step 2: Make it more specific.

Step 3: Connect the purpose to one author choice.

Step 4: Explain how the choice affects the reader.

Embedded Passage for Evaluation

Passage: "Before the Storm"

Emergency alerts are most useful when people understand them before a crisis begins. A warning that says "seek shelter" may be too general for a family living in a mobile home, a college student in a dorm, or an older adult who cannot drive. Local agencies should publish plain-language guides that explain where to go, what to bring, and how to get help before severe weather arrives. Clear preparation does not create panic. It gives people time to make safer choices.

Notebook Directions

  1. State the author's specific purpose.
  2. Identify one example the author uses.
  3. Explain how the example supports the purpose.
  4. Evaluate whether the final two sentences are effective.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author's purpose is weather.

Correct: The author's purpose is to persuade local agencies to provide clear, specific emergency guidance before severe weather occurs.

Teachable explanation: Weather is the topic, not the purpose. The purpose explains what the author wants readers or agencies to understand or do.
