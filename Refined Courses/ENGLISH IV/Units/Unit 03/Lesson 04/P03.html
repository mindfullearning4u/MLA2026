P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Validity depends on the connection between evidence and claim. Evidence can be true but still not prove the claim. Reasoning explains why the evidence matters.

Step 1: Find the claim.

Step 2: Find the evidence.

Step 3: Ask whether the evidence is relevant.

Step 4: Ask whether the evidence is enough.

Step 5: Explain any gap in reasoning.

Embedded Argument for Evaluation

Passage: "Longer Lunch Periods"

Schools should extend lunch periods by ten minutes. Students often rush through meals, and some throw away food because they do not have enough time to eat. A student survey at one school found that 61 percent of respondents wanted a longer lunch period. Longer lunch periods may also give students a needed mental break before afternoon classes. However, the argument would be stronger with evidence about nutrition, schedule effects, or whether longer lunches improve focus.

Notebook Directions

  1. Identify the claim.
  2. Identify the strongest evidence.
  3. Identify one limitation in the argument.
  4. Explain what additional evidence would improve validity.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The argument is valid because many students want longer lunch.

Correct: The survey is relevant, but the argument needs additional evidence about nutrition, schedule effects, or focus to prove that longer lunch would solve the stated problem.

Teachable explanation: Popular preference can support an argument, but it does not automatically prove that the proposed solution works.
