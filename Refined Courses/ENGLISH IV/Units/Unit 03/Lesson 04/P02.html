P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Evaluating Claims, Evidence, Reasoning, and Validity

Vocabulary

  • Claim: The position or conclusion an argument asks readers to accept.
  • Evidence: Facts, data, examples, or expert information used to support a claim.
  • Reasoning: The explanation that connects evidence to a claim.
  • Validity: The logical strength of an argument's reasoning.
  • Counterclaim: A different or opposing claim that challenges the argument.

Detailed Teaching: How to Evaluate an Argument

An argument is more than an opinion. To evaluate it, you need to separate the parts and test whether they work together.

Step 1: Identify the claim. Ask: What does the author want readers to believe or do?

Step 2: Identify the evidence. Ask: What facts, examples, or data does the author use?

Step 3: Identify the reasoning. Ask: How does the author explain the connection between evidence and claim?

Step 4: Test validity. Ask: Does the evidence logically support the claim, or is there a gap?

Step 5: Consider related arguments. Ask: What other claim or counterclaim might address the same issue?

Embedded Argument Passage

Passage: "Transit Passes for Students"

City leaders should provide discounted transit passes for high school students. Many students rely on buses or trains to reach school, jobs, libraries, and after-school programs. When transportation costs are high, students may miss opportunities that support academic and career readiness. A pilot program in one district increased after-school program attendance after transit discounts were introduced. Discounted passes would not solve every access problem, but they would remove one practical barrier for many students.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy the notebook title.
  2. Copy and define the five vocabulary terms.
  3. Identify the claim in the passage.
  4. Identify two pieces of evidence.
  5. Explain whether the reasoning is valid and why.

Model Example

The claim is that city leaders should provide discounted transit passes for high school students. The pilot program is effective evidence because it shows a connection between transit discounts and increased attendance in after-school programs. The reasoning is mostly valid because reduced transportation costs could logically remove a barrier to participation.
