Earth Space Science Unit 2 Lesson 2: Surface Features and Surface Processes

P01 Lesson Overview
Lesson Title: Surface Features and Surface Processes
Lesson Purpose: Connect surface features to formative surface processes.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.ESS.EAR.02

Support Standards: MLA.ESS.LAB.02

What You Will Learn

  • Read Earth/space evidence connected to Surface Features and Surface Processes.
  • Use the required visual, data, map, model, or source to support a science claim.
  • Explain reasoning with accurate Earth/Space Science vocabulary.

What You Will Do

  1. Read the lesson pages in order.
  2. Use the direct resource and the embedded model or table to collect evidence.
  3. Complete notebook evidence before the checkpoint.
  4. Use TOR support when you need clarification after using the lesson supports.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by using evidence from USGS; NASA Earth Observatory; NOAA, explaining your reasoning step by step, and staying inside the lesson standard.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you think like an Earth/Space Science student: observe evidence, record data, compare the model to the claim, and explain what the evidence supports.

Safety boundary: Surface Features and Surface Processes. This lesson is completed with safe digital, map, model, image, source, or data evidence. Do not perform fieldwork, observe hazardous weather, view the Sun directly, handle unknown materials, or use special equipment unless the Teacher of Record assigns a separate approved safety-controlled activity.

Required Direct Resource

Open this exact resource: NASA Earth Observatory: Weathering and Erosion

Direct URL: https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/features/Weathering

Click the link above. After the page opens, follow these steps exactly:

  1. Click the link and use the weathering explanation. Observe how surface material changes. Record one surface feature, the process that forms or changes it, and the evidence shown on the page.
  2. Observe the model, map, graph, data table, image, or source section named in the directions.
  3. Record the required evidence in your notebook before answering questions.
  4. Use the recorded evidence to write a claim-evidence-reasoning explanation connected to the lesson standard.

Evidence check: Your notes must include what you observed, what data or source detail you recorded, and how that evidence supports the lesson idea.
