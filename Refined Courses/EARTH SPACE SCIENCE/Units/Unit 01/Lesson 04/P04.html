P04 Worked Examples: Data Patterns, Uncertainty, and Claims

MLA Standard: MLA.ESS.LAB.03

Lesson boundary: Focus on how data support claims and how uncertainty limits claims. Do not turn this into a full climate-change unit.

Required visual/data/model: data table, graph, pattern/uncertainty chart, and claim-evidence-reasoning organizer

Direct Resource Task

Resource: NOAA Climate Data Primer

  1. Open the exact linked resource in a new tab.
  2. Use only the section named in this lesson task; do not move to unrelated pages.
  3. Observe: how climate data are collected, organized, displayed, and interpreted across place and time
  4. Record: one pattern, one possible uncertainty or limitation, and one claim that would need additional evidence
  5. Return to this lesson and use your recorded evidence in the notebook response, guided practice, and checkpoint.

Worked Example 1

Question: A table shows rainfall for one city over seven days. Which claim is best supported?

Correct answer: Rainfall changed during that week in that city.

  1. Identify the evidence or model named in the question.
  2. Eliminate answers that overclaim, ignore units/scale/source, or move outside the lesson standard.
  3. Choose the answer that matches the evidence and explain why.

Teachable feedback: The claim must match the location and time span of the evidence.

Worked Example 2

Question: Which statement best describes uncertainty?

Worked Example 3

Question: A graph has temperature in degrees Celsius on the y-axis and years on the x-axis. What should be checked before making a claim?

Correct answer: The time span, units, pattern, and missing data.

  1. Identify the evidence or model named in the question.
  2. Eliminate answers that overclaim, ignore units/scale/source, or move outside the lesson standard.
  3. Choose the answer that matches the evidence and explain why.

Teachable feedback: A valid claim requires careful reading of variables, units, time span, and data quality.

Common Mistake

A common mistake is treating any graph line as proof of a broad claim. The correction is to ask what location, time span, variable, units, and missing data are represented.

Correction

Slow down and tie every answer to the evidence, representation rules, and lesson boundary.
