Notebook Title

Unit 1 Lesson 01: Earth/Space Science as Evidence

Vocabulary

TermStudent-friendly meaning
EvidenceInformation from an observation, image, model, map, graph, data table, or reliable source that supports a scientific claim.
ClaimA science conclusion that must be supported by evidence.
ReasoningThe explanation that connects the evidence to the claim.
Reliable sourceA source with clear authorship, scientific purpose, and evidence that can be checked.

Step-by-Step Teaching

  1. Earth/Space Science uses evidence because many Earth and space systems are too large, too old, too distant, or too dangerous to study by direct handling. A scientist cannot hold the whole atmosphere, reach the center of Earth, or travel across the universe for every question, so scientists use images, maps, data, models, and trusted source records.
  2. A strong claim does not begin with what sounds familiar. It begins with what the evidence actually shows. For example, if an image shows clouds over an ocean, the observation is that clouds are visible above water. The inference might be that water vapor and atmospheric conditions are interacting, but that inference must be stated as an explanation, not as something directly seen.
  3. Source reliability matters because a weak source can make a weak claim look convincing. In this course, a reliable Earth/Space Science source usually comes from an agency, university, scientific organization, or carefully documented textbook source. A source should identify what evidence was used and should not ask you to accept a claim without support.
