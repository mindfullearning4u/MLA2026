P04 Worked Examples: Earth/Space Science as Evidence

MLA Standard: MLA.ESS.SCI.01; MLA.ESS.SCI.02

Lesson boundary: Use evidence and reliable sources only. Do not turn this into a general astronomy, geology, or climate lesson.

Required visual/data/model: CER organizer, source reliability checklist, and Earth image evidence table

Direct Resource Task

Resource: NASA Earth Observatory: A Walk Through Time

  1. Open the exact linked resource in a new tab.
  2. Use only the section named in this lesson task; do not move to unrelated pages.
  3. Observe: the image caption, source organization, and what can be directly seen versus inferred
  4. Record: one direct observation, one inference, one source detail, and one claim that the evidence can support
  5. Return to this lesson and use your recorded evidence in the notebook response, guided practice, and checkpoint.

Worked Example 1

Question: A student sees a NASA image showing clouds over the ocean. Which statement is the best observation?

Correct answer: White clouds are visible over a blue ocean area.

  1. Identify the evidence or model named in the question.
  2. Eliminate answers that overclaim, ignore units/scale/source, or move outside the lesson standard.
  3. Choose the answer that matches the evidence and explain why.

Teachable feedback: The correct answer states only what is directly visible. The other choices make inferences or broad claims that need more evidence.

Worked Example 3

Question: A claim says, 'Earth systems interact.' Which evidence would best support that claim?

Correct answer: A diagram showing water evaporating from the ocean and clouds forming in the atmosphere.

  1. Identify the evidence or model named in the question.
  2. Eliminate answers that overclaim, ignore units/scale/source, or move outside the lesson standard.
  3. Choose the answer that matches the evidence and explain why.

Teachable feedback: The diagram directly connects two Earth systems and shows the interaction. A claim needs evidence that matches the relationship being argued.

Common Mistake

A common mistake is treating every statement from a website as evidence. The correction is to point to the specific image, caption, data, map, or source detail that proves the claim.

Correction

Slow down and tie every answer to the evidence, representation rules, and lesson boundary.

Teacher of Record support: Contact the Teacher of Record when you can identify the evidence but cannot explain how it supports the claim, model, data pattern, or Earth-system interaction. Bring your notes and the exact step where your reasoning became unclear.
