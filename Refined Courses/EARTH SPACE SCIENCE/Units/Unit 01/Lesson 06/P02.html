Notebook Title

Unit 1 Lesson 06: Observations, Inferences, and Models

Vocabulary

TermStudent-friendly meaning
ObservationWhat the evidence directly shows.
InferenceAn explanation based on observations and background knowledge.
TheoryA broad scientific explanation supported by many lines of evidence.
ModelA representation used to explain or predict a system.

Step-by-Step Teaching

  1. An observation comes first because it anchors the explanation. In an Earth image, 'white cloud bands are visible' is an observation. 'Air is moving water vapor through the atmosphere' is an inference because it explains what may be happening.
  2. A model is not a toy answer. A model is a tool that represents a real system so scientists can explain patterns or test ideas. Maps, diagrams, images, computer simulations, and physical models can all be scientific models.
  3. A theory is not a wild guess. In science, a theory is a well-supported explanation built from many observations, models, and investigations. A single image may support an inference, but it does not establish a full theory by itself.
