P04 Worked Examples: Earth Systems Investigation Design

MLA Standard: MLA.ESS.SCI.01; MLA.ESS.LAB.03

Lesson boundary: Design investigations using safe official data or provided data. Do not require unsafe outdoor data collection.

Required visual/data/model: investigation question planner, variables table, evidence-quality checklist, and data-source record

Direct Resource Task

Resource: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information

  1. Open the exact linked resource in a new tab.
  2. Use only the section named in this lesson task; do not move to unrelated pages.
  3. Observe: how official climate summaries organize variable, location, time period, and source information
  4. Record: one investigation question, independent variable, dependent variable, controlled condition, data source, and evidence limitation
  5. Return to this lesson and use your recorded evidence in the notebook response, guided practice, and checkpoint.

Worked Example 1

Question: Which investigation question is testable with climate data?

Correct answer: How did average temperature in City A change from January to June?

  1. Identify the evidence or model named in the question.
  2. Eliminate answers that overclaim, ignore units/scale/source, or move outside the lesson standard.
  3. Choose the answer that matches the evidence and explain why.

Teachable feedback: The correct question names a variable and time span that can be measured.

Common Mistake

A common mistake is writing a broad question before checking whether data exist. The correction is to choose a variable, location, time period, and source before finalizing the question.

Correction

Slow down and tie every answer to the evidence, representation rules, and lesson boundary.

Teacher of Record support: Contact the Teacher of Record when you can identify the evidence but cannot explain how it supports the claim, model, data pattern, or Earth-system interaction. Bring your notes and the exact step where your reasoning became unclear.
