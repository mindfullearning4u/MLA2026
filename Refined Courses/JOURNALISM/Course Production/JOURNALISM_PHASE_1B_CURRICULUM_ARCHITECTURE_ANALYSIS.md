# Phase 1B: Journalism Curriculum Architecture Analysis

## Executive Summary

This architecture analysis establishes the Journalism course framework using the approved English I-IV, Speech Communication, and Creative Writing production model and the verified CPALMS `Journalism 1 (#1006300)` standards record as the primary standards source.

The course preserves the MLA mastery model:

- 6 units
- 8 lessons per unit
- Lessons 1-7 contain Lesson Quiz
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together
- Lesson 8 contains Unit Assessment
- Lesson 8 introduces no new primary standard

This document is architecture only. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, GIFT files, quiz JSON files, lesson JSON files, LMS pages, or student-facing instructional content.

## Course Identity

Journalism is a factual communication and publication course. The verified CPALMS course description supports instruction in:

- production of journalism across print, multimedia, web, and broadcast/radio platforms
- journalism history
- journalism ethics
- management techniques related to journalistic media
- reporting, media literacy, source verification, audience awareness, and publication

The architecture is not English Literature, Creative Writing, Speech Communication, or Technical Writing. ELA reading/writing benchmarks are interpreted through journalism practices: news writing, factual communication, source verification, media literacy, editorial writing, and publication.

## Six-Unit Architecture

| Unit | Unit Title | Primary Architecture Purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Journalism, News Judgment, and Ethics | Establishes journalism identity, factual communication, media purpose, ethics, format expectations, and collaborative newsroom norms. |
| 2 | Reporting, Source Verification, and Interviewing | Builds research questions, source reliability, evidence, paraphrase, interviewing, active listening, and inference. |
| 3 | News, Feature, and Editorial Writing | Develops news/expository writing, narrative feature techniques, argument/editorial writing, tone, vocabulary, and revision. |
| 4 | Media Literacy, Rhetoric, and Audience | Focuses on text structures, central idea support, author purpose, opposing claims, rhetoric, media design trends, and audience effects. |
| 5 | Digital Journalism, Multimedia, and Publication | Builds digital presentation, visual accuracy, sequential media, online collaboration, publication-ready writing, and production management. |
| 6 | Newsroom Portfolio, Publication, and Multimedia Synthesis | Synthesizes reporting, ethics, media literacy, writing, digital production, and publication without new primary standards. |
