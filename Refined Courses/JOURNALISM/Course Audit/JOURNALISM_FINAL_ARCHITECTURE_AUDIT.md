# Journalism Final Architecture Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit reviewed the full Journalism curriculum architecture package before lesson production. The audit compared the package against the approved English I, English II, English III, English IV, Speech Communication, and Creative Writing architecture frameworks; verified the applicable Florida course standards through the CPALMS public course record for `Journalism 1 (#1006300)`; validated MLA Journalism standard numbering and domain assignment; checked crosswalk, unit-level mapping, and lesson-level mapping coverage; audited all 48 lesson mapping rows; and confirmed that the course preserves the required six-unit, eight-lesson structure.

Initial audit status: `PASS`

Final audit status: `PASS`

The package uses the verified CPALMS Journalism 1 standard list as the primary source. CPALMS lists 33 standards for the current Journalism 1 course version. The course identity is correctly framed as factual journalism production across print, multimedia, web, and broadcast/radio platforms, with emphasis on reporting, source verification, ethics, media literacy, audience communication, digital journalism, and publication.

## 3. Strengths

- The architecture preserves the approved MLA mastery model.
- The package uses an official CPALMS course record rather than assuming a generic journalism standard set.
- All 33 CPALMS-listed standards are included exactly once in the standard inventory and crosswalk.
- Journalism-specific domains make the course easier to audit while preserving MLA numbering rules.
- ELA standards are applied to factual reporting, source verification, media literacy, and publication rather than to English literature.
- Visual Art/media standards are applied to digital journalism, multimedia, visual accuracy, and media production.
- The unit sequence moves from foundations and ethics to reporting, journalistic writing, media literacy, digital publication, and newsroom synthesis.
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together in every unit, contains Unit Assessment only, and introduces no new primary standards.

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Journalism 1 includes ELA reading/writing benchmarks that could drift toward English I. | Medium | Architecture applies all ELA benchmarks to factual journalism, source verification, audience, media literacy, and publication. |
| Journalism 1 includes visual/media standards that could become a media arts course. | Medium | Mapping ties visual/media work to journalism production, accuracy, and publication. |
| Unit 3 can become overloaded because news, feature, editorial, revision, conventions, vocabulary, and presentation converge. | Medium | Writing modes are separated across lessons and synthesized only after instruction. |
| Unit 5 can become production-heavy. | Medium | Digital production follows reporting, writing, and media literacy foundations. |
| Unit 6 could drift into new instruction. | Low | Unit 6 synthesizes previously taught standards only. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

These recommendations do not block certification.

- During future lesson production, maintain a journalism product bank so news, feature, editorial, broadcast, digital, and multimedia products remain distinct.
- Maintain a source-verification audit before drafting Unit 2 and Unit 3 lessons.
- Keep publication standards attached to verified reporting and edited journalistic products.
- Keep oral presentation standards tied to reporting briefings and broadcast communication, not general speech production.
