P04 Worked Example

Media Literacy and Audience Check

Embedded Source

Media Literacy and Audience Check. Task: The newsroom is reviewing a media post about a proposed later start time for school. Package Parts: headline, short article, two statistics, a student quote, and a graphic showing students asleep at desks. Purpose: Explain the issue and help readers understand competing views. Concern: The headline says Everyone Wants Later Start Times, but the source notes show mixed opinions from students, families, and staff. Evidence: one survey shows 58 percent of students support the change, while staff comments mention transportation and activity schedule concerns. Publication Need: The package must use accurate purpose, fair claims, responsible rhetoric, and audience-aware media choices.

Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify the audience need.

Step 2: Find the part of the package that may not meet that need.

Step 3: Explain the correction.

Modeled thinking: I should not approve work because it exists. I should approve it because it works for the audience.

Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify the evidence or media choice.

Step 2: Ask whether it supports the central purpose.

Step 3: Decide whether to keep, revise, move, or replace it.

Explicit reasoning: A correction should make the piece clearer and more credible.

Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Locate the standard skill.

Step 2: Apply it to the embedded source.

Step 3: Justify the final decision with a complete explanation.

Modeled thinking: My answer needs evidence from the source, not just my opinion.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Approve the media package because the headline sounds powerful.

Correct: Evaluate whether the headline, structure, evidence, purpose, and audience appeal work together responsibly.

Teachable explanation: Journalism revision and media checks are not cosmetic. They protect meaning, fairness, credibility, and audience trust.
