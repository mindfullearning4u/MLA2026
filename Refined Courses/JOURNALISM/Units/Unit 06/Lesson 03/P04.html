P04 Worked Example

Writing Package Revision

Embedded Source

Writing Package Revision. Task: The student newsroom is preparing a final article package about the spring club fair. Draft Problem: The article begins with a strong quote, but the second paragraph jumps to a list of clubs without explaining why the fair matters. Feedback: The editor says the article needs clearer purpose, stronger transitions, one sentence of evidence from attendance numbers, and corrected capitalization for club names. Available Evidence: last year had 18 clubs; this year has 27 clubs; student council says the fair was moved to the gym so more students could attend. Publication Need: The article must be clear, cohesive, accurate, and convention-ready before it appears in the final portfolio.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify the audience need.

Step 2: Find the part of the package that may not meet that need.

Step 3: Explain the correction.

Modeled thinking: I should not approve work because it exists. I should approve it because it works for the audience.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify the evidence or media choice.

Step 2: Ask whether it supports the central purpose.

Step 3: Decide whether to keep, revise, move, or replace it.

Explicit reasoning: A correction should make the piece clearer and more credible.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Locate the standard skill.

Step 2: Apply it to the embedded source.

Step 3: Justify the final decision with a complete explanation.

Modeled thinking: My answer needs evidence from the source, not just my opinion.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Fix only commas and spelling, then call the package revised.

Correct: Revise unclear evidence, improve idea order, strengthen transitions, and then check conventions.

Teachable Explanation: Journalism revision and media checks are not cosmetic. They protect meaning, fairness, credibility, and audience trust.
