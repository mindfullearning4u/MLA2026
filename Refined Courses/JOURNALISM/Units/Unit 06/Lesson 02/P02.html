P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Reporting Package Verification

Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Reporting Package Verification

Vocabulary

  • Evidence: information used to support a claim or decision.
  • Reasoning: the explanation that connects evidence to a conclusion.
  • Verification: checking whether information is accurate and supported.
  • Inference: a logical conclusion based on evidence and context.

Embedded Source

Reporting Package Verification. Task: A reporter is preparing a package about after-school tutoring attendance. Claim: Attendance increased after peer tutors began visiting ninth-grade classes. Evidence Available: sign-in sheets from September and October, one interview with the tutoring coordinator, one student quote, and a hallway observation. Verification Concern: the sign-in sheets show more names in October, but the reporter has not checked whether the increase came from classroom visits or another cause. Publication Need: the reporter must explain what the evidence proves, what it does not prove, and what still needs verification.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the claim. A reporting package usually contains statements that need support.

Step 2: Locate the evidence. Evidence may come from notes, interviews, documents, observations, or verified source material.

Step 3: Explain the reasoning. Do not just point to evidence. Explain how the evidence proves or limits the claim.

Step 4: Identify gaps. If a source is unclear, incomplete, or only one-sided, the claim needs more verification before publication.

Notebook Directions

Create a two-column chart. In the left column, list the key decision from the source. In the right column, explain the evidence or format rule that should guide the decision.

Model Example

Decision: Do not submit work only because it exists.

Reason: Journalism work must be selected, checked, revised, and connected to audience purpose before it belongs in a final product.
