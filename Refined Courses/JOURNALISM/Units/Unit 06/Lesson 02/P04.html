📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 06 | Lesson 02

P04 Worked Example

Reporting Package Verification

Embedded Source

Reporting Package Verification. Task: A reporter is preparing a package about after-school tutoring attendance. Claim: Attendance increased after peer tutors began visiting ninth-grade classes. Evidence Available: sign-in sheets from September and October, one interview with the tutoring coordinator, one student quote, and a hallway observation. Verification Concern: the sign-in sheets show more names in October, but the reporter has not checked whether the increase came from classroom visits or another cause. Publication Need: the reporter must explain what the evidence proves, what it does not prove, and what still needs verification.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify the journalism decision.

Step 2: Find the rule or evidence in the source.

Step 3: Explain why the rule or evidence matters for the audience.

Modeled thinking: I should not choose the fastest option. I should choose the option that protects credibility and publication quality.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify what is missing or weak.

Step 2: Decide whether the problem is format, revision, evidence, or verification.

Step 3: Recommend a correction that can be completed before publication.

Explicit reasoning: A correction is stronger when it names both the problem and the fix.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: State the claim or portfolio choice.

Step 2: Cite the source detail that supports or challenges it.

Step 3: Explain whether the item is ready, needs revision, or needs more verification.

Modeled thinking: If I cannot explain the decision with evidence, the decision is not ready.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Say a claim is verified because it sounds reasonable.

Correct: Use cited evidence from reliable reporting notes to explain and justify the claim.

Teachable Explanation: Journalism quality depends on the reason behind the decision, not just the decision itself. Always explain how the source supports the choice.
