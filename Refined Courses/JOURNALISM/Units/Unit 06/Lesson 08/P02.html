P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Putting It All Together: Unit 6 Synthesis

Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Final Course Synthesis Chart

Vocabulary

  • Synthesis: combining several learned skills to solve one larger problem.
  • Capstone: a final demonstration of learning across a course.
  • Publication-quality: accurate, revised, formatted, audience-ready work.
  • Final verification: the last evidence and readiness check before submission.

Embedded Source

Final Journalism Capstone Scenario. Task: The student newsroom is submitting the final course portfolio and publication package. Required evidence: a revised article, a verified reporting note, a media-literacy audience check, a multimedia sequence, a collaborative publication workflow, a final online portfolio page, and a short oral presentation outline. Current Risks: one article transition is weak, one claim needs clearer evidence reasoning, one media headline overstates the audience opinion, one photo caption lacks date context, and the team has not assigned final export and submission roles. Goal: submit one accurate, revised, audience-ready, publication-quality journalism portfolio that demonstrates skills from the full course.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify every remaining risk. The source lists writing, evidence, media, visual, and workflow problems.

Step 2: Connect each risk to a course skill. Weak transition connects to revision. Weak evidence reasoning connects to reporting. Overstated headline connects to media literacy. Missing date context connects to visual accuracy. Missing roles connect to collaboration and workflow.

Step 3: Prioritize corrections before submission. A final portfolio is not ready until all high-impact risks are corrected.

Step 4: Explain the audience impact. The reason for each correction is audience trust.

Notebook Directions

Create a synthesis chart with four columns: final risk, course skill, correction, and audience impact. Complete at least five rows.

Model Example

Final risk: one claim needs clearer evidence reasoning.

Course skill: evidence and reasoning.

Correction: add a sentence explaining how the verified note supports the claim.

Audience impact: readers can understand why the claim is credible.
