P04 Worked Example

Integrated Journalism Portfolio Preparation

Embedded Source

Integrated Journalism Portfolio. Task: The student newsroom is preparing a final portfolio page for public review. Required parts: one revised article, one verified evidence note, one accurate feature photo with caption, one short oral presentation outline, one publication-ready media package, and one reflection explaining audience purpose. Current Status: the article is revised but missing a final transition, the evidence note cites attendance numbers but does not explain why they matter, the feature photo is accurate but caption needs the date, and the oral outline lists topics but lacks a clear order. Audience: Teacher of Record, publication team, families, and students who need to see credible final journalism work. Publication Need: the team must synthesize reporting, revision, accurate visuals, format rules, publication readiness, and oral presentation.

Example 1: Evidence Note

Step 1: Identify the issue: attendance numbers are cited but not explained.

Step 2: Connect to evidence reasoning.

Step 3: Add reasoning that explains why the numbers matter.

Modeled thinking: Evidence is not enough by itself. The audience needs to understand how the evidence supports the claim.

Example 2: Accurate Photo Caption

Step 1: Identify the issue: the photo is accurate but the caption needs the date.

Step 2: Connect to visual accuracy.

Step 3: Add the date before publication.

Explicit reasoning: A caption is part of the reporting record. Missing context can weaken accuracy.

Example 3: Oral Outline

Step 1: Identify the issue: topics are listed but not ordered.

Step 2: Connect to oral presentation organization.

Step 3: organize the outline from purpose, to evidence, to final portfolio recommendation.

Modeled thinking: A presentation outline must guide listeners, not just list ideas.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Submit the portfolio because every required part exists somewhere in the folder.

Correct: Submit only after each part is revised, verified, captioned, organized, and connected to the audience purpose.

Teachable explanation: A final portfolio should show control. If the portfolio includes work that is not revised, verified, captioned, or organized, the audience cannot trust it as a final product.
