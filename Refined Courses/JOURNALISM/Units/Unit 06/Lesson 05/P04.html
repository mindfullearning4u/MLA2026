P04 Worked Example

Multimedia Package Preparation

Embedded Source

Multimedia Package Preparation. Task: The newsroom is preparing a final multimedia package about the new student garden. Required parts: a short digital presentation with three findings, a five-image sequence showing the planting process, one accurate feature photo, and a 20-second video clip with natural sound. Audience: students, families, and community partners. Production Concern: the image sequence currently starts with the finished garden before showing the planting steps, and the feature photo shows last year's garden sign. Publication Need: the package must use logical sequence, accurate visuals, and clear presentation choices before the final portfolio.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Identify the audience need.

Step 2: Identify which media or team choice affects that need.

Step 3: Recommend a correction that protects meaning.

Modeled thinking: I should decide based on how the audience will understand the final package.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Check sequence or workflow.

Step 2: Decide whether the order helps or confuses the audience.

Step 3: Move, revise, or verify the part that creates confusion.

Explicit reasoning: Order matters because journalism guides an audience through information.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Step 1: Check accuracy and readiness.

Step 2: Find the source detail that proves the risk.

Step 3: Explain the final decision with the standard.

Modeled thinking: A package is not ready until the correction is specific and evidence-based.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Put the most dramatic clip first even if it happens at the end and lacks context.

Correct: Sequence the clips so the audience understands what happened, why it matters, and what evidence supports it.

Teachable Explanation: Fast production choices can create public errors. Strong journalism uses the final check to protect accuracy, cohesion, and trust.
