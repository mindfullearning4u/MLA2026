P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Journalism Purpose and Publication Formats

Write today’s notebook notes under this title. Your notes should help you explain what journalism is, what format a piece uses, and how that format affects the journalist’s choices.

Vocabulary

  • Journalism: factual communication created to inform an audience about events, issues, people, or ideas.
  • Format: the type or structure of a journalism product, such as a news brief, feature, editorial, broadcast script, photo caption, or digital post.
  • Audience: the people the journalist expects to read, view, or hear the information.
  • Publication-ready: prepared clearly and accurately enough to be shared with an audience.
  • News judgment: the decision-making process journalists use to decide what information matters and how it should be presented.

Step 1: Understand the Purpose of Journalism

Journalism is not the same as creative writing or personal opinion writing. A journalist begins with facts that can be checked. The purpose is to help an audience understand what happened, why it matters, and what information is reliable.

A journalist asks: What is the verified information? Who needs to know it? What format will communicate it clearly? What details must be included so the audience is not misled?

Step 2: Separate Format from Topic

The topic is what the piece is about. The format is the type of journalism product. The same topic can appear in several formats.

Topic: A school opens a new media lab.

Possible formats: a short news brief announcing the opening, a feature story about students using the lab, an editorial arguing why media literacy matters, or a digital slideshow showing the lab equipment.

Do not identify a format only by topic. Look at purpose, structure, tone, evidence, and audience.

Embedded Journalism Example

Source Card: Falcon News Online

Headline: New Media Lab Opens for Student Reporting Projects

Text: Falcon High opened its new media lab Monday after six months of renovation. The lab includes podcast microphones, video-editing stations, and portable cameras. Journalism teacher Ms. Nguyen said the space will help students practice interviewing, fact-checking, and digital publishing. The first student-produced news segment is scheduled for Friday.

Format Clue: The piece gives verified facts about a recent school event. It answers who, what, when, and why in a direct way. That makes it a news brief, not an editorial or a feature profile.

Model Example

Question: Why is the embedded example a news brief?

Model answer: It is a news brief because it reports a recent event using factual details. It identifies who was involved, what happened, when it happened, and why it matters to students. It does not mainly tell a long human-interest story, argue an opinion, or use a dramatic scene.

Notebook direction: Copy the model answer, then underline the words that show journalism reasoning: factual details, who, what, when, why, does not argue.
