Notebook Title

Journalism Formats and Purpose

Vocabulary

Journalism: factual communication created for a public audience.

Publication format: the type of journalism piece, such as a news brief, feature, opinion column, photo caption, or multimedia post.

Audience: the readers, viewers, or listeners who receive the information.

Accuracy: the quality of being correct and supported by facts.

Attribution: naming where information came from, such as a person, document, or organization.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Start with the purpose. Journalism helps an audience understand something factual. The purpose may be to report a new event, explain an issue, describe a person, share a viewpoint, or connect an image to information.

Step 2: Identify the format. The format tells you what rules to follow. A news brief is short and fact-focused. A feature story gives more background and human detail. An opinion column makes a claim and supports it. A caption identifies an image. A multimedia post combines text with media such as video, audio, or images.

Step 3: Match the format to audience needs. Readers of a news brief need the main facts quickly. Readers of a feature need context. Readers of an opinion piece need a clear claim and reasons. Viewers of a photo need accurate identification.

Step 4: Check the accepted rules. Quality journalism follows the rules of its format so the audience knows what kind of information is being presented.

Notebook Task

Create a three-column chart in your notebook.

Column 1: Format

Column 2: Purpose

Column 3: Two accepted rules

Add these formats to your chart: news brief, feature story, opinion column, photo caption, and multimedia post. For each one, write the purpose and two rules that help the format create quality journalism.

Model row: News brief | Reports important facts quickly | Uses facts instead of opinion; answers who, what, when, where, why, and how when possible.
