📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 01 | Lesson 01

P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 1: Journalism Purpose and Publication Formats

In this lesson, you will learn why journalism exists, how different publication formats work, and how journalists make format-specific choices that serve readers with accurate information.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.JOUR.ETH.01 - Format-Specific Quality Work.

Support Standards: MLA.JOUR.MED.09 - Technology and Media History; MLA.JOUR.NEWS.06 - Academic and Journalistic Vocabulary.

What You Will Learn

  • The core purpose of journalism: to gather, verify, and communicate factual information for an audience.
  • How publication formats such as news briefs, feature stories, editorials, broadcasts, and digital posts shape journalistic choices.
  • How format-specific rules help journalists produce clear, accurate, useful work.
  • How journalism vocabulary helps you describe reporting decisions precisely.

What You Will Do

  • Study short journalism examples and identify their format, purpose, audience, and quality expectations.
  • Build notebook notes that separate facts, format rules, and audience needs.
  • Compare incorrect and correct journalistic choices.
  • Complete guided practice, independent work, a checkpoint submission, and a lesson quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Explain the purpose of journalism in your own words.
  • Identify format-specific expectations for different journalistic products.
  • Use journalism vocabulary accurately when explaining why a piece fits a format.
  • Submit checkpoint evidence that shows accurate format judgment and audience awareness.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.JOUR.ETH.01: I can follow the accepted rules of a journalism format so my work is clear, accurate, audience-ready, and appropriate for publication.

In student language: I can explain what kind of journalism piece I am creating and make choices that fit that format.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help: If you are unsure whether a piece is a news brief, feature, editorial, broadcast script, or digital post, contact your Teacher of Record before submitting your checkpoint.
