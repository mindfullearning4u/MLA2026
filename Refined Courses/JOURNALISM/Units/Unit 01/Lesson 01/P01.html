Lesson 1: Journalism Purpose and Publication Formats

Standards Covered

  • MLA.JOUR.ETH.01: Use the accepted rules governing a specific format to create quality work.
  • MLA.JOUR.MED.09: Identify how changes in media and technology affect communication.
  • MLA.JOUR.NEWS.06: Use academic and journalistic vocabulary clearly in speaking and writing.

What You Will Learn

You will learn that journalism is factual public communication. A journalist does not write every piece the same way. A short news brief, a feature story, an opinion column, a photo caption, and a multimedia post each follow different format rules.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to name the purpose of a journalism format, identify the rules that make that format work, and explain why following those rules helps readers trust the publication.

Why This Matters

Readers make fast decisions about whether a publication looks reliable. If a news brief includes unsupported opinion, readers may not trust it. If a caption leaves out who or what is shown, readers may misunderstand the image. Format rules protect accuracy, clarity, and audience trust.

Strong journalism starts with matching the message to the format.

What You Will Do

  • Define journalism purpose, audience, format, accuracy, and attribution.
  • Compare common journalism formats and their accepted rules.
  • Study a worked example that shows how format rules guide writing choices.
  • Practice choosing the best format and explaining the rule that fits it.
  • Complete a checkpoint that shows you can connect format rules to quality work.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery when you can identify a journalism format, explain its purpose, name at least two accepted rules for that format, and describe how those rules help create clear, trustworthy journalism.

Student-friendly standard connection: I can follow the rules of a journalism format so my work is accurate, clear, and appropriate for readers.

Ask TOR for help if: you are unsure whether a piece is news, opinion, feature, caption, or multimedia journalism.
