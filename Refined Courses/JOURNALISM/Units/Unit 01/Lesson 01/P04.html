P04 Worked Example

Use format rules to choose and improve a journalism piece.

Example 1: Choose the Format

Situation: A reporter has one paragraph to tell students that the cafeteria will begin serving breakfast at 7:15 a.m. on Tuesday.

Step 1: The information is timely and factual.

Step 2: The audience needs the main facts quickly.

Step 3: The best format is a news brief.

Answer: A news brief fits because it reports important facts in a short format.

Example 2: Check the Rule

Draft sentence: The cafeteria will finally serve breakfast early, which is clearly what everyone wanted.

Step 1: The sentence includes facts, but it also includes opinion.

Step 2: A news brief should use factual language unless a viewpoint is quoted and attributed.

Step 3: Revise the sentence so it reports the fact directly.

Improved sentence: The cafeteria will begin serving breakfast at 7:15 a.m. on Tuesday.

Example 3: Explain Quality

Question: Why is the improved sentence stronger journalism?

Step 1: It gives the reader the main fact.

Step 2: It uses clear, precise language.

Step 3: It follows the accepted rule of a news brief by avoiding unsupported opinion.

Final explanation: The improved sentence is stronger because it matches the format, gives useful information, and keeps opinion out of a factual news brief.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A writer can use the same style for every journalism format as long as the topic is interesting.

This is incorrect because journalism formats have different purposes and rules. Interest alone does not make a piece accurate or appropriate.

Correct: A writer should match the style, structure, and language to the journalism format and audience.

This is correct because quality journalism depends on using the accepted rules for the chosen format.
