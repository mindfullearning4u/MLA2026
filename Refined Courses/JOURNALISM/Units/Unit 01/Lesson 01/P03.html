P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Journalism Application

Now you will move from identifying a format to explaining why the format matters. A journalist does not choose a format randomly. The format controls how much background is needed, what voice is appropriate, how evidence is arranged, and what the audience expects.

Use this sequence: identify the format, identify the audience need, identify the format rules, then judge whether the piece meets those rules.

Step-by-Step: Judging Format-Specific Quality

Step 1: Name the format. Is it a news brief, feature, editorial, interview transcript, broadcast script, caption, or digital post?

Step 2: State the audience need. Does the audience need quick facts, background, a human-interest angle, a supported opinion, or multimedia context?

Step 3: Match the content to format rules. A news brief should be concise and factual. A feature should include human detail and context. An editorial should make a clear claim and support it with credible evidence.

Step 4: Make an effectiveness judgment. Explain whether the piece fits the format and what would improve it.

Embedded Practice Source

Source Card: Hallway Beat

Text: Senior Maya Brooks spent three weekends teaching younger students how to record interviews for the school podcast. She said the hardest part was helping new reporters listen instead of rushing to the next question. Her team’s first episode features student voices from the media lab opening.

Think: This source is not just a quick event announcement. It centers on a person, experience, and lesson learned. That points toward a feature format.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, answer in three labeled parts.

Part 1: Identify the likely format of the Hallway Beat source.

Part 2: Explain two clues that support your format choice.

Part 3: Write one sentence explaining how the journalist could strengthen the piece for publication.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

This is journalism because it is about school.

Why this is incorrect: The answer names the topic but does not identify the format or explain the rules of that format. Journalism is not defined only by being about school. The student must explain how the piece gathers and presents factual information for an audience.

Correct example:

This source would fit a feature story because it focuses on Maya Brooks’s experience, includes human-interest detail, and gives readers context about student podcast reporting.

Why this is correct: The answer identifies the format, gives specific evidence, and connects the evidence to journalism purpose and audience needs.
