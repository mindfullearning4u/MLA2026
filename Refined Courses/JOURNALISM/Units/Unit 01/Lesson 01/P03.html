P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Apply format rules to a journalism situation.

Scenario

The school library is opening a new study room after school. Students may reserve the room for group projects. The librarian says the room includes charging stations, a whiteboard, and six tables. The first day for reservations is Monday.

Step-by-Step Thinking

Step 1: Decide what the audience needs most. In this situation, students need clear facts about a new school resource.

Step 2: Choose the best journalism format. A news brief fits because the information is timely, factual, and useful.

Step 3: List the format rules. A news brief should be concise, factual, and organized around the most important information first.

Step 4: Avoid adding unsupported opinion. A sentence such as "This is the best library idea ever" does not belong in a straight news brief unless it is quoted and attributed to a source.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The new study room is obviously amazing, and everyone should go there right away.

This is incorrect because it uses opinion words instead of reporting facts. A news brief should not tell readers what to think.

Correct: The school library will open a new study room on Monday for students working on group projects.

This is correct because it gives clear, factual information and follows the purpose of a news brief.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, answer these questions in complete sentences.

  1. Which journalism format best fits the study room scenario?
  2. What is the purpose of that format?
  3. What two rules should the writer follow?
  4. Write one sentence that would belong in this format.
  5. Write one sentence that would not belong, and explain why.
