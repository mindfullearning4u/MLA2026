Notebook Title

Visual Design in Journalism

Vocabulary

Visual culture: the images, design styles, and media habits people see and use in daily communication.

Layout: the arrangement of text, images, headlines, captions, and white space.

Visual accuracy: using images or graphics in a way that represents facts honestly.

Design trend: a common visual style or technique used across media during a period of time.

Format rule: an accepted expectation for a journalism format, such as captioning images clearly.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Notice the design feature. Examples include large photos, bold headlines, short captions, charts, icons, color blocks, and mobile-friendly spacing.

Step 2: Ask what the feature helps the audience do. Does it guide attention, explain information, compare data, or show a scene?

Step 3: Check accuracy. A strong visual should not exaggerate, hide context, mislabel people, or make unsupported claims.

Step 4: Match the design to the format. A photo caption, infographic, social post, and feature page do not use visuals in exactly the same way.

Notebook Task

Create a three-column chart in your notebook.

Column 1: Visual design feature

Column 2: How it helps the audience

Column 3: Accuracy or format rule to check

Add five rows: headline size, photo choice, caption, chart or graph, and mobile-friendly spacing.
