P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Apply visual design thinking to a journalism layout.

Scenario

A school publication is designing a web story about the robotics team winning a regional competition. The team has a group photo, a close-up photo of the robot, and a chart showing scores from the final round.

Step-by-Step Thinking

Step 1: Choose the main visual. The group photo may show who won, while the robot close-up may show what the team built.

Step 2: Decide what the audience needs. Readers need to know who won, what happened, and why the competition matters.

Step 3: Check visual accuracy. The caption must correctly identify people, place, and event.

Step 4: Use the chart only if it helps readers understand the result without exaggerating the scores.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Use the most dramatic photo even if the caption cannot identify who or what is shown.

This is incorrect because visual impact does not replace accuracy. A photo used in journalism must be clear and truthfully identified.

Correct: Use a strong photo with a caption that accurately identifies the team, event, and context.

This is correct because the design supports the audience without weakening factual trust.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, design a short visual plan for the robotics story. Include:

  1. The main visual you would use.
  2. One caption rule you would follow.
  3. One reason the chart should or should not be included.
  4. One design choice that helps mobile readers.
