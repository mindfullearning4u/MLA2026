P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Headline Hierarchy

Scenario: A digital page places the breaking schedule update in small text below a large decorative graphic.

Step 1: Identify the purpose: readers need the schedule update quickly.

Step 2: Judge the hierarchy: the most important information is too low and too small.

Step 3: Revise the design: make the verified update the headline and move decoration below it.

Modeled thinking: In journalism, the most important verified information should be easiest to find.

Example 2: Caption Accuracy

Scenario: A photo shows students interviewing a club sponsor. The caption says, Students celebrate after winning the competition.

Step 1: Compare caption to image evidence.

Step 2: Identify the problem: the caption claims a celebration and competition win that the photo does not show.

Step 3: Correct the caption: Journalism students interview a club sponsor for a feature story about the club fair.

Modeled thinking: Captions are factual reporting, not decoration.

Example 3: Platform Fit

Scenario: A long paragraph about event times is posted as a small social media image.

Step 1: Identify the platform: a quick-scanning digital post.

Step 2: Identify the design issue: dense text is hard to read.

Step 3: Improve the design: use a clear headline, short verified bullet points, and a link to the full article.

Modeled thinking: Platform fit helps the audience get accurate information without confusion.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The best design is the one with the brightest colors and biggest image.

Why this is incorrect: Bright colors and large images can attract attention, but they do not guarantee accurate communication.

Correct example:

The best journalism design makes verified information easy to understand and uses images, captions, and layout accurately.

Why this is correct: The answer evaluates design by audience understanding and factual communication.
