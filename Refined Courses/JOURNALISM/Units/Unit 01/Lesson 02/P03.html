P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Journalism Application

Newsroom collaboration often becomes difficult when time is short. A deadline can pressure a team to publish quickly. However, speed does not remove the responsibility to verify facts, listen to concerns, and make ethical decisions.

Use this decision sequence: define the task, assign roles, confirm sources, listen for concerns, adjust the plan, and publish only what is ready.

Step-by-Step: Ethical Team Decision-Making

Step 1: Name the publication goal. What does the audience need?

Step 2: List the roles. Who is reporting, editing, designing, checking facts, and submitting?

Step 3: Identify risk. What could mislead the audience if it is wrong?

Step 4: Listen actively. Restate the concern before responding.

Step 5: Compromise ethically. Change length, angle, timing, or design if needed, but do not sacrifice accuracy.

Embedded Scenario for Notebook Application

Scenario: Photo Caption Problem

The team wants to publish a photo of students building a robotics display. The caption draft says, The robotics team won first place at regionals. A teammate says the team actually placed second, but the photographer already uploaded the caption and the article is due in 20 minutes.

Think: The problem is not just a typo. It is an accuracy problem that can damage audience trust and misrepresent students’ achievement.

Notebook Directions

Answer in three labeled parts.

Part 1: Identify the ethical issue.

Part 2: Write one active listening sentence a teammate could use.

Part 3: Write the ethical compromise or correction the team should make before publication.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The team should leave the caption because the article is due soon and second place is close to first place.

Why this is incorrect: The answer chooses deadline pressure over accuracy. Journalism cannot treat an incorrect result as acceptable because it is close. The audience deserves the verified fact.

Correct example:

The team should correct the caption to say second place, confirm the result with the coach or official record if possible, and publish only the verified information.

Why this is correct: The answer protects factual accuracy, uses teamwork to solve the problem, and keeps the publication deadline in mind without misleading the audience.
