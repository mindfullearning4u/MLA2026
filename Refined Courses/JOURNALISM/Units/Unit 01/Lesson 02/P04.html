P04 Worked Example

Use ethical collaboration to solve a newsroom production problem.

Example 1: Identify the Team Problem

Situation: The reporter has finished the interview notes, but the editor notices that the headline makes a claim the notes do not prove.

Step 1: The team problem is not just the headline. The problem is that the publication may overstate the facts.

Step 2: The ethical concern is accuracy.

Step 3: The team must revise before publication.

Example 2: Use Active Listening

Reporter says: I wanted the headline to be stronger so people would read the story.

Editor says: I hear that you want reader interest, and I also need the headline to match the evidence.

Step 1: The editor acknowledges the reporter's goal.

Step 2: The editor names the accuracy concern.

Step 3: The conversation stays focused on the work, not personal blame.

Example 3: Create the Compromise

Step 1: Keep the headline interesting but remove the unsupported claim.

Step 2: Ask the reporter to suggest two accurate headline options.

Step 3: Ask the editor to choose the clearest option and complete a final accuracy check.

Final solution: The team protects accuracy, keeps the deadline moving, and allows both teammates to contribute.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The editor should delete the reporter's headline without discussion and publish a new one.

This is incorrect because it may solve one problem while damaging team trust and skipping collaboration.

Correct: The editor should explain the accuracy issue, listen to the reporter's goal, and revise the headline through a quick shared process.

This is correct because it combines active listening, respectful compromise, accuracy, and time management.
