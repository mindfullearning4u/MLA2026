P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Format and Verification

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Scenario: A club fair room number changes.

Step 1: Verify the new room with the sponsor.

Step 2: Use a news brief or mobile update because the audience needs fast facts.

Step 3: Include who, what, when, where, and source confirmation.

Modeled thinking: The format choice depends on audience need and verified facts.

Worked Example 2: Design and Language Access

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Scenario: A digital story uses the phrase orientation prior to rotational sessions.

Step 1: Identify the barrier.

Step 2: Revise for clarity.

Revision: Go to the cafeteria first for a short welcome meeting before visiting club tables.

Modeled thinking: Clear language helps the audience act on the information.

Worked Example 3: Interview Tone

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Scenario: The reporter needs missing details from the sponsor.

Step 1: Use professional tone.

Step 2: Ask a clear question.

Message: Hello Ms. Rivera, I am writing the club fair news brief. Could you confirm the start time and first check-in location?

Modeled thinking: The message is respectful, clear, and useful for verification.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The graphic looks good, so the story is ready.

Why this is incorrect: Visual appeal cannot replace verified facts, source communication, and clear audience language.

Correct example:

The story is ready when the facts are verified, the format fits the audience, the design supports understanding, and the language is clear.

Teachable Explanation: It applies the full Unit 1 journalism foundation.
