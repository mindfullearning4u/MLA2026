P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Journalism Application

Journalists must be careful when simplifying language. The goal is not to remove important information. The goal is to make the important information understandable.

Use this sequence: identify the audience, find the barrier, preserve the fact, and revise for clarity.

Step-by-Step: Revise for Language Access

Step 1: Identify who needs the information.

Step 2: Find words, abbreviations, or sentence structures that may confuse the audience.

Step 3: Replace confusing wording with clear wording.

Step 4: Check that the verified fact did not change.

Embedded Scenario

Original notice: Participants should proceed to the multipurpose facility for orientation prior to rotational sessions.

Think: This sentence may confuse students who need simple directions.

Accessible revision: Students should go to the cafeteria first for a short welcome meeting before visiting club tables.

Notebook Directions

Part 1: Identify the communication barrier in the original notice.

Part 2: Explain what information must stay accurate.

Part 3: Write your own clear revision.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

Just remove hard words even if the directions become less specific.

Why this is incorrect: Removing details can make the message less useful and less accurate.

Correct example:

Use clear words while keeping the exact place, time, and action the audience needs.

Why this is correct: The revision supports access and protects factual communication.

Mastery Support: Language Access Revision Steps

  1. Circle words that may confuse a new student or English learner.
  2. Underline the facts that must not change.
  3. Replace confusing terms with common words.
  4. Break long directions into shorter steps.
  5. Reread the revision and check that the same facts are still present.

This process supports remedial learners by slowing the task down and supports accelerated learners by requiring accuracy plus audience awareness.
