P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Clarifying a Source Question

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Original: Can you discuss implementation outcomes?

Step 1: Identify the barrier: vague academic language.

Step 2: Preserve the fact-finding purpose.

Revision: What changed after the new schedule started?

Modeled thinking: The revised question is easier to answer and still gathers reportable information.

Worked Example 2: Clarifying Audience Directions

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Original: Report to the designated venue following dismissal.

Step 1: Identify the barrier: designated venue is unclear.

Step 2: Add exact information.

Revision: After school, go to Room 204 for the meeting.

Modeled thinking: Clear directions reduce confusion for readers.

Worked Example 3: Respectful Support

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Scenario: A source asks what publication means.

Step 1: Answer respectfully.

Step 2: Use plain language.

Response: Publication means sharing the finished story with readers, such as posting it on the school news site.

Modeled thinking: Respectful clarification helps the source participate.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

If a source does not understand, the reporter should move on to another source.

Why this is incorrect: This can exclude useful voices and reduce fairness.

Correct example:

The reporter should clarify the question respectfully and give the source a fair chance to respond.

Teachable Explanation: It supports inclusion, accuracy, and source trust.
