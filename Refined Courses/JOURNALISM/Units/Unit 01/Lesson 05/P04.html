P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Preparing Interview Questions

Scenario: You are reporting on a new student recycling station.

Step 1: Identify the reporting purpose: explain what changed and how students should use the station.

Step 2: Choose a source: the sponsor or student leader who organized the station.

Step 3: Write questions: What problem does the station solve? Where is it located? How should students sort materials?

Modeled thinking: These questions gather useful facts for the audience instead of asking for praise.

Example 2: Listening for a Follow-Up

Source response: We moved the sign-up table because students could not find it near the gym.

Step 1: Listen for the key detail: the table location changed because students had trouble finding it.

Step 2: Ask a follow-up: Where is the sign-up table now?

Step 3: Confirm the answer before publishing.

Modeled thinking: The follow-up turns a general explanation into specific information readers can use.

Example 3: Confirming a Fact

Source response: The meeting is usually after school on Wednesday, but this week we might move it.

Step 1: Notice uncertainty: might move is not confirmed.

Step 2: Ask for confirmation: Who can confirm the final meeting time?

Step 3: Mark the detail as unverified until confirmed.

Modeled thinking: Active listening includes noticing uncertainty, not just writing down the first answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The reporter should ask every prepared question even if the source already answered one of them.

Why this is incorrect: This ignores active listening. A reporter should adapt when the source gives useful information.

Correct example:

The reporter should listen to the answer, skip questions already answered, and ask follow-ups that clarify important details.

Why this is correct: This approach respects the source and improves accuracy.

Mastery Support: Interview Decision Checklist

Before you decide an interview response is ready for reporting, check each item:

  • Did the question connect to the reporting purpose?
  • Did the source provide a specific fact, explanation, or quote?
  • Did the reporter ask a follow-up when the answer was incomplete?
  • Did the reporter confirm uncertain details?
  • Did the reporter avoid biased or loaded wording?
