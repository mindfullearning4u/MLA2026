P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Revising a Demanding Message

Original: Send me your answers by lunch because I need this article done.

Step 1: Identify the tone problem: demanding and centered on the reporter.

Step 2: Add context and respect: explain the story and ask politely.

Revision: I am writing a student news article about the club fair. If possible, could you answer two questions by lunch so I can confirm the event details?

Modeled thinking: The revision is still clear about timing, but it respects the source.

Example 2: Removing Loaded Language

Original: Why did the committee waste money on the new banners?

Step 1: Identify loaded wording: waste money assumes the spending was wrong.

Step 2: Replace it with neutral wording.

Revision: What was the purpose of purchasing the new banners, and how was the cost approved?

Modeled thinking: The revised question still asks about money, but it does not accuse the source before facts are gathered.

Example 3: Matching Audience and Purpose

Original: Tell me everything about the meeting.

Step 1: Identify the problem: too vague.

Step 2: Connect the request to the reporting purpose.

Revision: I am reporting on the new meeting schedule. What date, time, and location should students know?

Modeled thinking: Clear purpose leads to clearer source answers.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

A reporter should use dramatic words so the source understands the story is important.

Why this is incorrect: Dramatic words can pressure the source and create bias.

Correct example:

A reporter should use clear, neutral, respectful words so the source understands the request and can answer accurately.

Why this is correct: This wording protects trust and factual communication.

Mastery Support: Professional Source Message Formula

Use this sentence frame when you are stuck:

Hello [source name], I am writing a student news story about [topic]. Could you help me confirm [specific fact or question] by [reasonable time]?

This formula works because it identifies the reporter purpose, gives context, asks a clear question, and respects the source time.
