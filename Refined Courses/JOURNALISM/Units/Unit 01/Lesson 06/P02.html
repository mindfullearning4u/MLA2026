P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Voice and Tone in Source Communication

Use this title for your notes. Your goal is to learn how word choice affects the way a source understands and responds to a journalist.

Vocabulary

  • Voice: the overall professional identity or presence created by communication choices.
  • Tone: the attitude conveyed through word choice and sentence structure.
  • Denotation: the literal meaning of a word.
  • Connotation: the feeling or association a word suggests.
  • Neutral language: wording that avoids bias, exaggeration, and pressure.

Step 1: Understand Professional Source Communication

Journalists often contact sources by email, message, phone, or in person. The words they choose can build trust or create resistance. A professional journalist is clear about the reporting purpose, respectful of the source time, and neutral about the topic.

A strong message says who you are, what story you are reporting, what information you need, and how the source can respond.

Step 2: Watch for Connotation

Two words can have similar denotations but different connotations. For example, said is neutral. admitted can suggest guilt or reluctance. In journalism, loaded connotations can unfairly shape how the audience views a source.

When communicating with sources, choose wording that is accurate, neutral, and respectful.

Embedded Source Message Example

Weak message: Hey, I need you to explain why the club fair got so disorganized. Answer fast because my article is due.

Professional message: Hello Ms. Rivera, I am writing a student news article about this year’s club fair. May I ask a few questions about how the event was organized and what students should know before attending?

Key idea: The professional message is clear, respectful, and neutral.

Model Example

Question: Why is the professional message stronger?

Model answer: It identifies the reporter, states the purpose, asks permission to ask questions, and uses neutral language. It does not blame the source or pressure the source to answer immediately.

Notebook direction: Copy the model answer and underline the words that show respect, clarity, and neutrality.

Mastery Support: How to Revise Tone Step by Step

When a message sounds unprofessional, do not rewrite randomly. Use this process:

  1. Identify the purpose. What information do you need from the source?
  2. Find the tone problem. Look for blame, pressure, slang, sarcasm, exaggeration, or vagueness.
  3. Replace loaded words. Use neutral words that ask for information instead of judging the source.
  4. Add context. Tell the source what story you are reporting.
  5. Ask clearly. Make the request specific enough to answer.
