P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Journalism Application

Tone matters because sources may stop cooperating if a reporter sounds careless, biased, demanding, or disrespectful. Tone also matters for accuracy. A source who feels pressured may give short answers, avoid details, or misunderstand the purpose of the interview.

The goal is not to sound fancy. The goal is to sound clear, professional, neutral, and focused on factual reporting.

Step-by-Step: Revise Tone

Step 1: Identify the communication purpose.

Step 2: Find words that sound biased, rude, vague, or too casual.

Step 3: Replace loaded words with neutral wording.

Step 4: Add clear context so the source understands the request.

Step 5: Check that the message is respectful and easy to answer.

Embedded Revision Scenario

Original message: Why did your team mess up the announcement schedule last week?

Problem: The phrase mess up has a negative connotation and assumes blame before the reporter gathers information.

Better message: Can you explain what caused the announcement schedule to change last week?

Notebook Directions

Answer in three labeled parts.

Part 1: Identify the word or phrase that creates the tone problem.

Part 2: Explain why the tone could harm source trust.

Part 3: Rewrite the message in a professional, neutral tone.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The message is fine because reporters should be direct, even if the source feels accused.

Why this is incorrect: Direct communication is useful, but accusatory wording can damage trust and bias the interview.

Correct example:

The message should be direct and neutral: Can you explain what caused the announcement schedule to change last week?

Why this is correct: The revised wording asks for information without blaming the source.

Mastery Support: Connotation Check

Use this test before sending a source message: Would the source feel accused, rushed, mocked, or confused? If yes, revise.

Loaded wording: Why did your team fail to organize the event?

Neutral wording: What challenges did the team face while organizing the event?

The neutral version still allows the reporter to gather accurate information, but it does not assume blame.
