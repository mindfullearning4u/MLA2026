📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 01 | Lesson 06

P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 6: Voice and Tone in Source Communication

In this lesson, you will learn how journalists use appropriate voice, tone, and word choice when communicating with sources and audiences.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.JOUR.INT.02 - Voice and Tone in Source Communication.

Support Standards: MLA.JOUR.NEWS.08 - Connotation, Denotation, and News Style; MLA.JOUR.INT.01 - Collaborative Techniques and Active Listening.

What You Will Learn

  • How voice and tone affect source trust.
  • How word choice can sound neutral, respectful, biased, casual, or aggressive.
  • How connotation and denotation affect journalistic communication.
  • How to revise source messages for professionalism and clarity.

What You Will Do

  • Analyze source communication examples.
  • Compare weak and professional tones.
  • Practice revising messages for neutrality and respect.
  • Complete guided practice, independent work, a checkpoint submission, and a lesson quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Identify tone problems in source communication.
  • Revise messages to be professional, neutral, and clear.
  • Explain how word choice affects trust and accuracy.
  • Submit a checkpoint message and revision explanation.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.JOUR.INT.02: I can use appropriate voice and tone when speaking or writing.

In student language: I can communicate with sources in a way that is respectful, neutral, and likely to produce accurate information.
