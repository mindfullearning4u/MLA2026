📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 01 | Lesson 03

P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Media History and Technology Change

In this lesson, you will learn how changes in media technology have changed how journalists gather information, produce news, and reach audiences.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.JOUR.MED.09 - Technology and Media History.

Support Standards: MLA.JOUR.MED.08 - Technology and Media Design Trends; MLA.JOUR.DIG.01 - Digital Presentations.

What You Will Learn

  • How print, radio, television, web publishing, mobile media, and social platforms changed journalism.
  • How technology affects speed, audience reach, verification, design, and presentation.
  • How to explain a technology change without saying that newer media automatically means better journalism.
  • How digital presentation choices can support factual reporting.

What You Will Do

  • Read a short embedded media-history source.
  • Build notes that connect media technology to reporting habits and audience access.
  • Analyze common mistakes about speed, accuracy, and digital publishing.
  • Complete guided practice, independent work, a checkpoint submission, and a lesson quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Identify how a media technology changed journalism practice.
  • Explain both the benefit and the responsibility created by a technology change.
  • Use accurate journalism and media vocabulary.
  • Submit a checkpoint explaining one media transition and its effect on factual communication.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.JOUR.MED.09: I can explain how technology has changed media and journalism over time.

In student language: I can describe how a change in media tools changed the way journalists report, publish, and serve audiences.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help: If you can identify a technology change but cannot explain how it changed journalism practice, ask your Teacher of Record for support before submitting your checkpoint.
