Lesson 3: Media History and Technology Change

Standards Covered

  • MLA.JOUR.MED.09: Identify transitions in media, technique, and focus to explain how technology has changed communication over time.
  • MLA.JOUR.MED.08: Investigate how technology and media design reflect trends in visual culture.
  • MLA.JOUR.DIG.01: Create digital presentations with coherent ideas and a clear perspective.

What You Will Learn

You will learn how journalism changes when technology changes. Printed newspapers, radio news, television broadcasts, websites, mobile alerts, podcasts, and social media posts all shape how audiences receive information.

You will focus on the connection between the tool, the format, and the audience. The main question is: How did a change in technology change what journalists could show, how quickly they could publish, or how audiences could respond?

Why This Matters

A journalist who understands media history can make smarter choices. A breaking-news alert is not built like a print feature. A podcast is not built like a photo caption. Each technology creates opportunities and limits, so journalists must choose the format that best serves the audience.

What You Will Do

  • Trace changes from print journalism to digital journalism.
  • Explain how technology affects speed, visuals, sound, audience access, and interaction.
  • Use a media-history timeline to analyze journalism choices.
  • Practice explaining a technology transition clearly and accurately.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery when you can identify a media technology transition and explain how it changed journalism technique, focus, or audience experience.

Student-friendly standard connection: I can explain how changes in media technology changed the way journalism is created and shared.

Ask TOR for help if: you can name a media tool but cannot explain how it changed journalism.
