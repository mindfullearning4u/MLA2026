P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Media History and Technology Change

Use this title for your notes. Your goal is to explain how technology changes journalism without losing the core responsibility to verify facts.

Vocabulary

  • Media technology: the tools and systems used to create, distribute, and receive information.
  • Platform: the place or system where journalism is published, such as print, broadcast, a website, or an app.
  • Distribution: how information reaches an audience.
  • Immediacy: the ability to share news quickly after information is gathered.
  • Verification: the process of checking information before publishing it.

Step 1: Technology Changes the Delivery, Not the Duty

Journalism has changed from printed newspapers to radio, television, websites, mobile alerts, podcasts, newsletters, and social platforms. Each technology changes how fast information travels, what evidence can be shown, and how audiences interact with news.

However, technology does not remove the journalist’s duty. Whether a story appears in print, audio, video, or a digital post, the information still must be accurate, fair, and clear.

Step 2: Analyze a Media Transition

To analyze a media transition, use four questions:

  1. What technology changed?
  2. How did it change speed, audience reach, or presentation?
  3. What new journalism opportunity did it create?
  4. What new responsibility or risk did it create?

This sequence prevents a shallow answer such as, Digital is better. Strong journalism analysis explains both function and responsibility.

Embedded Media-History Source

Source Card: From Print Bulletin to Mobile Alert

For many years, school news was distributed through printed bulletins or newspapers. Students often received updates after articles were written, edited, printed, and handed out. Today, student newsrooms can publish updates on websites or send mobile alerts within minutes. This faster distribution helps audiences learn about schedule changes, safety updates, and event results quickly. It also increases the risk that a newsroom may publish before checking a fact carefully.

Key idea: Faster technology improves access, but it also increases the need for verification routines.

Model Example

Question: How did mobile alerts change school journalism?

Model answer: Mobile alerts changed school journalism by making distribution faster and more direct. A newsroom can now send urgent information to students quickly. The responsibility is that reporters must verify the information before sending the alert because an error can spread immediately.

Notebook direction: Copy the model answer. Then label the technology change, the journalism opportunity, and the journalism responsibility.
