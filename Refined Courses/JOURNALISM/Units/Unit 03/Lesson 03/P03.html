P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from listing parts to building a responsible editorial argument. A strong editorial does not pretend that opposing concerns do not exist. It shows readers that the writer understands the full issue.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Check whether the claim matches the evidence. The survey supports interest in seating, especially if shade is available. It does not prove that construction is already possible.

Step 2: Use reasoning to connect evidence to claim. Explain that student interest justifies studying the proposal, not automatically approving it.

Step 3: Represent the counterclaim accurately. The facilities manager is not simply opposing students. The concern is space and safety.

Step 4: Write a fair rebuttal. A fair rebuttal answers the safety concern by proposing review steps, not by insulting the concern.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Source File: Editorial Planning Notes

The student editorial board is considering an editorial about whether the school should add more outdoor lunch seating. Current lunch tables fill quickly on clear days. A student survey with 86 responses found that 62 students said they would use additional outdoor seating if shade were available. The facilities manager says the school has limited space near the cafeteria and must keep walkways clear for safety. The principal says any proposal would need a cost estimate and a supervision plan before approval.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The school must add outdoor seating because almost everyone wants it, and safety concerns are just excuses.

Correct: Survey results show strong student interest in shaded outdoor seating, but the editorial should also address space, walkway safety, cost, and supervision before recommending approval.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version overstates the survey and dismisses a real counterclaim. The correct version uses evidence accurately and treats the opposing concern fairly.

Notebook Directions

Write one claim, one evidence sentence, one counterclaim sentence, and one rebuttal sentence. Each sentence must connect directly to the embedded source.
