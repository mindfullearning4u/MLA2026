P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Write a Supportable Claim

Weak claim: Outdoor lunch seating is the best idea ever.

Modeled thinking: That claim is too broad and emotional. It does not show what action should happen.

Stronger claim: The school should study whether shaded outdoor lunch seating can be added safely.

Reasoning: The stronger claim is specific, supportable, and realistic.

Example 2: Connect Evidence to Reasoning

Evidence: 62 of 86 surveyed students said they would use additional outdoor seating if shade were available.

Modeled thinking: The evidence shows student interest, especially with shade.

Reasoning sentence: Because most survey respondents said they would use shaded seating, the proposal is worth studying before the school dismisses it.

Reasoning: This connects the data to a careful action.

Example 3: Rebut a Counterclaim Fairly

Counterclaim: Space is limited and walkways must remain clear.

Modeled thinking: This concern is reasonable because safety matters.

Rebuttal: The editorial can recommend a walkway review and supervision plan before any seating is installed.

Reasoning: The rebuttal answers the concern instead of ignoring it.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use a counterclaim only to mock the other side.

Correct: State the counterclaim accurately, then respond with evidence or a practical solution.

Teachable explanation: Editorial credibility increases when readers see that the writer understands and answers serious concerns.
