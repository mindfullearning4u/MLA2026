P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Editorial Claims, Evidence, and Rebuttal

This lesson teaches how an editorial uses a clear claim, relevant evidence, and a fair rebuttal. Editorial writing may take a position, but it still must be grounded in accurate reporting.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.JOUR.NEWS.02 - Editorial Claims, Evidence, and Rebuttal.

Support Standards: MLA.JOUR.REP.02 - Evidence and Reasoning in Reporting; MLA.JOUR.MED.04 - Opposing Arguments and Claims.

What You Will Learn

  • How to write an editorial claim that can be supported.
  • How to choose evidence that actually connects to the claim.
  • How to explain reasoning instead of dropping in facts.
  • How to address a counterclaim fairly before responding to it.

What You Will Do

  • Study an editorial planning source file.
  • Separate claims, evidence, counterclaims, and rebuttal.
  • Practice building a short editorial argument.
  • Complete guided practice, independent work, checkpoint, and quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Write a supportable editorial claim.
  • Use relevant evidence from the source.
  • Explain how evidence supports the claim.
  • Respond fairly to an opposing concern.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

In student language: I can make a journalism-based argument by using evidence, explaining my reasoning, and responding fairly to a different viewpoint.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help

Ask your Teacher of Record for help if your editorial claim sounds like opinion without evidence or if your rebuttal ignores the opposing concern.
