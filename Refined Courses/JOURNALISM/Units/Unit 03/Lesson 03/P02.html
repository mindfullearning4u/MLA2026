P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Building an Editorial Claim from Evidence

Vocabulary

  • Editorial: journalism that presents a reasoned position on an issue.
  • Claim: the position the editorial wants readers to consider.
  • Evidence: facts, data, or source statements that support a claim.
  • Counterclaim: a reasonable opposing concern or viewpoint.
  • Rebuttal: a response that addresses the counterclaim with reasoning and evidence.

Embedded Source

Embedded Source File: Editorial Planning Notes

The student editorial board is considering an editorial about whether the school should add more outdoor lunch seating. Current lunch tables fill quickly on clear days. A student survey with 86 responses found that 62 students said they would use additional outdoor seating if shade were available. The facilities manager says the school has limited space near the cafeteria and must keep walkways clear for safety. The principal says any proposal would need a cost estimate and a supervision plan before approval.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Name the issue. The issue is whether the school should add more outdoor lunch seating.

Step 2: Write a claim that can be supported. A strong claim is not just Outdoor seating is good. A stronger claim is The school should consider adding shaded outdoor lunch seating if safety, space, and supervision needs can be addressed.

Step 3: Choose evidence. The survey result matters because 62 of 86 respondents said they would use additional outdoor seating if shade were available.

Step 4: Explain reasoning. The evidence suggests student interest, but the condition about shade matters. The claim should not ignore that condition.

Step 5: Identify the counterclaim. The facilities manager raises a real concern: limited space and clear walkways for safety.

Step 6: Plan a rebuttal. A fair rebuttal should acknowledge the safety concern and propose next steps, such as a cost estimate, supervision plan, and walkway review.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create four headings: Claim, Evidence, Counterclaim, and Rebuttal. Use the embedded source to fill each section with one accurate entry.

Model Example

Claim: The school should study whether shaded outdoor lunch seating can be added safely.

Evidence: 62 of 86 surveyed students said they would use additional outdoor seating if shade were available.

Counterclaim: Space near the cafeteria is limited and walkways must remain clear.

Rebuttal: The editorial can recommend a safety review and supervision plan before approval.
