P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Diagnosing a Draft Before Revising

Vocabulary

  • Revision: improving writing by changing content, organization, clarity, or style.
  • Clarity: how easy the writing is to understand.
  • Cohesion: how well ideas connect from sentence to sentence.
  • Convention: grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and spelling rules used for publication.
  • Publication readiness: the level of accuracy and polish needed before readers see the work.

Embedded Source

Embedded Draft for Revision

Original draft: The library pilot is starting next week and it is a really good idea because students need places and the buses are leaving at 5:10 and the librarian said the library is quiet and the school will look at sign-ins. Also students should use it because it helps.

Source facts available: The library will stay open until 5:00 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays for four weeks. Students must sign in at the front desk. Activity buses leave at 5:10 p.m. The librarian says the purpose is to give students a quiet place to finish homework after clubs and tutoring. The principal says the school will review sign-in numbers before deciding whether to continue the schedule.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Separate the draft from the source facts. The draft is messy. The source facts tell you what must stay accurate.

Step 2: Identify unsupported opinion. The phrase really good idea is opinion. A news revision should explain facts instead.

Step 3: Find sentence overload. The original draft tries to include too many details in one sentence. Readers need smaller, organized sentences.

Step 4: Order information logically. Start with the schedule change, then purpose, then student instructions, then the future review.

Step 5: Check conventions. Correct punctuation and sentence boundaries help the story look ready for publication.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create three headings: Unclear Wording, Better Order, and Convention Fixes. Use the embedded draft to list at least one item under each heading.

Model Example

Unclear wording: students need places.

Revision: students need a quiet place to finish homework after clubs and tutoring.

Why this works: The revision uses the source fact and tells readers exactly what kind of place students need.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this journalism skill feels new, begin by defining the key term, identifying the audience and purpose, and finding one fact, source detail, or text feature that connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete the task in order: identify the journalistic choice, connect it to accuracy, fairness, clarity, or audience need, and explain why it strengthens or weakens the piece.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by using precise journalism vocabulary, comparing two reporting or editing choices, or explaining how the choice affects credibility, ethics, or reader trust.
