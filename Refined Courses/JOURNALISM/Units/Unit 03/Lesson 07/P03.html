P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now turn notes into a spoken plan. Oral journalism has to be easy to hear and understand the first time. A listener cannot reread a sentence the way a reader can.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Keep sentences short. Long sentences are harder to follow when spoken.

Step 2: Use clear transitions. Phrases like Students should know or The pilot will be reviewed guide listeners.

Step 3: Avoid reading a full article too quickly. Speak from organized notes so pacing stays natural.

Step 4: Preserve accuracy. Oral presentation does not allow exaggeration or unsupported opinion.

Step 5: Check listener action. A good announcement tells listeners what they need to know or do.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Source File: Broadcast Briefing Notes

The student newsroom will give a one-minute morning announcement about the school library staying open until 5:00 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays during a four-week pilot program. The librarian says the schedule gives students a quiet place to finish homework after clubs and tutoring. Students must sign in at the front desk, and activity buses leave at 5:10 p.m. The principal says sign-in numbers will be reviewed before the school decides whether to continue the schedule.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The library is finally open late all week, so everyone should stay after school.

Correct: The library will stay open until 5:00 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays during a four-week pilot program.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version changes the schedule and adds opinion. The correct version gives listeners the verified schedule clearly.
