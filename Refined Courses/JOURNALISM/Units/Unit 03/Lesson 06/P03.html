P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now connect vocabulary to news style. A journalist does not choose complicated words just to sound smart. A journalist chooses accurate words and explains them when readers need help.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Decide whether a term is necessary. Contamination is useful because it names a real recycling problem.

Step 2: Explain the term in context. The article can say, Contamination happens when non-recyclable items are mixed with recyclable materials.

Step 3: Use derivation to support meaning. The suffix -able in recyclable signals ability or possibility.

Step 4: Revise vague language. Replace stuff, things, or bad items with specific nouns.

Step 5: Keep tone clear and respectful. Do not blame readers for confusion. Explain the sorting process clearly.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Source File: News Style Vocabulary Notes

The student newspaper is writing about a new recycling initiative. The environmental club will place clearly labeled bins near the cafeteria, gym, and library. The club adviser says the initiative is meant to reduce contamination, which happens when non-recyclable items are mixed with recyclable materials. A student volunteer says concise signs will help students sort items quickly. The article must explain the initiative, define unfamiliar terms, and use precise news style.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The club is doing a thing to stop bad trash from messing up recycling.

Correct: The environmental club is starting a recycling initiative to reduce contamination, which happens when non-recyclable items are mixed with recyclable materials.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect sentence is vague and informal. The correct sentence uses precise vocabulary and defines the key term in context.

Notebook Directions

Write one sentence that explains contamination in a news-style way. Then underline the word or phrase that helps readers understand the term.
