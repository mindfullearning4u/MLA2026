P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Using Vocabulary and Word Parts for Clear News Style

Vocabulary

  • Initiative: a planned action or program meant to solve a problem.
  • Contamination: the mixing of unwanted material into something else.
  • Recyclable: able to be processed and used again.
  • Concise: brief but clear.
  • Derivation: how a word is formed from roots, prefixes, or suffixes.

Embedded Source

Embedded Source File: News Style Vocabulary Notes

The student newspaper is writing about a new recycling initiative. The environmental club will place clearly labeled bins near the cafeteria, gym, and library. The club adviser says the initiative is meant to reduce contamination, which happens when non-recyclable items are mixed with recyclable materials. A student volunteer says concise signs will help students sort items quickly. The article must explain the initiative, define unfamiliar terms, and use precise news style.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify words readers may not know. In this source, initiative, contamination, recyclable, and concise may need support.

Step 2: Use context first. The source explains contamination as non-recyclable items being mixed with recyclable materials.

Step 3: Use word parts. In recyclable, the suffix -able means able to be. So recyclable means able to be recycled.

Step 4: Define terms naturally. A news article should define unfamiliar words without sounding like a dictionary list.

Step 5: Choose precise news style. Instead of writing bad stuff in the bins, write non-recyclable items mixed with recyclable materials.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create three headings: Word, Context Clue, and Student-Friendly Explanation. Complete the chart for initiative, contamination, recyclable, and concise.

Model Example

Word: contamination

Context clue: non-recyclable items are mixed with recyclable materials.

Student-friendly explanation: Contamination happens when the wrong items are placed in recycling bins.

Learner Support Path

Standard path: Complete the task in order: identify the journalistic choice, connect it to accuracy, fairness, clarity, or audience need, and explain why it strengthens or weakens the piece.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by using precise journalism vocabulary, comparing two reporting or editing choices, or explaining how the choice affects credibility, ethics, or reader trust.
