P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Publication Convention Check: From Rough Draft to Reader-Ready Copy

Vocabulary

  • Convention: a standard rule for grammar, punctuation, capitalization, spelling, and formatting.
  • Copyediting: checking writing for accuracy, clarity, style, and conventions before publication.
  • Sentence boundary: the place where one complete sentence ends and another begins.
  • Direct quotation: a source's exact words placed inside quotation marks.
  • Publication readiness: the level of polish needed before an article is shared with readers.

Embedded Source

Embedded Draft for Publication Check

Draft: the drama club will perform Our Town friday at 7 p.m. in the auditorium tickets are five dollars and students can buy them at lunch. drama teacher Ms. Rivera said the cast has practiced since september. The club president said, we want people to see how much work everyone has done.

Source facts available: The Drama Club will perform Our Town on Friday at 7:00 p.m. in the auditorium. Tickets cost $5 and may be purchased during lunch. Drama teacher Ms. Rivera said the cast has practiced since September. Club president Lena Ortiz said, "We want people to see how much work everyone has done."

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Compare the draft to the source facts. Do not guess. Use the source facts to protect names, titles, dates, times, prices, and quotes.

Step 2: Correct capitalization. The draft should capitalize The Drama Club, Friday, September, and proper names.

Step 3: Correct sentence boundaries. The first draft runs several ideas together. Break them into complete sentences.

Step 4: Correct quotation punctuation. Exact source words need quotation marks, and the speaker should be identified accurately.

Step 5: Check title formatting and numbers. The play title should be handled consistently, and ticket cost should match the source fact: $5.

Step 6: Read the edited version as a reader. If the article looks careless, readers may question the reporting.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create four headings: Capitalization, Punctuation, Quote Accuracy, and Source Fact Check. Under each heading, list one correction needed in the draft.

Model Example

Error: friday

Correction: Friday

Why this works: Days of the week are capitalized in publication-ready writing, and the correction does not change the source meaning.
