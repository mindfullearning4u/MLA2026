📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 03 | Lesson 01

P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Building an Expository News Brief from Source Notes

Vocabulary

  • Expository news writing: journalism that explains verified information clearly.
  • Lead: the opening sentence or sentences that give the most important news.
  • Essential fact: information readers need first to understand the story.
  • Supporting detail: information that adds explanation after the essential facts.
  • Attribution: naming where information came from.

Embedded Source

Embedded Source File: Library Hours News Brief Notes

The school library will stay open until 5:00 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays beginning next week. The librarian says the change is meant to give students a quiet place to finish homework after clubs and tutoring. The pilot program will run for four weeks. Students must sign in at the front desk, and activity buses will leave at 5:10 p.m. The principal says the school will review sign-in numbers before deciding whether to continue the schedule.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the news. The news is not simply that the library exists. The news is that the school library will stay open later on Tuesdays and Thursdays beginning next week.

Step 2: Identify who is affected. Students who need a quiet place after clubs and tutoring are directly affected. Families may also need to know about the 5:10 p.m. activity bus.

Step 3: Choose the lead information. A strong expository news lead should include what is changing, when it starts, and why it matters.

Step 4: Add supporting details in order. After the lead, explain the four-week pilot, sign-in requirement, bus time, and how the school will decide whether to continue.

Step 5: Keep the tone factual. Do not write that the plan is amazing or terrible. Explain what sources said and what readers need to know.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create three headings: Lead Facts, Supporting Details, and Source Attribution. Under each heading, list information from the embedded source. Do not add information that is not in the source.

Model Example

Lead Facts: Library open until 5:00 p.m.; Tuesdays and Thursdays; begins next week.

Supporting Detail: Pilot runs for four weeks and sign-in numbers will be reviewed.

Attribution: The librarian explains the purpose; the principal explains how the decision will be reviewed.
