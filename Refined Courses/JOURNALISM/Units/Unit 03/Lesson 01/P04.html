P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Choose Lead Facts

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: Which facts belong first?

Modeled thinking: Readers need the change, time, days, and start date before extra explanation.

Strong lead facts: library open until 5:00 p.m.; Tuesdays and Thursdays; begins next week.

Reasoning: These facts answer the immediate reader question: What is changing?

Example 2: Add Source Attribution

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Source detail: The librarian says the change gives students a quiet place after clubs and tutoring.

Modeled thinking: Because this reason comes from a source, I should attribute it.

Publication-ready sentence: The librarian said the schedule is meant to give students a quiet place to finish homework after clubs and tutoring.

Reasoning: Attribution tells readers where the explanation came from.

Example 3: Organize Supporting Details

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Detail set: sign in at the front desk; buses leave at 5:10 p.m.; school reviews sign-in numbers.

Modeled thinking: Put practical student instructions before the future decision process.

Order: sign-in rule, bus time, review of sign-in numbers.

Reasoning: Students need to know what to do before they need to know how the program will be evaluated.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Bury the schedule change after several background sentences.

Correct: Put the schedule change in the lead, then add reason, logistics, and next steps.

Teachable explanation: News writing respects the reader by giving the most important verified information first.
