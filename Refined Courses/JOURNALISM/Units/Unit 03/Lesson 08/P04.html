P04 Worked Example

Example 1: News Brief Choice

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Explain the recycling initiative.

Modeled thinking: A news brief needs verified facts first.

Example: The environmental club will place clearly labeled recycling bins near the cafeteria, gym, and library to help students sort materials correctly.

Reasoning: This sentence explains what is happening, where, and why.

Example 2: Editorial Choice

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Build an editorial claim.

Modeled thinking: The claim should be supportable and connected to the source.

Example: The school should consider adding short recycling reminders to morning announcements because concise guidance may help students sort items correctly.

Reasoning: The claim uses the volunteer comment about concise signs and applies it carefully to reminders.

Example 3: Oral Briefing Choice

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Tell students what to do.

Modeled thinking: A spoken announcement must be short and action-focused.

Example: Look for labeled recycling bins near the cafeteria, gym, and library, and check the signs before placing items in the bins.

Reasoning: The sentence gives listeners a clear action.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use one dramatic sentence for every Unit 3 task.

Correct: Match the writing or presentation choice to the purpose: news, feature, editorial, revision, vocabulary, or oral briefing.

Teachable explanation: Unit 3 synthesis requires choosing the right journalism tool for the task.
