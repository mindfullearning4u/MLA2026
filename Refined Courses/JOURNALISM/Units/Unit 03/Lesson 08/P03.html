P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now use the synthesis chart to make journalism decisions. Unit 3 mastery means you can tell the difference between a factual news task, a feature task, an editorial task, a revision task, a vocabulary task, and an oral presentation task.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Match the form to the purpose. Use a news brief to explain the initiative, a feature to highlight people, an editorial to argue a position, and an oral briefing to inform listeners quickly.

Step 2: Keep evidence connected to the task. Do not use a feature detail as if it proves an editorial claim unless the connection is explained.

Step 3: Revise for readers. Remove vague wording and define unfamiliar terms naturally.

Step 4: Protect source meaning. Synthesis does not mean mixing facts carelessly. Each choice must stay accurate.

Step 5: Prepare for assessment. When answering Unit Assessment items, identify the standard skill first, then choose the answer that best applies it.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Unit 3 Synthesis Scenario

The student newspaper is preparing a package about a new school recycling initiative. Source notes show that labeled bins will be placed near the cafeteria, gym, and library. The environmental club adviser says the goal is to reduce contamination, which happens when non-recyclable items are mixed with recyclable materials. A student volunteer says concise signs will help students sort items quickly. The editorial board is considering an opinion piece arguing that the school should also add a short recycling reminder to morning announcements. The package needs a factual news brief, a feature angle, an editorial claim with evidence and rebuttal, clear revision, publication conventions, vocabulary support, and a short oral briefing.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The package should say recycling will solve the school trash problem because everyone supports it.

Correct: The package can report that labeled bins are planned and explain that the initiative aims to reduce contamination by helping students sort items correctly.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version overstates the source. The correct version uses verified facts and explains the purpose accurately.

Notebook Directions
