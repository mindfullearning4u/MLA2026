P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Feature writing can be more descriptive than a straight news brief, but it must still be accurate. A feature opening should invite the reader into the story while staying grounded in reported facts.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Start with a concrete detail. A bike repair, a careful note, or a student walking home can create a clear image.

Step 2: Connect the detail to meaning. The detail should lead readers toward the story angle, not distract them.

Step 3: Avoid invented emotion. You can write what Maya said or did. You cannot claim what she secretly felt unless the source says it.

Step 4: Maintain journalistic tone. Feature writing may be vivid, but it should still be fair, respectful, and source-based.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Source File: Feature Profile Notes

Senior Maya Chen repairs donated bicycles after school and gives them to students who need transportation. She started after noticing two classmates walking more than two miles home after practice. Maya says fixing the bikes takes patience because every repair teaches her something different. Her adviser says Maya keeps careful notes, asks for help when a repair affects safety, and treats each recipient respectfully. One student says receiving a bike made it easier to stay for tutoring.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Maya is the school's greatest hero, saving every student from impossible problems.

Correct: After noticing classmates walking more than two miles home after practice, senior Maya Chen began repairing donated bicycles for students who needed transportation.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version exaggerates and makes unsupported claims. The correct version uses a real source detail and creates a clear human-interest angle.

Notebook Directions

Write one feature opening sentence using a concrete source detail. Then explain how the sentence shows point of view without inventing information.
