Putting It All Together: Unit 5 Synthesis

Embedded Source Reminder

Unit 5 Digital Publication Scenario. Project: The journalism staff is preparing a final online package called New Student Guide. Required parts: a digital presentation explaining three key findings, a sequential image strip showing how to find student services, an accurate feature photo, a revised article, and an online publication page. Team Needs: the package must be cohesive, visually accurate, captioned, reviewed, exported, submitted on time, and appropriate for new students and families. Production Risk: one image is outdated, one caption is vague, the article needs final revision, and the team has two days before publication.

Deeper Learning: Synthesis Before Assessment

In a synthesis lesson, you do more than remember definitions. You decide how several standards work together in one realistic situation.

For example, a package can have a strong digital presentation but still fail if the image is outdated. It can have accurate visuals but still fail if the team misses the deadline. It can meet the deadline but still fail if the article was not revised for clarity.

Your job is to judge the whole package, not one isolated part.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Treat Lesson 8 as a place to add new standards or new skills that were not taught earlier.

Correct: Use Lesson 8 to synthesize Unit 5 skills: digital presentation, sequence, visual accuracy, publication workflow, time management, and collaboration.

Teachable explanation: Lesson 8 is a mastery synthesis. The goal is not to surprise students with new material. The goal is to show that previously taught skills can be used together to solve a complete journalism production problem.

Notebook Directions

Write one paragraph explaining why a final review must check digital presentation, visual accuracy, sequence, revision, collaboration, and deadline workflow together.
