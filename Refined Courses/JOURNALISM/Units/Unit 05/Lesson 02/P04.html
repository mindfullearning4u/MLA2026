P04 Worked Example

Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: Why does the video start with a wide shot?

Modeled thinking: A wide shot gives context before details. Viewers need to see the station before they see labels.

Answer: The wide shot introduces the hallway station so viewers know where the recycling process happens.

Reasoning: This makes the sequence easier to follow.

Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: Why use a close-up of labels?

Modeled thinking: The audience needs to read the labels to choose the correct bin.

Answer: The close-up helps viewers see the categories clearly before the student makes a choice.

Reasoning: This shot supports accuracy and audience understanding.

Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: Why should background sound stay low?

Modeled thinking: The narration teaches the steps. If music covers it, the audience may miss the instructions.

Answer: Background sound should stay low so narration remains clear.

Reasoning: Sound must support the journalism purpose, not distract from it.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The video is accurate as long as it shows a recycling bin somewhere.

Correct: The video is accurate only if it shows the real labels and the bottle going into the correct bin.

Teachable explanation: Visual accuracy matters because viewers may copy what they see.
