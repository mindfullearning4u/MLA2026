P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now evaluate the sequence. A sequence works when each shot prepares the audience for the next shot. If the order is confusing, the audience may learn the wrong process.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Check the beginning. The wide shot establishes location.

Step 2: Check the middle. The close-up and student decision show what the viewer should notice and do.

Step 3: Check the action. The correct bin placement confirms the process.

Step 4: Check the ending. The final screen summarizes the lesson in one memorable sentence.

Step 5: Check accuracy. If the video shows the wrong bin, the audience may repeat the wrong behavior.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Storyboard: Hallway Recycling Video

Project Goal: Create a 30-second news video showing how students should use the new hallway recycling stations.

Sequence: Shot 1: wide shot of the hallway station. Shot 2: close-up of the labels for paper, bottles, and trash. Shot 3: student holds a plastic bottle and pauses before choosing a bin. Shot 4: student places the bottle in the correct bin. Shot 5: final screen says Check the label before you drop it.

Sound Plan: Short narration explains each step. Light background sound is allowed only if it does not cover the narration.

Accuracy Note: The video must show the real labels and correct bin placement so viewers do not learn the wrong process.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Begin with a fast montage of students walking because it looks exciting.

Correct: Begin with a wide shot of the recycling station so viewers know where the process happens.

Teachable explanation: An exciting shot is not automatically useful. The first shot must orient the audience to the setting and purpose.

Notebook Directions

Write two sequence-evaluation sentences. One sentence should explain why the order works. The other should explain what accuracy problem could confuse viewers.
