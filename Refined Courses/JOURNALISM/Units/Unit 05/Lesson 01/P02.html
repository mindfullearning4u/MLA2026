P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Planning a Digital Reporting Presentation

Set up your notebook to separate the reporting topic, goal, evidence, visual choice, and audience need.

Vocabulary

  • Digital presentation: a slide-based or screen-based communication that organizes ideas for an audience.
  • Perspective: the focused angle or point of view the presentation develops.
  • Coherent: organized so the audience can follow the ideas easily.
  • Credible evidence: trustworthy information that supports the presentation.
  • Caption: text that explains a visual and connects it to the report.

Embedded Source

Embedded Presentation Plan: Transit Safety Report

Reporting Topic: Students crossing the east parking lot after dismissal.

Source Notes: The school safety aide counted thirty-seven students crossing between parked cars on Tuesday. Two students said the marked crosswalk is hard to see when buses are lined up. The assistant principal said the school can add temporary cones while it reviews permanent signs.

Presentation Goal: Explain the safety concern clearly and recommend a short-term solution without exaggerating the problem.

Draft Slide Sequence: Slide 1: headline and central finding. Slide 2: photo of the crossing area with caption. Slide 3: key evidence from the count and student comments. Slide 4: recommendation for cones and clearer crosswalk markings. Slide 5: source note and next step.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the reporting topic. The report is about students crossing the east parking lot after dismissal.

Step 2: Identify the presentation goal. The goal is to explain the safety concern and recommend a short-term solution without exaggerating.

Step 3: Choose the central evidence. The count of thirty-seven students, student comments about the crosswalk, and the assistant principal statement are the strongest evidence.

Step 4: Build a logical sequence. Start with the finding, show the location, explain evidence, give the recommendation, and end with source notes and next steps.

Step 5: Match visual to purpose. A photo of the crossing area helps the audience see the problem, but it must have a caption that explains what to notice.

Step 6: Protect accuracy. Do not use dramatic wording that makes the situation sound worse than the evidence shows.

Notebook Directions

Create a five-row planning chart: topic, goal, evidence, visual, and audience need. Fill in each row using the embedded source.

Model Example

Evidence: Thirty-seven students crossed between parked cars on Tuesday.

Presentation use: This detail belongs on an evidence slide because it shows the concern is observable and specific.
