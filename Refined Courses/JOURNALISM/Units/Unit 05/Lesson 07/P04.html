P04 Worked Example

Integrated Digital Publication Package

Embedded Source

Digital Publication Package. Project: The journalism team is preparing a web package called Cafeteria Changes Explained. Required parts: a short explanatory article, one accurate feature photo, a three-image sequence showing the lunch line process, a 30-second audio caption, and a publication checklist. Audience: students and families who need clear, accurate information before Monday. Visual Accuracy Concern: one photo shows an old menu board that is no longer used. Workflow Concern: the package must be reviewed, captioned, exported, and submitted by Friday at 3:00 p.m. Publication Goal: the final package must use accurate visuals, clear sequence, coherent digital presentation, and an organized online layout.

Example 1: Visual Accuracy

Question: Should the old menu-board photo be used?

Step 1: Identify what the image communicates. It tells the audience what the cafeteria looks like.

Step 2: Compare the image to current facts. The menu board is no longer used.

Step 3: Make a publication decision. Replace the image or add accurate context.

Reasoning: Accuracy matters more than color, size, or convenience.

Example 2: Sequence

Question: How should the three-image process be ordered?

Step 1: Find the process the audience needs to understand: the lunch line.

Step 2: Put images in time order: enter the line, choose food, check out.

Step 3: Add captions that explain each step.

Reasoning: Sequence turns separate images into useful information.

Example 3: Deadline Workflow

Question: What should happen before Friday at 3:00 p.m.?

Step 1: List remaining tasks: review, captions, export, submit.

Step 2: Place accuracy checks before export.

Step 3: Submit only after all parts are complete.

Reasoning: A package that is exported before it is checked can spread errors quickly.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Publish the old menu-board photo because it is bright and fills the page.

Correct: Replace or recaption the image so the visual record matches the current cafeteria change.

Teachable explanation: Digital journalism requires the team to check what images imply. A misleading visual can damage credibility even if the written article is accurate.
