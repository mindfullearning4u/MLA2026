P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Collaborative Revision and Online Publication Planning

Set up your notebook to track feedback, revision action, publication element, and audience purpose.

Vocabulary

  • Collaborative platform: an online space where team members draft, comment, revise, and prepare work.
  • Publication-ready: polished enough to share with the intended audience.
  • Deck: a short summary line under a headline.
  • Alt text: text that describes an image for accessibility.
  • Export: preparing a final file or page for publication.

Embedded Source

Embedded Publication Scenario: Collaborative Online Feature Package

Publication Task: A student newsroom team is preparing an online feature package about the school garden reopening.

Draft Elements: The reporter wrote a 450-word feature. The photo editor added three images with captions. The copy editor left comments asking for a clearer transition between the garden history and the reopening date. The web editor needs a headline, deck, image order, alt text, and export-ready layout.

Audience: Students, families, and community volunteers who may want to visit or help maintain the garden.

Feedback Notes: One paragraph repeats the same background detail twice. One caption says "students working" but does not identify the garden bed. The final paragraph needs a clearer call to action for volunteers.

Publication Requirement: The team must revise for clarity, respond to comments, maintain format-specific quality, and export a polished online article for the intended audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the audience. The article is for students, families, and community volunteers.

Step 2: Identify revision needs. The draft repeats one background detail, needs a clearer transition, needs better captions, and needs a clearer call to action.

Step 3: Connect feedback to action. A comment about transition means the writer should add a sentence connecting garden history to the reopening date.

Step 4: Prepare publication elements. The web editor needs headline, deck, image order, alt text, and layout.

Step 5: Tailor to audience. The call to action should tell volunteers how they can help or visit.

Step 6: Export only after revision. Publication-ready work must be clear, cohesive, accessible, and formatted correctly.

Notebook Directions

Create a four-column chart: feedback note, revision action, publication element, and audience purpose. Add at least four entries from the embedded source.

Model Example

Feedback note: Caption says "students working" but does not identify the garden bed.

Revision action: Revise the caption to name the garden bed and explain what students are doing.

Audience purpose: Readers understand the image and how it connects to the garden reopening.
