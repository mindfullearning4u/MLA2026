P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Collaborative Media Package Planning

Set up your notebook to track package part, team concern, compromise, and audience effect.

Vocabulary

  • Media package: a group of related journalism pieces published together.
  • Cohesive: connected and consistent in tone, structure, and purpose.
  • Compromise: a respectful solution that balances team needs.
  • Package order: the sequence in which article, video, gallery, and quote card appear.
  • Tone: the attitude or feeling created by media choices.

Embedded Source

Embedded Collaboration Scenario: Multimedia Spirit Week Package

Project: A student media team is creating a multimedia package about Spirit Week for the school website.

Package Parts: A short article, a 20-second recap video, a photo gallery, and a captioned quote card from the student council president.

Team Conflict: The video editor wants fast music and quick cuts. The reporter wants slower pacing so the quote is clear. The photo editor wants the gallery first because the photos are strong. The web editor says the package needs a consistent tone, accurate captions, and a logical order for readers.

Audience: Students, families, and staff who want a clear recap of the week.

Collaboration Need: The team must compromise respectfully, maintain a cohesive package, use accurate visuals, and prepare publication-ready work.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the package purpose. The team is creating a clear Spirit Week recap for students, families, and staff.

Step 2: Identify each team member concern. The video editor wants energy, the reporter wants clarity, the photo editor wants strong photos first, and the web editor wants cohesion.

Step 3: Evaluate audience need. The audience needs a clear recap, not just the fastest or flashiest sequence.

Step 4: Build a compromise. Use energetic music at a low level, keep the quote clear, place the article first for context, then video and gallery.

Step 5: Protect accuracy. Captions must identify events and people accurately before publication.

Step 6: Check cohesion. Every part should feel like it belongs to the same Spirit Week package.

Notebook Directions

Create a chart with four columns: team member, concern, possible compromise, and audience benefit. Add at least four rows from the embedded source.

Model Example

Concern: Video editor wants fast music and quick cuts.

Compromise: Use upbeat music at a lower volume and slow down during the student council quote.

Audience benefit: The package feels energetic while the quote remains understandable.
