P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now deepen the analysis by evaluating the media package. Evaluation means explaining how well the package helps the audience understand the issue and compare claims.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Judge balance. A balanced package gives readers enough information to understand both sides.

Step 2: Judge evidence quality. Survey data is specific, while a general concern may need more support.

Step 3: Judge structure. A news brief followed by opposing editorials creates a logical path from information to interpretation.

Step 4: Judge audience usefulness. A sidebar titled Questions Still Under Review signals that the issue is not fully settled.

Step 5: Write a complete evaluation. Name a feature or claim, cite evidence, and explain its effect on audience understanding.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Media Package: Lunch Schedule Debate

Media Text A: News Brief The school is considering a ten-minute extension to lunch. The proposal would shorten afternoon advisory by ten minutes. The principal says the school is reviewing hallway crowding, lunch line data, and afternoon instructional time before making a decision.

Media Text B: Editorial Excerpt Supporting the Change Students need a longer lunch because current lines leave some students with fewer than ten minutes to eat. A survey from the student newspaper found that 72 percent of respondents support a longer lunch. Supporters argue that a longer lunch would reduce rushing and help students return to class more focused.

Media Text C: Editorial Excerpt Opposing the Change The school should not lengthen lunch if it reduces advisory. Advisory gives students time for grade checks, teacher conferences, and makeup work. Opponents argue that the better solution is to improve line flow instead of removing academic support time.

Media Features: The package includes a comparison chart, a hallway photo caption, and a sidebar titled Questions Still Under Review.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The support argument is better because longer lunch sounds good.

Correct: The support argument has specific survey evidence, but the opposition raises a relevant concern about losing advisory time.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer is personal preference. The correct answer compares evidence and recognizes both sides of the issue.

Notebook Directions

Write two evaluation sentences: one about evidence strength and one about media feature effectiveness. Each sentence must cite the embedded source.
