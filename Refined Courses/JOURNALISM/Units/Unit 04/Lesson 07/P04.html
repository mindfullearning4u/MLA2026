📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 04 | Lesson 07

P04 Worked Example

Example 1

Question: What is the shared issue?

Modeled thinking: I look for the topic that appears in all three texts. Each part discusses whether lunch should be extended.

Answer: The shared issue is whether the school should extend lunch by ten minutes even though it would shorten advisory.

Reasoning: This answer includes the proposal and the tradeoff.

Example 2

Question: Which evidence supports the change?

Modeled thinking: I look in the support editorial for evidence. It says some students have fewer than ten minutes to eat and that 72 percent support the change.

Answer: The survey and lunch-line details support the claim that students need more time.

Reasoning: These details are specific and relevant to the proposed lunch extension.

Example 3

Question: How does the sidebar help the audience?

Modeled thinking: A sidebar titled Questions Still Under Review shows what the school has not decided yet.

Answer: The sidebar helps readers understand that the decision requires more evidence about crowding, lunch lines, and advisory time.

Reasoning: The feature supports careful audience judgment instead of oversimplifying the issue.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The media package is balanced because it has three parts.

Correct: The media package is balanced because it gives neutral information, a supporting editorial, an opposing editorial, and features that help readers compare unresolved questions.

Teachable explanation: Counting parts is not enough. You must explain how the parts function for the audience.
