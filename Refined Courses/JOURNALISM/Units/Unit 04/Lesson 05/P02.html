P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Mood, Figurative Language, and Caption Effects

Set up your notebook so you can connect words and media details to the mood they create.

Vocabulary

  • Mood: the feeling or atmosphere created for the audience.
  • Figurative language: language that means more than its literal wording.
  • Imagery: words or media details that help readers picture a scene.
  • Connotation: the feeling or association a word carries.
  • Effect: what a choice causes readers to notice, feel, or understand.

Embedded Source

Embedded Media Text: Photo Essay Caption Package

Title: The Quiet Work Before Opening Night

Intro: The auditorium is dim except for the blue glow of the stage lights. A ladder leans beside the curtain, scripts rest open on the front row, and a student sweeps sawdust from the edge of the set.

Caption 1: Behind the bright posters and applause, the stage crew builds a small world from tape, paint, and patient hands.

Caption 2: The empty seats wait in silence while the cast repeats the final scene for the fifth time.

Caption 3: By Friday night, the nervous quiet will become music, movement, and a room full of voices.

Design note: The package uses low-light photographs, close-up images of hands and tools, and short captions to create a reflective mood about preparation.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read for atmosphere. The text uses dim light, empty seats, silence, and patient hands. These details create a quiet, reflective mood.

Step 2: Find figurative language. The phrase builds a small world is not literal. It suggests the stage crew creates an entire performance space from small materials.

Step 3: Notice connotation. Words such as patient, quiet, and wait slow the pace and create calm focus.

Step 4: Connect image and caption. Low-light photographs and close-ups support the reflective mood by drawing attention to careful preparation.

Step 5: Explain the effect. The audience is encouraged to respect the hidden work before the performance, not just the final applause.

Notebook Directions

Create a two-column chart: Language or Media Choice and Mood Effect. Add four entries from the embedded source.

Model Example

Language or media choice: "The empty seats wait in silence."

Mood effect: This creates a quiet, expectant mood because the seats are described as if they are waiting for the performance to begin.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this journalism skill feels new, begin by defining the key term, identifying the audience and purpose, and finding one fact, source detail, or text feature that connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete the task in order: identify the journalistic choice, connect it to accuracy, fairness, clarity, or audience need, and explain why it strengthens or weakens the piece.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by using precise journalism vocabulary, comparing two reporting or editing choices, or explaining how the choice affects credibility, ethics, or reader trust.
