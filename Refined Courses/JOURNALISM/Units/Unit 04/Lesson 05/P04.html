P04 Worked Example

Example 1

Question: What mood does the source create?

Modeled thinking: I look for repeated details. The source repeats dimness, silence, waiting, and preparation.

Answer: The source creates a reflective and expectant mood.

Reasoning: Reflective fits the quiet preparation, and expectant fits the sense that the performance is about to begin.

Example 2

Question: How does figurative language affect mood?

Modeled thinking: The phrase "builds a small world" is figurative because the crew is not literally building a world.

Answer: The phrase creates a thoughtful mood by showing the stage crew as careful creators, not just workers moving objects.

Reasoning: The figurative language makes the preparation feel meaningful.

Example 3

Question: How do the images support the mood?

Modeled thinking: Low-light photos and close-ups slow the audience down and focus attention on small details.

Answer: The images support a quiet, reflective mood because they emphasize hidden preparation instead of bright performance energy.

Reasoning: The media choices match the captions and create a consistent effect.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The caption is descriptive because it has details.

Correct: The caption creates a reflective mood by using quiet images such as empty seats, dim light, and patient hands.

Teachable explanation: Description is not the full analysis. You must explain the emotional or atmospheric effect created by the details.
