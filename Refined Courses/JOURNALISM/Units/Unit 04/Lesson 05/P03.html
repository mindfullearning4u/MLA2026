P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from identifying mood to explaining how mood is built. A strong answer names the mood, gives evidence, and explains why that evidence creates the feeling.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Avoid vague mood words. Words like good, bad, or interesting are too general. Use precise words such as reflective, tense, hopeful, urgent, or calm.

Step 2: Choose evidence that actually creates the mood. Empty seats, silence, dim light, and repeated practice all contribute to a reflective mood.

Step 3: Explain figurative language. When the stage crew "builds a small world," the phrase suggests imagination, care, and transformation.

Step 4: Connect to journalism purpose. The media package helps readers value preparation and behind-the-scenes labor.

Step 5: Write the analysis. Use this frame: The phrase ___ creates a ___ mood because ___.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Media Text: Photo Essay Caption Package

Title: The Quiet Work Before Opening Night

Intro: The auditorium is dim except for the blue glow of the stage lights. A ladder leans beside the curtain, scripts rest open on the front row, and a student sweeps sawdust from the edge of the set.

Caption 1: Behind the bright posters and applause, the stage crew builds a small world from tape, paint, and patient hands.

Caption 2: The empty seats wait in silence while the cast repeats the final scene for the fifth time.

Caption 3: By Friday night, the nervous quiet will become music, movement, and a room full of voices.

Design note: The package uses low-light photographs, close-up images of hands and tools, and short captions to create a reflective mood about preparation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The mood is happy because the story is about a play.

Correct: The mood is reflective because the source focuses on dim lights, silence, waiting seats, and careful preparation before the performance.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer guesses from the topic. The correct answer uses evidence from language and media details to explain the mood.

Notebook Directions

Write two mood-analysis sentences. Each sentence must name a precise mood, cite one source detail, and explain how that detail creates the mood.
