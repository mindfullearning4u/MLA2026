P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 5: Mood and language effects

This lesson teaches how figurative language, connotation, imagery, and caption choices create mood in journalistic media. Mood is not just a feeling word. In media literacy, mood is an effect created by specific choices that shape how an audience experiences a story.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.JOUR.MED.05 - Figurative Language and Mood.

Support Standards: MLA.JOUR.NEWS.08 - Connotation, Denotation, and News Style; MLA.JOUR.MED.03 - Author Purpose, Appeals, and Language.

What You Will Learn

  • How mood is created by words, images, and captions.
  • How figurative language affects audience response.
  • How connotation changes the feeling of a media text.
  • How to explain language effects with evidence.

What You Will Do

  • Read an embedded photo essay caption package.
  • Identify mood and the choices that create it.
  • Explain how specific words and images affect readers.
  • Complete notebook work, guided practice, independent work, checkpoint, and quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Name the mood accurately.
  • Use source evidence to explain the mood.
  • Analyze figurative language and connotation.
  • Submit checkpoint evidence aligned to MLA.JOUR.MED.05.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

In student language: I can explain how language and media choices create a mood for the audience.
