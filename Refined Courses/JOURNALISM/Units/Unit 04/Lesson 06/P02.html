P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Explaining Rhetoric in an Editorial Media Post

Set up your notebook to track claim, purpose, appeal, evidence, and audience effect.

Vocabulary

  • Rhetoric: choices used to influence an audience.
  • Claim: the position the writer wants readers to accept.
  • Appeal: a persuasive strategy such as logic, safety, fairness, or emotion.
  • Audience: the readers or viewers the message is designed to reach.
  • Effectiveness: how well the rhetorical choices support the purpose.

Embedded Source

Embedded Media Text: Editorial Media Post

Headline: Keep the Courtyard Open After School

Opening: The courtyard should remain open for one hour after dismissal because students need a supervised place to wait, study, and meet before activities.

Body: Last month, more than forty students waited outside the front office each afternoon before clubs, tutoring, or rides home. A supervised courtyard would move students away from crowded sidewalks and give staff a clear place to monitor. The proposal is not asking for unlimited campus access. It asks for one supervised hour, clear behavior rules, and a sign-in table.

Rhetorical choices: The post uses a practical appeal by naming the time limit and supervision plan. It uses a safety appeal by mentioning crowded sidewalks. It uses a fairness appeal by explaining that students waiting for activities need a reasonable place to be.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the claim. The post claims the courtyard should remain open for one hour after dismissal.

Step 2: Identify the purpose. The writer wants the school audience to support the proposal.

Step 3: Find a practical appeal. The post names a one-hour limit, behavior rules, and a sign-in table. This makes the proposal seem manageable.

Step 4: Find a safety appeal. The post mentions crowded sidewalks and supervision. This makes the proposal seem safer than the current situation.

Step 5: Find a fairness appeal. The post explains that students waiting for clubs, tutoring, or rides need a reasonable place to be.

Step 6: Explain rhetorical effect. The rhetoric makes the proposal seem controlled, safe, and fair.

Notebook Directions

Create a chart with four columns: Rhetorical Choice, Source Evidence, Audience Effect, and How It Supports the Claim. Add three entries.

Model Example

Rhetorical choice: Practical appeal.

Source evidence: One supervised hour, clear behavior rules, and a sign-in table.

Audience effect: The plan seems organized and realistic.

Connection to claim: This supports the claim by answering concerns about uncontrolled campus access.
