P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now evaluate rhetoric. Evaluation means deciding how well the rhetorical choices support the claim for the intended audience.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Match appeal to audience concern. Administrators may worry about supervision, so the supervision plan directly addresses that concern.

Step 2: Look for specific evidence. The detail about more than forty students waiting outside gives the problem size.

Step 3: Identify a possible counterclaim. Some readers may worry that open courtyard time could become too loose or noisy.

Step 4: Notice how rhetoric responds. The post says the proposal is not unlimited access and includes rules and sign-in.

Step 5: Write an effectiveness judgment. Explain whether the rhetoric makes the claim more persuasive and why.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Media Text: Editorial Media Post

Headline: Keep the Courtyard Open After School

Opening: The courtyard should remain open for one hour after dismissal because students need a supervised place to wait, study, and meet before activities.

Body: Last month, more than forty students waited outside the front office each afternoon before clubs, tutoring, or rides home. A supervised courtyard would move students away from crowded sidewalks and give staff a clear place to monitor. The proposal is not asking for unlimited campus access. It asks for one supervised hour, clear behavior rules, and a sign-in table.

Rhetorical choices: The post uses a practical appeal by naming the time limit and supervision plan. It uses a safety appeal by mentioning crowded sidewalks. It uses a fairness appeal by explaining that students waiting for activities need a reasonable place to be.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The rhetoric is strong because students want the courtyard open.

Correct: The rhetoric is strong because it uses practical details, safety concerns, and fairness appeals to address audience concerns about supervision and student needs.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer relies on preference. The correct answer names the rhetorical choices and explains how they influence the audience.

Notebook Directions

Write two effectiveness judgments. Each one must name a rhetorical appeal, cite source evidence, and explain how the appeal supports the claim.
