P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: What is the purpose of the media post?

Modeled thinking: I look for what the writer asks readers to do. The post invites students to reduce wasted food and packaging.

Answer: The purpose is to persuade students to reduce cafeteria waste through specific lunch habits.

Reasoning: This answer is stronger than saying "the post is about waste" because it includes the writer action: persuade students to change behavior.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: Which detail creates a logical appeal?

Modeled thinking: A logical appeal uses facts, numbers, or clear reasons. The detail about twelve full trash bags is measurable evidence.

Answer: The trash-bag count creates a logical appeal because it proves the problem is visible and measurable.

Reasoning: The detail gives readers a concrete reason to believe action is needed.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: How does the final sentence use language?

Modeled thinking: I notice positive words: cleaner, calmer, responsible. These words make the action sound helpful instead of annoying.

Answer: The final sentence uses positive connotations to make waste reduction feel practical and responsible.

Reasoning: The words shape the audience response and support the persuasive purpose.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The phrase "small daily choices" is important because it sounds nice.

Correct: The phrase "small daily choices" makes the action feel manageable, which supports the purpose of persuading students to participate.

Teachable Explanation: A style comment is not enough. You must explain how the words affect readers and connect that effect to purpose.
