P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now deepen your analysis. Strong media analysis does not stop at naming an appeal. It explains how the appeal works on the audience.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Start with purpose. The purpose is to persuade students to reduce cafeteria waste.

Step 2: Choose one rhetorical choice. Use evidence, emotional wording, credible source information, or a call to action.

Step 3: Ask what the audience might think. Concrete evidence may make students think the problem is measurable. Positive wording may make action feel possible.

Step 4: Write an effect sentence. Use this frame: The writer uses ___ to help readers ___.

Step 5: Check accuracy. Your explanation must match the source. Do not claim the text uses fear if the wording is mostly positive and responsible.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Media Text: Student Campaign Web Post

Headline: Join the Cafeteria Waste Challenge

Opening: Every lunch tray tells a story about our school choices. This month, the student newspaper invites every table to reduce wasted food and packaging.

Body: Last week, cafeteria staff counted twelve full trash bags after one lunch period. The environmental club says many unopened milk cartons and packaged snacks were thrown away. The post asks students to take only what they plan to eat, place unopened items on the share table when allowed, and sort bottles into recycling bins. It ends with the line, Small daily choices can make our cafeteria cleaner, calmer, and more responsible.

Sidebar: Three Ways to Help Today: take only what you need, use the share table correctly, and recycle bottles before leaving.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author uses rhetoric because the article is about trash.

Correct: The author uses the detail about twelve full trash bags as a logical appeal to show that cafeteria waste is a real problem.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer only names the topic. The correct answer names the specific choice, identifies the appeal, and explains how it supports the author purpose.
