P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Author Purpose and Rhetorical Appeals in a Media Post

Set up your notebook so you can separate topic, purpose, appeal, language, and audience effect.

Vocabulary

  • Purpose: what the writer wants to accomplish.
  • Rhetoric: language and strategies used to influence an audience.
  • Appeal: a way of persuading readers, such as logic, credibility, or emotion.
  • Connotation: the feeling or association a word creates beyond its dictionary meaning.
  • Effect: what a choice does to the reader.

Embedded Source

Embedded Media Text: Student Campaign Web Post

Headline: Join the Cafeteria Waste Challenge

Opening: Every lunch tray tells a story about our school choices. This month, the student newspaper invites every table to reduce wasted food and packaging.

Body: Last week, cafeteria staff counted twelve full trash bags after one lunch period. The environmental club says many unopened milk cartons and packaged snacks were thrown away. The post asks students to take only what they plan to eat, place unopened items on the share table when allowed, and sort bottles into recycling bins. It ends with the line, Small daily choices can make our cafeteria cleaner, calmer, and more responsible.

Sidebar: Three Ways to Help Today: take only what you need, use the share table correctly, and recycle bottles before leaving.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the topic. The topic is cafeteria waste.

Step 2: Identify the purpose. The writer wants students to reduce waste by changing lunch habits.

Step 3: Find logical appeal. The text uses the detail about twelve full trash bags and unopened items. That evidence gives readers a reason to believe the problem is real.

Step 4: Find emotional appeal. The phrase Every lunch tray tells a story asks readers to see their choices as meaningful.

Step 5: Analyze language. Words such as cleaner, calmer, and responsible have positive connotations. They make the action feel useful and mature.

Step 6: Connect choice to purpose. The writer uses evidence and positive language to persuade students that reducing waste is practical and worth doing.

Notebook Directions

Create a three-column chart: Text Detail, Appeal or Language Choice, and How It Supports Purpose. Add at least three entries from the embedded source.

Model Example

Text detail: Twelve full trash bags after one lunch period.

Appeal or language choice: Logical appeal.

How it supports purpose: It gives concrete evidence that waste is a real problem, so readers have a reason to change behavior.
