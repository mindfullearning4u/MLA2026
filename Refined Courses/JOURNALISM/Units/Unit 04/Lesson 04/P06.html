P06 Independent Work

Instructions

Use the embedded opposing arguments to show that you can compare claims and evaluate evidence. Write in complete sentences and avoid personal preference unless it is supported by evidence.

Embedded Source

Embedded Media Text: Two Editorial Drafts on the Same Topic

Topic: Should the school create a phone-free lunch area?

Argument A: The school should create a phone-free lunch area because many students need a place to talk without screens taking over the table. A guidance counselor says some students report feeling ignored when everyone around them is scrolling. A pilot table in the media center attracted twenty students during one week, and several said it helped them meet classmates. Supporters say the area would be optional, so students who want to use phones at lunch could still sit elsewhere.

Argument B: The school should not create a phone-free lunch area because lunch is one of the few flexible times students have during the day. Many students use phones to check family messages, manage club reminders, or finish quick tasks. A student council survey found that sixty-eight percent of respondents opposed lunch phone restrictions. Opponents argue that a separate area may divide students instead of teaching respectful conversation habits for all tables.

Part A

State the claim of Argument A and the claim of Argument B in one complete comparison sentence.

Part B

Choose the strongest evidence from each argument. Explain why each detail supports its claim.

Part C

Evaluate which argument is more effective for a school audience. Your answer must include evidence and at least one limitation.
