P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

How Media Structure Guides Readers

Vocabulary

  • Media text: a communication product that may include words, images, layout, audio, video, or interactive features.
  • Text structure: the way information is organized.
  • Feature: a design or information element such as a headline, sidebar, caption, or map.
  • Sidebar: a boxed or separate section that gives quick supporting information.
  • Caption: text that explains an image.

Embedded Source

Embedded Media Text: School Safety Web Article

Headline: New Bike Rack Area Opens Near East Gate

Subheading: The school adds marked parking spaces and a short safety checklist for student cyclists.

Body: The school opened a new bike rack area near the east gate on Monday. The article begins with the schedule change, then explains why the location was chosen, then lists safety reminders. A sidebar titled Before You Ride reminds students to lock bikes, walk bikes on sidewalks, and use helmets. A photo caption identifies the east gate entrance and notes that the rack area is visible from the front office. A map shows the route from the bike racks to the main courtyard.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the main structure. The article begins with the schedule change, explains the location, and then gives safety reminders. This structure moves from news to explanation to action.

Step 2: Identify the headline. The headline tells readers the main news quickly: a new bike rack area is open.

Step 3: Identify the subheading. The subheading adds two important details: marked parking spaces and a safety checklist.

Step 4: Identify the sidebar. The sidebar gives quick action steps before students ride.

Step 5: Identify the caption and map. The caption explains the image, and the map helps readers locate the route.

Step 6: Explain function. Naming a feature is not enough. You must explain how it helps readers understand, locate, remember, or act on information.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create three columns: Feature, Information It Gives, and How It Helps the Audience. Fill in the chart for headline, subheading, sidebar, caption, and map.

Model Example

Feature: Sidebar titled Before You Ride.

Information: Lock bikes, walk bikes on sidewalks, and use helmets.

Audience help: The sidebar gives quick safety actions without making readers search through the whole article.
