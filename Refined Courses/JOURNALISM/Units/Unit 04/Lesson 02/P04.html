📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 04 | Lesson 02

P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Topic vs. Central Idea

Topic: arriving on time.

Modeled thinking: The topic is too broad by itself. I need to state what the article says about arriving on time.

Central idea: consistent arrival helps students begin class prepared.

Reasoning: This sentence states the message, not just the subject.

Example 2: Detail Support

Detail: late students miss opening instructions.

Modeled thinking: Opening instructions prepare students for the class period.

Support explanation: This detail supports the central idea because it shows one way lateness can prevent students from starting prepared.

Reasoning: The detail is directly relevant.

Example 3: Structure Support

Structure: campaign introduced, problem explained, source details given, advisory action described.

Modeled thinking: This order moves from message to support to action.

Function: The structure helps readers understand why the campaign exists and what advisory teachers will do.

Reasoning: Structure develops the central idea across the text.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Any detail in the article automatically supports the central idea equally.

Correct: A detail supports the central idea when it directly develops the main message.

Teachable explanation: Evaluation requires judging the connection between evidence and idea, not just listing facts.
