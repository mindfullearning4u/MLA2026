P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Finding Central Idea and Support

Vocabulary

  • Topic: the subject a text is about.
  • Central idea: the main point or message the text develops about the topic.
  • Supporting detail: a fact, example, quote, or explanation that develops the central idea.
  • Relevant evidence: evidence that directly connects to the central idea.
  • Development: the way details build and explain an idea across a text.

Embedded Source

Embedded Media Text: Attendance Campaign Article

Headline: Advisory Campaign Encourages Students to Arrive on Time

Body: The article explains that the school is starting a two-week advisory campaign about arriving on time. The central idea is that consistent arrival helps students begin class prepared. The article supports this idea with three details: the assistant principal says students miss opening instructions when they arrive late, a teacher says the first five minutes often include warm-up practice, and a student mentor says reminders in advisory can help students plan morning routines. The article ends by explaining that advisory teachers will review arrival tips each Monday.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Identify the topic. The topic is the advisory campaign about arriving on time.

Step 2: Identify the central idea. The central idea is that consistent arrival helps students begin class prepared.

Step 3: Find supporting details. The assistant principal says late students miss opening instructions. A teacher says warm-up practice often happens in the first five minutes. A student mentor says advisory reminders can help students plan routines.

Step 4: Explain connection. Each detail supports preparedness: instructions, practice, and planning routines all help students start class ready.

Step 5: Evaluate support. Strong support directly explains the central idea. Weak support may be interesting but does not develop the main message.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create four headings: Topic, Central Idea, Supporting Detail, and How It Supports. Fill the chart with at least three supporting details from the embedded source.

Model Example

Topic: advisory campaign about arriving on time.

Central Idea: consistent arrival helps students begin class prepared.

Supporting Detail: a teacher says the first five minutes often include warm-up practice.

How It Supports: arriving on time helps students participate in the warm-up instead of missing it.
