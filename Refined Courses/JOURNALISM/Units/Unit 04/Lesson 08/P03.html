Now turn your chart into evaluation. A synthesis response does not list skills. It explains how the parts work together.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Start with the issue: replacing printed flyers with a digital board.

Step 2: Identify competing needs: quick updates, access, visibility, and information overload.

Step 3: Evaluate features. A sidebar supports clarity; too much information could reduce usability.

Step 4: Evaluate rhetoric. The headline uses opportunity language to make the change sound positive.

Step 5: Evaluate claims. Strong claims use specific examples such as outdated room numbers or survey/access concerns.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Unit 4 Synthesis Source: Student Media Critique File

Topic: A school proposal to replace printed club flyers with a weekly digital activity board.

News Brief: The activities office is testing a digital board that lists club meetings, tryout dates, volunteer events, and academic team deadlines. The board would appear on hallway screens and the school website every Monday.

Support Argument: Supporters say the digital board would reduce paper waste, update quickly, and help students find information in one place. They point to last month, when three club flyers had outdated room numbers after schedule changes.

Opposing Argument: Opponents say printed flyers are easier to notice during passing time and better for students who do not check the website. They argue that a digital board may be ignored if it contains too much information at once.

Media Features and Language: The draft package uses the headline One Board, Every Opportunity, a sidebar titled What Changes Monday, a screenshot caption, and the phrase a clearer path through the noise of school announcements.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The digital board is better because technology is newer.

Correct: The digital board argument is stronger when it uses evidence about outdated room numbers and faster updates, but it must also address student access and information overload.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect answer relies on a general assumption. The correct answer evaluates evidence, counterclaims, and audience needs.

Notebook Directions

Write three synthesis sentences: one about features, one about opposing claims, and one about rhetoric or mood.
