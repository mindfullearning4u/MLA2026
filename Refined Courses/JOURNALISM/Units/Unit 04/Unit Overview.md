# JOURNALISM - Unit 04 Overview: Media Literacy, Rhetoric, and Audience

---

## Unit Overview

This unit develops media literacy through analysis of journalistic media texts, audience purpose, rhetoric, opposing claims, evidence, mood, language effects, and media features.

Students learn how media texts are structured, how central ideas are supported, how authors use rhetorical appeals and language, and how opposing arguments can be compared for validity and effectiveness. The unit emphasizes audience understanding, evidence-based reasoning, and careful evaluation of media messages.

Instruction and assessment are aligned to the approved MLA Journalism standards for Unit 4. Students demonstrate mastery by analyzing embedded media texts, explaining media features and language effects, comparing claims, evaluating evidence, and synthesizing media literacy skills in a Unit Assessment.

## Unit Assessment Alignment

The Unit Assessment evaluates student mastery of all Unit 4 primary standards. Students must demonstrate consistent proficiency across:

- media text structures and features;
- central idea support;
- author purpose, appeals, and language;
- opposing arguments and claims;
- figurative language and mood;
- rhetoric in media texts;
- evidence-based reasoning;
- audience analysis.

Assessment items require students to analyze embedded media texts, compare opposing claims, evaluate evidence and validity, explain language and mood effects, and synthesize media literacy concepts in student-facing journalism contexts.

---

## Mastery Expectation

Students are expected to demonstrate a minimum of 80% mastery across all required instructional components, including Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, Lesson Quizzes, and the Unit Assessment.

Students are not advanced to the next unit until mastery is achieved. Mastery is determined through consistent performance across multiple measures and is not based on a single assessment.

Lesson 8 contains the Unit Assessment. Lessons 1-7 contain Lesson Quizzes.

---

## Journalism Connection

This unit asks students to think like media-literate journalists and readers. Students evaluate how media messages are built, how language and rhetoric shape interpretation, and how evidence supports or weakens claims.

By the end of the unit, students should be able to analyze media texts for structure, purpose, evidence, rhetoric, language effect, mood, audience impact, and claim validity.
