Deeper Learning: Why Scope Protects Reporting

A story without scope becomes too large. A journalist may collect random facts and still fail to answer the audience question.

Step 1: Name the audience need.

Step 2: Name the question that answers that need.

Step 3: Name the sources that can verify the answer.

Step 4: Remove details that do not help answer the question.

Notebook Task: For the embedded scenario, write one broad topic, one focused reporting question, and one scope sentence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

I can choose a source or question because it sounds interesting, even if it does not answer the reporting need.

Why this is incorrect: Journalism decisions must serve the verified reporting purpose. Interesting is not enough. The choice must help answer the question, clarify the facts, or protect the audience from confusion.

Correct example:

I should choose the question or source that directly helps verify the specific information the audience needs.

Why this is correct: The decision is focused, evidence-based, and connected to the reporting purpose.
