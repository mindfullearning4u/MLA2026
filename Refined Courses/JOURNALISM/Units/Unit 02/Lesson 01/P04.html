P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Broad Topic to Focused Question

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Broad topic: school parking.

Step 1: Audience need: students need to know why parking is harder this month.

Step 2: Focused question: What caused the recent parking shortage, and what options do student drivers have?

Step 3: Scope: the story covers the shortage and options, not every transportation issue.

Worked Example 2: Source Match

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: Why did the media center change its morning hours?

Step 1: The media specialist can verify the reason.

Step 2: Students can explain the effect.

Step 3: A rumor from social media is not enough because it cannot verify the decision.

Worked Example 3: Removing Off-Scope Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Question: How does the new tutoring schedule affect ninth graders?

Step 1: A quote about cafeteria prices is off scope.

Step 2: A counselor explanation of the schedule is on scope.

Step 3: Keeping only relevant information makes the report clearer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The first answer that seems possible is good enough.

Why this is incorrect: A possible answer is not the same as a verified, useful, publication-ready reporting decision.

Correct example:

A strong reporting decision is checked against the question, the source, the evidence, and the audience need.

Teachable Explanation: This process slows the journalist down and prevents weak or unsupported reporting.
