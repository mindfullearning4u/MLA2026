P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Reporting Questions and Scope: Notebook Part 1

Write this title at the top of your notes. Your notes should show the decision process, not only the final answer.

Vocabulary

  • Reporting question: the focused question a journalist is trying to answer.
  • Scope: the boundary of a story, including what will and will not be covered.
  • Angle: the specific focus that makes a broad topic reportable.
  • Source: a person, record, or observation that can provide information.

Embedded Reporting Scenario

Scenario: The student newsroom wants to report on long lines at the cafeteria. Some students say the issue is caused by a new payment system. Others say more students are buying lunch this semester. The staff needs a focused reporting plan before interviewing anyone.

Everything you need for the lesson is included here. Do not search for another passage or source.

Step-by-Step: From Topic to Reporting Question

Step 1: Start with a broad topic, such as cafeteria food, bus delays, or club participation.

Step 2: Ask what the audience needs to know. A student audience may need a practical answer, not a general discussion.

Step 3: Narrow the topic into one answerable question. For example, cafeteria food becomes: What changed in the lunch menu this semester, and how are students responding?

Step 4: Check whether the question can be answered with sources and evidence. If no one can verify it, revise the question.

Step 5: Write the scope: this story will cover the menu change and student response; it will not cover every cafeteria issue.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, write the lesson title, copy the key vocabulary you need, and complete the notebook task using the embedded scenario or source material on this page. Show your thinking in steps so your Teacher of Record can see how you made the journalism decision.

Model Example

Model response frame: The best journalism decision is to identify the reporting task, use the embedded evidence, and explain how the choice protects accuracy, audience understanding, and publication quality.
