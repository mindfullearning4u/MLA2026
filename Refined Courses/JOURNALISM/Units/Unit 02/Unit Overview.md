# JOURNALISM - Unit 02 Overview: Reporting, Source Verification, and Interviewing

---

## Unit Overview

This unit develops the reporting foundation students need before drafting publishable journalism. Students learn how to shape reporting questions, define the scope of a story, evaluate source reliability, comprehend complex source material, make evidence-based inferences, paraphrase accurately, connect evidence to reasoning, and plan purposeful interviews.

The unit is designed for mastery-based independent learning. Students are not expected to already know how to verify information, question sources, paraphrase accurately, or plan interviews. Each lesson teaches the reporting skill step by step, models the skill, shows common mistakes, and requires students to submit checkpoint evidence before moving forward.

Instruction and assessment align to the approved MLA Journalism standard inventory and the approved Unit 2 lesson-level mapping.

## Unit Assessment Alignment

The Unit 2 Assessment evaluates whether students can apply the full reporting process before publication.

Students demonstrate mastery by:

- identifying focused reporting questions;
- separating verified information from unverified claims;
- evaluating source reliability and validity;
- reading source material accurately;
- making reasonable inferences without overstating evidence;
- paraphrasing source information without changing meaning;
- connecting evidence to reasoning;
- planning neutral interview questions tied to verification needs;
- explaining how source verification protects accuracy and trust.

Lesson 8 is the Unit 2 synthesis lesson. It introduces no new primary standard and contains the Unit Assessment instead of a lesson quiz.

---

## Mastery Expectations

Students are expected to demonstrate mastery across lesson notebook tasks, guided practice, independent work, checkpoint submissions, lesson quizzes, the Unit 2 Pretest, and the Unit 2 Assessment.

Students should be able to explain not only what answer is correct, but why the reporting choice protects accuracy, source meaning, and audience trust.

Students who struggle should use the step-by-step lesson models, common mistake explanations, guided practice feedback, and Teacher of Record support before resubmitting checkpoint evidence.

---

## Self-Paced Learning Design

Unit 2 lessons are built for students learning primarily from the curriculum itself.

Each lesson provides:

- explicit vocabulary support;
- embedded source material when needed;
- step-by-step instruction;
- modeled reporting decisions;
- common mistake correction using red incorrect and green correct examples;
- guided practice with detailed feedback;
- independent work in Part A, Part B, and Part C;
- checkpoint submission directions;
- mastery criteria aligned to the lesson standards.

---

## Problem-Solving Connection

This unit asks students to solve reporting problems. Students determine what is known, what is not yet verified, which sources are reliable, what evidence supports a claim, and which interview questions can fill gaps before publication.
