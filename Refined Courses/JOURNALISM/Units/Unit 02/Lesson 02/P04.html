P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Official Record

Question: Did the club fair date change?

Step 1: The fact is the date.

Step 2: The school calendar or activities director can verify it.

Step 3: A hallway rumor is not enough.

Example 2: Expert Source

Question: Why did the science lab safety rule change?

Step 1: The science department chair has direct knowledge.

Step 2: A student opinion may show reaction but cannot verify the reason.

Step 3: Use both if the story needs reason and impact.

Example 3: Bias Check

Question: Should students support a fundraiser?

Step 1: The club president may know the goal but also wants support.

Step 2: Ask for records or a sponsor confirmation.

Step 3: Explain source role clearly so readers understand context.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The first answer that seems possible is good enough.

Why this is incorrect: A possible answer is not the same as a verified, useful, publication-ready reporting decision.

Correct example:

A strong reporting decision is checked against the question, the source, the evidence, and the audience need.

Why this is correct: This process slows the journalist down and prevents weak or unsupported reporting.
