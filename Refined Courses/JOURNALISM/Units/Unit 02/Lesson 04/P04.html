P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Reasonable Inference

Step 1: Evidence: students cannot find the form.

Step 2: Evidence: sign-ups are low.

Step 3: Inference: access to the form may be affecting tutoring participation.

Example 2: Evidence Limit

Step 1: The notes mention three students.

Step 2: Three students do not represent every student.

Step 3: A careful report says some students, not all students.

Example 3: Evidence and Reasoning

Step 1: Claim: The form may need clearer placement.

Step 2: Evidence: students and a teacher report confusion.

Step 3: Reasoning: If students cannot find the form, fewer students may sign up for available tutoring.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

I only need one phrase from the source, even if the rest of the source changes the meaning.

Why this is incorrect: Quoting or paraphrasing one phrase without context can mislead the audience.

Correct example:

I should read the whole source, identify the key details, and explain how the evidence supports my reporting decision.

Why this is correct: It keeps the report accurate, fair, and tied to evidence.
