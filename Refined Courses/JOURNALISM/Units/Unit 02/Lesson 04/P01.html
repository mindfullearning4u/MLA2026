P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 4: Inference in Reporting

This lesson teaches inference in reporting as a reporting skill. Because this is a self-paced course, the lesson explains the process step by step before you practice independently.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.JOUR.REP.04.

Support Standards: MLA.JOUR.REP.03, MLA.JOUR.REP.02.

What You Will Learn

  • How to read a reporting source carefully.
  • How to separate stated facts from reasonable conclusions.
  • How to explain your thinking with evidence instead of guessing.

What You Will Do

  • Read the embedded source material.
  • Build notebook evidence in two parts.
  • Study worked examples and common mistakes.
  • Complete guided practice, independent work, checkpoint submission, and a lesson quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Use the lesson steps without skipping evidence.
  • Explain how the source supports the reporting decision.
  • Submit checkpoint evidence aligned to MLA.JOUR.REP.04.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can read source material carefully and use it to make accurate reporting decisions.

Student Road Map for This Journalism Lesson

Use this overview to understand the reporting, media, ethics, writing, or publication skill in Inference in Reporting. Before moving forward, make sure you can name the product or decision the lesson expects and how mastery will be judged. This course is self-paced, so use the page directions as your instruction. Read the embedded source, scenario, model, or newsroom task first. Then identify the exact journalism responsibility from MLA.JOUR.REP.04, MLA.JOUR.REP.03, MLA.JOUR.REP.02, decide what information is verified, explain why it matters to the audience, and keep the response inside the lesson focus. Do not invent facts, quotes, images, or source details. If the task asks for writing, draft in clear journalistic language. If it asks for analysis, point to the words, source details, media features, or evidence that prove the answer.
