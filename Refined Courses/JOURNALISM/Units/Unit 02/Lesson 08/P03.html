P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now use your synthesis table to make accurate reporting decisions. Unit 2 mastery means you can explain not only what a source says, but also what the source information does and does not prove.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Test each claim. Ask, What exact evidence supports this claim?

Step 2: Avoid overstatement. If the source gives limited information, use careful wording such as some students said or the counselor said.

Step 3: Preserve source meaning. A paraphrase should not make the source stronger, weaker, or more dramatic than it was.

Step 4: Use interviews to fill gaps. If a question remains unanswered, create a neutral follow-up question rather than guessing.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Unit 2 Reporting Scenario

The student newspaper is preparing a report about after-school tutoring sign-ups. The counselor says sign-ups increased after announcements were posted in advisory classes. A math teacher says some students still do not know where the form is located. Four students say reminders helped them remember the deadline. The reporter has not yet checked the sign-up data, interviewed the tutoring coordinator, or confirmed whether sign-ups increased in every grade level.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Tutoring sign-ups increased in every grade because announcements fixed the problem.

Correct: The counselor said sign-ups increased after advisory announcements, but the reporter still needs data to confirm whether the increase happened in every grade.

Teachable Explanation: The incorrect sentence turns a source statement into an unsupported broad claim. The correct sentence attributes the statement and names the verification gap.

Notebook Directions

Write one careful paraphrase of the counselor statement and one follow-up question for the tutoring coordinator. Explain how each protects accuracy.
