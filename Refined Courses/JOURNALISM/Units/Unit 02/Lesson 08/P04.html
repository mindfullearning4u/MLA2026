P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Verified Information

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Source detail: A math teacher says some students still do not know where the form is located.

Modeled thinking: This is a source statement from one teacher. It supports a possible awareness problem, but it does not prove how many students are affected.

Careful use: A math teacher reported that some students still need help finding the sign-up form.

Example 2: Evidence and Reasoning

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Evidence: Four students say reminders helped them remember the deadline.

Reasoning: This evidence suggests reminders may support student awareness, but it is limited because it represents only four students.

Publication-ready wording: Several student comments suggest reminders may help students remember tutoring deadlines.

Example 3: Interview Follow-Up

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Gap: Whether sign-ups increased in every grade level.

Question: Can you share sign-up totals by grade level before and after the advisory announcements?

Reasoning: The question asks for specific data and does not assume the answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Students forgot tutoring because the school did not explain the form well.

Correct: One teacher said some students still do not know where the form is located, so the reporter should ask how the form location is communicated.

Teachable explanation: The incorrect version blames the school without evidence. The correct version stays with the source detail and creates a responsible follow-up step.
