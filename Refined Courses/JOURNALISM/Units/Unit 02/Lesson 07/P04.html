📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 02 | Lesson 07

P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identify a Reporting Gap

Scenario detail: The reporter has not confirmed how many students have used the app.

Modeled thinking: I cannot say the app is popular or unpopular until I know usage information.

Reporting decision: Ask for usage data or interview a source who tracks app activity.

Reasoning: This turns a guess into a verifiable reporting task.

Example 2: Write a Neutral Interview Question

Weak question: Why are students unhappy with the app?

Step 1: Remove the assumption that students are unhappy.

Step 2: Ask about experience instead of pushing a complaint.

Improved question: What has your experience been like using the ordering app this week?

Reasoning: The improved question lets the source report positive, negative, or mixed experience.

Example 3: Connect Evidence to Follow-Up

Evidence: Two students say the app is confusing.

Modeled thinking: Two students can show a concern exists, but they do not prove all students are confused.

Follow-up: Interview additional students and ask the technology assistant what questions students have asked most often.

Reasoning: The follow-up checks whether the issue is isolated or broader.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Two students said the app is confusing, so the story should report that the app failed.

Correct: Two students reported confusion, so the reporter should gather more evidence before judging how well the app works.

Teachable explanation: A small number of comments can guide follow-up reporting, but they should not become a broad conclusion without more evidence.
