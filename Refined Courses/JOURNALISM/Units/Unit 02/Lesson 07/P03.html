P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now move from listing information to making reporting decisions. A strong reporter does not ask random questions. A strong reporter asks questions that solve a specific reporting problem.

Deeper Learning

Step 1: Match each gap to a source. If the gap is wait-time data, ask someone who has access to records. If the gap is student experience, ask students who used the app.

Step 2: Make each question answerable. A source can answer, How many students used the app on Monday? more clearly than Is the app good?

Step 3: Keep tone professional. Interview questions should not accuse, embarrass, or lead the source.

Step 4: Connect the answer to the story focus. If a question will not help verify the story, revise it or remove it.

Embedded Source Reminder

Embedded Source File: School Cafeteria Interview Planning Notes

The student newspaper is preparing a story about a new cafeteria ordering system. A cafeteria manager says the goal is to shorten lunch lines. Two students say the app is confusing during the first week. A technology assistant says the school will post a how-to guide by Friday. The reporter has not yet confirmed how many students have used the app or whether the line wait time has changed.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Why did the school launch an app that confused everyone?

Correct: What feedback have students shared about using the ordering app during the first week?

Teachable explanation: The incorrect question assumes the app confused everyone. That is not verified. The correct question invites evidence, keeps the tone neutral, and allows the source to provide useful information.

Notebook Directions

Revise one weak or leading question into a neutral interview question. Then explain which missing detail your revised question is designed to verify.
