📰 JOURNALISM | Unit 02 | Lesson 07

P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Integrated Reporting Plan: What Do I Know, What Must I Verify, and Who Should I Ask?

Vocabulary

  • Reporting plan: a step-by-step plan for gathering accurate story information.
  • Verified fact: information confirmed by reliable evidence or a credible source.
  • Follow-up question: a question asked after initial reporting to fill a gap or confirm a detail.
  • Neutral wording: wording that does not pressure the source toward one answer.

Embedded Source

Embedded Source File: School Cafeteria Interview Planning Notes

The student newspaper is preparing a story about a new cafeteria ordering system. A cafeteria manager says the goal is to shorten lunch lines. Two students say the app is confusing during the first week. A technology assistant says the school will post a how-to guide by Friday. The reporter has not yet confirmed how many students have used the app or whether the line wait time has changed.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Name the story focus. The story is not simply cafeteria food. It is the new cafeteria ordering system and whether it is helping lunch lines.

Step 2: List confirmed source information. The manager says the goal is to shorten lines. Two students say the app is confusing. The technology assistant says a how-to guide will be posted by Friday.

Step 3: List what is not confirmed yet. The reporter does not know how many students have used the app or whether wait times have changed. These gaps matter because the story cannot claim the system works or fails without evidence.

Step 4: Decide who to interview next. Strong follow-up sources include the cafeteria manager, technology assistant, students who used the app, and someone who can provide wait-time or usage data.

Step 5: Write neutral questions. A neutral question does not assume the answer. Instead of asking, Why is the app confusing?, ask, What parts of the app have been easy or difficult to use?

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, create three columns: Confirmed, Needs Verification, and Interview Follow-Up. Use the embedded source to place at least two items in each column. Then write one neutral interview question for a source who could help verify the story.

Model Example

Confirmed: A technology assistant says a how-to guide will be posted by Friday.

Needs Verification: Whether wait times have actually changed.

Interview Follow-Up: Ask the cafeteria manager, What information do you have about line wait times before and after the ordering system began?

Why this works: The question is specific, neutral, and connected to evidence the story needs.
