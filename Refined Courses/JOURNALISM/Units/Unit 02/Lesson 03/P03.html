P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Deeper Learning: Paraphrasing Without Distortion

A journalist paraphrases to make source information clearer, but the paraphrase must keep the same meaning.

Step 1: Keep the key facts.

Step 2: Remove unnecessary wording.

Step 3: Do not add emotion, blame, or certainty the source does not provide.

Strong paraphrase: The media center will open thirty minutes later for three weeks while technology maintenance takes place, and students may use Room 114 before 7:45 a.m.

Notebook Task: List the five key facts from the memo, then write a one-sentence paraphrase.

Common Mistake

Incorrect example:

The source seems to suggest something, so I can report it as a fact.

Why this is incorrect: Journalism requires careful reading. A suggestion, pattern, or clue is not automatically a verified fact.

Correct example:

I can report stated facts as facts and label reasonable conclusions as inferences supported by evidence.

Why this is correct: The answer protects accuracy and shows the reader where the reasoning comes from.
