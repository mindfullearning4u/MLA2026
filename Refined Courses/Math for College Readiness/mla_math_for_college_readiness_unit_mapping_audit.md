# MLA Math for College Readiness Unit Mapping Audit

## Executive Summary

This audit reviews `MCR/mla_math_for_college_readiness_unit_mapping_v1.md` as an instructional sequencing document only. Standards coverage, benchmark coverage, and crosswalk accuracy were not re-audited.

The unit mapping remains aligned to the approved MLA Hybrid Model. It balances College Algebra readiness, SAT/ACT readiness, quantitative reasoning, financial literacy, data literacy, probability and risk, applied geometry, workforce readiness, and mathematical communication. The course does not drift into Algebra I remediation, Algebra II remediation, consumer math only, or narrow test preparation.

The sequence is instructionally sound overall. Recommended revisions are primarily pacing and scaffolding improvements: strengthen prerequisite callouts in Unit 1, make spreadsheet use more explicit before complex finance tasks, reduce overload in selected finance and geometry lessons, and improve the transition from probability rules to conditional probability and expected value.

**Final Determination:** PASS WITH REVISIONS

---

## Strengths

- The six-unit sequence preserves the approved Hybrid Model.
- Quantitative reasoning is placed first, which supports all later applied domains.
- Applied algebra appears early enough to support College Algebra readiness without becoming a full Algebra II review.
- Data literacy precedes financial and probability decision making, which is instructionally appropriate.
- Financial literacy receives a full unit and is not treated as a minor application topic.
- Probability and risk are placed after data and finance, giving students relevant contexts for expected value and decision making.
- Applied geometry and measurement are placed last as a synthesis of units, formulas, proportional reasoning, and workforce modeling.
- Lesson 8 in each unit is used for synthesis rather than major new content.
- Advanced learner extensions are present in every unit and remain optional.

---

## Findings

### Finding 1: Unit 1 Is Logical but Contains a Sharp Abstraction Increase

Unit 1 moves from ratios, percents, estimation, and reasonableness into formulas, function notation, and average rate of change. This is a strong readiness progression, but Lesson 5 combines formulas, variables, and function notation, then Lesson 6 immediately moves to average rate of change.

For readiness-level learners, this may create cognitive load if function notation, table interpretation, graph interpretation, and units of rate are not explicitly scaffolded.

### Finding 2: Unit 2 Supports College Algebra Readiness

Unit 2 is rigorous enough for Math for College Readiness. Linear equations, functions, inequalities, systems, exponential models, piecewise relationships, and algebraic modeling are appropriate.

The main risk is that piecewise relationships may feel abstract if students have not seen enough concrete step-function contexts such as taxes, shipping, overtime pay, utility rates, and tiered pricing.

### Finding 3: Unit 3 Is Well Sequenced

Data displays, center and variability, comparing data, bivariate data, correlation/causation, sampling, and statistical reports form a coherent progression.

Correlation appropriately follows scatterplots. Statistical reports appropriately follow displays, variability, association, causation, and sampling. This unit is one of the strongest in the mapping.

### Finding 4: Unit 4 Is Strong but Spreadsheet Reasoning Appears Too Late

The finance sequence is realistic: income and taxes, budgeting, interest, credit/debt, insurance, retirement, and spreadsheet-supported decisions.

However, spreadsheet-supported financial decisions appear in Lesson 7, after students have already completed budgeting, interest, credit, debt, insurance, and retirement. Since spreadsheets are a practical tool for nearly all finance topics, students would benefit from explicit spreadsheet setup earlier in the unit.

### Finding 5: Unit 4 Lesson 4 Is Overloaded

Credit, Loans, and Debt currently owns four MLA standards and four Florida benchmarks. Instructionally, credit, finance charges, buying power, and debt payoff plans are high-value topics, but together they can overload readiness-level students.

This is a pacing concern rather than a standards concern. The lesson likely needs careful internal chunking or a revised distribution in a future mapping revision.

### Finding 6: Unit 5 Probability Progression Is Mostly Sound

The sequence from probability foundations to empirical probability, compound events, conditional probability, counting, expected value, and risk is defensible.

The main instructional concern is that conditional probability may be easier after students spend more time with two-way tables and relative frequency. Counting methods are appropriately placed after probability rules, because counting should support probability rather than become isolated procedure.

### Finding 7: Unit 6 Is Coherent but Formula Load Is Concentrated

Measurement, units, similarity, area, surface area, volume, density, coordinate geometry, and right-triangle modeling form a sound applied geometry sequence.

Unit 6 Lesson 3 combines area, surface area, and volume. This may be overloaded for readiness-level students because it requires formula selection, substitution, unit interpretation, two-dimensional reasoning, and three-dimensional reasoning in one lesson.

Density is placed appropriately after area, surface area, and volume because students need measurement units and volume reasoning before density.

## Quantitative Reasoning Review

Quantitative reasoning remains the central identity of the course. Unit 1 establishes quantities, units, ratios, rates, percents, estimation, formulas, rate of change, and weighted averages. Later units continue using reasonableness checks, constraints, interpretation, and decision making.

The progression is logical:

1. Identify quantities and units.
2. Use ratios, rates, and percents.
3. Estimate and check reasonableness.
4. Apply formulas and functions.
5. Interpret rates of change.
6. Use weighted reasoning for decisions.

Recommended improvement: make reasonableness checks and units visible in every later unit, especially finance, probability, and measurement.

---

## Financial Literacy Review

Financial literacy receives appropriate emphasis through a dedicated unit. The sequence from income and taxes to budgeting, interest, credit, insurance, retirement, and spreadsheet-supported decisions is realistic and student-friendly.

The main issue is tool timing. Spreadsheet-supported decision making should not first become explicit near the end of the finance unit. Students should begin using basic spreadsheet formulas during income, budgeting, and interest lessons so the tool becomes part of the reasoning process.

Financial literacy is not overrepresented. It is appropriately central for a Math for College Readiness course.

---

## Workforce Readiness Review

Workforce readiness is appropriately represented through:

- Spreadsheet use
- Quantitative communication
- Financial decision making
- Data interpretation
- Risk evaluation
- Measurement and unit conversion
- Applied geometry
- Multi-step modeling

The strongest workforce-readiness units are Units 3, 4, 5, and 6. Unit 1 also supports workforce readiness through estimation, units, reasonableness, and weighted averages.

Recommended improvement: add explicit communication expectations to applied tasks so students practice explaining decisions, assumptions, tradeoffs, and limitations.

---

## College Algebra Readiness Review

The course supports College Algebra readiness through:

- Linear equations in context
- Linear functions and graph interpretation
- Inequalities and constraints
- Systems of equations
- Exponential models
- Piecewise relationships
- Function notation
- Average rate of change
- Algebraic modeling

The course avoids becoming Algebra II remediation. It uses algebra as a tool for modeling and interpretation rather than extending into advanced symbolic manipulation.

Recommended improvement: Unit 2 should preserve enough symbolic fluency within applied contexts. Students should still solve, graph, and interpret algebraic models rather than only read completed models.

## Sequencing Analysis

### Unit 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem Solving

The unit is placed correctly and begins with the right ideas. Ratios and percents precede formulas and functions, and estimation/reasonableness appears before average rate of change.

Potential issue: Lesson 5 and Lesson 6 create a steep climb into abstraction. Readiness-level students may need more explicit review of coordinate graphs, input/output tables, independent and dependent variables, and units of rate.

### Unit 2: Algebra in Real-World Contexts

The sequence is appropriate. Equations precede functions, functions precede inequalities and systems, and exponential models appear after linear models. Piecewise relationships are placed late enough to rely on prior function and constraint reasoning.

Potential issue: piecewise relationships should be strongly contextualized. Without contexts such as tax brackets, parking rates, pay tiers, or shipping costs, the topic can feel like Algebra II review.

### Unit 3: Data Literacy and Interpretation

The unit is well sequenced. Display literacy precedes center/variability, bivariate data precedes correlation and causation, and sampling precedes statistical reports.

No major lesson movement is recommended.

### Unit 4: Financial Literacy and Personal Finance

The financial sequence is realistic and useful. Income and taxes logically precede budgeting. Interest precedes credit, debt, and retirement. Insurance appropriately bridges finance and risk.

Potential issue: spreadsheet reasoning is too late as a named lesson. It should be introduced as a tool earlier, even if Lesson 7 remains the culminating spreadsheet-supported decision lesson.

### Unit 5: Probability and Decision Making

The probability sequence is mostly sound. Foundations and empirical probability precede compound and conditional probability, which then support counting, expected value, and risk decisions.

Potential issue: conditional probability should include strong two-way-table support. Expected value is correctly placed after probability foundations.

### Unit 6: Measurement, Modeling, and Applied Geometry

The unit is appropriately placed at the end because it synthesizes units, proportional reasoning, formulas, geometry, and modeling.

Potential issue: Lesson 3 may be overloaded by combining area, surface area, and volume. Density is correctly placed after volume and unit work.

---

## Advanced Learner Review

Advanced learner extensions are present and appropriate. Strong opportunities include:

- SAT-style applied reasoning
- ACT-style mixed reasoning
- Spreadsheet investigations
- Data interpretation challenges
- Financial comparison projects
- Probability simulations
- Measurement design tasks
- Open-ended modeling prompts

Advanced students may still need more rigorous extension options in three places:

- Unit 2: optimization-style constraints and model comparison
- Unit 4: spreadsheet-based debt payoff and retirement simulations
- Unit 6: multi-step design constraints involving cost, volume, scale, and measurement error

These should remain optional and should not change mastery expectations.

---

## Final Determination

**PASS WITH REVISIONS**

The MCR unit mapping is fundamentally sound and preserves the approved Hybrid Model. The recommended revisions do not require redesigning the course or changing standards coverage. They focus on pacing, prerequisite callouts, spreadsheet timing, and cognitive load management for readiness-level learners.
