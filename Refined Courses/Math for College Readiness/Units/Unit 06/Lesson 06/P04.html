P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Worked Example 1

Question: A right triangle has legs 6 feet and 8 feet. Find the hypotenuse.

Visual: right triangle model

  1. Step 1: Use a2 + b2 = c2.
  2. Step 2: 6 × 6 + 8 × 8 = c2.
  3. Step 3: 36 + 64 = 100, so c2 = 100.
  4. Step 4: c = √100 = 10 feet.

Worked Example 2

Question: A ladder is 13 feet long and reaches a window 12 feet high. How far is the ladder base from the wall?

  1. Step 1: The ladder is the hypotenuse because it is across from the right angle.
  2. Step 2: Use a2 + 12 × 12 = 13 × 13.
  3. Step 3: a2 + 144 = 169, so a2 = 25.
  4. Step 4: a = √25 = 5 feet.

Worked Example 3

Question: From a 30-degree angle, a ramp has hypotenuse 20 feet. Which ratio would find the height opposite the angle?

  1. Step 1: The height is opposite the 30-degree angle.
  2. Step 2: The ramp length is the hypotenuse.
  3. Step 3: Sine compares opposite / hypotenuse.
  4. Step 4: Use sine because the problem connects opposite and hypotenuse.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: In a ladder problem, the wall height is always the hypotenuse.

The hypotenuse is across from the right angle. In a ladder problem, the slanted ladder is usually the hypotenuse.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The slanted ladder is the hypotenuse because it is the longest side and is across from the right angle.

Drawing and labeling the triangle prevents using the wrong side as c.
