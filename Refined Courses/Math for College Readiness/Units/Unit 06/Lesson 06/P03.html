Trigonometric Ratios

Trigonometry helps when a right triangle includes an angle and a side length. The three basic ratios compare two sides of a right triangle.

  • Sine compares opposite / hypotenuse.
  • Cosine compares adjacent / hypotenuse.
  • Tangent compares opposite / adjacent.

The opposite side is across from the angle you are using. The adjacent side touches the angle but is not the hypotenuse.

Choosing a Tool

Use the Pythagorean Theorem when you know two side lengths and need a third side. Use sine, cosine, or tangent when an angle is part of the problem.

A diagram is not optional in real modeling. Draw the right triangle, label the known information, and then choose the relationship.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: In every right triangle, the longest side is one of the legs.

This reverses the side roles. The hypotenuse is always the longest side and is across from the right angle.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The hypotenuse is the longest side and is across from the right angle.

Label the hypotenuse first before using a2 + b2 = c2 or choosing a trigonometric ratio.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, draw one right triangle with an angle marked. Label the opposite side, adjacent side, and hypotenuse from that angle.
