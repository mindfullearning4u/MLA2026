Perimeter and Area from Coordinates

When a rectangle is drawn on a coordinate plane, you can find its side lengths by comparing x-values and y-values. After you know the side lengths, perimeter and area work the same way they did before.

  1. Find the horizontal side length from the x-values.
  2. Find the vertical side length from the y-values.
  3. For perimeter, add all side lengths around the figure.
  4. For area, multiply base by height when the figure is a rectangle.

Reading the Grid Carefully

The coordinate values give position, not the side length by themselves. A point at x = 8 is not automatically 8 units away from a point at x = 2 unless you subtract the values. Distance is the difference between positions.

Use positive distance values because distance tells how far apart points are.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: The distance from (3, 5) to (10, 5) is 10 units because one x-value is 10.

This uses a coordinate as a distance. A coordinate tells location, not the distance between two points.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The distance from (3, 5) to (10, 5) is 10 - 3 = 7 units.

The y-values are the same, so the segment is horizontal. Subtract the x-values to find the distance.
