Surface Area and Volume

Surface area and volume both use three-dimensional objects, but they answer different questions. Surface area tells how much material is needed to cover the outside. Volume tells how much space is inside.

For a rectangular prism, surface area can be found by adding the areas of all six faces. Volume can be found with length × width × height.

  1. If the question asks about wrapping, painting, covering, or outside material, think surface area.
  2. If the question asks about filling, holding, capacity, or space inside, think volume.
  3. Use square units for surface area because each face is flat.
  4. Use cubic units for volume because three dimensions are multiplied.

Unit Check

The final unit gives a strong clue about whether the measurement matches the question. If your answer for volume is in square feet, the unit does not match the idea of inside space. If your answer for area is in cubic feet, the unit suggests three-dimensional space when the question only asked for a flat region.

Use the label as a final check before submitting your work.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: A box that is 4 feet by 3 feet by 2 feet has volume 4 + 3 + 2 = 9 cubic feet.

This adds dimensions instead of multiplying length, width, and height. Volume counts three-dimensional space.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: Volume = length × width × height = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cubic feet.

The cubic unit shows that three dimensions were used.
