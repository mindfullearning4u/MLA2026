P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem-Solving Habits

In this lesson, you will learn how to make sense of real-world math problems before solving them. You will practice identifying the numbers, units, question, assumptions, and reasonableness of an answer.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

MLA.MCR.MC.1: I can explain my mathematical reasoning clearly using words, numbers, tables, graphs, formulas, and units.

MLA.MCR.MC.2: I can choose appropriate strategies and representations to solve real-world problems.

MLA.MCR.MC.3: I can interpret results in context and check whether answers are reasonable.

Mapping Note: This is a support-only lesson in the approved Unit 1 mapping. It builds the communication and reasoning habits needed for the primary Quantitative Reasoning standards that follow.

What You Will Learn

  • How to identify the quantities, units, and unknown in a real-world problem.
  • How to state an assumption before solving when a situation needs clarification.
  • How to choose a strategy that matches what the problem is asking.
  • How to explain whether an answer is reasonable using context and estimation.

What You Will Do

  • Read real-world situations slowly and identify what each number means.
  • Write notebook notes that explain problem-solving habits in your own words.
  • Work through examples step by step before trying independent problems.
  • Complete guided practice, independent work, and a checkpoint task.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Show organized notebook work using quantities, units, assumptions, and explanations.
  • Complete independent work so your Teacher of Record can see whether you are ready to move forward.
  • Submit a checkpoint that explains your reasoning in complete sentences.
  • Demonstrate at least 80% mastery on the lesson quiz or complete Teacher of Record intervention before another randomized attempt is released.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

Readiness Support: Strong problem solving begins before calculation. You need to know what the numbers mean, what units belong with them, what you are trying to find, and whether your answer makes sense.

In student language: I can explain how I solved a real-world math problem and check whether my answer makes sense.
