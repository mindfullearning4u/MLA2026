P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem-Solving Habits

In this lesson, you will begin Math for College Readiness by practicing habits used to identify quantities, units, assumptions, and reasonableness in real-world problems.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

MLA.MCR.MC.1: I can explain my mathematical reasoning clearly using words, numbers, tables, graphs, formulas, and units.

MLA.MCR.MC.2: I can choose appropriate strategies and representations to solve real-world problems.

MLA.MCR.MC.3: I can interpret results in context and check whether answers are reasonable.

Mapping Note: This is a support-only lesson in the approved Unit 1 mapping. It does not introduce new primary benchmark ownership.

What You Will Learn

  • How to identify quantities, units, and what a problem is asking.
  • How to state assumptions before solving a real-world problem.
  • How to check whether an answer is reasonable in context.
  • How to explain mathematical thinking clearly.

What You Will Do

  • Take notes on problem-solving habits.
  • Practice identifying given information, unknown information, and units.
  • Use estimation and context to judge reasonableness.
  • Complete independent work and a checkpoint response.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Submit notebook evidence with organized notes and independent work.
  • Submit a checkpoint explaining quantities, units, assumptions, and reasonableness.
  • Demonstrate at least 80% mastery on the lesson quiz or complete Teacher of Record intervention before another randomized attempt is released.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

Readiness Support: Strong quantitative reasoning begins before calculation. You need to know what the numbers mean, what units belong with them, and whether your answer makes sense.

In student language: I can explain how I solved a real-world math problem and check whether my answer makes sense.
