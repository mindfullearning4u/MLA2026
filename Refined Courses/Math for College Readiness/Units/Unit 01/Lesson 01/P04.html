P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Study all three worked examples carefully. Each example shows the thinking, not just the final answer.

Worked Example 1: Compare Two Choices

Problem: A tutoring center charges $18 per hour. A student needs 5 hours. Another center charges a flat $100 for the same amount of tutoring. Which option costs less?

Step 1: Identify quantities and units. The quantities are $18 per hour, 5 hours, and $100 flat cost.

Step 2: State an assumption. Assume both centers provide the same amount and quality of tutoring.

Step 3: Find the hourly center cost.

18 x 5 = 90

Step 4: Compare. $90 is less than $100.

Answer: The hourly center costs less by $10.

Reasonableness: $18 is close to $20, and 5 x 20 = 100. Since $18 is less than $20, an exact cost of $90 is reasonable.

Worked Example 2: Use Units to Choose a Strategy

Problem: A bus travels 210 miles in 5 hours. What does 210 divided by 5 represent?

Step 1: Identify the units. The distance is 210 miles. The time is 5 hours.

Step 2: Understand the division. Dividing miles by hours gives miles per hour.

210 divided by 5 = 42

Step 3: Interpret. The bus travels 42 miles per hour.

Reasonableness: 5 groups of about 40 miles equals about 200 miles, which is close to 210 miles.

Worked Example 3: Check an Answer for Reasonableness

Problem: A student buys 7 notebooks for $9 each. The student says the total is $63. Is the answer reasonable?

Step 1: Identify the structure. There are 7 equal groups of $9.

Step 2: Estimate. $9 is close to $10, and 7 x 10 = 70.

Step 3: Calculate exactly.

7 x 9 = 63

Step 4: Decide if reasonable. $63 is close to the estimate of $70 and a little less because $9 is less than $10.

Answer: Yes, $63 is reasonable.

Common Mistake

Problem: A student buys 7 notebooks for $9 each. What is the total cost?

Incorrect:

7 + 9 = 16 dollars

Why it is wrong: The situation does not ask for 7 plus 9. It says there are 7 notebooks and each one costs $9, so $9 is repeated 7 times.

Correct:

7 x 9 = 63 dollars

Correction: Use multiplication for equal groups. The student spends $63.
