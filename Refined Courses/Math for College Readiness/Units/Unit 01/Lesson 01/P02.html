Notebook Title

Lesson 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem-Solving Habits

Why Quantitative Reasoning Comes First

Quantitative reasoning means using numbers, units, and context to make sense of a situation.

Before solving, identify what is known, what is unknown, and what the answer should mean.

A correct calculation is not enough if the answer does not make sense in the situation.

Problem-Solving Habits

  • Identify quantities: Find the numbers and what they represent.
  • Track units: Attach labels such as dollars, miles, hours, items, or points.
  • Name the unknown: State what the problem is asking you to find.
  • State assumptions: Decide what must be true for your method to work.
  • Check reasonableness: Use estimation and context to decide whether the result makes sense.

Vocabulary

  • Quantity: A measurable amount in a situation.
  • Unit: The label that tells what a quantity measures.
  • Assumption: Something accepted as true while solving a problem.
  • Reasonableness: Whether an answer makes sense based on context, units, and estimate.
  • Context: The real-world situation surrounding a problem.

Example 1: Identify Quantities and Units

Problem: A student works 6 hours and earns $15 per hour. What quantities are given?

Quantity 1: 6 hours

Quantity 2: $15 per hour

Unknown: Total dollars earned

Reasoning: The units show that hours and dollars per hour work together to find total dollars.
