Notebook Title

Lesson 1: Quantitative Reasoning and Problem-Solving Habits

Vocabulary

  • Quantity: A number or amount in a situation. Example: 6 hours, $15, 4 miles, or 12 students.
  • Unit: The label that tells what a number measures. Example: dollars, hours, miles, pounds, or points.
  • Unknown: The value you are trying to find.
  • Assumption: Something you accept as true while solving because the problem does not state every detail.
  • Reasonableness: A check that asks whether the answer makes sense based on the context, units, and estimate.
  • Context: The real-world story or situation around the numbers.

Why Quantitative Reasoning Comes First

Quantitative reasoning means using numbers, units, and context to understand a situation. It is the thinking you do before, during, and after calculation.

A student can calculate correctly and still answer incorrectly if the student does not understand what the numbers mean. For example, the number 12 could mean 12 dollars, 12 hours, 12 miles, or 12 people. The unit changes the meaning.

In this course, you will often solve real-world problems. That means your answer should not be only a number. A strong answer should include what the number represents and why it makes sense.

How to Read a Real-World Math Problem

Read the problem once to understand the story. Read it a second time to identify the math information.

  1. Find the question. Ask, "What am I being asked to find?"
  2. List the quantities. Write each number with its unit.
  3. Name the unknown. State what the final answer should represent.
  4. Choose a strategy. Decide whether the situation is asking for a total, difference, comparison, rate, or other relationship.
  5. Check reasonableness. Estimate or use context to decide whether the answer makes sense.

Mini-Example: Identify Quantities and Units

Problem: A student works 6 hours and earns $15 per hour. What information is given?

Step 1: Find the question. The question asks for the information that is given, not the final pay yet.

Step 2: List the quantities with units. The quantities are 6 hours and $15 per hour.

Step 3: Name the unknown. If the next question asks for total pay, the unknown would be total dollars earned.

Why this matters: Hours and dollars per hour work together because the unit "per hour" tells you the amount is repeated for each hour.
