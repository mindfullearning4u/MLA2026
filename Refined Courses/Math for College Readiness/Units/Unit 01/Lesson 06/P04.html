P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Study all three worked examples carefully. Each example shows the thinking, not just the final answer.

Example 1

Problem: A car travels 50 miles in 1 hour and 170 miles in 4 hours. Find the average rate of change.

  1. Change in distance: 170 - 50 = 120 miles.
  2. Change in time: 4 - 1 = 3 hours.
  3. 120 divided by 3 = 40.
  4. The average rate is 40 miles per hour.

Final check: The answer includes the correct unit and matches the question.

Example 2

Problem: A table shows cost $20 at 2 items and $44 at 5 items. Find the rate of change.

  1. Change in cost: 44 - 20 = 24 dollars.
  2. Change in items: 5 - 2 = 3 items.
  3. 24 divided by 3 = 8.
  4. The cost increases by $8 per item.

Final check: The answer includes the correct unit and matches the question.

Example 3

Problem: A water tank has 100 gallons at 0 minutes and 70 gallons at 6 minutes. Find the average rate of change.

  1. Change in gallons: 70 - 100 = -30 gallons.
  2. Change in time: 6 - 0 = 6 minutes.
  3. -30 divided by 6 = -5.
  4. The tank loses 5 gallons per minute.

Final check: The answer includes the correct unit and matches the question.

Common Mistake

Incorrect:

rate = final output divided by final input

Why it is wrong: Rate of change compares differences between two points, not only the final values.

Correct:

rate = change in output divided by change in input

Correction: Use the relationship described in the problem, not just the numbers that appear first.
