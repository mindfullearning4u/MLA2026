P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Study all three worked examples carefully. Each example shows the thinking, not just the final answer.

Worked Example 1

Problem: A student has a test average of 80 weighted 70% and homework average of 90 weighted 30%. Find the weighted average.

  1. Step 1: Write weights as decimals: 70% = 0.70 and 30% = 0.30.
  2. Step 2: Multiply: 80(0.70) = 56 and 90(0.30) = 27.
  3. Step 3: Add: 56 + 27 = 83.
  4. Step 4: The weighted average is 83.

Final check: The answer includes the correct unit and matches the question.

Worked Example 2

Problem: Research is 40%, writing is 40%, and presentation is 20%. Scores are 85, 90, and 95. Find the final score.

  1. Step 1: Convert weights: 0.40, 0.40, 0.20.
  2. Step 2: Multiply: 85(0.40)=34, 90(0.40)=36, 95(0.20)=19.
  3. Step 3: Add: 34 + 36 + 19 = 89.
  4. Step 4: The final score is 89.

Final check: The answer includes the correct unit and matches the question.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Explain why a high score in a small category may not change the final average much.

  1. Step 1: Look at the weight first.
  2. Step 2: A small weight means the category has less influence.
  3. Step 3: A 100 on a 10% category contributes 10 points.
  4. Step 4: The size of the weight controls the impact.

Final check: The answer includes the correct unit and matches the question.

Common Mistake

Incorrect:

(80 + 90) divided by 2 = 85

Why it is wrong: The categories do not count equally, so a regular average is not appropriate.

Correct:

80(0.70) + 90(0.30) = 83

Correction: Use the relationship described in the problem, not just the numbers that appear first.
