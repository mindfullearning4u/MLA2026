Proportional Relationships

A proportional relationship keeps the same rate or ratio.

If 4 notebooks cost $10, then 8 notebooks cost $20 because both quantities doubled.

The relationship is proportional when the cost per notebook stays the same.

Example: Use a Proportion

Problem: A business buys 5 boxes for $40. At the same rate, how much would 8 boxes cost?

Unit rate: 40 divided by 5 = 8, so the cost is $8 per box.

Use the rate: 8 boxes x $8 per box = $64.

Answer: 8 boxes would cost $64.

Reasonableness: 8 boxes should cost more than 5 boxes, and $64 is more than $40.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not compare total costs only when quantities are different.

Example: $10 for 4 notebooks and $14 for 7 notebooks cannot be compared fairly by total price alone.

Correct comparison: Find dollars per notebook for each option.
