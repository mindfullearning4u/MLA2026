P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Worked Example 1: Identify association

Problem: A scatterplot shows points rising from left to right.

Visual: rising scatterplot

  1. Step 1: Look at the overall direction of the points.
  2. Step 2: Rising from left to right means y tends to increase as x increases.
  3. Step 3: This is a positive association.

Answer: The variables have a positive association.

Worked Example 2: Interpret slope

Problem: A line of fit predicts score = 62 + 5h, where h is hours studied.

  1. Step 1: Identify the slope: 5.
  2. Step 2: Connect the slope to the variables.
  3. Step 3: For each additional hour studied, the predicted score increases by 5 points.

Answer: The slope means about 5 more points per additional study hour.

Worked Example 3: Use a residual

Problem: A model predicts 84, but the actual value is 80.

  1. Step 1: Residual = actual value - predicted value.
  2. Step 2: Compute 80 - 84 = -4.
  3. Step 3: The negative residual means the actual value was 4 below the prediction.

Answer: The residual is -4, so the model overpredicted by 4.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving only a number, graph direction, or model with no explanation.

Correct: State the result and explain what it means in the data context. Data answers must communicate meaning.
