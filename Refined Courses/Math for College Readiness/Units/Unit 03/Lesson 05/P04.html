P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Example 1: Identify correlation

Problem: A scatterplot shows that students who sleep more hours tend to have higher test scores.

  1. Identify the variables: sleep hours and test score.
  2. The pattern shows that higher sleep hours are associated with higher scores.
  3. This is correlation because the variables move together.

Answer: The data show a positive correlation between sleep hours and test scores.

Example 2: Avoid false causation

Problem: A city finds that sunscreen sales and pool attendance both increase in summer.

  1. Both variables increase together.
  2. A third factor, hot sunny weather, may explain both increases.
  3. Do not claim sunscreen sales cause pool attendance.

Answer: The relationship is correlation, not proof of causation.

Example 3: Evaluate a claim

Problem: An ad says a study proves a snack improves memory because students who ate it scored higher.

  1. Ask whether students were randomly assigned.
  2. Ask whether other factors, such as study time, were controlled.
  3. If those details are missing, the claim may be stronger than the evidence.

Answer: The data may show an association, but the claim needs stronger evidence to prove causation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Repeating a claim exactly as written without judging the evidence.

Correct: Identify the evidence, check for limits, and write a conclusion that is supported by the data.
