Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Correlation, Causation, and Claims

Vocabulary

  • Correlation: a relationship or association between two variables.
  • Causation: when one event or variable directly causes another.
  • Association: a pattern showing that two variables tend to occur together.
  • Claim: a statement someone makes about data.
  • Evidence: data or reasoning that supports a claim.
  • Confounding variable: another factor that may explain an observed relationship.
  • Responsible conclusion: a conclusion that does not say more than the data can support.

Learn the Idea

A correlation means two variables are related in some way. It does not automatically mean one variable caused the other. For example, ice cream sales and air conditioner use may both increase during hot weather. The shared factor is temperature, not that ice cream causes air conditioner use.

  1. Identify the population, sample, variables, or claim.
  2. Decide what relationship or conclusion is being described.
  3. Look for missing information, bias, or other possible explanations.
  4. Write a cautious interpretation that matches the evidence.

Notebook Task

Copy the vocabulary and the four-step process into your notebook. Add one original example of a data claim from school, work, sports, health, social media, or advertising.
