Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 2: Correlation, Causation, and Claims

Build the Reasoning

When you evaluate a claim, ask what the data actually show. Look for the variables, the sample, the wording of the conclusion, and whether another factor could explain the relationship. A strong claim matches the evidence and avoids cause-and-effect language unless the study design supports it.

A complete response explains what is known, what is not known, and what conclusion is reasonable. This helps prevent misleading or exaggerated data claims.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Treating a relationship or survey result as proof without checking the sample, wording, or possible other explanations.

Correct: Match the conclusion to the evidence. State limits clearly when the data do not prove the strongest claim.

Notebook Task

Write one exaggerated claim and one corrected responsible claim. Explain why the corrected version is more accurate.
