P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Example 1: Read a two-way table

Problem: A table compares grade level and whether students use tutoring.

  1. Identify the two categorical variables: grade level and tutoring use.
  2. Use row or column totals to compare groups fairly.
  3. Write the comparison using counts or percents.

Answer: A two-way table helps compare tutoring use across grade levels.

Example 2: Evaluate a report claim

Problem: A headline says a new schedule improves grades because grades were higher after the schedule changed.

  1. Identify the claim: the schedule caused higher grades.
  2. Ask whether other factors changed too.
  3. If other factors were not controlled, the report may show association but not proof of causation.

Answer: The claim may be too strong without stronger evidence.

Example 3: Identify a limitation

Problem: A poll about school lunches surveys only students buying lunch on pizza day.

  1. The population is all students.
  2. The sample is students buying lunch on one special day.
  3. That sample may overrepresent students who like school lunch.

Answer: The conclusion should be limited because the sample may be biased.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving only a number or repeating a claim with no explanation of evidence or limits.

Correct: State the result, explain what it means, and describe any limits in the data context.
