P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Example 1: Choose a display for categories

Problem: A survey asks 60 students how they get to school: bus, car, walk, or bike.

  1. The data are categorical because each answer is a label.
  2. A bar graph is appropriate because it compares category counts.
  3. The graph should label each transportation method and show the frequency for each one.

Answer: Use a bar graph because the data compare categories.

Example 2: Choose a display for numerical values

Problem: A class records the number of minutes each student studies in one evening.

  1. The data are numerical because minutes are measured with numbers.
  2. If there are many values, group them into intervals such as 0-15, 16-30, and 31-45 minutes.
  3. A histogram is appropriate because it shows how the numerical data are distributed across intervals.

Answer: Use a histogram because the data are numerical and can be grouped into intervals.

Example 3: Interpret a display

Problem: A bar graph shows 18 students chose music, 12 chose sports, 9 chose games, and 6 chose art as favorite activities.

  1. Identify the largest bar: music has 18 students.
  2. Identify the smallest bar: art has 6 students.
  3. Compare using subtraction: 18 - 6 = 12 more students chose music than art.

Answer: Music was most common, art was least common, and music had 12 more students than art.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Writing only the final number or graph choice with no explanation.

Correct: State the answer and explain what it means in the context. Data answers must communicate meaning, not just procedure.
