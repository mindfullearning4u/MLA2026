Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Data Displays and Representations

Vocabulary

  • Data: information collected to answer a question.
  • Categorical data: data sorted into groups or labels, such as favorite app or type of transportation.
  • Numerical data: data measured or counted with numbers, such as cost, time, age, or score.
  • Frequency: how many times a value or category appears.
  • Relative frequency: the part of the total represented by a category, often written as a percent.
  • Bar graph: a display that compares categories.
  • Histogram: a display that groups numerical data into intervals.
  • Line graph: a display that shows change over time.

Learn the Idea

Before choosing a graph, decide what kind of data you have. Categorical data use names or labels. Numerical data use numbers that measure or count something. This decision matters because the display must match the data type.

Start by reading the question, identifying what was measured or counted, and deciding what the numbers or categories represent. Do not calculate first. In data literacy, the first step is understanding the context.

  1. Name the question being answered by the data.
  2. Identify whether each variable is categorical or numerical.
  3. Look for units, labels, and the total number of observations.
  4. Choose a display or summary that matches the data type.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, write the vocabulary terms and copy the four-step process. Then create a small example of your own with at least five data values or categories.
