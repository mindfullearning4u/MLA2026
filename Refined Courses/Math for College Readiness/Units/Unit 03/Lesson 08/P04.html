P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Example 1: Combine display and context

Problem: A bar graph shows 40 students chose videos and 25 chose printed notes.

  1. Identify the display as a bar graph.
  2. Compare category counts: videos have 15 more students.
  3. Explain the result in context.

Answer: More students chose videos than printed notes, by 15 students.

Example 2: Combine center and spread

Problem: Two classes have the same median, but Class B has a larger range.

  1. Same median means the typical score is similar.
  2. Larger range means Class B is more spread out.
  3. Use both facts in the conclusion.

Answer: The classes have similar centers, but Class B is less consistent.

Example 3: Combine claim and sampling

Problem: A survey says a program caused improvement, but only volunteers responded.

  1. Volunteers may not represent the full population.
  2. The report may show improvement among respondents.
  3. The sample limits the strength of the conclusion.

Answer: The claim should be cautious because the sample may be biased.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving only a number or repeating a claim with no explanation of evidence or limits.

Correct: State the result, explain what it means, and describe any limits in the data context.
