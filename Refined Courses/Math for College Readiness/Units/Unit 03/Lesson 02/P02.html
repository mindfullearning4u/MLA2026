Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Measures of Center and Variability

Vocabulary

  • Mean: the balance point found by adding the values and dividing by the number of values.
  • Median: the middle value when the data are ordered from least to greatest.
  • Mode: the value or category that appears most often.
  • Range: the difference between the greatest and least values.
  • Quartiles: values that divide ordered data into four parts.
  • Interquartile range: Q3 - Q1, the spread of the middle half of the data.
  • Outlier: a value far away from the rest of the data.
  • Shape: the overall pattern of a data set, such as clustered, spread out, or skewed.

Learn the Idea

A measure of center helps describe a typical value in a data set. The mean uses every value, so it can be pulled by very large or very small numbers. The median is the middle value, so it is often better when a data set has an outlier.

Start by reading the question, identifying what was measured or counted, and deciding what the numbers or categories represent. Do not calculate first. In data literacy, the first step is understanding the context.

  1. Name the question being answered by the data.
  2. Identify whether each variable is categorical or numerical.
  3. Look for units, labels, and the total number of observations.
  4. Choose a display or summary that matches the data type.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, write the vocabulary terms and copy the four-step process. Then create a small example of your own with at least five data values or categories.
