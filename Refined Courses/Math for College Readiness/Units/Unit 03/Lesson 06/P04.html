P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Example 1: Identify population and sample

Problem: A town surveys 200 residents to estimate opinions of all town residents.

  1. The population is all town residents.
  2. The sample is the 200 residents surveyed.
  3. The conclusion should be about the town only if the sample represents the town well.

Answer: Population: all town residents. Sample: the 200 residents surveyed.

Example 2: Recognize bias

Problem: A sports store asks only basketball players which sport is most popular.

  1. The question is about sport popularity.
  2. The sample includes only basketball players.
  3. This sample is likely biased toward basketball.

Answer: The sample is biased because it does not represent all possible customers or athletes.

Example 3: Improve a sample

Problem: A school surveys only first-period students about lunch options.

  1. First-period students may not represent the whole school.
  2. A better method includes students from different grades and schedules.
  3. Random selection across the school would reduce bias.

Answer: A random sample from all grade levels would better represent the school.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Repeating a claim exactly as written without judging the evidence.

Correct: Identify the evidence, check for limits, and write a conclusion that is supported by the data.
