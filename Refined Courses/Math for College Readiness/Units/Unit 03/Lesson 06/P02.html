Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Sampling and Bias

Vocabulary

  • Population: the entire group a question is about.
  • Sample: the smaller group actually measured or surveyed.
  • Representative sample: a sample that reflects the population reasonably well.
  • Bias: a systematic problem that makes results unfair or misleading.
  • Random sample: a sample chosen by chance so each member has a fair opportunity to be selected.
  • Convenience sample: a sample chosen because it is easy to reach.
  • Survey wording: the way a question is written, which can influence responses.

Learn the Idea

A sample is useful only if it helps represent the population. If the population is all students in a school, surveying only students in one club may leave out important groups. Sampling decisions affect what conclusions are reasonable.

  1. Identify the population, sample, variables, or claim.
  2. Decide what relationship or conclusion is being described.
  3. Look for missing information, bias, or other possible explanations.
  4. Write a cautious interpretation that matches the evidence.

Notebook Task

Copy the vocabulary and the four-step process into your notebook. Add one original example of a data claim from school, work, sports, health, social media, or advertising.
