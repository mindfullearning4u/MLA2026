Notebook Title

Lesson 4: Systems of Equations and Decision Making

Vocabulary

  • System of equations: Two or more equations considered together.
  • Solution of a system: The point or values that make all equations true.
  • Intersection: The point where two graphs meet.
  • Break-even point: The point where two costs, plans, or values are equal.
  • Substitution: Solving by replacing a variable with an equivalent expression.
  • Elimination: Solving by combining equations to remove a variable.

Why This Lesson Matters

Algebra helps people make decisions when there is an unknown quantity, a limit, or two competing choices.

Before solving, identify what the variable represents and what the symbols or equations mean in the situation.

If Plan A costs 20 + 10m and Plan B costs 50 + 5m, the break-even point happens when 20 + 10m = 50 + 5m.

Step-by-Step Thinking Process

  1. Read the situation and identify the decision or question.
  2. Define the variable clearly.
  3. Write the inequality, equation, or system that matches the context.
  4. Solve one step at a time and keep the comparison or equality accurate.
  5. Interpret the result using units and context.
  6. Check whether the answer is reasonable.
