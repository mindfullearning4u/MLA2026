Notebook Title

Lesson 5: Exponential Models in Context

Vocabulary

  • Exponential model: A model where a quantity is multiplied by the same factor over equal intervals.
  • Initial value: The starting amount when the input is 0.
  • Growth factor: The multiplier used when a quantity increases.
  • Decay factor: The multiplier used when a quantity decreases.
  • Percent change: The percent increase or decrease each interval.
  • Asymptote: A line a graph approaches but does not cross in the model.

Why This Lesson Matters

Many real-world situations do not change by simple addition every time. Some multiply by a factor, and some change rules depending on the input.

Before solving, identify the starting value, rule, interval, multiplier, or boundary that controls the model.

If a savings account starts with $200 and grows by 5% each year, the model can be written A = 200(1.05)t. The 200 is the initial value and 1.05 is the growth factor.

Step-by-Step Thinking Process

  1. Read the situation and identify what changes.
  2. Identify the starting value, multiplier, interval, or boundary.
  3. Write the model or choose the correct rule.
  4. Evaluate or interpret the model one step at a time.
  5. Write the answer with units and context.
  6. Check whether the answer is reasonable.
