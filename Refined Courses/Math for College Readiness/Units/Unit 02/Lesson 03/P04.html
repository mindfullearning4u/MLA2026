P04 Worked Example

Study all three worked examples carefully. Each example shows the thinking, not just the final answer.

Example 1

Problem: A student can spend at most $60 on tickets that cost $15 each. Write and solve an inequality.

  1. Define the variable: t = number of tickets.
  2. At most means less than or equal to.
  3. Write the inequality: 15t = 60.
  4. Divide by 15: t = 4.
  5. The student can buy 4 or fewer tickets.

Final check: The solution is interpreted in the real-world situation.

Example 2

Problem: A worker needs to earn at least $120 and earns $15 per hour. Write and solve an inequality.

  1. Define the variable: h = number of hours.
  2. At least means greater than or equal to.
  3. Write 15h = 120.
  4. Divide by 15: h = 8.
  5. The worker needs at least 8 hours.

Final check: The solution is interpreted in the real-world situation.

Example 3

Problem: A delivery order has a $5 fee plus $3 per mile. The budget is no more than $26. Find possible miles.

  1. Define the variable: m = miles.
  2. Write 5 + 3m = 26.
  3. Subtract 5: 3m = 21.
  4. Divide by 3: m = 7.
  5. The delivery can be 7 miles or fewer.

Final check: The solution is interpreted in the real-world situation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect:

15t < 60, so t < 4

Why it is wrong: The phrase at most includes the boundary amount. The correct symbol is =, not <.

Correct:

15t = 60, so t = 4

Correction: Keep the algebra connected to the wording of the situation.
