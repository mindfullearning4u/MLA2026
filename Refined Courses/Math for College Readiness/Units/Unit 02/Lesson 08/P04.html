P04 Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Study all three worked examples carefully. Each example shows the thinking, not just the final answer.

Worked Example 1

Problem: A phone plan costs $30 plus $12 per month. Find the cost for 5 months.

  1. Step 1: Identify the model type: linear cost model.
  2. Step 2: Write C = 30 + 12m.
  3. Step 3: Substitute m = 5.
  4. Step 4: C = 30 + 12(5) = 90.
  5. Step 5: The cost for 5 months is $90.

Final check: The solution is interpreted in the real-world situation.

Worked Example 2

Problem: A budget is at most $75. Tickets cost $15 each. Find possible ticket counts.

  1. Step 1: Identify the model type: inequality.
  2. Step 2: Write 15t = 75.
  3. Step 3: Divide by 15: t = 5.
  4. Step 4: Ticket counts must be whole numbers.
  5. Step 5: The person can buy 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 tickets.

Final check: The solution is interpreted in the real-world situation.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Two plans are A = 20 + 10m and B = 50 + 5m. Find the break-even point.

  1. Step 1: Identify the model type: system or break-even equation.
  2. Step 2: Set the models equal: 20 + 10m = 50 + 5m.
  3. Step 3: Subtract 5m and 20: 5m = 30.
  4. Step 4: Divide by 5: m = 6.
  5. Step 5: The plans cost the same after 6 months.

Final check: The solution is interpreted in the real-world situation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect:

The answer is 6

Why it is wrong: A number alone is not enough in a real-world model. The answer must include units and context.

Correct:

The plans cost the same after 6 months

Correction: The final answer must be mathematically correct and meaningful in the situation.
