P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Worked Example 1

Question: A number cube is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 1 or 2?

  1. Step 1: The events do not overlap.
  2. Step 2: P(1) = 1/6 and P(2) = 1/6.
  3. Step 3: Add: 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3.

Worked Example 2

Question: A number cube is rolled. What is the probability of rolling an odd number or a number less than 3?

  1. Step 1: Odd outcomes are {1, 3, 5}.
  2. Step 2: Less than 3 outcomes are {1, 2}.
  3. Step 3: The overlap is {1}.
  4. Step 4: The union is {1, 2, 3, 5}, so the probability is 4/6 = 2/3.

Worked Example 3

Question: A fair coin is flipped and a number cube is rolled. What is the probability of tails and rolling a 6?

  1. Step 1: The coin and cube are independent.
  2. Step 2: P(tails) = 1/2 and P(6) = 1/6.
  3. Step 3: Multiply: 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: For heads and rolling a 3, add 1/2 + 1/6.

This uses the addition rule even though both events must happen.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: For heads and rolling a 3, multiply 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.

The word and tells you both independent events must occur.
