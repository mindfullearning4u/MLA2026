Reading a Two-Way Table

A two-way table organizes two categories at the same time. For example, students might be classified by grade level and by whether they ride the bus. The inside cells are joint counts. The totals on the edge are marginal totals.

Student GroupRides BusDoes Not Ride BusTotal
Grade 11181230
Grade 12141630
Total322860

The relative frequency for a randomly selected student riding the bus is 32/60. That means 32 out of 60 students in the data set ride the bus.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: To find the probability that a student rides the bus, use 18/60 because 18 is the first bus count in the table.

This uses only one cell. The question asks about all students who ride the bus, so the column total is needed.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: Use 32/60 because the total number of students who ride the bus is 32 out of the grand total of 60.

Always decide whether the question asks for one cell, one row, one column, or the whole table.

Notebook Task

Copy the two-way table and label one joint frequency, one row total, one column total, and the grand total.
