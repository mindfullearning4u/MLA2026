Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Experimental and Theoretical Probability

Vocabulary

  • Theoretical probability: the expected probability based on equally likely outcomes.
  • Experimental probability: probability based on observed results from trials or data.
  • Empirical probability: another name for probability based on collected data.
  • Relative frequency: part / total from a data set or table.
  • Two-way table: a table that organizes counts for two categories at once.
  • Joint frequency: a count inside the body of a two-way table.
  • Marginal total: a row total, column total, or grand total.

Learn the Idea

Theoretical probability tells what should happen if all outcomes are equally likely. Experimental probability tells what actually happened in data. These may be different, especially when the number of trials is small.

Example: A fair coin has a theoretical probability of heads equal to 1/2. If a student flips the coin 20 times and gets heads 12 times, the experimental probability is 12/20 = 3/5 = 60%.

  1. Identify the outcome or group being asked about.
  2. Find the correct count for that outcome or group.
  3. Find the correct total being compared to.
  4. Write relative frequency as count / total and explain it in context.

Notebook Task

Copy the vocabulary and the four-step process into your notebook. Then write one sentence explaining how theoretical probability and experimental probability are different.
