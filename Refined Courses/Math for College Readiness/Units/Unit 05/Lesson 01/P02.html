Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Probability Foundations

Vocabulary

  • Outcome: one possible result.
  • Sample space: the complete list of possible outcomes.
  • Event: the outcome or group of outcomes being described.
  • Complement: everything in the sample space that is not in the event.
  • Union: outcomes that are in one event, the other event, or both.
  • Intersection: outcomes that are in both events at the same time.
  • Equally likely: each outcome has the same chance of happening.

Learn the Idea

Probability begins with careful reading. Before calculating, ask: What can happen? What outcome am I looking for? Are all outcomes equally likely? A sample space prevents guessing because it shows the full set of possibilities.

  1. Name the action, such as rolling a number cube or choosing a card.
  2. List the sample space. For one number cube, the sample space is {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
  3. Circle or name the outcomes that match the event.
  4. Describe the event in words before writing a probability.

For an equally likely sample space, probability is favorable outcomes / total outcomes. For example, rolling an even number on a number cube has outcomes {2, 4, 6}, so the probability is 3/6 = 1/2 = 50%.

Notebook Task

Copy the vocabulary and four-step process into your notebook. Then create one sample space from everyday life, such as choosing a shirt color, selecting a snack, spinning a game spinner, or rolling a number cube.
