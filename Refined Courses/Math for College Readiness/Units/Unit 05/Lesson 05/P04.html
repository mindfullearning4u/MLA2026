P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Worked Example 1

Question: A password has one letter followed by one digit. There are 4 letter choices and 3 digit choices. How many passwords are possible?

  1. Step 1: There are 4 choices for the letter.
  2. Step 2: There are 3 choices for the digit.
  3. Step 3: Multiply: 4 × 3 = 12 passwords.

Worked Example 2

Question: Four students run for president and vice president. How many ordered results are possible?

  1. Step 1: President has 4 choices.
  2. Step 2: After president is chosen, vice president has 3 choices.
  3. Step 3: Order matters because the offices are different.
  4. Step 4: Multiply: 4 × 3 = 12 ordered results.

Worked Example 3

Question: Four students are available, and two will be chosen for the same committee. How many groups are possible?

  1. Step 1: If order were counted, there would be 4 × 3 = 12 ordered pairs.
  2. Step 2: Each group is counted twice, such as Ana-Ben and Ben-Ana.
  3. Step 3: Divide by 2: 12 / 2 = 6 groups.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: For a two-person committee from 4 people, count 4 × 3 = 12 groups.

This treats the committee as if order matters.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: A two-person committee from 4 people has 6 groups.

Since order does not matter, divide the 12 ordered pairs by 2.
