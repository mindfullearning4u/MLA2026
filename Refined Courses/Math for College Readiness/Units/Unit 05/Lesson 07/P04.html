P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each example in order. Notice how the work identifies the context, selects a strategy, calculates carefully, and interprets the result.

Worked Example 1

Question: Option A has a 1/2 chance to gain $10 and a 1/2 chance to lose $2. What is the expected value?

  1. Step 1: (1/2)($10) = $5.
  2. Step 2: (1/2)(-$2) = -$1.
  3. Step 3: Expected value is $4, so the decision is favorable on average.

Worked Example 2

Question: Option B has a 1/10 chance to gain $100 and costs $15 to try. Is it favorable by expected net value?

  1. Step 1: Expected gross value is (1/10)($100) = $10.
  2. Step 2: Subtract cost: $10 - $15 = -$5.
  3. Step 3: The expected net value is negative, so it is unfavorable on average.

Worked Example 3

Question: One plan is safer with smaller reward. Another plan is riskier with higher reward. What should a written recommendation include?

  1. Step 1: Probability of success and failure for each plan.
  2. Step 2: Possible reward and possible loss.
  3. Step 3: Expected value if enough information is available.
  4. Step 4: A clear statement about assumptions and risk tolerance.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: A risky choice is always bad.

Risk is not automatically bad. It must be compared with reward, probability, and context.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: A risk can be reasonable if the expected value, evidence, and consequences support it.

A complete recommendation explains both the numbers and the possible downside.
