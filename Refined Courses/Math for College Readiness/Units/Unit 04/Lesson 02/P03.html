Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 2: Budgeting and Net Worth

Build the Reasoning

Net worth measures financial position at one point in time. It is not the same as income. To find net worth, add all assets, add all liabilities, and subtract liabilities from assets. A person can have income but still have low or negative net worth if debts are larger than assets.

A complete financial answer includes the calculation, the label, and a short interpretation. The interpretation matters because money decisions are based on what the number means.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Using the first number you see without checking whether it is income, expense, asset, liability, gross pay, or net pay.

Correct: Identify what each amount represents first, then choose the calculation that matches the financial question.

Notebook Task

Write one incorrect financial setup and one corrected setup. Explain why the corrected setup matches the situation.

Teacher-Style Thinking Guide

In Budgeting and Net Worth, treat the notebook task as a worked investigation, not a quick answer. First identify what the problem gives you, what it asks you to find or decide, and which representation is most useful: equation, table, graph, diagram, probability model, financial comparison, or written claim.

Next, explain the reason for each step. For example, if you divide, state what rate, unit, probability, or comparison the division creates. If you choose a formula or model, state why the context fits that model and what each variable represents.

This work should connect directly to MLA.MCR.FL.1, MLA.MCR.FL.3, so every answer must show the calculation, representation, or explanation required by the standard.

A strong notebook entry includes labeled work, a final interpretation sentence, and a quick reasonableness check. Ask yourself whether the answer is too large, too small, outside a possible range, missing a unit, or unsupported by the evidence shown.
