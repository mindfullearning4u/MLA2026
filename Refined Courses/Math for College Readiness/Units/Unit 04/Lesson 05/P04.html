P04 Guided Practice / Worked Example

Example 1: Calculate annual premium

Problem: An insurance plan costs $75 per month.

  1. Identify monthly premium: $75.
  2. Identify 12 months in one year.
  3. Multiply: 75 Ã— 12 = 900.

Answer: The annual premium cost is $900.

Example 2: Compare deductible risk

Problem: Plan A has a $500 deductible. Plan B has a $1,500 deductible.

  1. A deductible is paid before insurance covers certain costs.
  2. Plan B requires more out-of-pocket money before coverage begins.
  3. Plan A has lower deductible risk.

Answer: Plan A has the lower deductible risk.

Example 3: Compare total possible cost

Problem: A plan has annual premiums of $1,200 and a deductible of $800.

  1. Annual premium is the predictable yearly cost.
  2. Deductible is a possible out-of-pocket cost if a covered event occurs.
  3. Add for a simple possible-cost comparison: 1,200 + 800 = 2,000.

Answer: A simple premium-plus-deductible comparison is $2,000.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Writing only a dollar amount without explaining whether it is premium cost, deductible risk, contribution total, growth, or ending balance.

Correct: Label the amount and explain what it means for the financial decision.
