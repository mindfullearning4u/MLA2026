Notebook Title

Notebook Task Part 1: Simple and Compound Interest

Vocabulary

  • Principal: the starting amount of money borrowed, saved, or invested.
  • Interest: money earned on savings or charged on borrowed money.
  • Rate: the percent used to calculate interest.
  • Simple interest: interest calculated only on the original principal.
  • Compound interest: interest calculated on the principal plus previously earned interest.
  • Balance: the total amount after interest is added or payments are made.
  • Compounding period: how often interest is added.
  • Spreadsheet growth table: a table that tracks balance over time.

Learn the Idea

Interest is the cost of using money or the reward for saving money. Simple interest grows by the same amount each period because it uses only the original principal. Compound interest can grow faster because interest is added to the balance and then future interest is based on the new balance.

  1. Identify the financial question being asked.
  2. List principal, rate, time, payment, fee, or term when given.
  3. Choose the calculation that matches the situation.
  4. Write the answer with dollars and a sentence explaining what it means.

Notebook Task

Copy the vocabulary and the four-step process into your notebook. Add one original example involving saving, borrowing, credit, a loan, or debt repayment.
