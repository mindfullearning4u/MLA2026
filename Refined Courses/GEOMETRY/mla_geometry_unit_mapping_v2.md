**Reference Files:** `mla_geometry_standards_crosswalk.xlsx`; `mla_geometry_unit_mapping_v1.md`; `mla_geometry_unit_mapping_audit.md`; `Geometry_Alternative_Sequence_Analysis.md`  
**Purpose:** This document establishes when and in what order Geometry concepts should be taught. The standards crosswalk establishes what must be taught; this unit mapping establishes the instructional blueprint.

---

## Course Sequencing Principles

This revision preserves the six-unit course structure, eight lessons per unit, Lesson 8 synthesis model, Florida B.E.S.T. coverage, Common Core alignment, SAT alignment, ACT alignment, and MLA mastery framework.

The course is sequenced by prerequisite skill progression, conceptual development, student readiness, cognitive scaffolding, support for struggling learners, and college/career readiness.

The revised instructional progression is:

Geometry Foundations -> Reasoning, Proof, and Basic Transformations -> Triangles, Congruence, and Quadrilaterals -> Similarity, Right Triangles, and Trigonometry -> Circles and Circle Measurement -> Coordinate Geometry, Measurement, and Modeling

## Unit 1: Foundations of Geometry

**Unit Purpose:** Establish the reasoning foundation for Geometry through precise diagrams, vocabulary, construction reasoning, conditional reasoning, argument evaluation, and introductory proof.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.FND.1, MLA.GEO.FND.2, MLA.GEO.RP.1, MLA.GEO.RP.4, MLA.GEO.RP.5, MLA.GEO.RP.6

**Unit Justification:** Geometry begins with precise diagram interpretation, construction fluency, logical reasoning, and proof habits so students have a stable foundation before triangle, transformation, similarity, circle, and coordinate-geometry work.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Basic geometric vocabulary, measuring and classifying angles, simple algebraic reasoning, coordinate-plane familiarity, and willingness to support conclusions with diagram evidence.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports proof, transformations, triangles, similarity, circles, coordinate geometry, measurement, and modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Construction challenges; diagram evidence analysis; conditional-statement sorting; counterexample tasks; introductory proof-completion tasks; open-ended diagram labeling tasks.

## Unit 2: Reasoning, Proof, and Basic Transformations

**Unit Purpose:** Develop logical reasoning, introductory proof habits, and visual transformation reasoning that supports later congruence and similarity.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.RP.4, MLA.GEO.RP.6, MLA.GEO.RP.5, MLA.GEO.RP.1, MLA.GEO.CGM.1, MLA.GEO.CGM.2, MLA.GEO.CGM.5, MLA.GEO.CGM.6

**Unit Justification:** Reasoning and proof remain early, but proof fluency is treated as developmental. Basic transformations move earlier so students have a visual model for congruence before triangle congruence proofs.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Geometric vocabulary, angle relationships, segment relationships, diagram labeling, coordinate plane foundations, distance, and construction precision.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports triangle congruence, quadrilateral proof, similarity, dilations, coordinate transformations, circle similarity, coordinate proof, and modeling explanations.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Proof extension tasks; counterexample challenges; transformation composition challenges; SAT-style diagram logic; ACT-style angle relationship problems; student-created valid and invalid arguments.

## Unit 3: Triangles, Congruence, and Quadrilaterals

**Unit Purpose:** Develop understanding of triangle properties, congruence relationships, geometric reasoning, and quadrilateral proof before coordinate proof.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.TRI.2, MLA.GEO.TRI.1, MLA.GEO.TRI.3, MLA.GEO.RP.2, MLA.GEO.RP.3

**Unit Justification:** Triangles come after proof and basic transformations because students can now connect congruence criteria to rigid motion and formal justification. Quadrilateral relationships move earlier so students learn properties synthetically before Unit 6 coordinate proof.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Angle relationships, segment relationships, proof structure, transformations, constructions, congruent figures, and diagram marking.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports similarity, right-triangle trigonometry, coordinate proof, circle inscribed figures, and geometric modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Triangle proof extensions; congruence error analysis; quadrilateral proof challenges; construction-based congruence tasks; SAT/ACT triangle and quadrilateral applications; open-ended design constraints.

## Unit 4: Similarity, Right Triangles, and Trigonometry

**Unit Purpose:** Develop proportional reasoning, similarity transformations, right-triangle relationships, and trigonometric applications.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.CGM.3, MLA.GEO.CGM.4, MLA.GEO.SIM.2, MLA.GEO.SIM.3, MLA.GEO.SIM.1, MLA.GEO.TRI.4, MLA.GEO.TRI.5

**Unit Justification:** Similarity follows congruence because students can extend same-size reasoning into proportional same-shape reasoning. Dilations and similarity transformations are placed before similar triangles and right-triangle applications so students understand similarity visually before using it procedurally.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Congruence, transformations, ratios, proportions, triangle relationships, square roots, algebraic equation solving, and calculator/tool use.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports circle similarity, arc length, sector area, coordinate modeling, scale factor effects, and SAT/ACT trigonometry readiness.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Dilation investigations; similarity proof challenges; indirect measurement tasks; SAT/ACT right-triangle problems; trigonometric modeling; multiple-representation error analysis.

## Unit 5: Circles and Circle Measurement

**Unit Purpose:** Develop circle relationships, circle constructions, circle measurement, and circle applications without overloading the unit with coordinate circle equations.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.CIR.8, MLA.GEO.CIR.5, MLA.GEO.CIR.6, MLA.GEO.CIR.4, MLA.GEO.CIR.1, MLA.GEO.CIR.2, MLA.GEO.CIR.3, MLA.GEO.CIR.7

**Unit Justification:** Circles follow proof, constructions, transformations, similarity, and trigonometry because circle relationships draw on angle reasoning, proportional reasoning, construction fluency, and measurement. Coordinate circle equations move to Unit 6 where coordinate prerequisites are active.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Angle relationships, triangle/quadrilateral relationships, constructions, similarity, proportional reasoning, circumference, area, and basic algebraic equation solving.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports coordinate circle equations, conic cross-sections, measurement, scale factor effects, and geometric modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Circle theorem error analysis; construction challenges; SAT/ACT circle measurement problems; inscribed polygon investigations; open-ended circle modeling; proof extensions involving arcs, chords, tangents, and secants.

**Unit Purpose:** Apply geometric concepts using coordinate methods, circle equations, measurement formulas, scale effects, spatial visualization, and real-world modeling.

## Revision Summary

### What Changed

- Unit 2 was revised from `Reasoning and Proof` to `Reasoning, Proof, and Basic Transformations`.
- Basic transformations and rigid-motion congruence moved from Unit 5 to Unit 2.
- Similarity transformations and dilations moved from Unit 5 to Unit 4.
- Quadrilateral relationships and proofs moved from Unit 6 to Unit 3.
- Unit 5 was revised from `Transformations and Circles` to `Circles and Circle Measurement`.
- Circle equations and graphing circles moved from Unit 5 to Unit 6.
- Unit 6 measurement was redistributed into separate lessons for coordinate area/2D area, surface area, volume, scale effects, and spatial modeling.
- Trigonometric ratios and right-triangle applications were separated into two lessons in Unit 4.

### Why It Changed

- Transformations were moved earlier because rigid motion is a conceptual foundation for congruence and should support triangle congruence rather than appear after it.
- Proof remains early, but proof fluency is now developed across the course instead of being concentrated too heavily in Unit 2.
- Quadrilateral properties were moved earlier so students learn synthetic quadrilateral reasoning before coordinate proof.
- Circle concepts were redistributed so Unit 5 focuses on circle geometry, constructions, and measurement, while coordinate circle equations appear in the coordinate geometry unit.
- Measurement and modeling were split to reduce formula overload and separate 2D measurement, surface area, volume, scale, and spatial visualization.

### Expected Instructional Benefit

- Struggling learners receive more visual support before formal proof and less cognitive overload in Units 5 and 6.
- Advanced learners receive stronger opportunities for transformation-based reasoning, proof extension, coordinate challenges, and modeling investigations.
- SAT readiness is strengthened through earlier transformation reasoning, clearer similarity/trigonometry development, coordinate circle equations, and distributed measurement practice.
- ACT readiness is strengthened through cleaner geometry skill clusters, improved formula selection practice, and stronger applied modeling progression.
- Mastery-based instruction is strengthened because major skills are sequenced from readiness to application to synthesis, with Lesson 8 reserved for integration rather than first exposure.

## Instructional Sequencing Validation

This sequence is educationally sound because it begins with Geometry readiness: vocabulary, diagrams, coordinate plane review, distance, midpoint, measurement tools, and constructions. These access skills support proof, transformations, congruence, similarity, circles, coordinate geometry, measurement, and modeling.

Reasoning and proof appear early because proof is a coursewide habit. Basic transformations now appear early as a visual bridge into congruence, reducing abstraction for students who struggle with formal proof. Triangle congruence follows transformations so rigid motion can support congruence criteria. Quadrilateral reasoning follows triangle proof so students can use established proof tools before coordinate classification.

Similarity and trigonometry follow congruence because proportional reasoning extends same-size relationships into same-shape relationships. Dilations are placed before similar triangles so students can understand similarity as a transformation concept. Circle geometry follows similarity, constructions, angle reasoning, and proportional reasoning. Coordinate circle equations move to Unit 6 because they rely on distance, midpoint, algebraic interpretation, and coordinate modeling.

The final unit applies the full course toolkit through coordinate proof, coordinate circle equations, 2D measurement, surface area, volume, scale effects, spatial visualization, and modeling. The measurement sequence moves from concrete 2D area to 3D surface area and volume before scale and modeling.

The course supports mastery-based learning because each lesson includes named prerequisites, a reason for placement, future-learning connections, and consistent mastery evidence: Notebook Evidence, Checkpoint Submission, and Lesson Quiz. Lesson 8 in every unit remains `Putting It All Together` to consolidate learning before advancement.
