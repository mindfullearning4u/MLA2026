# MLA Geometry Unit Mapping Instructional Sequencing Audit

**Reviewed File:** `mla_geometry_unit_mapping_v1.md`  
**Audit Lens:** Veteran high school Geometry teacher review  
**Audit Scope:** Instructional sequencing only. This audit does **not** evaluate standards coverage.

---

## Executive Summary

The overall course arc is instructionally reasonable: foundations -> proof -> triangles -> similarity/right triangles -> transformations/circles -> coordinate geometry/measurement/modeling.

The main concerns are not the six-unit structure itself. The concerns are:

- Proof may become too abstract too early unless proof is scaffolded heavily.  
- Transformations are placed later than ideal for a transformation-based Geometry course.  
- Unit 5 combines too much: transformations, circle similarity, circle equations, arcs/chords, tangents/secants, constructions, sectors, and circle applications.  
- Unit 6 Lesson 3 and Unit 6 Lesson 5 are overloaded.  
- Some prerequisite skills should be made more explicit before proof, transformations, circles, surface area/volume, and trigonometry.

**Instructional determination:** Pass with Revisions

---

## 1. Lessons Placed Too Early

### Unit 2: Reasoning and Proof

Reasoning and proof belong early in Geometry, but Unit 2 may be too proof-heavy too soon for struggling students.

Specific concern:

- Unit 2 Lesson 4, `Introduction to Geometric Proofs`, appears before students have much theorem-rich geometric content beyond segments and angles.

This can work if the proof expectations are introductory and highly scaffolded. If students are expected to write full formal proofs too quickly, cognitive load will be high.

**Recommendation:** Keep Unit 2, but make Lesson 4 a low-stakes proof-structure lesson using segment and angle relationships only. Delay more complex formal proof fluency until triangle proofs in Unit 3.

### Unit 5 Lesson 4: Circle Fundamentals

This lesson includes circle vocabulary, circle similarity, and equations of circles. That is a lot at the start of the circle sequence.

Circle equations require coordinate distance reasoning and completing-square-style algebra readiness, which may fit better near coordinate geometry.

**Recommendation:** Keep basic circle vocabulary and circle similarity in Unit 5, but consider moving circle equations to Unit 6 coordinate geometry or making them a separate bridge lesson before coordinate modeling.

---

## 2. Lessons Placed Too Late

### Transformations

Transformations are placed in Unit 5, after proof, triangles, congruence, similarity, and right triangles. In many modern Geometry sequences, transformations are introduced before or alongside congruence and similarity because they provide the conceptual definition of congruence and similarity.

Students would benefit from earlier exposure to:

- Translations  
- Reflections  
- Rotations  
- Dilations  
- Rigid vs. non-rigid transformations  
- Mapping figures on the coordinate plane

**Recommendation:** Consider moving a basic transformations mini-sequence earlier, ideally after Unit 1 or before triangle congruence. If the six-unit structure must remain unchanged, add transformation readiness notes and informal transformation examples in Units 1 and 3.

### Quadrilateral Proofs

Parallelogram and trapezoid proof standards are placed in Unit 6 Lesson 1, `Coordinate Proofs`. That is late for quadrilateral properties.

Instructionally, quadrilaterals often fit after triangles and before circles/measurement. Students need quadrilateral theorem exposure before coordinate proof of quadrilaterals.

**Recommendation:** Move quadrilateral relationships/proofs earlier, ideally after Unit 3 or inside a revised Unit 4 before right-triangle trigonometry. If not moved, add explicit quadrilateral property instruction before Unit 6 coordinate proof.

---

## 3. Overloaded Lessons

### Unit 5 Lesson 7: Circle Applications

This lesson includes:

- Inscribed/circumscribed circle constructions  
- Regular polygons inscribed in circles  
- Arc length  
- Sector area  
- Graphing circle equations  
- Real-world circle applications

That is too much for one lesson. These topics require different skill sets: construction, proportional arc/sector measurement, and coordinate equation graphing.

**Recommendation:** Split conceptually if possible:

- Circle constructions  
- Arc length and sector area  
- Circle equations and graphing  

If only one lesson slot is available, make Lesson 7 an application/review lesson and move circle equations to Unit 6.

### Unit 6 Lesson 3: Area and Surface Area

This lesson combines coordinate area, 2D area, and 3D surface area. For struggling students, this is too broad.

Area and surface area look similar but require different reasoning:

- 2D area: polygons and composite figures  
- Coordinate area/perimeter: distance, coordinates, formulas  
- Surface area: nets, visible faces, 3D structure

**Recommendation:** Separate area/perimeter from surface area if possible. A cleaner sequence would be:

- Area and perimeter of 2D figures  
- Surface area of 3D figures  
- Volume of 3D figures

### Unit 6 Lesson 5: Geometric Modeling

This lesson combines:

- Cross-sections  
- Solids of rotation  
- Conic cross-sections

These are all spatial visualization tasks, but together they can become visually and cognitively demanding.

**Recommendation:** Keep as one lesson only if it is exploratory and visual. Use diagrams, physical models, or dynamic visuals. Avoid heavy symbolic work here.

### Unit 4 Lesson 7: Real-World Applications

This lesson owns both trigonometric ratios and right-triangle applications with the Pythagorean Theorem. That may be too much if trig ratios are new.

**Recommendation:** Introduce trig ratios in Lesson 6 or split special right triangles and trig more clearly. Lesson 7 should focus on applications after students have already practiced sine, cosine, and tangent.

---

## 4. Missing Prerequisite Skills

The map should explicitly call out these prerequisite skills before they are needed:

- Algebraic equation solving before distance, midpoint, circle equations, and right-triangle applications  
- Square roots and simplifying radicals before distance, Pythagorean Theorem, and special right triangles  
- Slope review before coordinate proof and parallel/perpendicular line reasoning  
- Parallel line angle relationships before triangle angle theorems and angle proofs  
- Ratio/proportion fluency before similarity, dilations, trig ratios, scale factors, arc length, and sector area  
- Compass/straightedge tool routines before construction-based lessons  
- Diagram marking conventions before proof and congruent triangle lessons  
- Formula substitution and units before area, surface area, volume, and modeling  
- Calculator use for trig ratios before real-world right-triangle applications  
- Net interpretation before surface area  
- 3D vocabulary before volume, cross-sections, and solids of rotation

---

## 5. Cognitive Load for Struggling Students

Struggling students would likely encounter unnecessary cognitive overload in four places:

1. **Unit 2 proof sequence** if proofs become formal too quickly.
2. **Unit 5 circles sequence** because transformations and circles are combined in the same unit.
3. **Unit 5 Lesson 7** because it combines constructions, measurement, coordinate graphing, and applications.
4. **Unit 6 Lesson 3** because area, coordinate area, and surface area are compressed together.

The course would be more accessible if major multi-skill lessons are split or if some topics become explicit prerequisite supports rather than new mastery-heavy content.

---

## 6. Advanced Student Challenge

Advanced students are not necessarily under-challenged because each unit includes extension ideas. However, the extension ideas should be operationalized into actual optional tasks.

Recommended optional extensions:

- Proof alternatives: paragraph proof, flow proof, two-column proof, and coordinate proof  
- Construction challenges with constraints  
- Dynamic geometry investigations using transformations and loci  
- SAT/ACT mixed geometry problem sets  
- Real-world design tasks involving area, volume, scale, and constraints  
- Error analysis of invalid proofs or incorrect diagrams  
- Coordinate proof challenges involving multiple methods  
- Optimization-style measurement tasks for surface area and volume

These should remain optional and should not change mastery expectations.

---

## 7. Recommended Lesson Moves

### Recommendation 1: Move Basic Transformations Earlier

**Current:** Unit 5 Lessons 1-3  
**Recommended:** Introduce basic transformations after Unit 1 or before triangle congruence.

Reason:

Transformations provide the conceptual foundation for congruence and similarity. Students should see rigid motions before they justify triangle congruence through rigid transformations.

### Recommendation 2: Move Quadrilateral Properties Before Unit 6

**Current:** Unit 6 Lesson 1  
**Recommended:** Place quadrilateral properties/proofs after triangles and before coordinate proof.

Reason:

Students should learn quadrilateral properties synthetically before using coordinate geometry to classify or prove them.

### Recommendation 3: Separate Circle Equations from Early Circle Fundamentals

**Current:** Unit 5 Lesson 4  
**Recommended:** Move circle equations closer to Unit 6 coordinate geometry or create a dedicated circle-coordinate lesson.

Reason:

Circle equations rely on coordinate distance reasoning and algebraic interpretation. They are not the same cognitive task as circle vocabulary and circle similarity.

### Recommendation 4: Reduce Unit 5 Lesson 7 Load

**Current:** Circle constructions, arc length, sector area, and graphing circle equations in one lesson.  
**Recommended:** Make this lesson focused on either circle constructions and measurement applications, or move circle graphing to Unit 6.

Reason:

Construction, measurement, and coordinate graphing are different modalities and should not be introduced together as one mastery target.

### Recommendation 5: Split Area and Surface Area

**Current:** Unit 6 Lesson 3  
**Recommended:** Separate 2D area/perimeter from 3D surface area if future revision allows.

Reason:

2D and 3D measurement require different visualization and formula-selection skills.

---

## Suggested Revised Flow

If a future v2 revision is approved, a stronger instructional flow would be:

1. Foundations of Geometry  
2. Reasoning, Proof, and Basic Transformations  
3. Triangles, Congruence, and Quadrilaterals  
4. Similarity, Right Triangles, and Trigonometry  
5. Circles and Circle Measurement  
6. Coordinate Geometry, 3D Measurement, and Modeling

This preserves the broad intent while reducing late-unit overload and aligning transformations more naturally with congruence and similarity.

---

## Final Determination

**Pass with Revisions**

The current mapping is instructionally usable, but revisions are recommended before it becomes the final academy-wide Geometry blueprint.

The most important fixes are:

- Move or preview transformations earlier.  
- Reduce Unit 5 circle overload.  
- Move quadrilateral proof/property development earlier than Unit 6.  
- Separate area/surface area/volume more cleanly.  
- Strengthen prerequisite callouts before proof, similarity, trigonometry, circles, and 3D measurement.
