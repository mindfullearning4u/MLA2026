Lesson: Coordinate Relationships and Circle Equations

Notebook Title

Coordinate Relationships and Circle Equations

Purpose: Use coordinate geometry to solve problems involving lines, circles, triangles, quadrilaterals, and circle equations.

Vocabulary

  • Identify the mathematical object: the coordinate grid, image/preimage, graph, or coordinate relationship.
  • Use precise Geometry vocabulary from the lesson title and standards: MLA.GEO.CGM.9, MLA.GEO.CIR.9, MLA.GEO.CIR.10.
  • Record givens, markings, measurements, and conclusions separately.

Teaching Sequence

Step 1: Read the lesson task and underline the givens. A given may be a marked segment, angle measure, coordinate, radius, side length, scale factor, or construction step.

Why: Geometry conclusions must come from evidence, not from how a figure appears.

Step 2: Label the coordinate grid, image/preimage, graph, or coordinate relationship. Add names for points, sides, angles, arcs, coordinates, or dimensions before you calculate or justify anything.

Step 3: Choose the definition, theorem, construction property, or formula that matches the labeled evidence.

Step 4: Write one sentence that connects the evidence to the conclusion for Coordinate Relationships and Circle Equations.

Teacher-Guided Thinking for Notebook Part 1

Read this as your teacher modeling the first layer of the lesson. In Coordinate Relationships and Circle Equations, do not begin by guessing from the diagram or choosing a formula too quickly. First name what the problem is asking, identify the givens, markings, measurements, coordinates, or relationships you can see, and connect the task to MLA.GEO.CGM.9, MLA.GEO.CIR.9, MLA.GEO.CIR.10. This keeps your work aligned to the approved unit and lesson mapping.

Step 1: Copy the vocabulary and underline words that tell you what object, theorem, construction, transformation, measurement, proof idea, or model is being used. Step 2: Write what each point, segment, angle, arc, side length, coordinate, or table entry represents before calculating. Self-check: If a classmate asked why this method works, you should be able to answer with diagram evidence and a sentence, not only a number. If your notes do not explain the why, add one more line before moving to Part 2.
