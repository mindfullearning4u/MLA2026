P04 Concept Development / Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Copy a Segment

Problem: Construct a segment congruent to segment PQ starting at point R.

Step 1: Draw a ray with endpoint R.

Step 2: Open the compass from P to Q.

Step 3: Without changing the compass opening, place the compass point on R and mark point S on the ray.

RS = PQ

Answer: Segment RS is a copy of segment PQ.

Reasoning: The fixed compass opening transfers the length of PQ to the new segment.

Example 2: Copy an Angle

Problem: Construct an angle congruent to ∠JKL with vertex M.

Step 1: Draw a ray with endpoint M.

Step 2: Draw an arc centered at K that crosses both sides of ∠JKL.

Step 3: Draw the same arc from M on the new ray.

Step 4: Transfer the distance between the original arc intersections onto the new arc.

m∠NMP = m∠JKL

Answer: Angle NMP is a copy of angle JKL.

Reasoning: The arcs transfer the original angle opening to the new vertex.

Example 3: Explain a Correction

Problem: A student copies a segment by estimating its length with their eyes.

Incorrect:

Draw the copied segment so it looks about the same length.

Why it is wrong: Estimating by sight does not guarantee a congruent segment.

Correct:

Use the compass opening from the original segment to mark the copied segment.

Reasoning: A compass transfers the exact distance from the original segment.

Common Mistake

A common construction mistake is changing the compass opening before transferring a distance.

Incorrect:

Open the compass to AB, then adjust it before marking the copy.

Why it is incomplete: Adjusting the compass changes the distance being copied.

Correct:

Open the compass to AB and keep that opening fixed while marking the copy.
