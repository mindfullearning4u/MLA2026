Notebook Title

Lesson 4: Segment and Angle Constructions

Why Constructions Matter

A construction is a precise geometric drawing made using tools such as a compass and straightedge.

Construction is not sketching. A sketch may look close, but a construction uses tool settings and arcs to preserve length or angle measure.

In this lesson, the goal is to copy a segment or angle accurately using geometric tools.

Construction Tools

  • Compass: A tool used to transfer distances and draw arcs or circles.
  • Straightedge: A tool used to draw straight lines, rays, or segments.
  • Compass opening: The distance between the compass point and pencil point.
  • Arc: Part of a circle drawn with a compass.
  • Constructed copy: A new segment or angle made to match the original.

Copying a Segment

Goal: Construct a segment congruent to segment AB.

  1. Draw a ray with endpoint C.
  2. Place the compass point on A and open the compass to B.
  3. Without changing the compass opening, place the compass point on C.
  4. Mark point D on the ray where the compass arc intersects the ray.

CD = AB

Reasoning: The compass opening transfers the length of segment AB to the new ray.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not change the compass opening while transferring a segment length.

Incorrect:

Reset the compass opening before marking point D.

Correct:

Keep the compass opening fixed from AB when marking point D.

Why: Changing the compass opening changes the transferred length.
