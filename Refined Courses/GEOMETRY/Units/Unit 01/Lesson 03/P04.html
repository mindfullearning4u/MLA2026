P04 Concept Development / Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Build a Supported Argument

Problem: A diagram shows angle X and angle Y with matching arc marks. What conclusion is supported?

Step 1: State the claim. Angle X is congruent to angle Y.

Step 2: Name the evidence. The angles have matching arc marks.

m∠X = m∠Y

Answer: Angle X is congruent to angle Y.

Reasoning: Matching arc marks are evidence that angles have equal measure.

Example 2: Identify a Flawed Argument

Problem: A student says two lines are parallel because they look like they never meet.

Step 1: Identify the claim. The lines are parallel.

Step 2: Identify the evidence. The student only used appearance.

Answer: The argument is flawed.

Reasoning: A parallel conclusion needs markings, a given statement, or another valid reason. Appearance alone is not enough.

Example 3: Explain a Correction

Problem: A student says a conjecture is always true because it worked once.

Incorrect:

The conjecture is true because one example worked.

Why it is wrong: One example can suggest a conjecture, but it does not provide enough support for a general conclusion.

Correct:

One example can help form a conjecture, but the claim needs more evidence or reasoning before it is accepted.

Reasoning: A valid mathematical argument needs enough relevant evidence to support the claim.

Common Mistake

A common readiness mistake is giving a conclusion without evidence.

Problem: A student claims two segments are congruent.

Incorrect:

The segments are congruent.

Why it is incomplete: The claim may be true, but no evidence is given.

Correct:

The segments are congruent because they have matching tick marks.
