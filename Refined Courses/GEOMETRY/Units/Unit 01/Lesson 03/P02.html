Notebook Title

Lesson 3: Reasoning, Conjectures, and Mathematical Arguments

Why Reasoning Comes Before Proof

Geometry proof depends on smaller reasoning habits. Before writing formal proofs, you need to make clear claims and support them with evidence.

A claim without evidence is not a mathematical argument. A strong argument explains why a conclusion follows.

In this lesson, the goal is to decide whether reasoning is supported, incomplete, or flawed.

Reasoning Vocabulary

Mathematical reasoning uses language carefully.

  • Claim: A statement that someone says is true.
  • Conjecture: A claim based on observations or patterns that still needs support.
  • Evidence: Information that supports a claim or conclusion.
  • Argument: A set of statements that explains why a conclusion is supported.
  • Flawed reasoning: Reasoning that is incomplete, unsupported, or based on an incorrect idea.

Parts of a Valid Mathematical Argument

  • Clear claim: State what you are trying to show.
  • Relevant evidence: Use definitions, labels, markings, given information, or a checked pattern.
  • Reasoning: Explain how the evidence supports the claim.
  • Conclusion: State the result in precise mathematical language.

Example 1: Claim and Evidence

Claim: Segment AB is congruent to segment CD.

Evidence: Segment AB and segment CD have matching tick marks.

Reasoning: Matching tick marks show congruent segments.

Conclusion: AB = CD.

Example 2: A Conjecture

Observation: Three examples show that a segment with matching tick marks is congruent to another segment with the same marking.

Conjecture: Matching tick marks show congruent segments.

Explanation: A conjecture is based on observations, but it should still be supported with definitions, markings, or given information before being used as a conclusion.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not treat a guess as an argument.

Claim: Two angles are congruent.

Incorrect:

The angles are congruent because they look the same.

Correct:

The angles are congruent because they have matching arc marks.

Why: Matching arc marks are evidence. Appearance alone is not enough support.
