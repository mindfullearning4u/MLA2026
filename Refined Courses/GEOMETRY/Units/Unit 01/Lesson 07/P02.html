Notebook Focus: Valid Arguments

A valid argument uses accepted facts, definitions, or relationships to support a conclusion.

A conclusion is not valid just because it looks true in a diagram.

  • Evidence: given information, definitions, angle relationships, or proven facts.
  • Conclusion: the statement supported by the evidence.
  • Reason: the explanation connecting evidence to the conclusion.

Line and Angle Evidence

Line and angle relationships often provide proof evidence.

  • Vertical angles are congruent.
  • Angles that form a linear pair are supplementary.
  • Supplementary angles have measures that add to 180Â°.

Observation vs Conclusion

  • Observation: Two angles appear to have the same measure.
  • Valid conclusion: The angles are congruent if the diagram gives matching marks or a theorem applies.
  • Not enough: The angles look equal.

Common Misconception

Do not use appearance as the only reason for a conclusion.

Incorrect:

The angles are congruent because they look equal.

Correct:

The angles are congruent because they are vertical angles.
