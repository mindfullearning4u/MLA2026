P04 Concept Development / Worked Examples

Teacher Guided

Example 1: Valid Angle Argument

Given: ∠1 and ∠2 are vertical angles.

Conclusion: ∠1 ≅ ∠2.

Reason: Vertical angles are congruent.

Example 2: Linear Pair Reasoning

Given: ∠A and ∠B form a linear pair.

Statement: m∠A + m∠B = 180°.

Reason: Angles that form a linear pair are supplementary.

Example 3: Counterexample

Claim: All quadrilaterals are rectangles.

Counterexample: A rhombus with no right angles is a quadrilateral, but it is not a rectangle.

Conclusion: The claim is false.

Example 4: Explain a Correction

A student concludes two lines are parallel because they look parallel.

Incorrect:

The lines are parallel because they look parallel.

Why it is incomplete: Appearance is not proof. The argument needs a valid reason.

Correct:

The lines are parallel because a pair of corresponding angles is congruent.

Error Check

A common proof mistake is giving a statement without a reason.

Incorrect:

∠1 ≅ ∠2. Reason: obvious.

Correct:

∠1 ≅ ∠2. Reason: vertical angles are congruent.
