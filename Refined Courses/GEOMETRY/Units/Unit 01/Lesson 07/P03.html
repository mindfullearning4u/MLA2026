Notebook Focus: Counterexamples

A counterexample is one example that shows a statement is false.

Counterexamples are used to disprove universal claims.

Claim: All rectangles are squares.

Counterexample: A rectangle with side lengths 4 and 7 is not a square.

Proof by Contradiction

A proof by contradiction starts by assuming the opposite of what you want to prove.

  1. Assume the opposite of the desired conclusion.
  2. Use definitions, relationships, or accepted facts to reason from that assumption.
  3. Reach an impossible or contradictory result.
  4. Conclude the original statement must be true.

Proof Structure

  • Given: information provided by the problem.
  • Prove: the conclusion that must be justified.
  • Statements: logical steps in the argument.
  • Reasons: definitions, properties, or theorems that support each step.

Common Misconception

One supporting example does not prove a universal claim.

Incorrect:

The claim is true because one example worked.

Correct:

A universal claim needs a valid argument, and one counterexample can disprove it.
