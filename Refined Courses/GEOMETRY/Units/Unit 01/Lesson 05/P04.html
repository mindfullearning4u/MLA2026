P04 Concept Development / Worked Examples

Teacher Guided

Example 1: Segment Bisector

Construct a bisector of segment AB.

Step 1: Open the compass to more than half the length of AB.

Step 2: Draw equal arcs from A and B above and below the segment.

Step 3: Draw a line through the two arc intersections.

Conclusion: The line crosses AB at midpoint M, so AM = MB.

Example 2: Perpendicular Bisector

Use the same construction from Example 1 and identify the extra relationship.

The constructed line passes through midpoint M and forms right angles with segment AB.

AM = MB and the bisector is perpendicular to AB.

Reasoning: A perpendicular bisector must both bisect the segment and meet it at a right angle.

Example 3: Angle Bisector

Construct a bisector of ∠ABC.

Step 1: Draw an arc centered at B that intersects both sides of the angle.

Step 2: From the two arc intersections, draw equal arcs inside the angle.

Step 3: Draw ray BD through the interior arc intersection.

m∠ABD = m∠DBC

Example 4: Explain a Correction

A student says a ray is an angle bisector because it looks centered.

Incorrect:

The ray bisects the angle because it looks halfway between the sides.

Why it is incomplete: Appearance does not prove the two angle measures are equal.

Correct:

The ray bisects the angle because equal compass arcs locate a point that creates two congruent angles.

Error Check

A common construction mistake is changing the compass opening before drawing matching arcs.

Incorrect:

Draw arcs from A and B using different compass openings.

Why it is incomplete: Different openings do not preserve equal distance from the endpoints.

Correct:

Use the same compass opening from both endpoints so matching arcs support the bisector.
