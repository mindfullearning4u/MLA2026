P04 Worked Example

Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Example 1: Use Given Information

Problem: A diagram shows points D, E, and F on one straight line, with E between D and F. What can be concluded?

Step 1: Identify the information shown. The points are on one straight line.

Step 2: Use precise vocabulary. Points on the same line are collinear.

D, E, and F are collinear.

Answer: D, E, and F are collinear, and E is between D and F.

Reasoning: The conclusion comes directly from the diagram description.

Example 2: Explain What a Marking Means

Problem: A diagram shows angle JKL with a square corner mark at K.

Step 1: Interpret the marking.

m∠JKL = 90°

Step 2: Name the angle type.

Answer: Angle JKL is a right angle.

Reasoning: A square corner mark is evidence that the angle measures 90°.

Example 3: Explain a Correction

Problem: A drawing shows two angles that look the same size, but there are no angle measures or matching arc marks.

Incorrect:

The angles are congruent because they look equal.

Why it is wrong: The conclusion is based on appearance, not evidence.

Correct:

The angles cannot be concluded congruent unless measures, matching arc marks, or given information support it.

Reasoning: Geometry claims must be supported by diagram markings, labels, given statements, or proven relationships.

Common Mistake

A common readiness mistake is writing a conclusion without explaining what evidence supports it.

Problem: A diagram shows segment AB and segment CD with matching tick marks.

Incorrect:

AB = CD

Why it is incomplete: The statement may be correct, but the response does not explain the evidence.

Correct:

AB = CD because matching tick marks show congruent segments.
