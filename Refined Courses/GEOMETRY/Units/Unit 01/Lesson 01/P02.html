Notebook Title

Lesson 1: Geometry Readiness Diagnostic and Mathematical Thinking

Why Geometry Readiness Comes First

Geometry depends on seeing structure, reading diagrams carefully, and explaining why a conclusion is supported.

A readiness diagnostic is not a punishment. It is a tool that helps you and your Teacher of Record understand what support you need before formal proof, construction, and measurement work begins.

In this lesson, the goal is to observe carefully, label information clearly, and notice the difference between evidence and assumption.

Geometry Thinking Habits

Strong Geometry students do more than name shapes. They use habits that make diagram reasoning visible.

  • Observe carefully: Identify labels, markings, and relationships shown in the diagram.
  • Use evidence: Support conclusions with given information or visible markings.
  • Avoid assumptions: Do not conclude lengths, angle measures, parallel lines, or congruence unless the diagram or directions support it.
  • Use precise language: Name points, segments, rays, angles, and lines accurately.
  • Explain briefly: Write enough reasoning so another person can follow your conclusion.

Vocabulary

  • Diagram: A drawing that represents geometric objects and relationships.
  • Given information: Information stated in words or shown with labels or markings.
  • Conclusion: A statement that follows from evidence.
  • Assumption: A statement treated as true without enough evidence.
  • Evidence: Information that supports a conclusion.

Example 1: Make Observations from a Diagram

Diagram Description: Points A, B, and C are shown on one straight path. Point B is between A and C.

Observation: A, B, and C are collinear points.

Evidence: The points are shown on the same straight path, and B is located between A and C.

Conclusion: Segment AB and segment BC are parts of segment AC.

Example 2: Support a Conclusion with Evidence

Diagram Description: Segment MN and segment PQ each have one matching tick mark.

Conclusion: Segment MN is congruent to segment PQ.

MN = PQ

Evidence: Matching tick marks show congruent segments.

Explanation: Because the segments have matching congruence marks, the diagram supports the conclusion that the two segment lengths are equal.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Do not assume that two segments are equal just because they look equal in a drawing.

Diagram Description: Segment RS and segment TU look about the same length, but there are no length labels or matching tick marks.

Incorrect:

RS = TU because they look the same.

Correct:

RS and TU cannot be concluded congruent without labels, measurements, or matching tick marks.

Why: Geometry conclusions must be supported by evidence, not appearance alone.
