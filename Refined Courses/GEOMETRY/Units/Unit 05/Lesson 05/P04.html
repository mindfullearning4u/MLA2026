P04 Concept Development / Worked Examples

Key Concept

Circle constructions depend on locating the correct center or tangent point. Angle bisectors locate an incenter, perpendicular bisectors locate a circumcenter, and a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.

Worked Example 1

Example: To construct a circumscribed circle of a triangle, construct perpendicular bisectors of two sides. Their intersection is the circumcenter, and a circle centered there through one vertex passes through all three vertices.

Worked Example 2

Example: The incenter is found by constructing perpendicular bisectors of the sides.

Error Check

Incorrect:

The incenter is found by constructing angle bisectors of the triangle.

Correct:

The guided practice checks triangle circle centers, inscribed regular polygons, and tangent-line construction reasoning.
