# MLA Geometry Unit Mapping v1

**Reference Files:** `mla_geometry_standards_crosswalk.xlsx`; `mla_geometry_standards_audit.md`  
**Purpose:** This document establishes when and in what order Geometry concepts should be taught. The standards crosswalk establishes what must be taught; this unit mapping establishes the instructional blueprint.

---

## Course Sequencing Principles

This course is sequenced by prerequisite skill progression, conceptual development, student readiness, cognitive scaffolding, best instructional practices, support for struggling learners, and college/career readiness.

The course does not follow Florida B.E.S.T., Common Core, SAT, or ACT benchmark order. The instructional progression is:

Geometry Foundations → Reasoning and Proof → Triangles and Congruence → Similarity and Right Triangles → Transformations and Circles → Coordinate Geometry → Measurement → Modeling

## Unit 1: Foundations of Geometry

**Unit Purpose:** Develop geometric vocabulary, spatial reasoning, measurement foundations, coordinate geometry readiness, and construction skills.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.CGM.7, MLA.GEO.FND.1, MLA.GEO.FND.2

**Unit Justification:** Geometry begins with tools, vocabulary, coordinate readiness, measurement language, and construction fluency. This is Geometry readiness, not Algebra remediation.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Middle grades geometry vocabulary, ruler/protractor familiarity, basic coordinate plane awareness, arithmetic with whole numbers and fractions.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports proof, transformations, triangles, similarity, circles, coordinate geometry, and measurement modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Construction challenges; coordinate-plane scavenger hunts; SAT-style distance/midpoint questions; angle classification error analysis; open-ended diagram labeling tasks.

## Unit 2: Reasoning and Proof

**Unit Purpose:** Develop logical reasoning, geometric justification, and proof-writing skills.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.RP.4, MLA.GEO.RP.6, MLA.GEO.RP.5, MLA.GEO.RP.1

**Unit Justification:** Reasoning and proof are placed early so students can use logic throughout the course instead of treating proof as an isolated topic.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Geometric vocabulary, angle relationships, segment relationships, diagram labeling, and construction precision.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports triangle proofs, transformation justifications, circle proofs, coordinate proofs, and modeling explanations.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Proof extension tasks; counterexample challenges; SAT-style logic with diagrams; ACT-style angle relationship problems; student-created valid and invalid arguments.

## Unit 3: Triangles and Congruence

**Unit Purpose:** Develop understanding of triangle properties, congruence relationships, and geometric reasoning.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.TRI.2, MLA.GEO.TRI.1, MLA.GEO.TRI.3

**Unit Justification:** Triangles come after proof because they provide the first major structure where students use proof repeatedly with concrete figures.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Angle relationships, segment relationships, proof structure, constructions, and diagram marking.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports similarity, right-triangle trigonometry, transformations, coordinate proof, and geometric modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Triangle proof extensions; congruence error analysis; construction-based congruence challenges; SAT/ACT triangle applications; open-ended design constraints using congruent triangles.

## Unit 4: Similarity and Right Triangles

**Unit Purpose:** Develop proportional reasoning and right-triangle relationships.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.SIM.3, MLA.GEO.SIM.1, MLA.GEO.TRI.4, MLA.GEO.TRI.5

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Triangle classification, triangle angle relationships, congruence, ratios, proportions, square roots, and basic equation solving.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports transformations, circles, scale factor measurement, coordinate geometry, and real-world modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Similarity proof challenges; indirect measurement investigations; right-triangle modeling; SAT/ACT trigonometry tasks; Desmos or dynamic geometry explorations of scale factor.

## Unit 6: Coordinate Geometry, Measurement, and Modeling

**Unit Purpose:** Apply geometric concepts using coordinate methods, measurement formulas, and real-world modeling.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.GEO.CGM.8, MLA.GEO.RP.2, MLA.GEO.RP.3, MLA.GEO.CGM.9, MLA.GEO.CGM.10, MLA.GEO.CGM.13, MLA.GEO.CGM.15, MLA.GEO.CGM.14, MLA.GEO.CGM.11, MLA.GEO.CGM.12, MLA.GEO.CGM.16, MLA.GEO.SIM.4

**Unit Justification:** The final unit uses the full Geometry toolkit: proof, triangles, similarity, transformations, circles, coordinate methods, measurement, and modeling.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Coordinate plane, distance/midpoint, slope, proof, transformations, circles, area formulas, volume formulas, surface area formulas, and proportional reasoning.

**Future Learning Connections:** Prepares students for Algebra II, Precalculus, statistics/technical fields, SAT/ACT quantitative reasoning, and real-world geometric modeling.

**Extension Challenge Ideas:** Coordinate proof challenges; geometric modeling investigations; cross-section visualization tasks; surface area/volume design optimization; SAT/ACT multi-step geometry problems; open-ended real-world measurement projects.

## Instructional Sequencing Validation

This sequence is educationally sound because it begins with Geometry readiness: vocabulary, diagrams, coordinate plane review, distance, midpoint, measurement tools, and constructions. These are the access skills students need before formal proof, triangle congruence, similarity, transformations, circles, and coordinate modeling.

Reasoning and proof appear early because proof is a coursewide habit, not a late-course topic. Triangles and congruence follow proof because triangles provide the first major structure for repeated theorem use. Similarity and right triangles follow congruence because proportional reasoning extends same-size relationships into same-shape relationships and prepares students for trigonometry.

Transformations are placed after congruence and similarity so students can reinterpret those ideas as mappings. Circles follow transformations because circle similarity, circle constructions, arcs, chords, tangents, secants, and circle equations draw on prior proof, proportional reasoning, constructions, and coordinate readiness. The final unit applies the full course toolkit through coordinate geometry, measurement, spatial visualization, scale effects, and modeling.

The course supports mastery-based learning because each lesson has named prerequisites, a reason for placement, future-learning connections, and consistent mastery evidence: Notebook Evidence, Checkpoint Submission, and Lesson Quiz. Lesson 8 in every unit is reserved for "Putting It All Together" to consolidate learning before advancement.
