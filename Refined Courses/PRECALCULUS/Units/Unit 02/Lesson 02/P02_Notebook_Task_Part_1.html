Notebook Title

Polynomial Functions of Degree Three or Higher

Begin a new notebook page with this title. Leave space under the vocabulary because you will use these words while solving and explaining problems.

Vocabulary

  • Degree: the greatest power of x in a polynomial.
  • Leading coefficient: the coefficient of the term with the greatest power.
  • End behavior: how the graph behaves far left and far right.
  • Turning point: a place where a graph changes from increasing to decreasing or decreasing to increasing.
  • Multiplicity: the number of times a factor repeats.
  • Relative maximum: a high point compared with nearby points.
  • Relative minimum: a low point compared with nearby points.

Lesson Foundation

This lesson focuses on higher-degree polynomial graphs, zeros, turning points, end behavior, and key features.

Read each polynomial representation carefully. Before calculating, identify whether the question is asking about a zero, factor, theorem, graph feature, or end behavior.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Identify the form of the polynomial or representation.
  2. Name the theorem, feature, or graph behavior that applies.
  3. Carry out the algebraic or graphical reasoning one step at a time.
  4. Write a sentence explaining what your result means.
