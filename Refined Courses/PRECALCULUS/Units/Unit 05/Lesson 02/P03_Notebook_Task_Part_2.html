Deepening the Concept

The main idea in this lesson is calculating distance and midpoint on the complex coordinate plane and solving problems involving complex numbers.

A complete solution must explain what each part of the complex number means. When the problem is geometric, the complex number should be treated as a coordinate point.

Notebook Task

Create a three-column organizer labeled Algebraic Form, Complex Plane Meaning, and Reasoning Step.

Use the organizer to connect each expression or answer to its location, movement, or relationship on the complex plane.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use only the real parts when finding distance or midpoint between complex numbers.

Correct: Treat a + bi as the point (a, b), then use both coordinates in the distance or midpoint formula.

A complex number has a horizontal real coordinate and a vertical imaginary coordinate. Distance and midpoint require both coordinates.
