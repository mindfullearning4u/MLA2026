Study each example carefully. Every step is shown because the reasoning matters as much as the final answer.

Worked Example 1

  1. Identify the real and imaginary parts.
  2. Decide whether the problem is asking for an operation, point, distance, midpoint, or interpretation.
  3. Use the matching algebraic or coordinate-plane method.
  4. State the result and explain what it means.

Worked Example 2

  1. Rewrite the complex number or pair of numbers as needed.

  3. Show each calculation step in order.
  4. Check that the result fits the geometric representation.

Worked Example 3

Problem: Multiply i(4 - 3i) and identify how the result changes the real and imaginary parts.

  1. Translate the situation into complex-number or coordinate-plane language.
  2. Select the correct operation or formula.
  3. Carry out the calculation one step at a time.
  4. Explain the final answer in words.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Add complex numbers by adding every visible number together as one total.

Correct: Add real parts together and add imaginary parts together, then represent the result on the complex plane.

Complex numbers have two coordinated parts. The real part controls horizontal movement, and the imaginary part controls vertical movement.
