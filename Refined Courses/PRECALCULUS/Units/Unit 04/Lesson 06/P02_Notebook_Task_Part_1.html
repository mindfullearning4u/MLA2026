Notebook Title

One-Variable Trigonometric Ratio Problems

Begin a new notebook page with this title. Leave space under the vocabulary because you will use these words while solving and explaining problems.

Vocabulary

  • Trigonometric ratio: a relationship such as sine, cosine, tangent, cosecant, secant, or cotangent.
  • One-variable problem: a problem where one unknown value must be solved.
  • Inverse trigonometric function: a function used to find an angle from a known ratio.
  • Reference angle: the acute angle connected to a trigonometric ratio.
  • Constraint: a restriction from the context, such as a positive length or a realistic angle.
  • Extraneous solution: a value that appears from algebra but does not fit the original problem or context.

Lesson Foundation

This lesson focuses on solving mathematical and real-world problems involving one-variable trigonometric ratios.

Start by identifying the structure of the expression or equation. Then choose the identity, ratio, or solving method that matches that structure.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Identify the expression, ratio, equation, or context.
  2. Write the identity, ratio, or formula before substituting.
  3. Simplify or solve one step at a time.
  4. Check restrictions, signs, and context before giving the final answer.
  5. Write a sentence explaining why the result is reasonable.
