Deepening the Concept

The main idea in this lesson is proving and applying Pythagorean identities to calculate trigonometric ratios and solve problems.

Identity work is not random algebra. Every replacement must come from a known identity, formula, reciprocal relationship, or ratio relationship.

Notebook Task

Create a three-column organizer labeled Expression, Identity or Formula Used, and Reason.

Use the organizer to explain each transformation instead of only writing calculations.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: sin2(x) + cos2(x) = 2 because sine and cosine each come from the unit circle.

Correct: sin2(x) + cos2(x) = 1 because the unit circle has radius 1.

The Pythagorean identity comes from x2 + y2 = 1 on the unit circle. Since cosine is the x-coordinate and sine is the y-coordinate, cos2(x) + sin2(x) = 1.
