# MLA Precalculus Unit Mapping v1

## Purpose

This document establishes the official MLA Precalculus instructional blueprint. The approved standards crosswalk defines what must be taught; this mapping defines when concepts are taught and in what instructional order.

Instruction is sequenced by mathematical dependency, conceptual development, student readiness, College Algebra readiness, Trigonometry readiness, Calculus readiness, STEM readiness, and best instructional practice. Standards are not sequenced according to Florida B.E.S.T., Common Core, SAT, or ACT document order.

## Proposed Structure Analysis

The prompt-proposed six-unit structure is strong in broad intent, but it is not the strongest structure for the approved v2 standards architecture.

The main issue is Unit 6, "Limits Foundations and Calculus Readiness." The approved crosswalk documents `MLA.PC.LIM` as a reserved calculus-readiness domain, not a Florida benchmark-bearing domain. Creating a full primary unit around limits would leave that unit with no direct Florida benchmark ownership and could imply a full Calculus curriculum, which the audit explicitly cautioned against.

The revised structure below preserves calculus readiness while avoiding orphaned standards. It also moves conics, polar, parametric, complex, and vector relationships into a stronger dependency sequence:

1. Advanced functions first, because all later Precalculus work depends on function behavior, composition, inverses, model interpretation, and multiple representations.
2. Trigonometric foundations before trigonometric functions, because radian measure, unit circle reasoning, identities, and triangle trigonometry are prerequisites for periodic modeling.
3. Trigonometric functions and applications before polar/parametric work, because polar and parametric relationships require trigonometric and coordinate-plane fluency.
4. Conics and complex-plane relationships before vectors/polar/parametric modeling, because students need coordinate structures and complex-plane representation before advanced STEM representations.
5. Vectors, polar coordinates, and parametric relationships together, because they emphasize multiple representations, motion, direction, magnitude, and STEM modeling.
6. Sequences, series, and calculus readiness last, because sequences/series and difference quotients support transition to advanced mathematics while informal limits remain embedded readiness support.

## Approved Revised Unit Structure

1. Unit 1: Advanced Function Analysis and Modeling
2. Unit 2: Trigonometric Foundations and Identities
3. Unit 3: Trigonometric Functions and Applications
4. Unit 4: Conics and Complex Plane Relationships
5. Unit 5: Vectors, Polar Coordinates, and Parametric Relationships
6. Unit 6: Sequences, Series, and Calculus Readiness

---

## Course Structure

- 6 units
- 8 lessons per unit
- Lesson 8 in every unit: Putting It All Together
- Every approved MLA Precalculus standard is mapped once as a primary instructional target.
- Support-only review, synthesis, or calculus-readiness lessons do not create duplicate primary benchmark ownership.

## Unit 1: Advanced Function Analysis and Modeling

**Unit Purpose:** Develop deep understanding of advanced function behavior, multiple representations, transformations, composition, inverses, polynomial/radical/rational/exponential/logarithmic models, systems, and piecewise functions.

**Standards Covered:** MLA.PC.FA.1, MLA.PC.FA.2, MLA.PC.FA.3, MLA.PC.FA.4, MLA.PC.FA.5, MLA.PC.FA.7, MLA.PC.FA.8, MLA.PC.FA.9, MLA.PC.FA.10, MLA.PC.FA.11, MLA.PC.FA.12, MLA.PC.FA.13, MLA.PC.EX.1, MLA.PC.EX.2, MLA.PC.MOD.1, MLA.PC.MOD.2

**Unit Justification:** Precalculus begins with advanced function analysis because every later topic depends on reading, comparing, transforming, combining, and interpreting functions. This is a readiness bridge, not an Algebra II remediation unit.

**Prerequisite Knowledge:** Algebra I and Algebra II function notation, graph interpretation, domain and range, polynomial/radical/rational/exponential/logarithmic functions, equation solving, transformations, and technology-supported graphing.

**Future Learning Connections:** Supports trigonometric functions, polar/parametric relationships, conics, vectors, sequences, rates of change, and Calculus readiness.

**Advanced Learner Extensions:** SAT-style function comparison, ACT function interpretation, transformation investigations, composition/inverse justification, model-selection challenges, technology graph investigations, and open-ended modeling tasks.

# Appendix 3: Instructional Sequencing Validation

The final sequence is educationally stronger than the original proposed structure because it avoids creating a full standards-bearing limits unit where the approved crosswalk has no primary limits benchmark ownership. It preserves calculus readiness by embedding informal limit concepts in the final unit through difference quotients, rates of change, end behavior, asymptotic behavior, and function behavior near points.

The sequence begins with advanced function analysis because Precalculus is fundamentally a function-centered bridge course. Students then develop trigonometric foundations before trigonometric functions and applications. Conics and complex numbers follow trigonometry because complex polar form depends on trigonometric reasoning. Vectors, polar coordinates, and parametric equations follow conics and complex-plane relationships because they require advanced coordinate and representation fluency. Sequences, series, and calculus-readiness topics conclude the course because they bridge Precalculus to College Algebra, Calculus, and STEM pathways.

This structure follows mathematical dependency rather than standards-document order and supports mastery-based progression through repeated evidence: Notebook Evidence, Checkpoint Submission, Lesson Quiz, and Unit Assessment.
