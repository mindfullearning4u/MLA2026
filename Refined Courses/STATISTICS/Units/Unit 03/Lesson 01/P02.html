Notebook Title: Correlation and Causation

Set up your notes so you can clearly separate what the data show from what the data do not prove.

Vocabulary

  • Bivariate data: data involving two variables measured on the same individuals or cases.
  • Association: a relationship or pattern between two variables.
  • Correlation: a numerical or visual relationship between two numerical variables.
  • Causation: a relationship where a change in one variable directly produces a change in another variable.
  • Confounding variable: another variable that may explain an observed relationship.

What Association Means

An association means two variables appear to be related. For example, if students who study more hours also tend to earn higher quiz scores, study time and quiz score have a positive association.

  1. Identify the two variables.
  2. Ask whether the variables appear to move together.
  3. Describe the direction: positive, negative, or no clear association.
  4. State the conclusion in context.

Careful wording: Say "is associated with" unless the study design supports a cause-and-effect conclusion.

Notebook Task

Copy and complete this frame:

When I see two variables related in data, I can conclude: The variables are associated if a pattern appears. I cannot conclude causation unless the evidence supports cause-and-effect reasoning.

Then write one example from school, health, sports, or work where two variables might be associated.
