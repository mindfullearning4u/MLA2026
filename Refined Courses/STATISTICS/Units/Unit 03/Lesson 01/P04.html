📘 STATISTICS | Unit 03 | Lesson 01

P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Read each model in order. Notice how the example identifies the statistical situation, selects the correct method, completes the work, and explains the conclusion in context.

Study each example. The goal is to make conclusions that match the evidence.

Worked Example 1

Situation: As weekly practice time increases, free-throw percentage tends to increase.

  1. Variables: practice time and free-throw percentage.
  2. Pattern: both tend to increase together.
  3. Conclusion: practice time is positively associated with free-throw percentage.
  4. Causation? Not proven by this statement alone.

Worked Example 2

Situation: A store notices that higher outdoor temperature is connected with higher ice cream sales.

  1. Variables: outdoor temperature and ice cream sales.
  2. Pattern: higher temperature is linked with higher sales.
  3. Conclusion: temperature and ice cream sales are positively associated.
  4. A causal explanation may be reasonable, but the data statement alone still needs careful wording.

Worked Example 3

Situation: Students with longer commute times tend to have lower attendance rates.

  1. Variables: commute time and attendance rate.
  2. Pattern: as commute time increases, attendance tends to decrease.
  3. Conclusion: commute time is negatively associated with attendance rate.
  4. Causation is not proven because other factors may affect attendance.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Commute time causes low attendance for every student.
Correct: Longer commute time is associated with lower attendance in the data. This conclusion is accurate because it describes the pattern without claiming that commute time is the only cause.

Teachable Explanation: A statistic is only useful when it answers the question being asked and is interpreted in the context of the data.
