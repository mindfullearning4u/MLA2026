Association in a Two-Way Table

An association may exist when the distribution of one variable changes across categories of the other variable. For example, if a much larger share of online students prefer video notes compared with in-person students, learning format and note preference may be associated.

Counts are a starting point. In later lessons, relative frequencies make comparisons clearer when group sizes are different.

Notebook Task

A table shows 30 of 40 online students prefer video notes, while 15 of 40 in-person students prefer video notes. Write a sentence explaining whether format and note preference may be associated.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The biggest count always proves an association.
Correct: Association depends on how distributions compare across groups. Large counts can happen just because one group has more people.
