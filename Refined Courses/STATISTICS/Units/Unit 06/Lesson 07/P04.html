P04 Worked Example

Study each example step by step. The goal is to explain whether a decision method is fair.

Example 1: Drawing Lots

Four students place identical slips of paper into a box. One slip is drawn without looking.

Step 1: There are 4 equally likely slips.

Step 2: Each student has probability 1/4.

Conclusion: The method is fair if the slips are identical and each student has one slip.

Example 2: Random Number Generator

A random number from 1 to 80 chooses one of four groups. Group A gets 1-20, B gets 21-40, C gets 41-60, and D gets 61-80.

Step 1: Each group receives 20 numbers.

Step 2: Each probability is 20/80 = 1/4.

Conclusion: The method is fair.

Example 3: Biased Method

Five students are eligible, but the teacher asks for volunteers and chooses the first hand raised.

Step 1: The fastest or most confident student has an advantage.

Step 2: The probabilities are not controlled or equal.

Conclusion: This method is not fair for equal selection.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A random number generator is always fair."

Correct: "A random number generator is fair only when the number assignments match the intended probabilities."

The tool can be random, but the setup can still be unfair.
