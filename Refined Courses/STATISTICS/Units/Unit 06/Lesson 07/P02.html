Notebook Title: What Makes a Decision Fair?

A decision method is fair when the people or outcomes that should have the same chance actually receive the same probability.

Vocabulary

  • Fair decision: a decision method that gives equal chances when equal chances are required.
  • Random selection: choosing by chance rather than preference.
  • Equally likely: outcomes that have the same probability.
  • Bias: an unfair advantage or disadvantage in a selection method.
  • Random number generator: a tool that produces numbers by chance within a chosen range.

Step 1: Identify Who Should Have a Chance

Before deciding whether a method is fair, identify the eligible outcomes. If four students are equally eligible to go first, then each student should have probability 1/4, or 25%.

Step 2: Match the Method to the Probability

A fair method must create the intended probabilities. Drawing one name from four identical slips gives each name probability 1/4. Choosing the loudest volunteer does not, because personality affects the result.

Notebook Task Part 1

Describe a situation where four people should have an equal chance. State the probability each person should receive and name one fair selection method.
