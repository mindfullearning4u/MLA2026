Instructions

Answer each part using complete sentences. Show the fraction or division you use to turn each count into a probability.

Part A

A cafeteria records the number of snacks bought by 30 students: 0 snacks for 6 students, 1 snack for 15 students, and 2 snacks for 9 students. Build the empirical distribution.

Part B

Use your distribution from Part A to calculate the empirical expected value.

Part C

Explain what the empirical expected value means in the context of snacks bought per student.

Independent Work Guidance

For independent work, complete each part as evidence of mastery rather than as a list of short answers. In Part A, write the given data or scenario and identify the relevant variable, display, or statistic. In Part B, show the process: organize the table, read the graph, calculate the value, or compare the choices using the mapped lesson standard. In Part C, write a conclusion that a reader can understand without seeing your scratch work. Your response should answer the question, use the vocabulary from Empirical Probability Distributions and Expected Value, and avoid unsupported claims. If your answer depends on a graph or table, name the feature you used.
