Notebook Title: Empirical Probability Distributions

Empirical probability comes from what actually happened in repeated trials or collected observations.

Vocabulary

  • Empirical probability: probability estimated from observed results.
  • Frequency: the number of times a value occurs.
  • Relative frequency: frequency divided by total trials.
  • Empirical distribution: a probability distribution built from observed relative frequencies.
  • Empirical expected value: the average value predicted by the observed distribution.

Step 1: Start With Observed Counts

Suppose a prize wheel is spun 50 times. The result $0 occurs 25 times, $2 occurs 15 times, and $5 occurs 10 times.

These are observed counts. They do not come from assuming the wheel is fair. They come from what happened in the trials.

Step 2: Convert Counts to Probabilities

Use empirical probability = count / total trials.

  • P($0) = 25/50 = 0.50
  • P($2) = 15/50 = 0.30
  • P($5) = 10/50 = 0.20

The probabilities add to 1, so the empirical distribution accounts for all observed trials.
