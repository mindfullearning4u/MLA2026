Notebook Title: Recognizing Binomial Situations

A binomial situation counts how many successes happen in a fixed number of repeated trials.

Vocabulary

  • Binomial situation: a repeated-trial situation with two outcomes on each trial: success or failure.
  • Trial: one repetition of the chance process.
  • Success: the outcome being counted.
  • Failure: the outcome that is not counted as success.
  • n: the number of trials.
  • p: the probability of success on one trial.

Four Features to Check

  1. There is a fixed number of trials.
  2. Each trial has two categories: success or failure.
  3. The probability of success stays the same.
  4. The trials are independent or treated as independent.

If these features are present, a binomial model may be appropriate.

Example Context

A student guesses on 10 multiple-choice questions. Each question has the same chance of being correct. Define success as getting a question correct.

  • n = 10 trials.
  • Success = correct answer.
  • Failure = incorrect answer.
  • p = probability of a correct answer on one question.

Notebook Task Part 1

Write one example of a binomial situation. Identify the trials, success, failure, n, and p. Then explain why the situation has only two outcome categories for each trial.
