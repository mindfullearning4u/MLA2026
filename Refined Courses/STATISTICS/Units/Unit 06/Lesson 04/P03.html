Notebook Title: Expected Value for Binomial Distributions

For a binomial situation, expected value gives the long-run average number of successes.

The Binomial Expected Value Formula

Expected successes = n Ã— p

Here, n is the number of trials and p is the probability of success on each trial. The result tells the average number of successes expected over many sets of n trials.

Example Calculation

A player takes 20 shots. The probability of making one shot is 0.35.

n = 20 and p = 0.35.

Expected successes = 20 Ã— 0.35 = 7.

The player is expected to make about 7 shots out of 20 over many sets of 20 shots.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Expected successes means exactly 7 shots will be made every time."

Correct: "Expected successes means the average number of made shots should be about 7 over many repeated sets of 20 shots."

Expected value describes long-run average behavior. It does not guarantee one exact result.
