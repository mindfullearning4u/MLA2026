P04 Worked Examples

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A survey reports 48% support with a margin of error of 3 percentage points.

Step 1: Build the interval: 45% to 51%.

Step 2: Interpret it as an estimate with uncertainty.

Conclusion: The true support could reasonably be near 50%.

Worked Example 2

A study randomly assigns students to two review methods and compares quiz growth.

Step 1: Treatments were assigned randomly.

Step 2: The design supports a treatment comparison.

Conclusion: Differences can be interpreted as evidence about the review methods.

Worked Example 3

A report claims one city is healthier because a convenience sample at a gym had lower resting heart rates.

Step 1: Check the sampling method.

Step 2: A gym sample may not represent the city.

Conclusion: The claim is weak because the sampling method is biased.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A sample statistic and a population parameter are the same thing.
Correct: A statistic estimates a parameter, but it is still based on sample data and has uncertainty.
