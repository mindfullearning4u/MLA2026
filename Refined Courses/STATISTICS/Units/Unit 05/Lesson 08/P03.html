Decision Guide

Use this guide: parameters describe populations; statistics come from samples; random samples support population inference; random assignment supports treatment comparisons; simulations show whether results are typical or unusual; reports must be checked for valid methods and justified conclusions.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Treat every data-based claim as equally strong.
Correct: The strength of a claim depends on sampling, design, variability, and whether the conclusion matches the evidence.

Teachable Explanation: When working with statistics, always connect the answer to the context, data type, and question being asked.

Common Confusion Check

Before moving on, test the idea in the lesson against a likely mistake. A student may choose a graph because it looks familiar instead of because it matches the data structure. Another student may compute a value correctly but interpret it without context. For Putting It All Together, the reliable process is to identify the variable, decide what the numbers or categories mean, choose the statistic or display that fits, and then explain the result in a complete sentence. If two choices seem close, compare what each choice would prove and reject the one that answers a different question or uses a different standard than the mapped Statistics standard.
