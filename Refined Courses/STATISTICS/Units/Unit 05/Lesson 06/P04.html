📘 STATISTICS | Unit 05 | Lesson 06

P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Read each model in order. Notice how the example identifies the statistical situation, selects the correct method, completes the work, and explains the conclusion in context.

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

Two random samples compare average commute time for City A and City B.

Step 1: The data come from two populations.

Step 2: Random samples support inference about each city.

Conclusion: Differences in sample means can support a population comparison.

Worked Example 2

Patients are randomly assigned to a new medicine or a standard medicine.

Step 1: Treatments are assigned by chance.

Step 2: Outcomes can be compared between treatment groups.

Conclusion: This design supports a treatment comparison.

Worked Example 3

A website randomly assigns users to two page layouts. Layout A has a 42% purchase rate and Layout B has a 35% purchase rate.

Step 1: Random assignment makes the groups more comparable.

Step 2: Compare the treatment outcomes.

Conclusion: Layout A performed better in this experiment.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A difference between two samples always proves the populations are different.
Correct: A sample difference is evidence, but sampling variability and study design must be considered before making a strong conclusion.

Teachable Explanation: A statistic is only useful when it answers the question being asked and is interpreted in the context of the data.
