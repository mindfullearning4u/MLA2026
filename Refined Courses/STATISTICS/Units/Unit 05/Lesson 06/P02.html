Notebook Title: Inference from Random Samples and Randomized Experiments

Build the foundation first. Read each explanation slowly and write the key ideas in your notebook.

Vocabulary

  • Inference: using sample or experiment data to make a reasoned conclusion.
  • Two populations: two full groups being compared.
  • Random sample: a sample selected by chance from a population.
  • Treatment: a condition assigned in an experiment.
  • Randomized experiment: an experiment that assigns treatments by chance.

Two Kinds of Comparisons

When comparing two populations, use data from two random samples to make an inference about the populations. When comparing treatments, use a randomized experiment so treatment groups are fair before treatment begins.

  1. Ask whether the groups are populations or treatments.
  2. Check whether random sampling or random assignment was used.
  3. Compare centers, percentages, or other appropriate statistics.
  4. State the conclusion in context.
