Notebook Title: Sampling Methods and Bias

Build the foundation first. Read each explanation slowly and write the key ideas in your notebook.

Vocabulary

  • Simple random sample: a sample chosen so every member has a fair chance.
  • Stratified sample: a sample chosen from important subgroups.
  • Cluster sample: a sample chosen by selecting whole groups.
  • Systematic sample: a sample chosen using a regular pattern after a starting point.
  • Convenience sample: a sample chosen because it is easy to reach.
  • Bias: a systematic problem that makes results favor some outcomes over others.

Sampling Methods

Sampling methods are plans for choosing the people or items that will provide data. The method matters because a poor method can make a sample unrepresentative.

  1. Simple random sampling uses chance across the full population.
  2. Stratified sampling makes sure important subgroups are included.
  3. Cluster sampling chooses whole naturally occurring groups.
  4. Systematic sampling follows a pattern, such as every 10th name after a random start.
  5. Convenience sampling uses whoever is easiest to reach and often creates bias.
