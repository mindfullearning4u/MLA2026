P04 Worked Examples

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A school wants opinions from each grade level, so it randomly selects 25 students from each grade.

Step 1: The grade levels are important subgroups.

Step 2: Students are randomly selected within each subgroup.

Conclusion: This is a stratified sample.

Worked Example 2

A researcher randomly chooses 5 classrooms and surveys every student in those classrooms.

Step 1: Whole classrooms are selected.

Step 2: The classroom is a naturally occurring group.

Conclusion: This is a cluster sample.

Worked Example 3

A store asks only customers who visit between 2:00 and 3:00 p.m. on a weekday about store hours.

Step 1: Only one time window is represented.

Step 2: Customers who shop evenings or weekends are left out.

Conclusion: This method may be biased.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Stratified and cluster sampling are the same because both involve groups.
Correct: Stratified sampling selects from every subgroup. Cluster sampling selects whole groups.
