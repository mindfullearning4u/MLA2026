Notebook Title: Evaluating Significance and Making Evidence-Based Decisions

Build the foundation first. Read each explanation slowly and write the key ideas in your notebook.

Vocabulary

  • Statistical significance: evidence that a difference is unlikely to be explained by random variation alone.
  • Simulation: repeated trials used to judge whether a difference is typical or unusual.
  • Practical significance: whether a difference is large enough to matter in context.
  • Statistical report: a written, visual, or digital claim based on data.

Evidence-Based Decisions

Statistical decisions should be based on evidence, not just a single number that looks different. A simulation can show what differences are typical if there is really no meaningful difference. Then the observed result can be compared to that simulated pattern.

  1. Identify the claim or decision being made.
  2. Check the data source and study design.
  3. Use simulation to decide whether the result is typical or unusual.
  4. Decide whether the result is meaningful in context.
