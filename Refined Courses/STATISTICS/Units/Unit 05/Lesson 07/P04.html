P04 Worked Examples

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A simulation assumes two treatments are equally effective. Most simulated differences are between -4 and 4 points. The observed difference is 12 points.

Step 1: Compare 12 to the simulated differences.

Step 2: 12 is outside the typical range.

Conclusion: The observed difference may be statistically meaningful.

Worked Example 2

A report says a new app improves scores, but users chose whether to use the app.

Step 1: Check the design.

Step 2: Users self-selected into the app group.

Conclusion: The report may show association, but a causal claim is limited.

Worked Example 3

A graph uses a vertical axis that starts at 94 instead of 0, making a small change look large.

Step 1: Check the scale.

Step 2: Decide whether the visual exaggerates the change.

Conclusion: The graph may be misleading even if the numbers are real.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A professional-looking report is automatically reliable.
Correct: Reliable reports must still be checked for sampling method, design, graph scale, and whether the conclusion follows from the evidence.
