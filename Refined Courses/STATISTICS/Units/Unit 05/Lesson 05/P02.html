Notebook Title: Surveys, Experiments, and Observational Studies

Build the foundation first. Read each explanation slowly and write the key ideas in your notebook.

Vocabulary

  • Survey: a study that asks questions to gather information.
  • Experiment: a study that assigns treatments to compare effects.
  • Observational study: a study that observes without assigning treatment.
  • Random assignment: using chance to assign subjects to treatments.
  • Confounding variable: another factor that may explain the result.

Choosing a Design

The research purpose tells you which design fits. Use a survey to estimate opinions or characteristics. Use an observational study to examine existing behavior without changing it. Use an experiment to test whether a treatment causes a change.

  1. Identify the question being asked.
  2. Decide whether treatment is assigned.
  3. Decide whether the goal is description, association, or cause-and-effect.
  4. Choose the design that matches the purpose.
