Worked Example 1

A school asks a random sample of students whether lunch should start earlier.

Step 1: The goal is to estimate student opinion.

Step 2: No treatment is assigned.

Conclusion: A survey is appropriate.

Worked Example 2

A researcher compares sleep hours and test scores without changing anyone's sleep schedule.

Step 1: The researcher observes existing behavior.

Step 2: No treatment is assigned.

Conclusion: This is an observational study, so causal claims are limited.

Worked Example 3

A clinic randomly assigns patients to Treatment A or Treatment B and compares recovery time.

Step 1: Treatments are assigned.

Step 2: Assignment is random.

Conclusion: This is an experiment and can support stronger treatment comparisons.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: An observational study proves that one variable caused the other.
Correct: Observational studies can show association, but possible confounding limits causal conclusions.

Teachable Explanation: A statistic is only useful when it answers the question being asked and is interpreted in the context of the data.
