P04 Worked Examples

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A model predicts that 50% of voters support a proposal. Simulated samples of 100 voters usually produce support between 40% and 60%.

Step 1: Compare the observed result to the simulated range.

Step 2: If the observed sample is 52%, it is typical.

Conclusion: A 52% result is consistent with the model.

Worked Example 2

The same model usually produces 40% to 60% support, but an observed sample shows 72% support.

Step 1: Compare 72% to the simulated results.

Step 2: 72% is outside the usual range.

Conclusion: The observed result may not be consistent with the model.

Worked Example 3

Two simulations use samples of size 25 and size 200.

Step 1: Smaller samples usually vary more.

Step 2: Larger samples usually cluster more tightly around the model value.

Conclusion: Sample size affects variation in estimates.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Simulation removes all uncertainty.
Correct: Simulation helps us understand uncertainty by showing how much results can vary.
