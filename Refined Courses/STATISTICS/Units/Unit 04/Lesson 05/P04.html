📘 STATISTICS | Unit 04 | Lesson 05

P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Read each model in order. Notice how the example identifies the statistical situation, selects the correct method, completes the work, and explains the conclusion in context.

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A student is selected from a club. P(senior) = 0.40, P(officer) = 0.25, and P(senior and officer) = 0.10.

Step 1: The question asks for senior or officer, so use the addition rule.

Step 2: Add 0.40 + 0.25 = 0.65.

Step 3: Subtract the overlap: 0.65 - 0.10 = 0.55.

Conclusion: P(senior or officer) = 0.55.

Worked Example 2

On one number cube, A = rolling a 1 and B = rolling a 6.

Step 1: A roll cannot be both 1 and 6.

Step 2: The events are mutually exclusive.

Step 3: P(1 or 6) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3.

Worked Example 3

In a class, 60% use flashcards, 50% use practice quizzes, and 30% use both.

Step 1: Add the two event probabilities: 60% + 50% = 110%.

Step 2: Subtract the overlap: 110% - 30% = 80%.

Conclusion: 80% use flashcards or practice quizzes.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: 60% + 50% = 110%, so the answer is 110%.
Correct: A probability cannot be more than 100%. Subtract the overlap to avoid double-counting.

Teachable Explanation: A statistic is only useful when it answers the question being asked and is interpreted in the context of the data.
