Independence in Plain Language

Two events are independent when the condition does not meaningfully change the probability. If the chance of finishing homework is about the same for students who have study hall and students overall, study hall and finishing homework may be approximately independent.

If the probability changes a lot after the condition is known, the events are probably dependent in that situation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: If two events can happen together, they must be independent.
Correct: Independence is about whether one event changes the probability of the other. Events can happen together and still be dependent.

Teachable Explanation: When working with statistics, always connect the answer to the context, data type, and question being asked.

Common Confusion Check

Before moving on, test the idea in the lesson against a likely mistake. A student may choose a graph because it looks familiar instead of because it matches the data structure. Another student may compute a value correctly but interpret it without context. For Interpreting Conditional Probability and Independence in Context, the reliable process is to identify the variable, decide what the numbers or categories mean, choose the statistic or display that fits, and then explain the result in a complete sentence. If two choices seem close, compare what each choice would prove and reject the one that answers a different question or uses a different standard than the mapped Statistics standard.
