Independence in Plain Language

Two events are independent when the condition does not meaningfully change the probability. If the chance of finishing homework is about the same for students who have study hall and students overall, study hall and finishing homework may be approximately independent.

If the probability changes a lot after the condition is known, the events are probably dependent in that situation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: If two events can happen together, they must be independent.
Correct: Independence is about whether one event changes the probability of the other. Events can happen together and still be dependent.
