📘 STATISTICS | Unit 04 | Lesson 03

P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Read each model in order. Notice how the example identifies the statistical situation, selects the correct method, completes the work, and explains the conclusion in context.

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A survey says 40% of all students choose online practice. Among students who need extra review, 65% choose online practice.

Step 1: Identify the condition: students who need extra review.

Step 2: Compare 65% to the overall 40%.

Conclusion: The condition changes the probability, so the events are not independent in this context.

Worked Example 2

A student asks, "What is the chance a pen writes in blue, given that it came from the front table?"

Step 1: The phrase "given that" tells you the condition.

Step 2: Look only at pens from the front table.

Step 3: Find the portion of those pens that write in blue.

Worked Example 3

A school reports that 30% of all students play a sport. Among students in band, 31% play a sport.

Step 1: Compare the conditional probability, 31%, with the overall probability, 30%.

Step 2: The probabilities are very close.

Conclusion: Being in band does not appear to change the chance of playing a sport by much.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A small difference always proves dependence.
Correct: In real contexts, small differences may be described as approximately independent. Explain the size of the change and what it means.

Teachable Explanation: A statistic is only useful when it answers the question being asked and is interpreted in the context of the data.
