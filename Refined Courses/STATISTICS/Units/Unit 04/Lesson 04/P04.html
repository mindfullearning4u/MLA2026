📘 STATISTICS | Unit 04 | Lesson 04

P04 Worked Examples

Step-by-step explanation: Read each model in order. Notice how the example identifies the statistical situation, selects the correct method, completes the work, and explains the conclusion in context.

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

In a survey of 100 students, 30 are seniors who prefer online review.

Step 1: The question describes both grade level and preference, so this is a joint count.

Step 2: Empirical probability = 30 out of 100 = 30%.

Conclusion: The probability of senior and online review is 30%.

Worked Example 2

A row for students who ride the bus has 50 students. Of those, 35 arrive before 8:00.

Step 1: The condition is riding the bus.

Step 2: Stay within the bus group, so the denominator is 50.

Step 3: 35 out of 50 = 70%.

Worked Example 3

Among students who study with notes, 82% pass. Among students who do not study with notes, 55% pass.

Step 1: Compare the two conditional percentages.

Step 2: 82% and 55% are not close.

Conclusion: Study method and passing do not appear approximately independent.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A larger count always means a larger conditional probability.
Correct: Conditional probability depends on the group total. Compare percentages, not just raw counts.

Teachable Explanation: A statistic is only useful when it answers the question being asked and is interpreted in the context of the data.
