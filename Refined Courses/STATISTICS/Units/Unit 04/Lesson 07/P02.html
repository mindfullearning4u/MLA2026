Vocabulary

  • Counting principle: multiply the number of choices for each step when choices happen in stages.
  • Permutation: an arrangement where order matters.
  • Combination: a selection where order does not matter.
  • Factorial: a product that multiplies whole numbers downward, such as 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1.

Choosing a Counting Method

Counting problems ask how many outcomes are possible. First decide whether the situation happens in stages, whether order matters, and whether items are being arranged or selected.

  1. If there are stages, multiply the number of choices at each stage.
  2. If order matters, use permutation reasoning.
  3. If order does not matter, use combination reasoning.
