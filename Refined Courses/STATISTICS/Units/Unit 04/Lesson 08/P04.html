P04 Worked Examples

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process before the final answer.

Worked Example 1

A survey asks whether students use notes and whether they pass. The question says, "among students who use notes."

Step 1: The word among gives a condition.

Step 2: Use only the students who use notes as the denominator.

Conclusion: This is a conditional probability situation.

Worked Example 2

A question asks for the probability that a student is in band or plays a sport, and some students do both.

Step 1: The word or points to the addition rule.

Step 2: Because some students do both, subtract the overlap.

Conclusion: Use P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B).

Worked Example 3

A club chooses 4 students from 10 for the same committee.

Step 1: The group has equal roles, so order does not matter.

Step 2: Use combination reasoning.

Conclusion: This is 10C4, not a permutation.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Treat every multi-step probability as independent.
Correct: Ask whether the first event changes the second probability. If it changes, use conditional probability.
