P04 Worked Example

Study all three examples. Each one shows the thinking process you should use before answering independently.

Worked Example 1: Finding Relative Frequency

Situation: 15 of 50 students prefer video notes.

  1. Part: 15.
  2. Total: 50.
  3. Divide: 15/50 = 0.30.
  4. Percent: 30%.

Worked Example 2: Planning a Segmented Bar

Situation: A group has 30% video, 50% written notes, and 20% practice problems.

  1. Draw one bar for 100%.
  2. Mark segments at 30%, 80%, and 100%.
  3. Label each segment.
  4. Use different colors or shading for each category.

Worked Example 3: Interpreting Segments

Situation: Grade 9 has 65% choosing tutoring and Grade 10 has 40%.

  1. Identify the category: tutoring.
  2. Compare percentages: 65% is greater than 40%.
  3. Conclusion: A larger share of Grade 9 students chose tutoring.
  4. Avoid claiming why unless the data include reasons.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Grade 10 has more students choosing tutoring, so Grade 10 has the stronger preference."

Problem: If the grade totals are different, counts alone can mislead.

Correct: "Compare the percentages for each grade before deciding which group has the stronger preference."

Correction: Use relative frequency to compare groups fairly.
