Notebook Task Part 2

Compare the data sets.

  1. Class A: 72, 76, 78, 80, 84 and Class B: 68, 70, 80, 90, 92.
  2. Store A wait times: 4, 5, 5, 6, 20 and Store B wait times: 6, 7, 7, 8, 9.

For each pair, compare center, spread, and the possible effect of outliers.

Common Mistakes

  • Comparing only means and ignoring variability.
  • Calling every extreme value an outlier without context.
  • Using the mean when a strong outlier makes the median more useful.
  • Forgetting to compare data sets in the original units.

Common Mistake Example

Incorrect: Choosing a statistical measure or display without checking what the data values represent.

Correct: First identify the statistical question, the variable, and the data type; then choose the measure, display, or interpretation that fits that context.

Teachable Explanation: Statistics decisions depend on context. A correct answer must connect the calculation or graph to what the data actually describe.

Common Confusion Check

Before moving on, test the idea in the lesson against a likely mistake. A student may choose a graph because it looks familiar instead of because it matches the data structure. Another student may compute a value correctly but interpret it without context. For Comparing Data Sets and Accounting for Outliers, the reliable process is to identify the variable, decide what the numbers or categories mean, choose the statistic or display that fits, and then explain the result in a complete sentence. If two choices seem close, compare what each choice would prove and reject the one that answers a different question or uses a different standard than the mapped Statistics standard.
