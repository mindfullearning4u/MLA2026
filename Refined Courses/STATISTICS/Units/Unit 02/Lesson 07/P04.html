P04 Worked Examples

Work through all three examples. Each one models calculation, interpretation, and conclusion writing.

Worked Example 1: Choose Resistant Measures

Problem: A data set is 4, 5, 6, 7, 40. Which measures are better for typical value and spread?

Step 1: Identify 40 as an outlier.

Step 2: Choose median for center.

Step 3: Choose IQR for spread.

Median and IQR

Reasoning: Median and IQR resist the effect of the outlier.

Worked Example 2: Reject a Normal Model

Problem: A distribution has two clear clusters. Should one normal model be used?

Step 1: Normal models have one main mound.

Step 2: Two clusters suggest different groups.

Step 3: Reject one normal model.

No

Reasoning: A single normal curve would hide the two-cluster structure.

Worked Example 3: Support a Normal Model

Problem: A histogram is symmetric, mound-shaped, and has no strong outliers.

Step 1: Check symmetry.

Step 2: Check for one peak.

Step 3: Check for no strong outliers.

A normal model may be appropriate.

Reasoning: The shape matches the basic expectations for a normal model.

Common Mistake

Problem: A data set is strongly skewed right.

Incorrect: Use mean and standard deviation because they are always best.

Mean and standard deviation can be distorted by skew and outliers.

Correct: Use median and IQR for a more resistant summary.
