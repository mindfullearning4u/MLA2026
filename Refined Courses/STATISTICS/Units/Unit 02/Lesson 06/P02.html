Notebook Title

Estimating Population Percentages from Normal Models

Vocabulary

Population Percentage: The estimated percent of a full population that falls in a category or interval.

Tail: An end region of a distribution, such as values above or below a cutoff.

Within: Between two values, usually around the mean in a normal model.

Model Estimate: An approximate answer based on a model rather than an exact count.

Concept Development

When data are reasonably modeled by a normal distribution, the empirical rule can estimate population percentages. The estimate is not an exact count. It is a model-based statement about what percent of values are expected in an interval.

Start by locating the cutoff or interval compared with the mean. Then decide whether the value is one, two, or three standard deviations away from the mean.

For example, if the mean is 100 and the standard deviation is 15, then 115 is one standard deviation above the mean and 85 is one standard deviation below the mean.
