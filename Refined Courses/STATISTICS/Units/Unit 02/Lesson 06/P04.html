STATISTICS | Unit 02 | Lesson 06

P04 Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Statistics requires calculation and interpretation.

Worked Example 1: Estimate Within One Standard Deviation

Problem: A normal model has mean 70 and standard deviation 5. About what percent of values are between 65 and 75?

Step 1: Recognize that 65 is one standard deviation below 70.

Step 2: Recognize that 75 is one standard deviation above 70.

Step 3: Use the empirical rule for one standard deviation.

About 68%

Reasoning: In a normal model, about 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean.

Worked Example 2: Estimate a Tail

Problem: A normal model has mean 100 and standard deviation 10. About what percent of values are above 110?

Step 1: Recognize that 110 is one standard deviation above the mean.

Step 2: About 68% is within one standard deviation, leaving 32% outside.

Step 3: Use symmetry to divide 32% into two tails.

About 16%

Reasoning: The upper tail above one standard deviation is about half of the 32% outside the central 68%.

Worked Example 3: Write a Population Statement

Problem: A normal model for adult commute times estimates that 95% are between 10 and 50 minutes. Write a conclusion.

Step 1: Identify the percentage: 95%.

Step 2: Identify the population and variable: adult commute times.

Step 3: Write the interval in context.

About 95% of adult commute times are between 10 and 50 minutes.

Reasoning: A statistical estimate should connect the percent, population, variable, and interval.

Common Mistake

Problem: A normal model has mean 60 and standard deviation 8. Estimate the percent above 68.

Incorrect: About 68%

The number 68 is a data value, not the percent. It is one standard deviation above the mean.

Correct: About 16%

Use the empirical rule: above one standard deviation is the upper tail, about 16%.
