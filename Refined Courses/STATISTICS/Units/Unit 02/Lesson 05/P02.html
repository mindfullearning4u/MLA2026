Notebook Title

Mean, Standard Deviation, and Normal Models

Vocabulary

Mean: The average value and balance point of a numerical data set.

Standard Deviation: A measure of how far data values typically are from the mean.

Normal Distribution: A symmetric, mound-shaped distribution described by its mean and standard deviation.

Empirical Rule: A rule for normal models: about 68% within one standard deviation, 95% within two, and 99.7% within three.

Concept Development

A normal model is used when a numerical data distribution is roughly symmetric, mound-shaped, and centered around one main peak. The mean marks the center of the model. The standard deviation describes the typical distance from the mean.

To build the basic structure of a normal model, start at the mean. Then mark values one standard deviation below and above the mean, two standard deviations below and above, and three standard deviations below and above.

Example: If the mean is 50 and the standard deviation is 5, then one standard deviation from the mean is 45 to 55, two standard deviations is 40 to 60, and three standard deviations is 35 to 65.
