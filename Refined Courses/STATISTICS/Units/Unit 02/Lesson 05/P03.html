Deeper Reasoning

A normal model is useful only when the data shape supports it. If the data are strongly skewed, have extreme outliers, or form two separate clusters, a normal model may give misleading percentage estimates.

The empirical rule gives approximate percentages for normal models: about 68% of values are within one standard deviation of the mean, about 95% are within two standard deviations, and about 99.7% are within three standard deviations.

When explaining a normal model, always name the mean, name the standard deviation, describe the interval, and state whether the model seems appropriate for the data shape.

Common Mistake

Problem: A data set has mean 40 and standard deviation 6. Find the interval within one standard deviation.

Incorrect: 0 to 6

This lists the size of the standard deviation but does not center the interval around the mean.

Correct: 34 to 46

Subtract and add the standard deviation from the mean: 40 - 6 and 40 + 6.
