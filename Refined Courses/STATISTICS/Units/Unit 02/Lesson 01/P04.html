STATISTICS | Unit 02 | Lesson 01

P04 Worked Examples

Study each example carefully. Statistics is not just calculation; each answer must be interpreted in the context of the data.

Worked Example 1: Find the Mean

Problem: Find the mean of 6, 8, 10, 12, 14.

Step 1: Add all values: 6 + 8 + 10 + 12 + 14 = 50.

Step 2: Count the number of values: 5.

Step 3: Divide total by count: 50 / 5 = 10.

Mean = 10

Reasoning: The mean is the balance point of the data set.

Worked Example 2: Find the Median

Problem: Find the median of 3, 11, 7, 9, 5.

Step 1: Order the values from least to greatest: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.

Step 2: Find the middle value.

Step 3: State the median.

Median = 7

Reasoning: The median is the center value after the data are ordered.

Worked Example 3: Choose a Better Center

Problem: A data set is 10, 12, 13, 14, 90. Which measure of center better represents a typical value?

Step 1: Notice that 90 is much larger than the other values.

Step 2: Recognize that the mean is pulled upward by the outlier.

Step 3: Choose the median for a typical value.

Median is better.

Reasoning: The median resists outliers better than the mean.

Common Mistake

Problem: Find the median of 8, 2, 10, 6, 4.

Incorrect:

Median = 10

Why it is wrong: The values were not ordered first.

Correct:

Order first: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, so median = 6.

Fix: Always order numerical data before finding the median.
