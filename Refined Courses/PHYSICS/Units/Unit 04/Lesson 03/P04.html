Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the visual evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 4 is visual. Strong physics reasoning does not guess from a word. It points to the crest, normal line, source-observer motion, spectrum row, frame label, focal point, or image point before choosing an answer.

QuestionStudent Reasoning Slow Walk
Who is moving?Relative motion between source and observer matters.
Toward or away?Toward compresses wavefronts; away spreads them.
What changes?Observed frequency changes, not the actual source frequency.
What does the listener hear?Higher frequency sounds like higher pitch.

Student reasoning move: First name the visual evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Approaching siren

Problem: An ambulance moves toward a listener. What happens to observed pitch?

  1. Step 1: The source moves toward the observer.
  2. Step 2: Wavefronts arrive closer together.
  3. Step 3: Closer arrivals mean higher observed frequency and pitch.

Answer: The pitch sounds higher.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Moving away

Problem: A train horn moves away from a platform. What happens to observed frequency?

  1. Step 1: The source moves away.
  2. Step 2: Wavefronts are spread out at the observer.
  3. Step 3: Fewer waves arrive each second.

Answer: Observed frequency is lower.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: No relative motion

Problem: A speaker and listener are both still. Is there a Doppler shift?

  1. Step 1: There is no relative motion.
  2. Step 2: Wavefront spacing at the observer is not compressed or stretched by motion.
  3. Step 3: Observed frequency stays the same.

Answer: No Doppler shift occurs.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The Doppler effect means the source changes the sound it produces.

Correct: The Doppler effect changes the observed frequency because source and observer move relative to each other.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the diagram, table, rule, or observer frame.
