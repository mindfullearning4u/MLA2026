P04 Worked Example

Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the visual evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 4 is visual. A veteran Physics teacher would not ask a student to guess from a word. The teacher would point to the crest, normal line, source-observer motion, spectrum row, frame label, focal point, or image point before choosing an answer.

Teacher move: First name the visual evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Reflection angle

Problem: A light ray strikes a mirror at 30 degrees from the normal. What is the reflection angle?

  1. Step 1: Use the law of reflection.
  2. Step 2: Angle of reflection equals angle of incidence.
  3. Step 3: The incident angle is 30 degrees.

Answer: The reflection angle is 30 degrees.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Normal line

Problem: Why is the normal line drawn?

  1. Step 1: It is perpendicular to the surface.
  2. Step 2: Optics angles are measured from it.
  3. Step 3: It prevents measuring from the wrong reference.

Answer: The normal line is the angle reference.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Refraction

Problem: Why can a ray bend entering glass?

  1. Step 1: The ray enters a different medium.
  2. Step 2: Light speed changes in the new medium.
  3. Step 3: A speed change at a boundary can change direction.

Answer: The ray refracts because the medium changes.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Angles in ray diagrams are measured from the mirror surface.

Correct: Angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the normal line.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the diagram, table, rule, or observer frame.
