Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the visual evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 4 is visual. Strong physics reasoning does not guess from a word. It points to the crest, normal line, source-observer motion, spectrum row, frame label, focal point, or image point before choosing an answer.

RelationshipWhat To Remember
Wavelength and frequencyThey move opposite directions.
Frequency and energyHigher frequency means higher photon energy at this level.
Vacuum travelEM waves do not need a material medium.
ApplicationsUse the spectrum region and energy level to justify the use.

Student reasoning move: First name the visual evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Compare radio and gamma

Problem: Which has higher frequency, radio or gamma rays?

  1. Step 1: Radio has long wavelength.
  2. Step 2: Gamma has very short wavelength.
  3. Step 3: Shorter wavelength corresponds to higher frequency.

Answer: Gamma rays have higher frequency.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Visible light

Problem: Why can visible light travel from the Sun through space?

  1. Step 1: Visible light is electromagnetic.
  2. Step 2: Electromagnetic waves do not require a medium.
  3. Step 3: Space can transmit EM radiation.

Answer: Visible light can travel through empty space.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Energy trend

Problem: Which EM wave has more energy: ultraviolet or infrared?

  1. Step 1: Ultraviolet has higher frequency than infrared.
  2. Step 2: Higher frequency means higher energy.
  3. Step 3: Choose ultraviolet.

Answer: Ultraviolet has more energy.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Electromagnetic waves need air to travel.

Correct: Electromagnetic waves can travel through empty space; mechanical waves need a medium.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the diagram, table, rule, or observer frame.
