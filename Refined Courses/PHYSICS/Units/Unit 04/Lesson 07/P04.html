P04 Worked Example

Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the visual evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 4 is visual. A veteran Physics teacher would not ask a student to guess from a word. The teacher would point to the crest, normal line, source-observer motion, spectrum row, frame label, focal point, or image point before choosing an answer.

Teacher move: First name the visual evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Converging lens

Problem: A converging lens has rays meeting on the far side. What type of image is shown?

  1. Step 1: Rays physically meet.
  2. Step 2: Meeting rays indicate a real image.
  3. Step 3: The image is on the side where rays converge.

Answer: The image is real.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Diverging lens

Problem: A diverging lens makes rays spread out, but traced-back rays meet on the object side. What image type is this?

  1. Step 1: Actual rays do not meet on the far side.
  2. Step 2: Back-traced rays appear to meet.
  3. Step 3: Appearing to meet means virtual image.

Answer: The image is virtual.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Ray diagram discipline

Problem: Why trace two rays instead of guessing?

  1. Step 1: One ray alone does not fix the image point.
  2. Step 2: Two rays show where paths meet or appear to meet.
  3. Step 3: The diagram provides evidence.

Answer: Two rays locate the image with evidence.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The image is always at the focal point.

Correct: The focal point guides ray paths; the image forms where rays meet or appear to meet.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the diagram, table, rule, or observer frame.
