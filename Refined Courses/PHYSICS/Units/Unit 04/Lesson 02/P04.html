Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the visual evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 4 is visual. Strong physics reasoning does not guess from a word. It points to the crest, normal line, source-observer motion, spectrum row, frame label, focal point, or image point before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 1: Medium change

Problem: A wave moves from a slow medium to a faster medium while source frequency stays the same. What happens to wavelength?

  1. Step 1: Use v = f x lambda.
  2. Step 2: Frequency stays the same.
  3. Step 3: If speed increases while frequency is constant, wavelength increases.

Answer: Wavelength increases.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Boundary behavior

Problem: Why can a wave reflect at a boundary?

  1. Step 1: A boundary is where the medium changes.
  2. Step 2: Not all energy must transmit into the new medium.
  3. Step 3: Some energy can bounce back as reflection.

Answer: A medium boundary can cause reflection.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Mechanical wave need

Problem: Why does sound not travel through empty space?

  1. Step 1: Sound is mechanical.
  2. Step 2: Mechanical waves require matter particles to vibrate.
  3. Step 3: Empty space has no medium for those vibrations.

Answer: Sound needs a medium, so it does not travel through a vacuum.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Changing the medium always changes the source frequency.

Correct: The medium can change speed and wavelength, while the vibrating source controls frequency.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the diagram, table, rule, or observer frame.
