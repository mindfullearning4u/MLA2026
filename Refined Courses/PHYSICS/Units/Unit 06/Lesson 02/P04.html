Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the evidence type, read labels and limits, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 6 is about using evidence carefully at scales and contexts where students cannot rely on direct everyday observation. Strong physics reasoning first identifies the scale, evidence source, model, observer frame, or decision criteria before making a conclusion.

StepReason
Identify the scaleDifferent forces dominate at different scales.
Compare rangeCosmic systems require long-range effects.
Use the force tableDo not choose a force by name alone.
Connect evidence to systemThe system size controls the explanation.

Student reasoning move: Name the evidence first, state the model or reliability rule second, then explain why the evidence supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 1: Orbit explanation

Problem: Which force best explains a planet orbiting a star?

  1. Step 1: The system is cosmic scale.
  2. Step 2: Gravity acts over long distances.
  3. Step 3: Gravity attracts masses.

Answer: Gravity best explains the orbit.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Nuclear scale

Problem: Why is the strong nuclear force not the main organizer of galaxies?

  1. Step 1: The strong force has very short range.
  2. Step 2: Galaxies are enormous systems.
  3. Step 3: Gravity acts across cosmic distances.

Answer: Its range is too short for galaxy-scale organization.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Force comparison

Problem: Why compare range and role?

  1. Step 1: Force names alone are not enough.
  2. Step 2: Range tells where a force can matter.
  3. Step 3: Role connects the force to evidence.

Answer: Comparison prevents mismatched explanations.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: All forces are equally useful at every scale.

Correct: Force explanations depend on range, magnitude, and the scale of the system.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips scale, model limits, source quality, observer frame, or decision criteria.
