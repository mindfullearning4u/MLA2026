P04 Worked Example

Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the evidence type, read labels and limits, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 6 is about using evidence carefully at scales and contexts where students cannot rely on direct everyday observation. A veteran Physics teacher would first identify the scale, evidence source, model, observer frame, or decision criteria before allowing a conclusion.

Teacher move: Name the evidence first, state the model or reliability rule second, then explain why the evidence supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 1: Train frame

Problem: A seat is fixed in a moving train. Is it moving in the train frame?

  1. Step 1: Choose the train frame.
  2. Step 2: The seat stays in the same place inside the train.
  3. Step 3: It is at rest in that frame.

Answer: It is at rest in the train frame.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Platform frame

Problem: Is the same seat moving in the platform frame?

  1. Step 1: Choose the platform frame.
  2. Step 2: The train passes the platform.
  3. Step 3: The seat moves with the train.

Answer: It is moving in the platform frame.

Worked Example 3: Model boundary

Problem: Why state model limits?

  1. Step 1: Course-level models simplify advanced ideas.
  2. Step 2: Unsupported claims can go beyond the standard.
  3. Step 3: A boundary keeps the answer accurate.

Answer: Model limits keep the explanation accurate.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Motion has only one description no matter the observer.

Correct: Motion descriptions depend on the selected reference frame.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips scale, model limits, source quality, observer frame, or decision criteria.
