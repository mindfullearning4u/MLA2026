Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the evidence type, read labels and limits, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 6 is about using evidence carefully at scales and contexts where students cannot rely on direct everyday observation. Strong physics reasoning first identifies the scale, evidence source, model, observer frame, or decision criteria before making a conclusion.

Worked Example 1: Solar system pattern

Problem: Planets orbit a star. What force organizes the system?

  1. Step 1: Identify the system: star and planets.
  2. Step 2: Masses attract by gravity.
  3. Step 3: Orbits show gravitational organization.

Answer: Gravity organizes the system.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Galaxy scale

Problem: Why is a galaxy a matter distribution pattern?

  1. Step 1: A galaxy contains many stars and gas.
  2. Step 2: The matter is not random; it is grouped.
  3. Step 3: Gravity helps shape the grouping.

Answer: A galaxy is a large-scale matter pattern.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Scale reasoning

Problem: Why use a scale model?

  1. Step 1: Cosmic distances are too large to view directly in normal classroom scale.
  2. Step 2: A model preserves relationships.
  3. Step 3: The model helps compare sizes and distances.

Answer: A scale model makes cosmic relationships understandable.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Cosmic systems are explained without forces or evidence.

Correct: Cosmic systems are interpreted with matter patterns, scale models, and physical forces such as gravity.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips scale, model limits, source quality, observer frame, or decision criteria.
