Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the evidence type, read labels and limits, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 6 is about using evidence carefully at scales and contexts where students cannot rely on direct everyday observation. Strong physics reasoning first identifies the scale, evidence source, model, observer frame, or decision criteria before making a conclusion.

QuestionStudent Reasoning Slow Walk
What evidence was collected?Models change because of evidence.
What old model failed?A revision fixes a limitation.
What new model explains it?The new model must fit the data.
What should not be claimed?Do not claim science changes randomly.

Student reasoning move: Name the evidence first, state the model or reliability rule second, then explain why the evidence supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 1: Scattering evidence

Problem: Some alpha particles deflected strongly. What did that suggest?

  1. Step 1: Most of the atom is empty space.
  2. Step 2: Strong deflection needs concentrated positive charge.
  3. Step 3: The atom has a small dense nucleus.

Answer: The nuclear model was supported.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Model revision

Problem: Why was the solid sphere model replaced?

  1. Step 1: New evidence showed internal structure.
  2. Step 2: The old model could not explain deflections.
  3. Step 3: A revised model explained the evidence.

Answer: Evidence made the old model incomplete.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Modern model

Problem: Why use an electron cloud model?

  1. Step 1: Electron behavior is not shown accurately as simple fixed paths.
  2. Step 2: The cloud model uses probability.
  3. Step 3: It better matches evidence.

Answer: The electron cloud model better represents electron location evidence.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Scientific models change because scientists guess randomly.

Correct: Scientific models change when new evidence shows a model is incomplete or needs revision.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips scale, model limits, source quality, observer frame, or decision criteria.
