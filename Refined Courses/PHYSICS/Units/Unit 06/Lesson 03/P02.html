Notebook Title

Lesson 3: Atomic Models and Evidence

Vocabulary

  • Atomic model: representation of atomic structure based on evidence.
  • Evidence: data or observations that support a claim.
  • Revision: change made when new evidence shows a model is incomplete.
  • Nucleus: dense central part of an atom.
  • Electron cloud: modern model region where electrons are likely found.

Step 1: Identify the Evidence Type

Before answering, identify whether the question gives a scale model, force comparison, atomic evidence timeline, reference-frame scenario, model/theory/law chart, reliability table, or decision matrix.

Step 2: Read Labels and Limits

Labels, scale, source quality, and model limits tell you what the evidence means. In Unit 6, most mistakes happen when students make a broad claim without reading the evidence.

Student Reasoning Slow Walk

Physics Unit 6 is about using evidence carefully at scales and contexts where students cannot rely on direct everyday observation. Strong physics reasoning first identifies the scale, evidence source, model, observer frame, or decision criteria before making a conclusion.

QuestionStudent Reasoning Slow Walk
What evidence was collected?Models change because of evidence.
What old model failed?A revision fixes a limitation.
What new model explains it?The new model must fit the data.
What should not be claimed?Do not claim science changes randomly.

Student reasoning move: Name the evidence first, state the model or reliability rule second, then explain why the evidence supports the conclusion.

Step 3: Connect Evidence to the Rule

After reading the visual, name the rule or relationship. Do not use a conclusion until the evidence tells you that it fits.
