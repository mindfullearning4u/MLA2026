Step 4: Use the Unit 6 Reasoning Routine

Use this routine for every Unit 6 problem.

  1. Identify the stimulus type.
  2. Read scale, source, frame, labels, model limits, and criteria.
  3. Choose the matching cosmic, atomic, frame, reliability, or decision rule.
  4. Interpret using the embedded evidence.
  5. Explain why the evidence supports the answer.

How to use PhET: Rutherford Scattering

  1. Click the link, then click the play or launch button for the simulation. If the page offers versions, use the HTML5 version.
  2. Start with the default screen or the first simulation screen. Turn on labels, vectors, values, paths, graphs, or field lines when those controls are available.
  3. Change only one variable at a time, such as mass, force, distance, charge, wavelength, or velocity. Watch what changes before adjusting another control.
  4. Record one observation from the model and identify the Physics relationship shown by that observation.
  5. Use the observation as evidence in the notebook task, guided practice, or checkpoint when the lesson asks for simulation evidence.

How to use OpenStax: Atomic Physics

  1. Click the link and stay on the exact OpenStax section that opens.
  2. Read the section title, first explanation, key equation or model, and any figure or table in the linked section.
  3. Identify the quantity, relationship, law, or model that matches the lesson.
  4. Record one formula, visual detail, or evidence statement from the section.
  5. Return to the lesson and use that evidence in the notebook task or checkpoint.

Support for Different Readiness Levels

Remedial support: Read one row, label, source, frame, or criterion at a time. Say what it proves before answering.

Standard support: Use the embedded diagram/table and explain the matching cosmic, atomic, reference-frame, reliability, or decision rule.

Accelerated extension: Predict how the conclusion changes if the scale, evidence quality, observer frame, model limitation, or decision constraint changes.

Question to ask yourself: What evidence would remove confusion for the student, and what does that evidence prove?

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Scientific models change because scientists guess randomly.

This is incorrect because it ignores the evidence or applies the wrong Unit 6 model.

Correct: Scientific models change when new evidence shows a model is incomplete or needs revision.

Teachable explanation: Correct Physics reasoning names the evidence, applies the rule, and explains the result in the mapped standard.
