Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the evidence type, read labels and limits, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 6 is about using evidence carefully at scales and contexts where students cannot rely on direct everyday observation. Strong physics reasoning first identifies the scale, evidence source, model, observer frame, or decision criteria before making a conclusion.

Before SolvingReason
Choose the evidence typeUnit 6 mixes scale, models, reliability, and decisions.
Identify mapped standardDo not add unrelated Earth/Space or advanced physics content.
Read the embedded tableThe table controls the answer.
Explain with evidenceThe visual or data must appear in the reasoning.

Student reasoning move: Name the evidence first, state the model or reliability rule second, then explain why the evidence supports the conclusion.

Worked Example 1: Cosmic evidence

Problem: A table shows planets orbiting a star. What Physics idea fits?

  1. Step 1: The system contains masses.
  2. Step 2: The scale is cosmic.
  3. Step 3: Gravity organizes orbital motion.

Answer: Use gravity and scale reasoning.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Model revision

Problem: A model changes after new evidence. What does this show?

  1. Step 1: Evidence was collected.
  2. Step 2: The old model had a limitation.
  3. Step 3: Science revised the model.

Answer: Models change when evidence supports revision.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Decision evidence

Problem: A technology choice lists benefits, risks, and data. What should the final answer include?

  1. Step 1: State the claim.
  2. Step 2: Use evidence from the matrix.
  3. Step 3: Explain the reasoning and constraints.

Answer: Use an evidence-based decision explanation.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use one unrelated idea for every Unit 6 problem.

Correct: Choose the cosmic, atomic, reference-frame, reliability, or decision model based on the embedded stimulus.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips scale, model limits, source quality, observer frame, or decision criteria.
