P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Find a possible outlier

Problem: Which time should be checked before drawing a conclusion?

  1. Step 1: Compare all values. Why: outliers stand away from the pattern.
  2. Step 2: Notice 2.1, 2.2, and 2.1 are close. Why: repeated closeness suggests precision.
  3. Step 3: Identify 5.8 s as unusual. Why: it is far from the other trials.

Answer: The 5.8 s value should be checked.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 2: Describe precision

Problem: Are the first three trials precise?

  1. Step 1: Look only at repeated closeness. Why: precision is about agreement among measurements.
  2. Step 2: Compare 2.1, 2.2, and 2.1 s. Why: these values are close together.
  3. Step 3: Classify them as precise. Why: repeated measurements agree closely.

Answer: The first three trials are precise.

Worked Example 3: Explain evidence limits

Problem: Why should the possible outlier be investigated?

  1. Step 1: Data support claims only when the measurement process is trustworthy. Why: errors can mislead.
  2. Step 2: A far value may be a timing mistake or a real event. Why: you cannot delete it without checking.
  3. Step 3: Document the decision. Why: transparent evidence handling supports scientific reliability.

Answer: Evidence must include attention to error and uncertainty.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Delete any number you do not like.

Correct: Check whether an unusual number is a measurement error before deciding how to use it.

Teachable explanation: The correct version names the evidence and uses the mapped physics concept. The incorrect version is vague or ignores the evidence.
