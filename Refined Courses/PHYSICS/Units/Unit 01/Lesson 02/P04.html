P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Choose a tool

Problem: Which tool should measure how far a toy car travels?

  1. Step 1: Identify the quantity: distance. Why: tool choice depends on the quantity.
  2. Step 2: Match distance to a length tool. Why: a ruler or meter stick measures position or distance.
  3. Step 3: Record the unit with the number. Why: 2.0 without meters or centimeters is incomplete.

Answer: Use a meter stick or metric ruler and report the measurement with a unit.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 2: Apply safety

Problem: A student wants to test a moving cart at the edge of a desk. What should happen first?

  1. Step 1: Identify the risk. Why: moving objects can fall or strike something.
  2. Step 2: Choose a control. Why: safety controls reduce risk before data collection.
  3. Step 3: Move the setup away from the edge or use provided virtual/video data. Why: the investigation must remain safe.

Answer: Safety comes before measurement.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 3: Read a measurement

Problem: A cart travels 0.75 m in 2.0 s. What makes the record complete?

  1. Step 1: Check for a number. Why: measurements need values.
  2. Step 2: Check for units. Why: units identify the quantity.
  3. Step 3: Check that both distance and time are labeled. Why: physics calculations depend on correct quantity labels.

Answer: The record is complete because it includes numbers and units.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The answer is 0.75 because units are optional.

Correct: The answer is 0.75 m because distance must include a length unit.

Teachable explanation: The correct version names the evidence and uses the mapped physics concept. The incorrect version is vague or ignores the evidence.
