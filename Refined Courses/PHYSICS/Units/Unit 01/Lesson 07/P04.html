Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Start by setting up the physics. The setup is part of the answer.

Physics often feels difficult because one sentence can contain several ideas at once. Slow the sentence down. First identify the object or system. Next identify the quantity being measured. Then identify the unit, direction, graph axis, table column, or model feature. Only after that should you calculate or choose an answer.

Student reasoning move: A student may think acceleration means fast. Correct that immediately: acceleration means velocity is changing. A slow object can accelerate, and a fast object can have zero acceleration if its velocity stays constant.

Worked Example 1: Identify acceleration

Problem: What happens to velocity each second in the table?

  1. Step 1: Compare adjacent velocity values. Why: acceleration is velocity change over time.
  2. Step 2: The velocity increases by 2 m/s each second. Why: the same increase shows constant acceleration.
  3. Step 3: Describe acceleration as 2 m/s per second east. Why: acceleration has velocity-change over time.

Answer: The object accelerates uniformly.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 2: Distinguish velocity and acceleration

Problem: If velocity is 6 m/s east at 3 s, is that the acceleration?

  1. Step 1: Velocity tells how fast and direction at that time. Why: velocity is not the change itself.
  2. Step 2: Acceleration tells how velocity changes. Why: acceleration uses differences between velocities.
  3. Step 3: Use the change per second, not the final velocity. Why: final velocity alone is not acceleration.

Answer: Acceleration is the rate of velocity change.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 3: Predict motion

Problem: If the pattern continues, what is the velocity at 4 s?

  1. Step 1: Identify the pattern: +2 m/s each second. Why: prediction uses the model pattern.
  2. Step 2: Add 2 m/s to the 3 s velocity of 6 m/s. Why: one more second passes.
  3. Step 3: Predict 8 m/s east. Why: the constant acceleration model continues.

Answer: The model predicts 8 m/s east at 4 s.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Acceleration means an object is always moving fast.

Correct: Acceleration means velocity is changing, even if the object is moving slowly.

Teachable explanation: The correct version names the evidence and uses the mapped physics concept. The incorrect version is vague or ignores the evidence.
