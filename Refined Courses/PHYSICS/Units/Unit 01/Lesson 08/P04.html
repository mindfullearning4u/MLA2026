P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Use mixed evidence

Problem: Which data show that velocity is increasing?

  1. Step 1: Look at the velocity column. Why: acceleration depends on velocity change.
  2. Step 2: Compare 0, 2, 4, and 6 m/s east. Why: each value is greater than the last.
  3. Step 3: State that velocity increases by 2 m/s each second. Why: this supports acceleration.

Answer: The velocity data show acceleration.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 2: Check a safety decision

Problem: A student wants to use a rolling cart near stairs. What should happen?

  1. Step 1: Identify the risk. Why: moving objects near stairs can fall or hit someone.
  2. Step 2: Select a safer option. Why: safe data can come from a simulation, video, or approved setup.
  3. Step 3: Document the safety control. Why: lab evidence must include safety planning.

Answer: Use a safer controlled or virtual setup.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 3: Build a full explanation

Problem: Use the table to write a motion claim.

  1. Step 1: Claim: the cart speeds up eastward. Why: a claim answers the question.
  2. Step 2: Evidence: velocity increases from 0 to 6 m/s east. Why: numbers make the claim checkable.
  3. Step 3: Reasoning: acceleration is change in velocity over time. Why: physics reasoning connects the data to the claim.

Answer: A strong Unit 1 answer combines data, units, direction, and reasoning.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The cart accelerates because the table has many numbers.

Correct: The cart accelerates because velocity changes from 0 to 6 m/s east over 3 seconds.

Teachable explanation: The correct version names the evidence and uses the mapped physics concept. The incorrect version is vague or ignores the evidence.
