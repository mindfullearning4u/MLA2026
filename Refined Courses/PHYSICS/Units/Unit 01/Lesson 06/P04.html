Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Start by setting up the physics. The setup is part of the answer.

Physics often feels difficult because one sentence can contain several ideas at once. Slow the sentence down. First identify the object or system. Next identify the quantity being measured. Then identify the unit, direction, graph axis, table column, or model feature. Only after that should you calculate or choose an answer.

FormulaMeaningWhen To Use It
speed = distance / timepath length divided by timewhen direction is not required

displacement = final position - starting positionhow far and which direction from start to finishbefore calculating velocity

Student reasoning move: Students often divide numbers before deciding whether they need speed or velocity. First ask: "Does the answer need direction?" If yes, use displacement and keep the direction.

Worked Example 1: Calculate velocity

Problem: A cart moves from 0 m to 9 m east in 3 s. What is its average velocity?

  1. Step 1: Find displacement: 9 m east. Why: velocity uses displacement with direction.
  2. Step 2: Find time: 3 s. Why: velocity compares displacement to time.
  3. Step 3: Divide 9 m by 3 s and keep direction. Why: 9/3 = 3 m/s east.

Answer: The average velocity is 3 m/s east.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 2: Read a position-time pattern

Problem: What does equal position change each second mean?

  1. Step 1: Compare the changes: 0 to 3 to 6 to 9. Why: equal intervals show the pattern.
  2. Step 2: Each second adds 3 m. Why: the rate is constant.
  3. Step 3: Interpret as constant velocity. Why: position changes by equal amounts in equal times.

Answer: The cart has constant velocity.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 3: Use a reference frame

Problem: A passenger appears still inside a moving bus. Why can another observer say the passenger is moving?

  1. Step 1: Identify the passenger's frame: inside the bus. Why: relative to the bus, the passenger may be still.
  2. Step 2: Identify the ground frame. Why: relative to the ground, the bus and passenger move.
  3. Step 3: Explain motion depends on reference frame. Why: position is described compared with something.

Answer: Motion descriptions require a reference frame.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Velocity is always the same as speed.

Correct: Velocity includes direction; speed does not.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version names the evidence and uses the mapped physics concept. The incorrect version is vague or ignores the evidence.
