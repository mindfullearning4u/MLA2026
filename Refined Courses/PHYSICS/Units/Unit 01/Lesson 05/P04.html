Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Start by setting up the physics. The setup is part of the answer.

Physics often feels difficult because one sentence can contain several ideas at once. Slow the sentence down. First identify the object or system. Next identify the quantity being measured. Then identify the unit, direction, graph axis, table column, or model feature. Only after that should you calculate or choose an answer.

Worked Example 1: Classify a scalar

Problem: Is 8 m a scalar or vector?

  1. Step 1: Check for magnitude. Why: 8 gives size.
  2. Step 2: Check for direction. Why: vectors require direction.
  3. Step 3: No direction is included. Why: magnitude only means scalar.

Answer: 8 m is a scalar distance.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 2: Classify a vector

Problem: Is 8 m east a scalar or vector?

  1. Step 1: Check magnitude: 8 m. Why: vectors need size.
  2. Step 2: Check direction: east. Why: direction is what makes it vector.
  3. Step 3: Classify as vector. Why: both magnitude and direction are present.

Answer: 8 m east is a vector displacement.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 3: Use units correctly

Problem: Why is 12 east incomplete for velocity?

  1. Step 1: Velocity needs a speed unit. Why: numbers need units.
  2. Step 2: Direction east is present. Why: direction alone is not enough.
  3. Step 3: Write 12 m/s east. Why: magnitude, unit, and direction are all needed.

Answer: A complete vector velocity includes magnitude, unit, and direction.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran physics teacher would slow down, identify the evidence, and connect the evidence to the concept before choosing an answer.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: All numbers with meters are vectors.

Correct: A vector must include magnitude and direction, such as 8 m east.

Teachable explanation: The correct version names the evidence and uses the mapped physics concept. The incorrect version is vague or ignores the evidence.
