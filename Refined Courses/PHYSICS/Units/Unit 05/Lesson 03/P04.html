Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the electrical evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 5 is about tracking charge, fields, paths, and energy transfer. Strong physics reasoning first points to the charge signs, field arrows, conductor table, circuit path, voltage-current-resistance values, power values, or device input-output model before choosing an answer.

QuestionStudent Reasoning Slow Walk
Can charge move easily?Easy motion points to a conductor.
Is charge movement blocked?Limited motion points to an insulator.
Can behavior be controlled?Controlled conductivity points to a semiconductor.
What is the application?Wires, coatings, and electronics use different material behaviors.

Student reasoning move: First name the electrical evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Copper wire

Problem: Why is copper used in wires?

  1. Step 1: Copper allows electrons to move easily.
  2. Step 2: Easy electron motion means low resistance.
  3. Step 3: That makes copper a conductor.

Answer: Copper is a conductor.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Rubber coating

Problem: Why is rubber used around wires?

  1. Step 1: Rubber resists electron movement.
  2. Step 2: Limited charge movement reduces unwanted current paths.
  3. Step 3: That makes rubber an insulator.

Answer: Rubber is an insulator.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Silicon

Problem: Why is silicon useful in electronics?

  1. Step 1: Silicon is not simply conductor or insulator.
  2. Step 2: Its conductivity can be controlled.
  3. Step 3: That makes it useful as a semiconductor.

Answer: Silicon is a semiconductor.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Every material either fully conducts or fully blocks charge.

Correct: Materials can be conductors, insulators, or semiconductors depending on how charges move.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the charge sign, field direction, circuit path, data value, or device system.
