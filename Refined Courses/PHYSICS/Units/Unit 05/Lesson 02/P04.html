Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the electrical evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 5 is about tracking charge, fields, paths, and energy transfer. Strong physics reasoning first points to the charge signs, field arrows, conductor table, circuit path, voltage-current-resistance values, power values, or device input-output model before choosing an answer.

Student reasoning move: First name the electrical evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Positive source

Problem: Field lines point away from a source charge. What is the source sign?

  1. Step 1: Field lines leave positive charges.
  2. Step 2: The arrows point outward.
  3. Step 3: The source is positive.

Answer: The source charge is positive.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Strong field

Problem: Where field lines are closer together, what does that mean?

  1. Step 1: Line spacing represents strength.
  2. Step 2: Closer lines mean more field lines per area.
  3. Step 3: The field is stronger.

Answer: The field is stronger there.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Potential energy

Problem: Why can charge position store energy?

  1. Step 1: A charge in a field can experience force.
  2. Step 2: Moving it changes its position in the field.
  3. Step 3: Position in the field can store electric potential energy.

Answer: Electric potential energy depends on charge position.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Electric field lines are just decoration and do not show force information.

Correct: Electric field lines show direction and relative strength of the electric field.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the charge sign, field direction, circuit path, data value, or device system.
