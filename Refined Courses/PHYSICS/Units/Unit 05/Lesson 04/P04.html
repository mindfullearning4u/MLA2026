Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the electrical evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 5 is about tracking charge, fields, paths, and energy transfer. Strong physics reasoning first points to the charge signs, field arrows, conductor table, circuit path, voltage-current-resistance values, power values, or device input-output model before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 1: Calculate current

Problem: A 12 V circuit has 4 ohms resistance. What is the current?

  1. Step 1: Use I = V / R.
  2. Step 2: Substitute I = 12 / 4.
  3. Step 3: Divide and label amperes.

Answer: Current is 3 A.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Increase resistance

Problem: Voltage stays 12 V while resistance rises from 4 ohms to 6 ohms. What happens to current?

  1. Step 1: Use I = V / R.
  2. Step 2: The denominator becomes larger.
  3. Step 3: Current decreases from 3 A to 2 A.

Answer: Current decreases.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Closed path

Problem: Why does a circuit need a closed path?

  1. Step 1: Charge needs a continuous path.
  2. Step 2: A break stops charge flow.
  3. Step 3: Without current, devices do not operate.

Answer: A closed path allows current to flow.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Increasing resistance increases current when voltage stays the same.

Correct: For the same voltage, increasing resistance decreases current.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the charge sign, field direction, circuit path, data value, or device system.
