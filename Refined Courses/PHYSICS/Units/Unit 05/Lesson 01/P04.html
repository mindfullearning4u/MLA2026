Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the electrical evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 5 is about tracking charge, fields, paths, and energy transfer. A veteran Physics teacher would first point to the charge signs, field arrows, conductor table, circuit path, voltage-current-resistance values, power values, or device input-output model before choosing an answer.

Teacher MoveWhy It Matters

Teacher move: First name the electrical evidence. Then state the rule. Then connect the evidence to the rule in one sentence.

Worked Example 1: Like charges

Problem: Two negatively charged objects are near each other. What happens?

  1. Step 1: Both signs are negative.
  2. Step 2: Like charges repel.
  3. Step 3: The force arrows point away from each other.

Answer: They repel.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Opposite charges

Problem: A positive sphere and negative sphere are near each other. What happens?

  1. Step 1: The signs are opposite.
  2. Step 2: Opposite charges attract.
  3. Step 3: The force arrows point toward each other.

Answer: They attract.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Charging by electrons

Problem: Why does an object become negatively charged after rubbing?

  1. Step 1: Electrons can transfer.
  2. Step 2: Gaining electrons adds negative charge.
  3. Step 3: The object has more negative charge than positive charge.

Answer: It gained electrons.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: All charged objects attract each other.

Correct: Like charges repel and opposite charges attract; the charge signs control the interaction.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the charge sign, field direction, circuit path, data value, or device system.
