Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the electrical evidence, read the labels, and choose the model or rule that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 5 is about tracking charge, fields, paths, and energy transfer. A veteran Physics teacher would first point to the charge signs, field arrows, conductor table, circuit path, voltage-current-resistance values, power values, or device input-output model before choosing an answer.

Worked Example 1: Calculate power

Problem: A device uses 12 V and 2 A. What is its power?

  1. Step 1: Use P = IV.
  2. Step 2: Substitute P = 12 x 2.
  3. Step 3: Multiply and label watts.

Answer: Power is 24 W.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Compare devices

Problem: Two devices use 12 V, but one uses 1 A and one uses 2 A. Which has more power?

  1. Step 1: Voltage is the same.
  2. Step 2: Power depends on current too.
  3. Step 3: 2 A gives 24 W, greater than 12 W.

Answer: The 2 A device has more power.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Energy rate

Problem: What does higher power mean?

  1. Step 1: Power is rate of energy transfer.
  2. Step 2: Higher power means more joules each second.
  3. Step 3: The device uses or transfers energy faster.

Answer: Higher power means faster energy transfer.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the diagram or data, use the correct rule, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Power in a circuit depends only on voltage.

Correct: Circuit power depends on voltage and current, using P = IV.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics visual evidence. The incorrect version skips the charge sign, field direction, circuit path, data value, or device system.
