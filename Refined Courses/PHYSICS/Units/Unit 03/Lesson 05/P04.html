Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 3 is about tracking energy and momentum carefully. First define the system. Next identify the quantity shown in the stimulus: energy form, force, distance, work, time, temperature, particle motion, or before/after momentum. Then choose the correct model or formula.

Worked Example 1: Calculate power

Problem: A machine does 100 J of work in 5 s. What is its power?

  1. Step 1: Write P = W / t. Why: power is work per time.
  2. Step 2: Substitute P = 100 J / 5 s. Why: use given work and time.
  3. Step 3: Divide and label watts. Why: J/s is W.

Answer: Power is 20 W.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Compare same work

Problem: Two machines do 100 J, but one takes 10 s and one takes 5 s. Which has more power?

  1. Step 1: Work is the same. Why: compare time effect.
  2. Step 2: Shorter time means more work per second. Why: power is rate.
  3. Step 3: 5 s machine has 20 W versus 10 W. Why: 100/5 > 100/10.

Answer: The 5 s machine has more power.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Avoid work-power confusion

Problem: Can two systems do the same work with different power?

  1. Step 1: Work is total energy transfer. Why: amount can match.
  2. Step 2: Power depends on time. Why: rate can differ.
  3. Step 3: Same work in less time means higher power.

Answer: Yes, same work can occur at different power.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Power and work mean the same thing.

Correct: Work is energy transferred; power is how fast that transfer happens.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, rate, or conservation relationship.
