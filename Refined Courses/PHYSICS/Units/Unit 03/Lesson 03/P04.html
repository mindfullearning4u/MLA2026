P04 Worked Example

Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 3 is about tracking energy and momentum carefully. First define the system. Next identify the quantity shown in the stimulus: energy form, force, distance, work, time, temperature, particle motion, or before/after momentum. Then choose the correct model or formula.

StepTeacher Slow Walk
1Draw or state the system boundary.
2Identify energy forms inside the boundary.
3Check whether energy crosses the boundary.
4If energy seems lost, ask where it went.
5State transformation or transfer, not disappearance.

Teacher move: A veteran Physics teacher would ask, "What is being tracked, what is the system boundary, and what quantity is given?" before allowing a formula.

Worked Example 1: System boundary

Problem: Why must you define the system before using conservation?

  1. Step 1: Choose what is inside. Why: energy accounting depends on the boundary.
  2. Step 2: Check exchanges with surroundings. Why: energy may leave the chosen system.
  3. Step 3: Then apply conservation. Why: total energy is conserved when all transfers are counted.

Answer: The system boundary tells what energy is being tracked.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Energy loss wording

Problem: A cart slows because of friction. Did energy disappear?

  1. Step 1: Observe slowing: kinetic energy decreases. Why: speed decreases.
  2. Step 2: Identify friction. Why: friction transfers energy to thermal energy.
  3. Step 3: State transformation. Why: energy changes form, not disappears.

Answer: Kinetic energy becomes thermal energy in the cart, track, and surroundings.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Ideal model

Problem: Why does an ideal model keep total mechanical energy constant?

  1. Step 1: Ideal means no energy leaves by friction or air resistance. Why: those transfers are excluded.
  2. Step 2: Potential and kinetic can trade. Why: energy transforms inside the system.
  3. Step 3: Total stays constant in the model. Why: conservation applies to the isolated model.

Answer: In an ideal isolated model, total mechanical energy stays constant.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A slowing object proves energy was destroyed.

Correct: A slowing object shows energy was transferred or transformed, often into thermal energy.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, rate, or conservation relationship.
