Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 3 is about tracking energy and momentum carefully. First define the system. Next identify the quantity shown in the stimulus: energy form, force, distance, work, time, temperature, particle motion, or before/after momentum. Then choose the correct model or formula.

Worked Example 1: Temperature meaning

Problem: What happens to average particle motion when temperature increases?

  1. Step 1: Temperature relates to average kinetic energy. Why: it measures particle motion on average.
  2. Step 2: Increasing temperature means more average kinetic energy. Why: particles move faster on average.
  3. Step 3: State the particle model. Why: microscopic motion explains observable temperature.

Answer: Average particle kinetic energy increases.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Heat direction

Problem: Which way does heat transfer between warm metal and cool water?

  1. Step 1: Identify temperatures. Why: heat transfer depends on temperature difference.
  2. Step 2: Heat moves from warmer to cooler. Why: thermal energy transfers down the temperature difference.
  3. Step 3: State metal to water if metal is warmer.

Answer: Heat transfers from the warmer object to the cooler object.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Matter state model

Problem: Why do gases fill a container?

  1. Step 1: Gas particles are far apart. Why: spacing is large.
  2. Step 2: They move freely. Why: motion is not fixed in place.
  3. Step 3: They spread to available volume. Why: particle model explains gas behavior.

Answer: Gas particles spread out and fill the container.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Temperature and heat are the same thing.

Correct: Temperature measures average particle kinetic energy; heat is energy transferred because of temperature difference.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, rate, or conservation relationship.
