Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 3 is about tracking energy and momentum carefully. First define the system. Next identify the quantity shown in the stimulus: energy form, force, distance, work, time, temperature, particle motion, or before/after momentum. Then choose the correct model or formula.

StepHow To Think
1Define the system as all colliding objects.
2Find total momentum before.
3Find total momentum after.
4Compare totals, not only one object.
5If totals match and no external net force is included, momentum is conserved.

Student reasoning move: Strong physics reasoning asks, "What is being tracked, what is the system boundary, and what quantity is given?" before using a formula.

Worked Example 1: Compare totals

Problem: Is total momentum conserved in the table?

  1. Step 1: Read total before: 6 kg m/s east. Why: use the system total.
  2. Step 2: Read total after: 6 kg m/s east. Why: compare before and after.
  3. Step 3: Totals match. Why: momentum is conserved in the closed-system model.

Answer: Total momentum is conserved.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: One object changes

Problem: Does Cart A losing momentum mean momentum was destroyed?

  1. Step 1: Cart A decreases from 6 to 2. Why: one object changed.
  2. Step 2: Cart B increases from 0 to 4. Why: another object gained momentum.
  3. Step 3: Total remains 6. Why: momentum transferred within the system.

Answer: Momentum was transferred, not destroyed.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: System boundary

Problem: Why include both carts in the system?

  1. Step 1: Collision forces act between the carts. Why: internal forces transfer momentum.
  2. Step 2: One cart alone does not show total conservation. Why: it can gain or lose momentum.
  3. Step 3: Both carts show the total before and after.

Answer: Both carts are needed to check conservation.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Momentum is not conserved if one object slows down.

Correct: Check total system momentum before and after; one object can lose momentum while another gains it.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, rate, or conservation relationship.
