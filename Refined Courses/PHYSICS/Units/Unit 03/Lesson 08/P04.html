Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 3 is about tracking energy and momentum carefully. First define the system. Next identify the quantity shown in the stimulus: energy form, force, distance, work, time, temperature, particle motion, or before/after momentum. Then choose the correct model or formula.

Before SolvingReason
Choose the conceptUnit 3 has several energy tools; do not use the wrong one.
Identify the systemConservation depends on the boundary.
Read the stimulusThe table or model tells which quantity is available.
Explain with evidenceA final number without meaning is not mastery.

Student reasoning move: Strong physics reasoning asks, "What is being tracked, what is the system boundary, and what quantity is given?" before using a formula.

Worked Example 1: Choose the right model

Problem: A question gives force and distance. Which concept fits?

  1. Step 1: Read the quantities: force and distance. Why: those are work variables.
  2. Step 2: Use W = Fd. Why: work is force times distance.
  3. Step 3: Do not use power unless time is given. Why: power is work per time.

Answer: Use work.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Rate question

Problem: A question gives 100 J and 5 s. Which concept fits?

  1. Step 1: Read quantities: work and time. Why: rate uses time.
  2. Step 2: Use P = W/t. Why: power is work per time.
  3. Step 3: Label answer in watts. Why: J/s is W.

Answer: Use power.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Conservation question

Problem: A table shows total momentum before and after. What should you compare?

  1. Step 1: Use totals, not one object. Why: conservation applies to system total.
  2. Step 2: Compare before and after values. Why: equality shows conservation.
  3. Step 3: Explain transfer if individual objects change.

Answer: Compare total system momentum before and after.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use the same formula for every Unit 3 problem.

Correct: Choose the formula or model based on the quantities and stimulus: energy form, transformation, conservation, work, power, thermal model, or momentum.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, rate, or conservation relationship.
