Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 3 is about tracking energy and momentum carefully. First define the system. Next identify the quantity shown in the stimulus: energy form, force, distance, work, time, temperature, particle motion, or before/after momentum. Then choose the correct model or formula.

StepHow To Think
1Identify the energy form before the process.
2Identify the energy form after the process.
3Use evidence such as speed, height, temperature, or light.
4Write the transformation as before -> after.

Student reasoning move: Strong physics reasoning asks, "What is being tracked, what is the system boundary, and what quantity is given?" before using a formula.

Worked Example 1: Ramp transformation

Problem: A skateboarder moves down a ramp and speeds up. What transformation occurs?

  1. Step 1: Before: high position. Why: height indicates gravitational potential energy.
  2. Step 2: After: faster motion. Why: speed indicates kinetic energy.
  3. Step 3: Write potential -> kinetic. Why: stored height energy becomes motion.

Answer: Gravitational potential energy transforms into kinetic energy.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Light transformation

Problem: A flashlight turns battery energy into light and warmth. What outputs occur?

  1. Step 1: Identify input: battery/electrical energy. Why: this starts the process.
  2. Step 2: Identify output: light and warmth. Why: observations show radiant and thermal energy.
  3. Step 3: Write electrical -> light + thermal. Why: energy changes form.

Answer: Electrical energy transforms into light and thermal energy.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Avoid single-output thinking

Problem: Why is a transformation often more than one output?

  1. Step 1: Real systems produce useful and less useful outputs. Why: energy spreads into several forms.
  2. Step 2: A bulb gives light and heat. Why: warmth is thermal evidence.
  3. Step 3: Include all observed outputs. Why: evidence controls the answer.

Answer: Transformations can produce more than one output form.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Energy disappears when it changes form.

Correct: Energy changes form; the evidence shows what forms are present before and after.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, rate, or conservation relationship.
