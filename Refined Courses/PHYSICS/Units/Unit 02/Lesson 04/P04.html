Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the object or system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 2 is about interactions. Slow down before solving. First identify the object or system. Next identify every force, mass, velocity, distance, or interaction in the stimulus. Then choose the correct model: free-body diagram, Newton's law, gravity model, orbit model, momentum table, or force comparison chart.

Worked Example 1: Distance effect

Problem: What happens to gravity when the same masses move farther apart?

  1. Step 1: Hold mass constant. Why: compare one variable.
  2. Step 2: Increase distance. Why: the question changes separation.
  3. Step 3: Gravity decreases. Why: gravitational force weakens with distance.

Answer: Gravity becomes weaker.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Mass effect

Problem: What happens if one mass increases and distance stays the same?

  1. Step 1: Hold distance constant. Why: isolate mass.
  2. Step 2: Increase mass. Why: more matter contributes more attraction.
  3. Step 3: Gravity increases. Why: larger mass means stronger gravitational attraction.

Answer: Gravity becomes stronger.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Use a model

Problem: Which case has strongest gravity: same masses far apart or larger masses near?

  1. Step 1: Compare masses. Larger masses strengthen gravity.
  2. Step 2: Compare distance. Nearer objects strengthen gravity.
  3. Step 3: Both factors favor the larger-near case.

Answer: Larger masses near each other have the strongest relative gravity.

Interpretation: This example shows how a veteran Physics teacher would slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Gravity only works on Earth.

Correct: Gravity acts between all masses, but its strength depends on mass and distance.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, direction, or relationship.
