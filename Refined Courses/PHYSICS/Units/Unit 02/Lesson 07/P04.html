Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the object or system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 2 is about interactions. Slow down before solving. First identify the object or system. Next identify every force, mass, velocity, distance, or interaction in the stimulus. Then choose the correct model: free-body diagram, Newton's law, gravity model, orbit model, momentum table, or force comparison chart.

Student reasoning move: Strong physics reasoning asks, "What object is being analyzed, what evidence is shown, and which law or model fits this evidence?" before using a calculation.

Worked Example 1: Identify force by system

Problem: Which force explains planets orbiting the Sun?

  1. Step 1: Planets and Sun have mass. Why: gravity acts on mass.
  2. Step 2: The distance is large. Why: gravity has long range.
  3. Step 3: Choose gravitational force. Why: it explains orbital attraction.

Answer: Gravity explains planetary orbits.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Identify electromagnetic force

Problem: Which force is most connected to electric circuits?

  1. Step 1: Circuits involve charge movement. Why: charge is the clue.
  2. Step 2: Electromagnetic force acts on charge. Why: electricity and magnetism are electromagnetic.
  3. Step 3: Choose electromagnetic force.

Answer: Electromagnetic force is most connected to circuits.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Avoid scale error

Problem: Should strong nuclear force explain why a dropped ball falls?

  1. Step 1: Dropped ball motion is macroscopic. Why: it is everyday scale.
  2. Step 2: Falling near Earth is due to gravity. Why: mass attracts mass.
  3. Step 3: Strong nuclear force acts at tiny nuclear range.

Answer: Gravity, not strong nuclear force, explains the falling ball.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: All forces work the same way at every scale.

Correct: Different fundamental forces act on different properties and ranges.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, direction, or relationship.
