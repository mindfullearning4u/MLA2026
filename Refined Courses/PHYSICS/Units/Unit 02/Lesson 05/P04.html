Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the object or system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 2 is about interactions. Slow down before solving. First identify the object or system. Next identify every force, mass, velocity, distance, or interaction in the stimulus. Then choose the correct model: free-body diagram, Newton's law, gravity model, orbit model, momentum table, or force comparison chart.

Worked Example 1: Explain an orbit

Problem: Why does the Moon orbit Earth instead of moving straight away?

  1. Step 1: The Moon has forward motion. Why: inertia keeps it moving.
  2. Step 2: Earth's gravity pulls inward. Why: gravity is an attractive force.
  3. Step 3: The combination curves the path. Why: forward motion plus inward pull creates orbit.

Answer: The Moon orbits because gravity changes its direction while it keeps moving forward.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Identify inward force

Problem: What provides the inward force for Earth's orbit around the Sun?

  1. Step 1: Choose the system: Earth-Sun. Why: identify interacting objects.
  2. Step 2: Gravity is attractive. Why: masses pull on each other.
  3. Step 3: The Sun's gravity pulls Earth inward. Why: this bends Earth's path.

Answer: The Sun's gravity provides the inward force.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Avoid the falling misconception

Problem: Is the Moon falling toward Earth?

  1. Step 1: Gravity accelerates Moon inward. Why: inward force changes direction.
  2. Step 2: Moon also moves forward. Why: tangential motion prevents straight fall.
  3. Step 3: The path curves continuously. Why: orbit is ongoing free-fall around Earth.

Answer: The Moon is in orbital motion, not a straight fall to Earth.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Orbit happens because there is no gravity in space.

Correct: Orbit happens because gravity pulls inward while the object keeps moving forward.

Teachable Explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, direction, or relationship.
