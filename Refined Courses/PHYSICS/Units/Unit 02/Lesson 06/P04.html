Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the object or system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 2 is about interactions. Slow down before solving. First identify the object or system. Next identify every force, mass, velocity, distance, or interaction in the stimulus. Then choose the correct model: free-body diagram, Newton's law, gravity model, orbit model, momentum table, or force comparison chart.

Worked Example 1: Calculate momentum

Problem: A 2 kg cart moves 3 m/s east. What is its momentum?

  1. Step 1: Write p = mv. Why: momentum is mass times velocity.
  2. Step 2: Substitute p = 2 kg x 3 m/s. Why: use mass and velocity.
  3. Step 3: Multiply and keep direction. Why: momentum is a vector when velocity has direction.

Answer: Momentum is 6 kg m/s east.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Compare momentum

Problem: Which has more momentum: 2 kg at 3 m/s or 4 kg at 1 m/s?

  1. Step 1: Calculate first: 2 x 3 = 6. Why: compare using momentum.
  2. Step 2: Calculate second: 4 x 1 = 4. Why: larger mass alone is not enough.
  3. Step 3: Compare 6 and 4. Why: 6 is greater.

Answer: The 2 kg cart at 3 m/s has more momentum.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Collision interaction

Problem: Why do both carts feel forces during a collision?

  1. Step 1: A collision is an interaction. Why: two objects affect each other.
  2. Step 2: Each cart exerts force on the other. Why: third-law pairs occur.
  3. Step 3: Forces can change momentum. Why: impulse changes motion.

Answer: Both carts exert forces that can change momentum.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The heavier object always has more momentum.

Correct: Momentum depends on both mass and velocity.
