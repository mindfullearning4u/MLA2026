Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the object or system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 2 is about interactions. Slow down before solving. First identify the object or system. Next identify every force, mass, velocity, distance, or interaction in the stimulus. Then choose the correct model: free-body diagram, Newton's law, gravity model, orbit model, momentum table, or force comparison chart.

Student reasoning move: Strong physics reasoning asks, "What object is being analyzed, what evidence is shown, and which law or model fits this evidence?" before using a calculation.

Worked Example 1: Choose the right tool

Problem: A question gives mass and velocity. Which concept should you use?

  1. Step 1: Read the quantities. Why: mass and velocity are clues.
  2. Step 2: Connect to formula p = mv. Why: momentum uses mass and velocity.
  3. Step 3: Use momentum, not F = ma. Why: acceleration is not provided.

Answer: Use momentum.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Analyze net force

Problem: A box has 12 N right and 5 N left. What concept applies?

  1. Step 1: Forces are listed on one object. Why: this is a free-body/net-force problem.
  2. Step 2: Subtract opposite directions. Why: opposite forces partially cancel.
  3. Step 3: Use Newton's laws to interpret motion change.

Answer: Use net force and Newton's laws.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Connect gravity and orbit

Problem: A model shows forward motion plus inward pull. What system is being explained?

  1. Step 1: Forward motion is inertia. Why: object continues moving.
  2. Step 2: Inward pull is gravity. Why: mass attracts mass.
  3. Step 3: Together they create orbital motion.

Answer: The model explains an orbit.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Use the same formula for every Unit 2 problem.

Correct: Choose the formula or model based on the quantities and stimulus in the question.
