Before the Worked Examples

Do not start by hunting for an answer choice. Set up the physics first. Identify the object or system, read the given stimulus, and choose the model or formula that belongs to that evidence.

Physics Unit 2 is about interactions. Slow down before solving. First identify the object or system. Next identify every force, mass, velocity, distance, or interaction in the stimulus. Then choose the correct model: free-body diagram, Newton's law, gravity model, orbit model, momentum table, or force comparison chart.

StepStudent Reasoning Slow Walk
1Choose one object to analyze. Do not mix forces on different objects.
2List every force acting on that object.
3Assign direction to each force.
4Combine opposite directions by subtracting smaller from larger.
5Use the net force to decide whether motion changes.

Student reasoning move: Strong physics reasoning asks, "What object is being analyzed, what evidence is shown, and which law or model fits this evidence?" before using a calculation.

Worked Example 1: Find net force

Problem: A cart has 12 N right and 5 N left. What is the net force?

  1. Step 1: Choose the object: the cart. Why: a free-body diagram focuses on one object.
  2. Step 2: Subtract opposite forces: 12 N - 5 N = 7 N. Why: opposite directions partially cancel.
  3. Step 3: Keep direction: right. Why: the larger force is to the right.

Answer: The net force is 7 N right.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 2: Balanced or unbalanced

Problem: A box has 8 N left and 8 N right. Are the forces balanced?

  1. Step 1: Compare magnitudes. Why: equal opposite forces cancel.
  2. Step 2: Directions are opposite. Why: this allows cancellation.
  3. Step 3: Net force is 0 N. Why: 8 N - 8 N = 0 N.

Answer: The forces are balanced.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Worked Example 3: Interpret motion change

Problem: Why can a nonzero net force change motion?

  1. Step 1: Net force is the total unbalanced push or pull. Why: it shows what is not canceled.
  2. Step 2: Unbalanced force changes velocity. Why: force causes acceleration.
  3. Step 3: Direction of change follows net force. Why: the larger force sets the direction.

Answer: A nonzero net force causes acceleration in the net-force direction.

Interpretation: This example shows how to slow down, identify the system, use the diagram/table/formula, and explain the result.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The biggest force always belongs in the answer even if it acts on another object.

Correct: Analyze forces acting on one object, then find the net force on that object.

Teachable explanation: The correct version uses the mapped Physics evidence. The incorrect version skips the system, model, unit, direction, or relationship.
