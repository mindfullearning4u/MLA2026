## Course Design

| Unit | Unit Title | Core Architecture Focus |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Scientific Thinking, Measurement, and Motion | Inquiry, lab practice, data, vectors, position, velocity, acceleration. |
| 2 | Forces and Newtonian Mechanics | Newton's laws, gravitational force, orbital relationships, momentum, and fundamental forces. |
| 3 | Work, Energy, Momentum, and Thermal Systems | Energy forms, conservation, work, power, heat transfer, temperature, and system reasoning. |
| 4 | Waves, Sound, Light, and Optics | Wave properties, sound, electromagnetic waves, light speed, frequency shifts, lenses, and mirrors. |
| 5 | Electricity, Magnetism, and Matter | Static charge, fields, current, voltage, resistance, power, materials, circuits, and electromagnetism applications. |
| 6 | Modern, Earth/Space, and Applied Physics Systems | Atomic models, matter in the universe, relativity concepts, physical principles in systems, and physics-informed decisions. |

## Lesson Pattern

Each unit contains eight mapped lessons. Lessons 1-7 introduce and develop primary standards. Lesson 8 is titled `Putting It All Together`, synthesizes standards already taught in that unit, and carries Unit Assessment evidence.

No Lesson 8 introduces a new primary standard.

## Readiness Alignment

Physics architecture includes Common Core literacy alignment, ACT Science alignment, SAT science reading/data alignment, college readiness, workforce readiness, quantitative reasoning, lab science expectations, virtual lab/data expectations, and scientific literacy.
