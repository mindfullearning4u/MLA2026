Claim–Evidence–Reasoning: Position vs. Time Graphs

Open Position vs. Time Graphs CER

Question investigated: How does the slope of a position-versus-time graph relate to how quickly an object reaches a destination?

1. Build the first argument

  1. Claim: write one sentence answering the question. Use the terms slope, velocity, and time correctly.
  2. Evidence: provide at least two specific observations or data points. Include coordinates or calculated slopes from two position-time lines when available.
  3. Reasoning: explain how slope = Δd/Δt connects the evidence to the claim and why a steeper line represents a different velocity.

2. Critique the argument

Because this is an asynchronous course, use the worksheet criteria for a structured self-critique unless your TOR assigns a peer reviewer. Identify at least two weaknesses in the first draft—such as an unsupported claim, vague evidence, missing units, incorrect slope reasoning, or an ignored alternative explanation—and state how each should be corrected.

3. Evaluate and revise

  1. Revise the Claim: write only the improved claim and use the most accurate physics vocabulary.
  2. Reassess the Evidence: give at least two pieces of evidence for the new claim; add missed evidence or reinterpret earlier evidence when needed.
  3. Reinforce the Reasoning: provide a detailed explanation using definitions, slope calculations, units, and scientific knowledge; address an alternative explanation if applicable.
Evidence checklist: first claim; at least two evidence statements; first reasoning paragraph; two-item critique; revised claim; at least two revised evidence statements; and revised reasoning paragraph.

Submit: include all three stages in Notebook Evidence Submission — U1 L1. Do not submit only the final paragraph.
