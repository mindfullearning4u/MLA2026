# Creative Writing Final Architecture Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit reviewed the full Creative Writing curriculum architecture package before lesson production. The audit compared the package against the approved English I, English II, English III, English IV, and Speech Communication architecture frameworks; verified the applicable Florida course standards through the CPALMS public course record for `Creative Writing 1 (#1009320)`; validated MLA Creative Writing standard numbering and domain assignment; checked crosswalk, unit-level mapping, and lesson-level mapping coverage; audited all 48 lesson mapping rows; and confirmed that the course preserves the required six-unit, eight-lesson structure.

Initial audit status: `PASS`

Final audit status: `PASS`

The package uses the verified CPALMS Creative Writing 1 standard list as the primary source. CPALMS lists 31 standards for the current Creative Writing 1 course version. The course identity is correctly framed as a writing-production course focused on creative expression in literary forms, genre modeling, narrative craft, fiction, poetry, revision, publication, peer feedback, and portfolio development. Creative Writing 2 was reviewed only as a progression reference and was not used to replace the Creative Writing 1 benchmark wording.

## 3. Strengths

- The architecture preserves the approved MLA mastery model.
- The package uses an official CPALMS course record rather than assuming a generic creative writing standard set.
- All 31 CPALMS-listed standards are included exactly once in the standard inventory and crosswalk.
- Creative Writing-specific domains make the course easier to audit while preserving MLA numbering rules.
- Literary benchmarks are mapped as craft-modeling supports for creative writing production.
- The unit sequence moves from writing foundations to narrative, fiction, poetry, revision, publication, and author portfolio synthesis.
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together in every unit.
- Lesson 8 contains Unit Assessment only and introduces no new primary standards.

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Creative Writing 1 includes literary reading benchmarks that could pull the course toward English Literature. | Medium | Architecture explicitly frames literary benchmarks as craft-modeling supports for writing production. |
| Unit 2 can become overloaded because narrative technique, point of view, style, and pacing converge. | Medium | Seven-lesson sequence separates narrative techniques, point of view, key elements, layers of style, narrator effects, and pacing. |
| Unit 3 can become overloaded because fiction, theme, adaptation, and perspective are dense. | Medium | Unit 3 uses dedicated progression from theme to adaptation, support, purpose, rhetoric, and perspective. |
| Unit 4 can become too narrow if poetry is treated only as figurative language. | Low | Poetry mapping includes ambiguity, layers of meaning, mood, diction, connotation, denotation, and style. |
| Unit 5 can become product-heavy if revision and publication are treated as separate projects. | Medium | Mapping ties revision, workshop, format, digital collaboration, and publication to prior writing products. |
| Unit 6 could drift into new instruction. | Low | Unit 6 uses previously taught standards only and functions as synthesis and transfer. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

These recommendations do not block certification.

- During future lesson production, build a genre/product bank before drafting lessons so narrative, fiction, poetry, revision, and publication tasks remain distinct.
- Maintain a craft-model text audit before production so literary benchmarks support writing craft rather than becoming literature-analysis endpoints.
- Preserve the Creative Writing 1 to Creative Writing 2 progression by keeping Creative Writing foundational while preparing students for continued development and more advanced narrative expectations.
- Keep peer feedback and publication controls attached to actual writing products during production.
