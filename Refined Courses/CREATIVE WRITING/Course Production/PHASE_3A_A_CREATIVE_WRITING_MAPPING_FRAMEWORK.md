# Phase 3A-A: Creative Writing Mapping Framework

## Executive Summary

This mapping framework defines how Creative Writing standards are distributed across the six-unit, eight-lesson MLA production structure. It preserves the English I-IV and Speech Communication architecture format while adapting the progression to writing craft, narrative production, fiction, poetry, revision, publication, peer feedback, and portfolio synthesis.

This document is mapping only. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, GIFT files, quiz JSON files, lesson JSON files, LMS pages, or student-facing instructional content.

## Mapping Principles

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| Standards-first architecture | All unit and lesson placements derive from the verified CPALMS Creative Writing 1 standard list. |
| Writing-production identity | Literary analysis benchmarks are used as craft models for student writing, not as a literature-course center. |
| Mastery model | Standards are introduced, practiced, reinforced, and synthesized across units. |
| Lesson 8 control | Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together, contains Unit Assessment only, and introduces no new primary standard. |
| Publication pathway | The course progresses from drafting and craft study to revision, workshop, portfolio, and publication-ready work. |

## Unit Progression Model

| Unit | Unit Title | Primary Domains | Progression Function |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Creative Writing, Voice, and Craft | WR, COM | Establishes writer identity, voice/tone, craft vocabulary, model reading, evidence-based craft talk, and workshop norms. |
| 2 | Narrative Craft, Point of View, Character, and Setting | NAR, FIC, WR | Builds narrative techniques, point of view, pacing, tension, mood, tone, and key fictional elements. |
| 3 | Fiction, Theme, Adaptation, and World Building | FIC, WR, NAR | Develops fiction through universal themes, adaptation, coming-of-age patterns, conflicting perspectives, and source transformation. |
| 4 | Poetry, Figurative Language, Mood, and Style | POE, WR, COM | Focuses on figurative language, mood, ambiguity, layers of meaning, and poetic style. |
| 5 | Revision, Workshop, Portfolio, and Publication | REV, PUB, COM | Builds revision through feedback, standard English, format rules, online collaboration, and publication-ready writing. |
| 6 | Author Portfolio, Presentation, and Publication Synthesis | PUB, COM, REV, WR | Synthesizes the full course through portfolio, author statement, presentation, and publication planning. |

## Workload Controls

| Risk Area | Control |
|---|---|
| Literary analysis drift | Each literary benchmark is mapped as craft modeling for creative production. |
| Unit 2 narrative overload | Narrative technique, point of view, pacing, setting, and style are sequenced across seven lessons. |
| Unit 3 fiction overload | Theme, adaptation, coming-of-age, and perspective are treated as fiction-development tools. |
| Unit 4 poetry overload | Poetry standards are protected in a dedicated unit instead of being compressed into general style lessons. |
| Unit 5 production overload | Revision, workshop, and publication are tied to prior writing products. |
