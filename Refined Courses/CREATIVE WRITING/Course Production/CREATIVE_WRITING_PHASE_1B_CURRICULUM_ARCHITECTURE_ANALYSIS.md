# Phase 1B: Creative Writing Curriculum Architecture Analysis

## Executive Summary

This architecture analysis establishes the Creative Writing course framework using the approved English I-IV and Speech Communication production model and the verified CPALMS `Creative Writing 1 (#1009320)` standards record as the primary standards source.

The course preserves the MLA mastery model:

- 6 units
- 8 lessons per unit
- Lessons 1-7 contain Lesson Quiz
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together
- Lesson 8 contains Unit Assessment
- Lesson 8 introduces no new primary standard

This document is architecture only. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, GIFT files, quiz JSON files, lesson JSON files, LMS pages, or student-facing instructional content.

## Course Identity

Creative Writing is a writing-production course. The verified CPALMS course description supports instruction in:

- developing writing and language skills for creative expression
- producing creative work in a variety of literary forms
- studying and modeling genres
- developing craft through literary models
- revising for clarity, cohesion, voice, tone, and audience
- preparing writing for publication-ready formats

The architecture is not an English literature course, Journalism course, Speech course, or Technical Writing course. Literary analysis benchmarks are used as craft-modeling supports for student creative production.

## Six-Unit Architecture

| Unit | Unit Title | Primary Architecture Purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Creative Writing, Voice, and Craft | Establishes creative writing identity, craft vocabulary, voice/tone, literary models, collaboration, and feedback routines. |
| 2 | Narrative Craft, Point of View, Character, and Setting | Builds narrative techniques, point of view, key elements, style, perspective, and creative scene development. |
| 3 | Fiction, Theme, Adaptation, and World Building | Develops fiction craft through theme, adaptation, coming-of-age patterns, conflicting perspectives, and extended fictional development. |
| 4 | Poetry, Figurative Language, Mood, and Style | Focuses on poetic ambiguity, figurative language, mood, layers of meaning, and style. |
| 5 | Revision, Workshop, Portfolio, and Publication | Builds feedback-based revision, conventions, format-specific quality, online collaboration, and publication-ready creative work. |
| 6 | Author Portfolio, Presentation, and Publication Synthesis | Synthesizes the full creative writing process through portfolio, author statement, presentation, and publication planning without new primary standards. |
