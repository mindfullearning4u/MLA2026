P04 Worked Example

Each example shows literal meaning, implied meaning, evidence, and writing transfer.

Worked Example 1: Layered Object

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Passage: Sari wore the medal under her shirt, even after the ribbon had frayed.

Step 1: Literal meaning: Sari keeps wearing an old medal.

Step 2: Evidence: The medal is hidden under her shirt, and the ribbon is frayed.

Step 3: Implied meaning: The medal may represent private pride, memory, or a past achievement she cannot let go.

Step 4: Transfer: Give a character an object that is physically worn down but emotionally important.

Worked Example 2: Pattern

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Passage: Each morning, Mateo erased the calendar square before breakfast. On the last day, he left the marker uncapped beside the blank page.

Step 1: Literal meaning: Mateo has been erasing calendar squares, then stops.

Step 2: Evidence: The repeated erasing creates a pattern, and the uncapped marker marks a change.

Step 3: Implied meaning: Mateo may be counting down to something difficult, and the final blank page suggests uncertainty or refusal to continue.

Step 4: Transfer: Repeat one action across a scene or draft, then change the action at an important moment.

Worked Example 3: Contrast

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Passage: The graduation banner stretched across the living room, but the suitcase by the stairs was already packed.

Step 1: Literal meaning: There is a celebration banner and a packed suitcase.

Step 2: Evidence: The banner suggests celebration, while the suitcase suggests leaving.

Step 3: Implied meaning: The contrast creates a bittersweet style because success and departure are happening together.

Step 4: Transfer: Place two details with different emotional meanings in the same scene.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Making every detail symbolic so the scene becomes confusing.

This is incorrect because readers still need a clear literal scene. Layered meaning works best when the surface action is understandable.

Correct: Keep the literal scene clear, then choose one or two details that also suggest deeper meaning through repetition, contrast, or placement.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
