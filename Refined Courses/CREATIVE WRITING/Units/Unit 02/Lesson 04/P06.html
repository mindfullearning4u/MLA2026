Checkpoint: Advanced Layers of Meaning and Style

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply advanced layers of meaning and style in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “Inventory”

One umbrella, broken at the hinge. Two mugs, neither matching. Four envelopes addressed but unstamped. Mara wrote each item on the moving-company form. Under “Reason for relocation,” she drew a line so carefully that it became a border.

Term support: A motif is a recurring element that develops meaning. Juxtaposition places elements together so their contrast or relationship becomes significant.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Analyze how the inventory form creates both literal and implied meaning. Use two exact details.
  2. Explain how the list structure, numerical pattern, and final image contribute to style and layered interpretation.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Add a sentence explaining exactly why Mara is moving and what every object symbolizes.
B. Strengthen the pattern of selected objects and let their relationship support more than one reasonable interpretation.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 375–475 word narrative or prose poem that uses an unconventional structure—such as a list, set of instructions, messages, or records—to tell a layered story.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—establish the structural pattern and a concrete situation.
  2. Development—repeat or vary at least three related details so literal information gains implied meaning.
  3. Turning point—break or transform the established pattern at a consequential moment.
  4. Closing—return to one earlier element with changed meaning and create a resonant final effect.

4. Revise and Explain

State two supported interpretations of your piece. Revise one structural or language choice so both remain possible but the evidence becomes stronger.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
