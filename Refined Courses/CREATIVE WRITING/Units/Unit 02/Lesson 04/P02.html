Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
literal meaningThe direct, surface meaning of words, actions, or events.The character closes the shop and walks home alone.
implicationA meaning suggested by evidence rather than directly stated.Closing early suggests she expects trouble, though the text never says so.
subtextThe unstated tension, motive, or meaning beneath dialogue and action.“Of course I trust you,” he says while locking the drawer.
symbolA concrete element that retains its literal role while gaining additional meaning through pattern and context.The ordinary key later represents control and secrecy.
resonanceThe extended emotional or intellectual significance a detail gathers across a work.Each appearance of the key deepens its emotional significance.
ambiguityA deliberate openness that permits more than one evidence-based interpretation.The open ending supports both hope and suspicion.
motifA recurring image, object, phrase, or situation that develops significance through repetition.A recurring train whistle marks moments when the character considers leaving.
juxtapositionPlacing contrasting elements near each other to sharpen difference or reveal a relationship.A wedding photograph is placed beside an unsigned separation form.
dictionA writer’s purposeful word choices and their levels of formality, precision, and connotation.Choosing “staggered” instead of “walked” implies exhaustion.
syntaxThe arrangement and rhythm of words, phrases, and clauses in sentences.A long sentence of memories ends with the fragment “Too late.”
connotationThe emotional or cultural associations a word carries beyond its dictionary meaning.“Home” carries associations of safety that the empty house complicates.
understatementDeliberately presenting something as less intense or important to create emphasis, irony, or restraint.Calling the disaster “a small inconvenience” creates restrained irony.
