Draft for Workshop: “The Trophy Shelf”

Every trophy in the hallway faced forward except one. Its brass runner looked toward the wall, and dust outlined the place where it had once stood. Coach Bell passed it each morning without turning it back. On Friday, a new student stopped beside the shelf and read the scratched name on its base. Coach Bell opened the gym door and waited.

1. Read the Layers

Complete three rows tracing literal action, implied meaning, and the stylistic choice that creates the layer.

Record three supported layers.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice better connects evidence, craft, and effect. The answer is not revealed here.

A. The turned trophy proves Coach Bell is ashamed of the runner and refuses to discuss the past.

B. The turned trophy and untouched dust suggest deliberate avoidance, while the coach’s final waiting action complicates total rejection by leaving a conversation possible.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a seven-to-nine-sentence scene with a literal action, a recurring object, and subtext that allows two defensible readings. Use a consistent style whose diction and sentence patterns support the intended tone.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one line to strengthen a second layer without stating the hidden meaning. Explain what interpretation the revision supports, what alternative remains possible, and how the wording fits the style.
