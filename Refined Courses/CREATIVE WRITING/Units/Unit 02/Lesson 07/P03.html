Direct Teaching: The Integrated Scene System

Eight-Step Scene Method

  1. Name the objective.
  2. Choose viewpoint and access limits.
  3. Make setting apply pressure.
  4. Arrange beats: action → reaction → choice.
  5. Plant one motif or setup.
  6. Control time around the turn.
  7. End with consequence and value shift.
  8. Audit every technique for contribution.

Pause and Choose: Make Setting Apply Pressure

Mara wants to inspect a hidden ledger before a storm alarm closes the archive. Which version makes setting alter her available choices?

A. “The archive had tall windows and many shelves. Mara admired the room before opening the ledger.”
Not the best choice. The description establishes appearance but does not restrict time, access, or action. The setting remains background decoration.
B. “The storm shutters began descending as Mara opened the ledger; she had time to photograph one page or carry the book outside.”
✓ Correct—the environment creates a decision. The closing shutters impose a deadline and force competing options. Setting now produces pressure that shapes the scene’s next beat.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Pause and Choose: End with a Consequence

After Mara offers to document the ledger instead of taking it, which ending turns her choice into a meaningful value shift?

A. “The shutters closed. It was a dramatic ending.”
Not the best choice. The physical event ends the scene, but the narration does not show how Mara’s choice changes knowledge, relationship, or value.
B. “As the shutters sealed, the archivist handed Mara the camera—trust replacing the permission she had tried to force.”
✓ Correct—the consequence completes the causal chain. Mara’s documented alternative changes the relationship from conflict to limited trust, so the ending grows from her decision rather than merely stopping the action.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Common Failure

A scene can contain beautiful description, dialogue, and conflict yet remain inert. If no choice changes pressure or relationship, revise the beat structure before polishing sentences.

Pause and Try: Build the Causal Chain

Before opening the response, place these beats in the strongest order: the alarm sounds; Mara wants the ledger; Mara offers to document it; the seed falls out; the shutters close. Ask yourself: Which event changes the next choice?

Check your thinking

Strong order: Mara wants the ledger → the seed falls out → the alarm sounds → Mara offers to document it → the shutters close. The seed supplies new evidence, the alarm increases pressure, and the offer becomes a reasoned response. That cause-and-effect chain gives the scene causal coherence.
