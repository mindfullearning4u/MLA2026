Step 4: Draft Around the Turning Point

A turning point is the moment when something changes: a character decides, discovers, reveals, refuses, notices, or understands. Build the scene around that moment.

Example turning point: The narrator decides not to open the envelope yet.

If the turning point is important, slow it down with action, thought, image, or silence.

Step 5: Use the Alignment Check

After drafting, check each craft choice.

Question 1: Does the point of view stay clear?

Question 2: Does the key element add meaning or style?

Question 3: Does the pacing create the intended reader effect?

Question 4: Does the narrator effect support the scene purpose?

If a choice does not support the purpose, revise it or remove it.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Draft a 6-8 sentence scene from your plan.

Part B: Underline the turning point.

Part C: Label one point-of-view choice, one key element, and one pacing choice.

Part D: Explain how those three choices work together.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Adding every Unit 2 skill randomly so the scene feels crowded.

This is incorrect because integration means craft choices support the same purpose, not that every sentence must show a different skill.

Correct: Choose a few craft moves that work together. For example, first person point of view, a repeated envelope detail, and slowed pacing can all support a scene about hesitation.

Need help? Ask your TOR if your scene uses many techniques but you cannot explain why they belong together.
