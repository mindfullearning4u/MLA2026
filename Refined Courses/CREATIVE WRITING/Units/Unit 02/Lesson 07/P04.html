Worked Example: Three Integrated Scene Models

Example 1 — Setting Creates the Choice

Setup: During the final bus departure, Ren wants an apology from his brother.

  1. Objective: obtain an honest answer.
  2. Pressure: the bus door will close in sixty seconds.
  3. Viewpoint: Ren notices avoidance but cannot know motive.
  4. Beats: question → joke → driver warning → Ren blocks the step.
  5. Turn: the brother hands back their mother’s key.
  6. Value shift: accusation becomes alarm.
  7. Payoff: the closing door makes delay consequential.

Example 2 — Subtext and Motif

Line: “Your tea is getting cold,” Aya says three times while her roommate packs.

  1. Literal action: warning about tea.
  2. Subtext: request to stay.
  3. Motif: cooling tea measures lost opportunity.
  4. Beat variation: offer → reminder → silent disposal.
  5. Turn: the roommate drinks it cold.
  6. Meaning: acceptance does not erase departure.
  7. Control: nobody explains the metaphor.

Example 3 — Pacing the Turn

Weak: “Nico opened the file and learned his signature was forged.”

  1. Compress routine searching.
  2. Pause on the familiar signature shape.
  3. Use close focalization as Nico compares the unusual final stroke.
  4. Delay certainty with one plausible explanation.
  5. Turn when a dated receipt disproves it.
  6. End on his choice to copy the file rather than confront anyone.
  7. Result: discovery produces forward consequence.
