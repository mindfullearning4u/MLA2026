The Seed Ledger

The community greenhouse was closing at six when Sol arrived to retrieve his late mother’s seed ledger. A ledger is a book used to record organized information. The director, Ms. Okafor, held it beside a box marked ARCHIVE.

“Copies will be available next month,” she said.

Sol needed the original because his mother had pressed seeds between its pages. Rain struck the glass roof harder. In ten minutes, the building’s flood shutters would seal automatically.

He asked again. Ms. Okafor tightened the archive tape. “Your mother donated records, not private property.”

A pale seed slipped from the ledger and landed between them. Sol recognized the silverleaf vine, extinct in the neighborhood after the river project. Ms. Okafor did too.

The shutter alarm began. Sol could take the ledger while she watched the control panel, but theft would make its research unusable. Instead he photographed the seed and offered to document every pressed specimen before the archive transfer.

Ms. Okafor cut the tape. “Five minutes,” she said.

When the shutters closed, the ledger remained in the archive box. The silverleaf seed rested inside a labeled envelope bearing both their names.
