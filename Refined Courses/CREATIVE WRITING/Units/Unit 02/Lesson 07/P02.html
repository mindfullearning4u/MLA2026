Notebook Title

Lesson 7: Integrated Narrative Scene Development

Vocabulary

  • Integrated scene: A focused story moment where multiple craft choices work together.
  • Scene purpose: The reason the scene exists in the narrative.
  • Reader effect: What the writer wants the reader to feel, notice, infer, or question.
  • Craft alignment: The way point of view, pacing, details, and tone support the same purpose.
  • Revision target: The specific part of a draft the writer chooses to improve.

Step 1: Choose the Scene Purpose

Before writing, decide what the scene must accomplish. A scene may reveal a secret, force a choice, show a relationship, increase tension, or change the reader's understanding of a character.

Weak purpose: Something happens.

Stronger purpose: The scene shows a character deciding whether to admit the truth.

The stronger purpose gives you a target for craft choices.

Step 2: Choose Point of View and Narrator Effect

Decide who tells the scene and how close the reader should feel. First person can feel immediate and personal. Third person limited can stay close to one character while giving a little distance. A narrator can be reliable, uncertain, ironic, or limited by a blind spot.

Planning sentence: I will use first person so the reader feels close to the narrator's hesitation.

Step 3: Choose Key Elements and Pacing

Choose one key element that carries meaning, such as an object, setting detail, repeated action, or contrast. Then choose where to slow down. Important moments usually need expansion. Routine actions can be compressed.

Planning sentence: I will use an unopened envelope as the object detail, and I will slow down the moment before the narrator opens it.
