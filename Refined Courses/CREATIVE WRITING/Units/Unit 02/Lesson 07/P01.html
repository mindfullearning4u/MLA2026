P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 7: Integrated Narrative Scene Development

In this lesson, you will combine Unit 2 skills into one controlled narrative scene. You will not practice isolated craft moves only. You will build a scene with narrative techniques, clear point of view, key elements, narrator effect, pacing, tension, mood, and tone working together.

Standards Covered

Primary Standards: MLA.CW.NAR.01 - Narrative Techniques and Point of View; MLA.CW.NAR.02 - Pace, Tension, Mood, and Tone.

Support Standards: MLA.CW.FIC.01 - Key Elements, Meaning, and Style; MLA.CW.FIC.03 - Narrator Perspective, Irony, and Satire; MLA.CW.FIC.05 - Advanced Key Elements and Style.

What You Will Learn

  • How to plan one scene before drafting it.
  • How to choose a point of view and keep it stable.
  • How to combine key elements, pacing, narrator perspective, and mood.
  • How to revise a scene so each craft choice does a clear job.

What You Will Do

  • Plan a scene using a step-by-step craft checklist.
  • Study an integrated model scene.
  • Draft a scene with a clear narrator and intended effect.
  • Revise for pacing, key elements, and reader understanding.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook shows a complete scene plan.
  • Your scene uses clear point of view and at least three narrative techniques.
  • Your scene uses pacing to create tension, mood, or tone.
  • Your quiz responses show at least 80% mastery of integrated narrative scene development.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

Unit 2 in student language: I can write a narrative scene that uses techniques, point of view, style, perspective, and pacing to shape the reader's experience.

Integration means the craft choices work together. A good scene is not a checklist pasted onto a paragraph. It is a focused moment where each choice helps the reader understand the character, conflict, and effect.

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help: Ask your TOR for help if your scene has many craft choices but they do not work together toward one reader effect.
