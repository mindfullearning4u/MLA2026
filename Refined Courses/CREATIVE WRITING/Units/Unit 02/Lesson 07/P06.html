Checkpoint: Integrated Narrative Scene Development

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply integrated narrative scene development in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Borrowed Room”

At midnight, Dev entered the apartment he had promised never to visit again. The furniture was covered, but a kettle whispered on the stove. On the table lay his childhood compass beside a note in handwriting he did not recognize: NORTH IS NOT WHERE YOU LEFT IT.

Term support: An integrated scene coordinates character, setting, conflict, point of view, pacing, tone, and theme so the parts reinforce one another.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Analyze how at least three narrative elements interact to create the central tension. Use exact evidence.
  2. Develop two supported interpretations of the compass or note and explain what additional detail would favor each.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Explain Dev’s entire history before he enters so the reader knows why every object matters.
B. Reveal necessary history through his selective reactions, connected details, and choices while the scene moves forward.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a polished 500–650 word scene using the source or an equally complex original premise. Integrate character, setting, conflict, controlled point of view, pacing, tone, and implied meaning.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—use an immediate hook and establish viewpoint, setting, and a consequential question.
  2. Development—connect character goal, setting details, and conflict; regulate information and pace.
  3. Turning point—reframe an earlier detail and force a choice that reveals character.
  4. Closing—show the consequence, return to a significant image with changed meaning, and imply a larger theme.
  5. Craft note—write 100–150 words explaining how three elements work together rather than separately.

4. Revise and Explain

Complete one structural revision and one language revision. Cite both changes and explain how they strengthen the scene as a whole.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.

80% Mastery Requirement
