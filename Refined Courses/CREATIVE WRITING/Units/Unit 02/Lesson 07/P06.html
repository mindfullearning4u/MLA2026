The Red Mitten

At the last train, Emi found a child’s red mitten on the empty platform. Her train doors were already open. Across the tracks, a father searched beneath a bench while a small girl cried.

Emi raised the mitten, but the announcement swallowed her voice. The pedestrian bridge was two flights up. Missing this train meant missing her nursing interview.

The warning bell sounded. Emi placed one foot inside the train, then saw the girl remove her other mitten and set it carefully on the bench—as if making the pair easier to find.

Emi stepped back. The doors closed.

She climbed the bridge. When she reached the opposite platform, the father had found the mitten on the bench but not the one in her hand. The girl looked at Emi and laughed.

Analysis

  1. Write an 80–100 word summary.
  2. Map objective, pressure, beats, turn, consequence, and value shift.
  3. Create Emi’s access map.
  4. Explain how setting produces stakes.
  5. Analyze the paired-mitten motif.
  6. Explain the subtext of the girl’s action.
  7. Graph tension by paragraph.
  8. Explain why the ending is a payoff.

Revision and Transfer

  1. Rewrite one beat from the father’s viewpoint.
  2. Change the pacing of the warning-bell moment and explain the effect.
  3. Write an alternate consequence that preserves Emi’s choice.
  4. Write a 320–380 word integrated scene with an annotated blueprint, viewpoint, setting pressure, subtext, motif, turn, and payoff.
