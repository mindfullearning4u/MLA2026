P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 1: Narrative Techniques and Transitions

In this lesson, you begin Unit 2 by learning how narrative writers move a reader through a story. You will study how action, dialogue, description, reflection, and transitions work together so a scene does not feel like a list of disconnected events.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.NAR.01 - Narrative Techniques and Point of View.

Support Standards: MLA.CW.WR.05 - Academic and Craft Vocabulary; MLA.CW.REV.01 - Revision Through Feedback.

What You Will Learn

  • How a narrative technique helps a story show events instead of only reporting them.
  • How action, dialogue, description, and reflection each do a different job in a scene.
  • How transitions help readers understand time, place, sequence, cause, and contrast.
  • How to revise a weak event list into a clearer narrative moment.

What You Will Do

  • Build a notebook section for Unit 2 narrative vocabulary.
  • Study short model passages that include action, dialogue, description, reflection, and transitions.
  • Explain why each craft move helps the reader follow the story.
  • Draft and revise a short original narrative moment.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook correctly defines and applies narrative craft vocabulary.
  • Your practice identifies narrative techniques and transitions in context.
  • Your checkpoint uses at least two narrative techniques and at least two transitions intentionally.
  • Your quiz responses show at least 80% mastery of narrative techniques and transitions.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.CW.NAR.01 in student language: I can write a narrative that uses story techniques, transitions, and a clear point of view so the reader can follow what happens and why it matters.

This lesson focuses on the first part of that standard: narrative techniques and transitions. Before a story can become complex, it must move clearly from one moment to the next.

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help: Ask your TOR for help if your draft feels like a list of events or if you cannot decide which transition shows the correct relationship between moments.
