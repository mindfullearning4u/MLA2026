P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue your notebook work by learning how to choose the right technique for the job the scene needs to do.

Step 4: Match the Technique to the Purpose

Do not add action, dialogue, description, reflection, or transitions randomly. Each technique should do a job.

If the reader needs to see movement, use action. Action is useful when a character makes a choice, reacts to pressure, hides something, reaches for something, runs, waits, or changes position.

If the reader needs to hear relationship or conflict, use dialogue. Dialogue is useful when characters disagree, avoid the truth, reveal information, interrupt each other, or say less than they mean.

If the reader needs to picture or feel the moment, use description. Description is useful when setting, object detail, sensory detail, or body language changes how the scene feels.

If the reader needs to understand why the moment matters, use reflection. Reflection is useful when a character realizes something, remembers something, questions a choice, or connects the event to a larger problem.

Guided Model

Draft: Noah was late. He ran to the bus stop. The bus left.

Question 1: What needs to be shown? The scene needs urgency and disappointment.

Question 2: Which techniques fit? Action can show Noah rushing. Description can show the bus leaving. Reflection can show why the missed bus matters.

Revision: Noah sprinted past the mailbox, one shoe untied, his backpack knocking against his ribs. At the corner, the bus doors folded shut. A second later, the red taillights slid away through the rain, and Noah remembered the audition form sitting unsigned in his folder.

Why it works: The revision turns a plain event into a scene. Action shows speed, description shows the bus leaving, and reflection reveals the stakes.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Write this plain event list: Tariq dropped the glass. Everyone looked at him. He left the room.

Part B: Decide what the scene should emphasize: embarrassment, anger, fear, or surprise.

Part C: Rewrite the event list as a short scene using at least two narrative techniques and two transitions.

Part D: Under your scene, explain which technique did the most important work and why.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The writer adds a transition before every sentence: First, Tariq dropped the glass. Then, everyone looked. Next, he left.

This is incorrect because the transitions are mechanical. They show order, but they do not improve the reader's experience or reveal why the moment matters.

Correct: When the glass shattered, every conversation at the table stopped. For one second, Tariq stared at the water spreading across the floor. Then he pushed back his chair and walked out before anyone could ask what was wrong.

This is correct because the transitions show cause and sequence while action and description reveal tension.

Need help? Ask your TOR if your transitions only say first, next, and finally without showing cause, contrast, time, place, or focus.
