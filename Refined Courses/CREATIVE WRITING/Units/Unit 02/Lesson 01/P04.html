P04 Worked Example

Study each example step by step. The goal is to see how a writer chooses techniques and transitions on purpose.

Example 1: Turning an Event List into a Scene

Starting event list: I found a letter. I read it. I hid it.

Step 1: Identify the important moment. The important moment is not only finding the letter. It is deciding to hide it.

Step 2: Choose techniques. Action can show the hand movement. Description can show the letter. Reflection can show the character's fear or choice.

Step 3: Add transitions that show movement. Use a time or focus transition instead of repeating then.

Revision: Under the loose floorboard, I found an envelope with my name written in blue ink. For a moment, I only held it. After I read the first line, I folded the paper smaller and smaller until it fit inside my sock drawer.

Explanation: The revision uses description, action, and reflection. The transition After I read the first line shows the cause of the hiding.

Example 2: Using Dialogue as a Narrative Technique

Starting event list: The sisters argued. One left. The other cried.

Step 1: Decide what the reader needs to hear. The reader needs to hear the conflict, not just be told that an argument happened.

Step 2: Write dialogue that reveals relationship. The line should sound connected to the characters and the problem.

Step 3: Add a transition showing contrast between the spoken words and the reaction.

Revision: "You always decide for both of us," Mina said. Across the kitchen, Laila kept her hand on the doorknob. "Someone has to," she answered, but her voice shook before she stepped outside.

Explanation: Dialogue reveals conflict and relationship. The place transition Across the kitchen helps the reader picture the scene. The contrast between Laila's words and shaking voice adds tension.

Example 3: Choosing Better Transitions

Weak draft: First, Eli opened the gate. Next, the dog barked. Then, Eli ran.

Step 1: Identify the relationship between moments. The dog barking causes Eli to run.

Step 2: Replace mechanical order transitions with cause and reaction.

Step 3: Add one descriptive detail so the scene has a clear image.

Revision: Eli lifted the rusted gate just high enough to squeeze through. The moment the dog barked from the porch, he dropped to his knees and crawled behind the hedge.

Explanation: The transition The moment shows cause and timing. The action creates a clearer scene than a list of steps.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Adding one line of dialogue automatically makes a scene strong.

This is incorrect because dialogue must reveal conflict, character, relationship, or information. Random dialogue can distract the reader.

Correct: Use dialogue when the exact words matter. If the same information can be shown more powerfully through action or description, choose the stronger technique.

Good writers choose techniques for a reason. They do not add every technique to every sentence.
