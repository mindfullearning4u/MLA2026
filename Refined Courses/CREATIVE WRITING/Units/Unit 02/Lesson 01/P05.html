Draft for Workshop: “The Turn”

Jalen reached the corner just as the rain began. He opened the map, but a gust folded it around his wrist. Across the street, the last bus lowered its steps. He ran toward it. Then he heard the dog barking from the locked garden behind him. Jalen stopped between the bus and the gate.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice better connects evidence, craft, and effect. The answer is not revealed here.

A. The passage has good transitions because it uses “but,” “then,” and “behind him.”

B. “Just as” makes the rain and arrival coincide, while “Then” interrupts Jalen’s run with the bark; the sequence places him between two urgent choices.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a six-to-eight-sentence scene in which a character’s plan is interrupted and a decision becomes necessary. Use at least three purposeful transitions or time cues. Make the order easy to follow without listing events mechanically.

4. Revise and Explain

Reorder or rewrite one sentence to improve continuity, pacing, or emphasis. Show the original and revision, then explain why the new placement or transition better supports the scene.
