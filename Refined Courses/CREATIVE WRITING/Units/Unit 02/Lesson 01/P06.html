Checkpoint: Narrative Techniques and Transitions

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply narrative techniques and transitions in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Platform Change”

The announcement crackled just as June lifted her suitcase. Platform Four, not Two. Travelers surged toward the stairs. She followed until a red scarf appeared beneath a bench—the scarf her sister had worn that morning. Ten minutes earlier, June had promised not to look back.

Term support: A transition guides readers across a change in time, place, action, or idea. Narrative techniques include pacing, dialogue, description, reflection, and sequencing.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Identify two narrative techniques and explain how each shapes the reader’s understanding or expectation.
  2. Explain how “Ten minutes earlier” functions as a transition and changes the meaning of June’s movement.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Add “Suddenly” before every new action so readers always know the scene is changing.
B. Use a precise time, place, or cause-and-effect transition only where the relationship between events needs clarification.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 300–400 word scene in which a character’s destination changes because of an unexpected discovery.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—begin in the middle of purposeful action and establish the character’s destination.
  2. Development—use description, dialogue, or reflection to reveal what is at stake; connect events with precise transitions.
  3. Turning point—introduce the discovery and adjust pacing so the decision receives emphasis.
  4. Closing—show the character’s changed direction and end with a consequence or meaningful image.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise two transitions: one that clarifies sequence and one that strengthens the emotional or logical connection between events. Explain each effect.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
