Checkpoint: Clearly Established Point of View

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply clearly established point of view in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “Across the Table”

I counted the sugar packets while Mr. Dorsey read my application. Three white, two yellow, one torn open. He tapped the final page. “You left this blank.” I knew which question he meant, but I asked him to point to it anyway.

Term support: Point of view is the position from which a story is narrated. Narrative distance describes how closely readers experience a character’s thoughts and perceptions.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Identify the point of view and cite two details that establish it. Explain what readers can and cannot know.
  2. Explain how the sugar-packet detail reveals the narrator’s state of mind without naming the emotion.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Switch briefly to Mr. Dorsey’s private thoughts so readers know whether he approves of the application.
B. Keep the narration within the established perspective and reveal Mr. Dorsey only through what the narrator can observe.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 325–425 word scene in first person or limited third person in which a character receives difficult information.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—establish the viewpoint immediately through what the focal character notices, thinks, or does.
  2. Development—filter setting and another character’s behavior through that viewpoint; keep knowledge limits consistent.
  3. Turning point—deliver the difficult information and show the focal character processing it.
  4. Closing—end with a choice or observation that reflects the character’s perspective without explaining everything.

4. Revise and Explain

Locate one sentence that breaks or weakens the point of view. Revise it to preserve the knowledge boundary and explain the improvement.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
