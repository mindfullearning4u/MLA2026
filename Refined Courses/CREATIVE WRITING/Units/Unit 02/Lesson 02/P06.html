The Returned Coat

Nia recognized the coat before the clerk removed it from the lost-and-found bag. Green wool, brass buttons, one sleeve repaired with black thread. Her mother had worn it the morning she left for the conference and returned without it.

“Found on the northbound train,” the clerk said.

Nia touched the repaired seam. Her mother hated visible mending. She would have replaced the whole sleeve before wearing black thread on green.

“Does it belong to you?”

Nia nearly said yes. Then she noticed a peppermint wrapper in the pocket and a transit receipt dated three days after her mother came home.

At dinner, the empty chair across from Nia held the green coat.

Her mother set down the serving spoon. “Where did you get that?”

Nia watched her face, but her mother looked only at the black stitches.

“The northbound train,” Nia said.

Her mother folded the sleeve inward before sitting.

Analysis

  1. Write an 80–100 word accurate summary.
  2. Identify the narrator position, focalizer, and access boundaries.
  3. Analyze the shift in psychic distance when Nia notices the repair.
  4. What inference does Nia make, and which details complicate it?
  5. Explain how the receipt creates dramatic irony or suspense.
  6. What can Nia legitimately infer from her mother’s gaze and sleeve movement?
  7. Identify one uncertainty the limited POV must preserve.
  8. Evaluate Nia’s reliability as an interpreter rather than a reporter.

Revision and Transfer

  1. Rewrite one paragraph from the mother’s limited viewpoint without revealing more than that moment allows.
  2. Explain how the rewrite changes reader sympathy, knowledge, and suspense.
  3. Write a 180–220 word original third-person-limited scene containing a meaningful object, a mistaken inference, observable counterevidence, and one close-distance sentence.
  4. Annotate four POV choices and explain how you prevented head-hopping.
