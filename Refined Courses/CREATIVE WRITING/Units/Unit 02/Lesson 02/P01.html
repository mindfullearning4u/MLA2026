P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 2: Clearly Established Point of View

In this lesson, you will learn how point of view controls what the reader knows, how close the reader feels to the narrator, and what kind of voice the story creates. A narrative needs a clearly established point of view so the reader is not confused about who is telling the story.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.NAR.01 - Narrative Techniques and Point of View.

Support Standards: MLA.CW.FIC.03 - Narrator Perspective, Irony, and Satire; MLA.CW.COM.03 - Voice and Tone.

What You Will Learn

  • How first person, third person limited, and third person omniscient point of view work.
  • How point of view affects what the reader can know.
  • How narrator distance changes voice, tone, and reader trust.
  • How to revise point-of-view shifts that confuse the reader.

What You Will Do

  • Identify the narrator in short model passages.
  • Compare how the same moment changes in first person and third person.
  • Revise a confusing point-of-view shift.
  • Draft a short scene with a clearly established point of view.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook defines and applies point-of-view vocabulary.
  • Your practice identifies who is telling the story and what that narrator can know.
  • Your checkpoint maintains one clear point of view in an original scene.
  • Your quiz responses show at least 80% mastery of point of view and narrator effect.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.CW.NAR.01 in student language: I can write a narrative with a clear point of view so the reader knows who is telling the story and how much that narrator can know.

Point of view is more than pronouns. It is the camera, microphone, and filter of the story. It controls what the reader sees, hears, understands, and questions.
