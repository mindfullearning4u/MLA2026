Independent Work: Control Point of View

Purpose and Directions

Apply the lesson skill through focused analysis, original writing, and purposeful revision. Complete every numbered task and submit this work with your vocabulary notes in Notebook Evidence Submission. Quality matters more than length.

Draft for Workshop: “The Window”

I saw the moving truck before Mom did. From the upstairs window, I watched a boy carry a lamp into the house next door. He paused beneath my window and looked up. I stepped behind the curtain. Downstairs, Mom called, “Have you met our new neighbors?” I said no, which was technically true.

1. Track Access and Limits

Complete three rows showing what the narrator observes, interprets, and cannot know.

Narrator knowsNarrator infersNarrator cannot know
Record three evidence-based rows.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice better connects evidence, craft, and effect. The answer is not revealed here.

A. The narrator knows the boy wants to meet because he looks up at the window.

B. The narrator can report the boy’s pause and upward look, but first-person limits prevent certainty about his intention; the concealment instead reveals the narrator’s own hesitation.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a six-to-eight-sentence first-person scene in which the narrator misreads or questions another person’s action. Keep the perspective consistent and distinguish observation from interpretation.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one sentence that reveals knowledge the narrator could not reasonably possess. Show the before-and-after versions and explain how the correction strengthens point-of-view consistency.
