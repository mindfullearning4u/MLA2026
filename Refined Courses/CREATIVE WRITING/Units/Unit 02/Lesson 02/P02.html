Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
point of viewThe position and information system through which a narrative is presented.The story is filtered through a child who misunderstands the adults’ argument.
narratorThe constructed voice or consciousness that tells or presents the story.The narrator describes the town with amused but critical language.
first personA participant narrator using I or we, limited to that narrator’s experience, memory, and inference.“I counted every step to the office” limits readers to the speaker’s experience.
third person limitedNarration using third-person pronouns while restricting interior access primarily to one focal character.She notices the locked drawer, but the narration does not reveal anyone else’s thoughts.
omniscient narratorA narrator capable of moving beyond one character’s knowledge, often with a distinct organizing voice.The narration reveals both siblings’ private fears and the history of the house.
focalizationThe consciousness, perception, or position through which narrative information is filtered.Although three characters speak, the scene remains filtered through Mara’s perceptions.
psychic distanceHow near or far narration feels from a character’s immediate thoughts and sensations.“He entered the room” feels distant; “His pulse struck his throat” feels close.
interior accessThe degree to which narration reveals a character’s private thought, memory, or feeling.Readers learn the focal character’s memory but not the visitor’s motive.
free indirect discourseThird-person narration that temporarily adopts a character’s idiom or thought pattern without quotation tags.He had ruined everything, hadn’t he? The third-person narration adopts his thought pattern.
narrator reliabilityThe degree to which a narrator’s account appears accurate, complete, and trustworthy.The narrator’s claim conflicts with details the reader can verify.
head-hoppingAn uncontrolled shift between characters’ private thoughts within a scene.Jumping from Lena’s private thought to Omar’s without a controlled transition causes head-hopping.
dramatic ironyA condition in which the reader understands something important that a character does not.Readers know the bridge is unsafe while the character confidently crosses it.

Match each point-of-view situation to the term that best explains it. Make a choice before checking, read the explanation, and retry any missed match. This is ungraded practice; no extra submission is required.
