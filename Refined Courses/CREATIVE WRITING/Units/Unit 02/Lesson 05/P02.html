Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record all twelve terms and complete definitions. Submit them with Page 5 Independent Work.

TermDefinition
narrator perspectiveThe narrator’s position, access, values, and interpretive habits.
reliabilityThe degree to which a narrator’s account appears accurate, complete, and trustworthy.
ironyA meaningful gap between appearance or expectation and a better-supported reality.
verbal ironyLanguage whose intended meaning differs from its literal wording.
situational ironyA result that meaningfully contradicts a justified expectation.
dramatic ironyA gap in which readers know something important that a character does not.
satireCritique that uses irony, exaggeration, parody, or incongruity to expose a flawed practice or belief.
satirical targetThe behavior, institution, belief, or practice being criticized.
implied normThe healthier value or standard against which satire asks readers to judge its target.
exaggerationPurposeful overstatement used to reveal a real tendency more clearly.
incongruityA revealing mismatch between elements that do not logically or ethically fit together.
deadpan toneA deliberately calm, matter-of-fact tone applied to absurd or troubling material.
