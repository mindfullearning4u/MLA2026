Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
narrator perspectiveThe narrator’s position, access, values, and interpretive habits.The narrator values order and interprets every delay as disrespect.
reliabilityThe degree to which a narrator’s account appears accurate, complete, and trustworthy.His confident account conflicts with dates and actions shown elsewhere.
ironyA meaningful gap between appearance or expectation and a better-supported reality.The safety committee creates a hazard while celebrating its success.
verbal ironyLanguage whose intended meaning differs from its literal wording.“What a brilliant plan,” she says after the roof collapses.
situational ironyA result that meaningfully contradicts a justified expectation.The prize for efficiency causes the longest delay of the year.
dramatic ironyA gap in which readers know something important that a character does not.Readers see the hidden warning, but the character enters the room unaware.
satireCritique that uses irony, exaggeration, parody, or incongruity to expose a flawed practice or belief.A story exaggerates pointless paperwork to criticize bureaucracy.
satirical targetThe behavior, institution, belief, or practice being criticized.The satire targets an institution that values appearances over results.
implied normThe healthier value or standard against which satire asks readers to judge its target.The critique implies that public decisions should serve people honestly.
exaggerationPurposeful overstatement used to reveal a real tendency more clearly.A five-page form is required to request a shorter form.
incongruityA revealing mismatch between elements that do not logically or ethically fit together.Officials praise transparency while locking every public record away.
deadpan toneA deliberately calm, matter-of-fact tone applied to absurd or troubling material.The narrator calmly reports that the “minor inconvenience” destroyed the building.
