Direct Teaching: Perspective, Irony, and Satire

The Evidence-Gap Method

  1. Record the claim: what does the narrator or character say is true?
  2. Collect visible evidence: what details can readers evaluate independently?
  3. Map knowledge: who knows what, and when?
  4. Name the gap: claim versus evidence, expectation versus result, or character knowledge versus reader knowledge.
  5. Classify carefully: verbal, situational, or dramatic irony.
  6. Explain the effect: tension, humor, criticism, sympathy, or revised judgment.
  7. For satire, identify target and norm: what is criticized, and what better value is implied?

Satire Design Frame

Target → Implied norm → Distortion → Consequence.

If a school rewards “efficiency” by grading essays without reading them, the target is performative assessment, the implied norm is attentive evaluation, the distortion is automated praise, and the consequence reveals who is harmed.

Ethical and Craft Limits

  • Target conduct, systems, or ideas—not vulnerable identity.
  • Make the implied norm understandable.
  • Exaggerate a real tendency rather than invent random absurdity.
  • Give consequences to the people affected, not only clever jokes.
  • Do not label every surprise “ironic.”
