The Quiet Park

The city opened its Quiet Park with a brass band. Mayor Bell called the music “a respectful introduction to silence” and spoke for forty minutes beneath a banner reading PLEASE REFRAIN FROM UNNECESSARY SOUND.

After the ceremony, visitors entered through gates that clicked loudly enough to startle birds from the pond. Signs prohibited conversation, laughter, and “audible reflection.” Park employees carried devices that flashed whenever sound exceeded the approved level.

Ten-year-old Micah sat beside his grandmother. She had lost much of her hearing and asked him to describe the birds. He leaned close and whispered. An employee’s device flashed red.

“The park protects everyone’s peace,” the employee whispered while writing the citation.

Micah’s grandmother read the paper, then began laughing. At first Micah tried to stop her. Then the employee laughed too. Three devices flashed. Across the pond, the mayor posed for photographs beside the silent brass band.

Analysis

  1. Write an 80–100 word objective summary.
  2. Identify the satirical target and implied norm.
  3. Explain the verbal irony in “respectful introduction to silence.”
  4. Identify one situational irony and justify the reasonable expectation it reverses.
  5. Map what Micah, his grandmother, the employee, the mayor, and readers understand.
  6. Analyze the incongruity of “audible reflection.”
  7. Explain how the employee’s whisper develops the critique.
  8. Analyze the grandmother’s laughter as a turning point and consequence.

Revision and Transfer

  1. Rewrite one paragraph from the mayor’s perspective while preserving the satire.
  2. Make one exaggerated detail subtler and explain the changed effect.
  3. Explain how the final image uses dramatic irony.
  4. Write a 260–320 word original satire with a defined target, implied norm, two irony types, one knowledge gap, and a meaningful consequence. Annotate each requirement.
