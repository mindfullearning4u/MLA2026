Lesson Overview: Narrator Perspective, Irony, and Satire

Lesson Focus

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.FIC.05 — control narrator perspective and irony to shape interpretation.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.COM.03 — sustain voice and tone; MLA.CW.FIC.04 — develop layered meaning.

What You Will Learn

  • Separate narrator claims from story evidence.
  • Distinguish verbal, situational, and dramatic irony.
  • Build satire around a clear target, norm, distortion, and consequence.
  • Control tone so critique remains precise rather than merely sarcastic.

Why This Matters

A narrator can misunderstand events while the story gives readers better evidence. Irony turns that gap into meaning. Satire extends the method by exaggerating a flawed practice so readers can judge both the target and the healthier standard it violates.

Mastery Targets

  • Map what narrator, reader, and characters know.
  • Explain irony using evidence and effect.
  • Identify the target and implied norm of satire.
  • Write critique that remains humane, purposeful, and controlled.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can use gaps between what is said, known, and shown to create irony and meaningful social critique.
