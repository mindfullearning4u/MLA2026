P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 5: Narrator Perspective, Irony, and Satire

In this lesson, you will learn how a narrator's perspective can shape what the reader believes, questions, or notices. You will also learn how irony and satire work as craft tools when a writer wants a difference between what is said and what is meant.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.FIC.03 - Narrator Perspective, Irony, and Satire.

Support Standards: MLA.CW.NAR.01 - Narrative Techniques and Point of View; MLA.CW.WR.04 - Rhetoric as Craft.

What You Will Learn

  • How narrator perspective influences what the reader knows and trusts.
  • How irony creates a gap between appearance and reality, or between words and meaning.
  • How satire uses exaggeration, contrast, or irony to criticize a behavior or idea.
  • How to use narrator perspective without confusing the reader.

What You Will Do

  • Study short narrator-perspective models.
  • Identify what a narrator notices, misses, or misunderstands.
  • Explain irony and satire using evidence from embedded passages.
  • Draft a short scene where narrator perspective creates a reader effect.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook defines narrator perspective, irony, and satire accurately.
  • Your practice explains how narrator perspective shapes reader trust or understanding.
  • Your checkpoint uses perspective, irony, or mild satire intentionally in an original scene.
  • Your quiz responses show at least 80% mastery of narrator perspective, irony, and satire.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.CW.FIC.03 in student language: I can explain and use narrator perspective to create effects such as irony, satire, uncertainty, or reader insight.

This standard matters for creative writing because narrators do not only report events. They filter events through attitudes, limits, blind spots, and assumptions.

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help: Ask your TOR for help if you cannot tell whether the narrator is reliable, limited, ironic, or satirical.
