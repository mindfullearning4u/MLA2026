Purpose and Directions

Apply the lesson skill through focused analysis, original writing, and purposeful revision. Complete every numbered task and submit this work with your vocabulary notes in Notebook Evidence Submission. Quality matters more than length.

Draft for Workshop: “Efficiency Day”

Principal Ward announced Efficiency Day with a twelve-page memo. Students were instructed to save time by attending a forty-minute assembly about saving time. From the back row, I watched three teachers search the memo for the room number. “Excellent planning,” I whispered as the late bell rang.

1. Separate Speaker from Target

Complete three rows identifying the narrator’s literal words, implied attitude, and the practice or idea being criticized.

Exact evidenceLiteral versus intended meaningTarget and effect
Record three evidence-based rows.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice better connects evidence, craft, and effect. The answer is not revealed here.

A. “Excellent planning” shows that the narrator sincerely admires the principal’s detailed organization.

B. The praise conflicts with the long memo, time-wasting assembly, and confused teachers, creating verbal irony that criticizes inefficient attempts to mandate efficiency.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a six-to-eight-sentence satirical scene that exposes one harmless contradiction in school or everyday life. Establish a clear narrator perspective, use irony purposefully, and criticize a practice rather than humiliating a person or group.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one line so the irony becomes understandable without announcing the joke. Show both versions, then explain the literal meaning, intended meaning, target, and respectful boundary.
