The Courtesy Counter

On Monday, the school installed a Courtesy Counter beside the main entrance. Its screen displayed a live number representing campus kindness. Students earned points for holding doors, complimenting posters, and using approved greetings.

Vice Principal Dorn announced that courtesy had risen 38 percent by lunch. He did not mention that the camera could not distinguish a held door from a blocked one.

Lena volunteered as a Counter Ambassador because the badge excused her from first-period announcements. Her job was to review “uncertain gestures.” The handbook defined kindness as any visible action that produced a positive data signal. When Lena asked how the system measured listening, the trainer suggested standing where the camera could see her nod.

By Wednesday, students rehearsed greetings before entering the camera’s range. A boy held the door for twenty-three people, including six who had not intended to enter. The counter reached 9,000. Dorn ordered a celebration.

During the assembly, custodian Ms. Ruiz dropped a box of cleaning supplies behind the stage. Lena heard it fall, but the camera faced the audience. She hesitated. Leaving her assigned position would cost fifty ambassador points. Onstage, Dorn praised students for making kindness “measurable, visible, and therefore real.”

Lena stepped away and helped Ms. Ruiz gather the bottles. Nobody applauded. The counter deducted fifty points and displayed a red arrow.

The next morning, Lena submitted her resignation. Under REASON, she wrote: Insufficient visibility. Dorn approved it as evidence that the program was identifying negative attitudes efficiently.

Part A — Perspective and Irony

  1. Write a 120–150 word objective summary.
  2. Identify what Dorn claims and three details the story shows.
  3. Map what Lena, Dorn, students, and readers know by Wednesday.
  4. Analyze one example of dramatic irony.
  5. Explain why the final approval is situational irony, not merely a surprise.

Part B — Satirical Design

  1. Identify the satirical target and implied norm.
  2. Trace three exaggerations back to recognizable real-world tendencies.
  3. Analyze the incongruity in “positive data signal.”
  4. Explain how the deadpan tone affects the critique.
  5. Who experiences the consequences of the system, and why does that matter?

Part C — Vocabulary and Revision

  1. Use context to determine and write a definition for ambassador as it is used in the passage.
  2. Explain the verbal irony in Lena’s resignation reason.
  3. Rewrite one paragraph from Dorn’s first-person perspective while preserving reader irony.
  4. Revise one exaggerated detail so it becomes subtler; explain the tradeoff.
  5. Identify one line that establishes the implied norm without directly stating it.

Part D — Original Satire

  1. Plan a satire with target, implied norm, distortion, knowledge gap, consequence, and ethical limit.
  2. Write a polished 550–650 word satire using controlled perspective, at least two forms of irony, deadpan tone, and a consequential ending.
  3. Write a 160–200 word rationale identifying the target and norm, quoting four craft choices, and explaining how you avoided attacking vulnerable identity.
