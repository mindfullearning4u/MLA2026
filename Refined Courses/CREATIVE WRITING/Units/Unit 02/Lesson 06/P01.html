P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 6: Pace, Tension, Mood, and Tone

In this lesson, you will learn how writers control how fast a scene feels, how tense it becomes, and what emotional atmosphere it creates. You will use pacing choices to shape tension, mood, and tone in original narrative writing.

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.NAR.02 - Pace, Tension, Mood, and Tone.

Support Standards: MLA.CW.COM.03 - Voice and Tone; MLA.CW.POE.02 - Figurative Language and Mood.

What You Will Learn

  • How pacing controls how fast or slow a scene feels.
  • How tension grows when a reader waits for an outcome, secret, choice, or consequence.
  • How mood is created through setting, imagery, sensory detail, and figurative language.
  • How tone is shaped by the narrator's attitude and word choice.

What You Will Do

  • Study short scenes with different pacing choices.
  • Identify sentence length, pauses, sensory details, and delayed information.
  • Revise a flat scene to create tension and mood.
  • Write a short original scene using pace to create a specific tone or mood.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook explains pacing, tension, mood, and tone accurately.
  • Your practice identifies craft choices that speed up or slow down a scene.
  • Your checkpoint uses pacing to create tension, mood, or tone intentionally.
  • Your quiz responses show at least 80% mastery of narrative pacing and effect.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.CW.NAR.02 in student language: I can write narratives with pacing that creates tension, mood, or tone.

This standard helps you control the reader's experience. A writer can slow a moment down to increase pressure, speed through routine events, or use imagery to make a scene feel uneasy, joyful, lonely, or tense.
