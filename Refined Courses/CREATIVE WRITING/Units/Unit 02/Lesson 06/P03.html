P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue by learning how to revise a flat scene into a paced scene.

Step 4: Use Sentence Length on Purpose

Short sentences can speed up action or create impact. Longer sentences can slow a moment down, add detail, or stretch suspense.

Fast pace: The latch clicked. I froze.

Slower pace: The latch clicked once, softly, and for a second I could hear only the rain ticking against the window.

Neither version is automatically better. The better choice depends on the effect the writer wants.

Step 5: Revise for Mood with Image and Figurative Language

Figurative language can help create mood when it grows from the scene. Do not add a comparison only to sound fancy. Add it when it sharpens the reader's feeling.

Plain: The clouds were dark.

Moody: The clouds pressed low over the field like a lid.

The comparison creates a heavy, trapped mood. The image supports the emotional atmosphere.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Making every sentence short because short sentences always create tension.

This is incorrect because too many short sentences can feel choppy or flat. Tension comes from purposeful pacing, stakes, delay, and detail.

Correct: Use a mix of sentence lengths. Slow down the important moment, then use a short sentence for impact when the pressure peaks.

Need help? Ask your TOR if your scene has a mood word but no image, sensory detail, pacing choice, or tone evidence to create that mood.
