P04 Worked Example

Each example shows how a pacing choice creates a reader effect.

Example 1: Slowing for Tension

Flat draft: I opened the closet and found the note.

Step 1: Decide the important moment. The discovery of the note matters.

Step 2: Add delay. Let the reader wait before the note appears.

Step 3: Add sensory detail. Use sound, touch, or sight.

Revision: The closet door stuck halfway. I pulled again, slowly, until the wood scraped the floor. Something white slid from the top shelf and landed at my feet.

Effect: The slower pace creates tension before the note is revealed.

Example 2: Speeding Through Routine Action

Overwritten draft: I put my left shoe on. Then I put my right shoe on. Then I tied one lace. Then I tied the other lace.

Step 1: Decide whether the action matters. Unless the shoes are important, this routine does not need space.

Step 2: Compress it.

Revision: I got ready in three minutes and ran for the bus.

Effect: Compression moves quickly through unimportant action so the important scene can begin.

Example 3: Creating Mood with Image

Plain draft: The room felt lonely.

Step 1: Replace the mood label with evidence.

Step 2: Choose a detail that supports the mood.

Revision: One paper cup sat on the table, its rim bent inward like it had been waiting too long.

Effect: The image creates a lonely mood without directly naming loneliness.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Telling the reader "this was tense" instead of using craft choices.

This is incorrect because the reader needs to feel tension through delay, stakes, pacing, and sensory detail.

Correct: Show tension by slowing the important moment, delaying information, and choosing details that make the reader anticipate what comes next.
