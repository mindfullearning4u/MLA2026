Worked Example: Three Pace-and-Tension Models

Example 1 — Expand the Decision

Summary draft: “Mira heard the alarm and decided to open the sealed door.”

Scene revision: “The alarm stopped. Behind the sealed door, something knocked twice, waited, then knocked once. Mira’s hand reached the release. The label said OXYGEN LOSS AFTER 90 SECONDS. Eighty-four glowed above the frame.”

  1. Goal: determine who is inside.
  2. Stakes: opening may endanger others; delay may kill someone.
  3. Pause: the knock pattern creates interpretation.
  4. Concrete clock: the definition is visible in context, so no specialized term is unexplained.
  5. Syntax: fragments narrow attention.
  6. Effect: the revision expands the irreversible choice.

Example 2 — Compress the Routine

Overwritten: Four paragraphs describe Tomas checking twelve windows before a storm.

Revision: “By dusk Tomas had latched every window except the nursery’s. That one stood open, the curtain breathing inward.”

  1. Summary compresses repeated checking.
  2. “Except” pivots from routine to anomaly.
  3. The personification of the curtain creates uneasy mood.
  4. The open window raises a question without hiding orientation.
  5. Scene should begin at Tomas’s next choice.
  6. Tradeoff: less procedure, stronger focus.

Example 3 — Tone Changes the Same Mood

Ominous mood, fearful tone: “The elevator descended past the final numbered floor. Lian gripped the rail.”

Ominous mood, dry tone: “The elevator continued below B3, apparently confident that labels were optional.”

  1. Event remains unexplained descent.
  2. Mood stays ominous because the location violates expectation.
  3. Fearful tone names bodily response.
  4. Dry tone uses understatement and personification.
  5. The dry version may make Lian seem observant or defensive.
  6. Choose tone based on narrator and larger purpose, not novelty.
