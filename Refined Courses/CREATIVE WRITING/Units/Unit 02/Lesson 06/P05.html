Independent Work: Control Pace and Tension

Purpose and Directions

Apply the lesson skill through focused analysis, original writing, and purposeful revision. Complete every numbered task and submit this work with your vocabulary notes in Notebook Evidence Submission. Quality matters more than length.

Draft for Workshop: “The Generator”

The hallway lights failed. For one second, the hospital wing was silent. Then the generator coughed below us. A red emergency lamp blinked once, twice, and went dark. Dr. Chen reached the stairwell door. From the other side came three slow knocks.

1. Track the Pressure

Complete three rows, then explain how pacing choices build tension and mood without confusing them with tone.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice better connects evidence, craft, and effect. The answer is not revealed here.

A. The short sentences make everything fast and scary.

B. The brief power failure is followed by counted blinks and delayed knocks; these pauses stretch a few seconds, increasing tension and creating an uneasy mood while the narration remains controlled.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a seven-to-nine-sentence scene in which a routine action becomes tense. Use sentence length, paragraphing, repetition, and selected detail to vary pace deliberately. State the intended mood and narrator tone before drafting.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one section to slow or accelerate a specific moment. Show the original and revision, then explain how the changed pacing affects tension, mood, and tone without claiming one sentence length always creates the same result.
