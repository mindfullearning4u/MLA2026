Checkpoint: Pace, Tension, Mood, and Tone

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply pace, tension, mood, and tone in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “Twenty Seconds”

The elevator stopped between floors. For twenty seconds, nothing happened. Then the emergency light blinked once, twice, and stayed dark. “It does that,” Ren said too quickly. Beside him, Laila placed her ear against the metal door. Somewhere above them, a cable answered with a single knock.

Term support: Pace is the speed at which readers experience events. Tension is uncertainty or pressure about what may happen. Mood is the feeling created for readers; tone is the narrator’s or writer’s attitude.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Trace how sentence length, repetition, and sound alter pace and tension. Cite exact evidence.
  2. Distinguish mood from tone in the passage and explain the evidence for each.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Use only short sentences and repeated danger words because fast pacing always creates the most tension.
B. Vary sentence length, pause on selected details, and control what information is delayed so pace serves the tension.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 400–500 word scene in which two characters wait during a time-sensitive problem. Control pace to create and then shift tension, mood, or tone.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—begin with the disruption and establish the time pressure through a precise detail.
  2. Development—alternate compressed action with slowed observation; use sound, setting, and dialogue to build tension.
  3. Turning point—reveal new information that changes the characters’ understanding and the scene’s pace.
  4. Closing—show the immediate response and end with a detail that confirms or complicates the mood.

4. Revise and Explain

Mark one fast passage and one slowed passage. Revise their sentence patterns or detail level, then explain how the contrast affects tension and mood.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
