Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
narrative elementA component of a story—such as character, conflict, setting, plot, or point of view—that contributes to its total effect.The locked room, family dispute, and storm each shape the story’s effect.
characterizationThe methods a writer uses to reveal a character through action, speech, thought, description, and response.Her refusal to open the letter reveals caution and loyalty.
conflictA meaningful struggle between competing wants, values, forces, or obligations.The character must choose between protecting a friend and telling the truth.
settingThe time, place, social conditions, and atmosphere in which events occur.A crowded summer festival makes secrecy difficult.
plotThe arranged sequence of causally connected events in a narrative.Each attempt to hide the map causes a new consequence.
turning pointA moment when new knowledge, choice, or consequence changes the direction of a narrative.Learning who wrote the note changes the character’s plan.
themeA developed insight about human experience expressed through the story’s patterns and consequences.The story develops the idea that loyalty requires honest choices.
motifA recurring image, phrase, object, situation, or idea that accumulates significance.Repeated images of cracked mirrors gather meaning across the story.
toneThe narrator’s or implied author’s attitude toward the subject and audience.The narrator describes the reunion with restrained regret.
styleA writer’s recognizable pattern of diction, syntax, imagery, figurative language, and rhythm.Long flowing sentences and precise sensory images form a recognizable style.
syntaxThe arrangement and rhythm of words, phrases, and clauses in sentences.A sudden fragment—“Gone.”—interrupts the earlier flowing syntax.
juxtapositionThe placement of contrasting elements near one another to sharpen meaning.A child’s bright drawing hangs beside the evacuation notice.

Vocabulary in Action

Match each story situation to the element or style term that best explains it. Make a choice before checking, read the explanation, and retry any missed match. This is ungraded practice; no extra submission is required.
