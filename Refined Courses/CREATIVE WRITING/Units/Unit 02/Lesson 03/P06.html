Checkpoint: Key Elements, Meaning, and Style

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply key elements, meaning, and style in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Empty Display Case”

Every Friday, Mina dusted the empty display case in the museum lobby. The brass label still read FIRST FLIGHT, 1911. Visitors rarely stopped. On the final Friday, a child pressed both hands to the glass and whispered, “Maybe the important part escaped.” Mina set down her cloth.

Term support: Style is the distinctive pattern of language and craft choices in a work. Key narrative elements include character, setting, conflict, plot, and theme.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Analyze how character, setting, and conflict begin working together. Use exact evidence.
  2. Explain how the final line and the child’s comment may develop meaning. Identify what remains ambiguous.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Explain the history of the missing object in a long opening paragraph so every element is clear.
B. Develop selected details, actions, and dialogue so the elements accumulate meaning while preserving a purposeful question.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 350–450 word scene centered on an ordinary place containing one unexplained absence. Integrate character, setting, conflict, and an implied central idea in a consistent style.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—hook the reader with the absence and establish the place through selected details.
  2. Development—connect the character’s goal to the setting and introduce a conflict that gives the absence meaning.
  3. Turning point—use dialogue, discovery, or action to reframe an earlier detail.
  4. Closing—resolve the immediate action while leaving a supported idea for readers to infer.

4. Revise and Explain

Identify one element that feels disconnected. Revise a detail or action so it supports both the plot and the central meaning; explain the connection.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
