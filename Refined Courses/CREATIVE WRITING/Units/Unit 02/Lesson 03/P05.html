Independent Work: Make Elements Work Together

Purpose and Directions

Apply the lesson skill through focused analysis, original writing, and purposeful revision. Complete every numbered task and submit this work with your vocabulary notes in Notebook Evidence Submission. Quality matters more than length.

Draft for Workshop: “The Waiting Room”

The clock had no second hand, yet Mina heard every minute. She sat beneath a mural of bright mountains while the receptionist watered a plastic plant. When the office door opened, Mina stood too quickly and knocked her folder to the floor. A single photograph slid out face down.

1. Connect Element to Meaning

Complete three rows, choosing from character action, setting, object, conflict, and style. Explain how the elements interact rather than listing them.

Element and evidenceConnection to another elementMeaning or effect
Record three connected observations.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice better connects evidence, craft, and effect. The answer is not revealed here.

A. The setting is a waiting room, the character is Mina, and the photograph is a symbol.

B. The cheerful mountain mural contrasts with Mina’s tense actions, while the face-down photograph turns her dropped folder into a meaningful complication rather than a random accident.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a six-to-eight-sentence scene in which setting, one object, and one character action work together to develop a conflict or meaning. Choose a deliberate style and avoid explaining the theme directly.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one element so it contributes more clearly to the whole scene. Show the original and revision, then explain how the change affects at least one other element.
