Notebook Title

Lesson 8: Unit 2 Narrative Craft Synthesis

Vocabulary Review

  • Narrative technique: Action, dialogue, description, reflection, pacing, sensory detail, or other craft move used to tell a story.
  • Point of view: The perspective from which a story is told.
  • Key element: A story part such as character, setting, conflict, object, image, or plot event.
  • Layered detail: A detail with literal and implied meaning.
  • Narrator perspective: The narrator's knowledge, attitude, limits, and interpretation.
  • Pace: How fast or slow a scene feels.
  • Mood and tone: The emotional atmosphere and narrator attitude.

Step 1: Review the Unit 2 Skill Chain

Use this order when studying a narrative scene.

First: Identify point of view and narrator knowledge.

Second: Identify narrative techniques and key elements.

Third: Explain literal and implied meaning.

Fourth: Explain pacing, tension, mood, and tone.

Fifth: Decide how the choices work together.

Step 2: Practice with a Synthesis Passage

Passage: I held the missing ticket under the hallway light. The paper had softened at the fold from being opened and closed too many times. Across the lobby, the bus doors sighed shut. I told myself I still had a minute, though the clock above the exit had already moved on.

Point of view: First person.

Key element: The missing ticket.

Pacing: The moment slows as the narrator watches the bus doors and clock.

Effect: The scene creates tension because the narrator is running out of time and may be denying reality.
