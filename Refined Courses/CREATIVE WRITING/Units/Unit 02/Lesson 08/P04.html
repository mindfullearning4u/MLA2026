P04 Worked Example

Review each example before the Unit Assessment.

Example 1: Point of View

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Passage: I saw Mina put the key in her pocket, but I did not know why her hands were shaking.

Step 1: Identify the point of view. The passage uses first person.

Step 2: Identify narrator knowledge. The narrator reports observation and admits what is unknown.

Step 3: Explain effect. The narrator seems reliable because the narrator does not overclaim.

Example 2: Key Element and Meaning

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Passage: The packed suitcase stood beside the celebration banner.

Step 1: Literal meaning: A suitcase is near a banner.

Step 2: Key element: The suitcase is an object detail.

Step 3: Implied meaning: The contrast suggests celebration and leaving at the same time.

Example 3: Pacing and Tension

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Passage: The latch clicked. I froze. Behind the door, someone breathed once, then stopped.

Step 1: Identify pacing. Short sentences create quick impact.

Step 2: Identify tension. The reader waits to learn who is behind the door.

Step 3: Explain effect. Sound and delay create a tense mood.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Treating every unusual detail as satire.

This is incorrect because satire must criticize a target using irony, exaggeration, or humor.

Correct: Decide what the detail does. It might create mood, imply meaning, reveal character, build tension, or create satire only if there is a clear target.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
