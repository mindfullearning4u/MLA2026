Checkpoint: Putting It All Together: Unit 2 Synthesis

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply putting it all together: unit 2 synthesis in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Final Broadcast”

During the town’s last radio broadcast, seventeen-year-old Noor discovers that the farewell script contains a sentence no one remembers approving. Outside the studio, residents gather beneath silent speakers. Inside, the clock counts down while Noor’s mentor says, “Read what is written.” The microphone light turns red.

Term support: Narrative synthesis means selecting and coordinating techniques so character, structure, perspective, language, and meaning form a coherent whole.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Analyze how point of view, setting, conflict, pacing, and symbolism interact in the source. Use exact evidence.
  2. Explain two defensible choices Noor could make and predict how each would alter tone, theme, and reader judgment.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Use every narrative technique from the unit in every paragraph to prove mastery.
B. Select and control the techniques that best develop the character’s decision, the scene’s progression, and the meaning readers infer.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a polished 700–900 word narrative using the source above or an approved original premise. Synthesize the full range of Unit 2 narrative skills.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—create a compelling hook, establish a controlled point of view, and introduce the central pressure.
  2. Development—build character, setting, and conflict through purposeful sequence, transitions, details, dialogue, and subtext.
  3. Escalation—control pace and information to intensify tension while maintaining a coherent mood and tone.
  4. Turning point—force a consequential choice that reinterprets an earlier detail or expectation.
  5. Closing—show the consequence, develop an implied theme, and end with a resonant image or action.
  6. Craft reflection—write 175–225 words with a central claim, three quoted details, and analysis of how the techniques work together.

4. Revise and Explain

Conduct two revision passes—development/structure and language/style. Cite at least one consequential change from each and explain how the revisions strengthen the complete narrative.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
