Direct Teaching: The Narrative Integration Matrix

Nine-Step Synthesis Process

  1. Name the controlling effect.
  2. Build objective, pressure, turn, and consequence.
  3. Choose focalizer and map access.
  4. Make setting restrict choice.
  5. Plan subtext and a motif.
  6. Graph pace and tension.
  7. Coordinate tone, diction, syntax, and sensory field.
  8. Draft, then revise structure before sentences.
  9. Annotate evidence and defend tradeoffs.

Pause and Choose: Diagnose the First Revision Need

A draft has polished imagery, but the main character’s objective is unclear and the ending causes no change. Which revision plan follows the integration matrix?

A. Add stronger verbs and more sensory details before changing the plot.
Not the best choice. Sentence-level polish cannot repair missing purpose, pressure, and consequence. It may make an inert structure sound smoother without making it meaningful.
B. Clarify the objective, build pressure toward a choice, and make the ending show a consequence before polishing style.
✓ Correct—the revision order follows dependency. Architecture controls what later viewpoint, pacing, and style choices must accomplish. Repairing the causal chain first prevents polishing the wrong version.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Revision Order

Revise in this order: purpose → architecture → viewpoint → information → pacing → style → conventions. Sentence polish cannot repair a scene without change.

Pause and Choose: Defend an Integrated Craft Choice

A writer slows the turning point, repeats a locked-window image, and keeps the focalizer uncertain about another character’s motive. Which rationale demonstrates integrated reasoning?

A. “I used pacing, symbolism, and point of view because stories need several techniques.”
Not the best choice. This lists devices but never explains how they work together, when they matter, or what readers can understand because of them.
B. “Slowing the choice lets readers study the repeated locked-window image; limiting access to motive makes that barrier suggest distrust without proving its cause.”
✓ Correct—the techniques produce one controlling effect. The rationale links time, motif, and viewpoint to a bounded reader inference and explains why the choices appear together at the turning point.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Quality Test

For each major choice, write: “Because I used ___ when ___, the reader can ___.” Remove or revise any element whose effect remains vague.

Pause and Try: Choose the Revision Priority

A draft has elegant sentences, but the main character’s objective is unclear and the ending causes no change. Which problem should the writer address first—and why?

Check your thinking

Revise the scene architecture first. Clarify the objective, then build pressure, a turn, and a consequence. Polishing diction before the scene has meaningful change would preserve the wrong structure. Once the causal chain works, style can strengthen the controlling effect.
