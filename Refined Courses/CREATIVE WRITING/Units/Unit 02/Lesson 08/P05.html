Before the Maps

On the final morning before the river library moved, Darius found a margin note in a donated atlas: The bridge was here before the maps admitted it. An atlas is a book of maps.

He wanted to preserve the note, but the digitization rules required clean pages. Outside, demolition crews prepared to remove the footbridge that connected the east neighborhood to the library.

The director gave him ten minutes. Darius searched older editions. Each omitted the bridge until the year after residents had built it themselves.

A patron arrived carrying a photograph of children crossing unfinished boards. She recognized her mother’s handwriting in the atlas.

The scanner alarm sounded. Darius could hide the marked page or erase it. Instead, he scanned the margin, photograph, and official maps as one record, then wrote an explanation of what each source could and could not prove.

When the library reopened online, the bridge appeared first as a blank line, then as boards, then as a printed symbol. The final image showed workers dismantling it while students crossed one last time.
