Worked Example: Three Evidence-Chain Revisions

Example 1 — Build Development

Idea: Convenience can conceal transferred labor.

  1. Setup: Rae praises an instant delivery service.
  2. Complication: a late package reveals a driver working during a storm.
  3. Counterevidence: the driver values flexible hours.
  4. Turn: Rae’s urgent reorder increases the same pressure she criticizes.
  5. Consequence: she changes both her purchase and the neighborhood deadline.
  6. Development: the idea moves beyond “delivery is bad” toward shared responsibility.

Example 2 — Remove a Vivid Distraction

A draft about a brother hiding medical bills includes a mysterious stray dog for three pages.

  1. The dog creates expectation of rescue or symbolism.
  2. No later choice connects it to secrecy or care.
  3. Its vividness competes with the evidence chain.
  4. Revision A: remove it.
  5. Revision B: make feeding the dog the behavior that exposes missing grocery money.
  6. Rule: a memorable detail needs narrative work, not merely atmosphere.

Example 3 — Use Counterevidence

Easy draft: A coach’s strict schedule only harms athletes.

  1. Add evidence that the schedule protects students from arbitrary favoritism.
  2. Show one athlete benefiting from predictable practice.
  3. Turn: the coach refuses an exception during a family emergency.
  4. Consequence: fairness as uniformity conflicts with fairness as responsiveness.
  5. Qualified idea: systems designed for fairness can become unjust when consistency replaces judgment.
  6. Counterevidence creates depth, not indecision.
