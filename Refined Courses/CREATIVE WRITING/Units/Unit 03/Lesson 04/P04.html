P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how central idea controls fiction details.

Worked Example 1: Keep a Supporting Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Central idea: A character is learning to accept help.

Detail: The character finally lets a teammate carry one box.

Step 1: The detail shows a change in behavior.

Step 2: The teammate's help connects directly to the idea.

Step 3: Keep the detail because it supports the central idea.

Worked Example 2: Cut a Distracting Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Central idea: A character is avoiding a difficult apology.

Detail: The scene pauses for a long description of a poster for an unrelated concert.

Step 1: The poster does not connect to the apology.

Step 2: The detail distracts from the conflict.

Step 3: Cut it or revise it so the poster connects to the relationship.

Worked Example 3: Change a Detail to Support the Idea

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Central idea: Memory can make a new place feel less lonely.

Weak detail: The room has a blue rug.

Step 1: The rug is not connected yet.

Step 2: Revise: The room has a blue rug the same color as the one in the narrator's grandmother's kitchen.

Step 3: The detail now supports memory and loneliness.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Keep every detail because more description always makes fiction stronger.

This is incorrect because extra details can weaken focus if they do not support the central idea.

Correct: Keep details that develop the central idea, and revise or cut details that distract from it.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
