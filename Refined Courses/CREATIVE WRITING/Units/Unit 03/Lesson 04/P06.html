New Source: The Map Room

Every afternoon, Nia dusted the town’s abandoned map room. The maps ended at the river, even though new houses now stood beyond it. She began penciling those streets onto scrap paper. When the council announced that the map room would close because “nothing here changes,” Nia placed her hand-drawn pages beside the official maps. Neighbors arrived one by one, pointing to homes, paths, and names that had been missing.

A central idea is a text’s main insight or controlling understanding. Supporting details are purposeful actions, images, facts, or consequences that develop that idea.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. What central idea emerges in the passage? Cite two details and explain how each develops it.
  2. Which detail most strongly changes the meaning of the map room? Explain how it connects the character’s action to the larger community.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which revision would develop the central idea more purposefully?

A. Add a long description of the dust because every story needs more imagery.
B. Repeat the contrast between the incomplete official maps and the neighbors’ lived knowledge because it strengthens the central idea.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write a 400–500 word fiction scene organized around one central idea.

  1. Opening: Establish a character goal and a setting that connects to the central idea.
  2. Development: Include at least three purposeful details—actions, images, or dialogue—that support the idea.
  3. Turning point: Use a decision or discovery to deepen the meaning.
  4. Resolution: End with a consequence or image that completes the idea without stating a moral.

After the scene, name the central idea and identify the three supporting details.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Remove or replace one interesting detail that does not support the central idea. Explain in one sentence how the revision improves unity.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
