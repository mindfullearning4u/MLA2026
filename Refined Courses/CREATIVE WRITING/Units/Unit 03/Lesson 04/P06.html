One Unit

Mina cataloged donations for the school pantry. A spreadsheet marked every can, box, and bag as one unit. The principal praised the rising total.

Then Mina noticed that many donated cans required openers, but the pantry had none. Several boxes contained ingredients families could not prepare without ovens. The total was accurate, yet it did not describe usefulness.

Mina added columns for preparation, dietary needs, and expiration. The count fell when unusable items were separated, and the principal frowned.

“We received fewer units,” he said.

“No,” Mina answered. “We learned more about them.”

The next donation notice requested complete meals rather than impressive numbers.

Analysis and Transfer

  1. Write an 80–100 word summary.
  2. State the central idea and a separate theme.
  3. Complete the Visual Evidence Chain.
  4. Identify three essential details.
  5. Explain how the accurate count becomes incomplete evidence.
  6. Identify the counterevidence.
  7. Analyze the principal’s line and Mina’s response.
  8. Explain how the final notice develops the central idea.
  9. Remove one detail and test coherence.
  10. Add one supporting detail without distracting.
  11. Rewrite the ending to qualify the idea differently.
  12. Write a 350–450 word story that develops a central idea through an annotated evidence chain, counterevidence, turn, and consequence.
