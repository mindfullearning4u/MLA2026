Notebook Title

Lesson 4: Central Idea Support as Fiction Development

Vocabulary

  • Central idea: The main controlling idea a passage or scene develops.
  • Support: Story evidence that develops the central idea.
  • Relevant detail: A detail that helps develop the central idea.
  • Irrelevant detail: A detail that distracts because it does not support the central idea.
  • Development: The way an idea grows through conflict, character choice, setting, dialogue, or imagery.
  • Revision decision: A choice to keep, cut, add, or change a detail.

Step 1: State the Central Idea

A central idea is the controlling thought the scene develops. In fiction, it is often connected to character, conflict, or theme.

Weak central idea: The scene is about a girl.

Stronger central idea: The scene shows that avoiding a hard truth can make a problem grow.

The stronger central idea gives the writer a clear direction for choosing details.

Step 2: Match Details to the Idea

Central idea: Avoiding a hard truth can make a problem grow.

Supporting detail: The character pushes the unopened letter under a stack of books each time someone asks about it.

This supports the idea because the action shows avoidance. If the scene instead spent a paragraph describing unrelated shoes, that detail would not support the central idea unless the shoes connected to the conflict.

Step 3: Decide Keep, Cut, Add, or Change

When revising, ask what each detail does.

Keep details that support the central idea.

Cut details that distract and do not matter.

Add details when the central idea needs clearer evidence.

Change details that are interesting but not yet connected to meaning.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary words and definitions.

Part B: Write the stronger central idea from Step 1.

Part C: List one supporting detail, one distracting detail, and one revision decision.
