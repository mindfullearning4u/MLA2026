P04 Worked Example

Study each example before guided practice. Each example shows how to move from passage evidence to theme.

Example 1: Topic to Theme

Passage: Imani wanted the lead role, but she stayed after rehearsal to help the new student learn the smallest part.

Step 1: Name the topic: ambition and kindness.

Step 2: Identify the choice: Imani helps someone else even though she wants attention too.

Step 3: Write the theme: Personal ambition does not have to erase kindness toward others.

Example 2: Evidence to Explanation

Passage: The village well had been dry for weeks. When the first rain came, Jori filled one cup and carried it to the neighbor who had refused to speak to her.

Step 1: Evidence: Jori shares the first water with a neighbor who has treated her coldly.

Step 2: Meaning: The action shows generosity when resources are scarce.

Step 3: Theme explanation: The passage suggests that compassion matters most when it is least convenient.

Example 3: Writer Choice and Theme

Passage: Noah kept the broken compass because it had belonged to his sister. At the fork in the trail, he turned the compass in his palm, then chose the road she had always wanted to take.

Step 1: Key craft choice: The compass is a meaningful object.

Step 2: Character action: Noah chooses the road connected to his sister.

Step 3: Theme: Memory can guide a person forward instead of holding the person in the past.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The theme is "Noah has a compass."

This is incorrect because it summarizes a detail instead of explaining a larger idea.

Correct: The compass helps develop the theme that memory can guide future choices.

This is correct because it explains what the object means and how it connects to Noah's action.
