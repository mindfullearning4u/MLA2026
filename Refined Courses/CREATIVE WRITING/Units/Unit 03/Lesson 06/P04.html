P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how conflicting perspectives create coming-of-age growth.

Example 1: Starting Belief

Passage: Lena thought moving meant losing every part of home.

Step 1: Identify belief: Moving equals loss.

Step 2: Identify blind spot: Lena does not yet understand that memory can travel with her.

Step 3: This sets up coming-of-age change.

Example 2: Conflicting Perspectives

Passage: Lena wants to throw away the cracked bowl. Her mother packs it carefully because it belonged to Lena's grandmother.

Step 1: Lena sees the bowl as broken clutter.

Step 2: Her mother sees it as memory.

Step 3: The conflict creates space for Lena's understanding to change.

Example 3: Changed Understanding

Passage: In the new apartment, Lena places the cracked bowl on the windowsill before unpacking anything else.

Step 1: Action shows change.

Step 2: Lena now treats the bowl as meaningful.

Step 3: Her coming-of-age moment is realizing that home can be carried through memory.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Coming of age means the character has a birthday.

This is incorrect because coming of age is about growth in understanding, not just age.

Correct: Coming of age means the character's perspective changes because of conflict, experience, or realization.
