Worked Example: Three Earned-Change Models

Example 1 — Revise Obedience Into Judgment

Weak: A teen matures by accepting that her coach is always right.

  1. Initial belief: asking for rest shows weakness.
  2. Coach’s defensible view: consistency builds trust.
  3. Counterevidence: a teammate hides an injury.
  4. Choice: the protagonist reports the injury and risks her position.
  5. Consequence: teammate receives care; trust with coach is strained.
  6. Earned change: she distinguishes commitment from silence.

Example 2 — Two Defensible Perspectives

A student wants to publish a family history; an aunt wants to keep one painful event private.

  1. Student values preservation and accuracy.
  2. Aunt values consent and protection.
  3. Both possess different evidence.
  4. Turn: another relative requests inclusion.
  5. Choice: publish a documented gap rather than the private account.
  6. Growth: the student learns that accuracy includes naming limits.

Example 3 — Change Through Action

A protagonist who mocks a neighbor’s emergency supplies faces a blackout.

  1. Do not end with “He learned preparation mattered.”
  2. Counterevidence: supplies help, but hoarding excludes others.
  3. Choice: he organizes shared distribution rather than copying the neighbor.
  4. Cost: he gives up his own guaranteed portion.
  5. Revised action shows a qualified insight about preparation and community.
  6. Ending preserves disagreement over how much is enough.
