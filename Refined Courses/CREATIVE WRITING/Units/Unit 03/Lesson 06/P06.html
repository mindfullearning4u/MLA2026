Checkpoint: Coming of Age and Conflicting Perspectives

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

New Source: The Apprenticeship Letter

Sana held the acceptance letter behind her back. The coastal workshop had chosen only four apprentices, and she was one of them. Her grandfather studied the fishing nets piled beside the door. “Your hands are needed here,” he said. Sana looked at the patched roof, then at the train schedule tucked inside the envelope. “If I never learn to build stronger boats,” she answered, “our work stays exactly as fragile as it is now.”

A coming-of-age arc shows a young person gaining maturity through difficult choices. Conflicting perspectives are differing interpretations shaped by each person’s values, experience, and needs.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Explain the values and concerns behind Sana’s perspective and her grandfather’s perspective. Use evidence for both.
  2. What choice could reveal Sana’s growth without making either character simply right or wrong?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which plan creates a more complex coming-of-age conflict?

A. Make the grandfather selfish and unreasonable so readers automatically support Sana.
B. Give both characters valid concerns, then show Sana making a choice that accepts responsibility for its consequences.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write a 450–600 word coming-of-age scene with two credible perspectives.

  1. Opening: Establish the young character’s goal, the relationship, and what is at stake.
  2. Development: Use dialogue and action to reveal the reasoning behind both perspectives.
  3. Turning point: Require the young character to make a difficult choice and accept a cost.
  4. Resolution: Show a consequence that demonstrates growth without erasing the conflict.

Add a 3–4 sentence reflection explaining how the turning point shows maturity.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Find one place where a perspective seems flat or unfair. Add a detail that makes the character’s reasoning more credible without resolving the disagreement.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
