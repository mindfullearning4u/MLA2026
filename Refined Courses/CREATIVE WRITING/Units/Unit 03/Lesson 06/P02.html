Notebook Title

Lesson 6: Coming of Age and Conflicting Perspectives

Vocabulary

  • Coming of age: A story pattern in which a character grows in understanding or maturity.
  • Perspective: A character's way of seeing a situation.
  • Conflicting perspectives: Different characters understand the same situation in different ways.
  • Realization: A new understanding that changes a character.
  • Blind spot: Something a character does not yet understand.
  • Turning point: The moment when a choice, event, or realization changes the direction of the scene.

Step 1: Identify the Starting Belief

A coming-of-age scene often begins with a character who believes something incomplete.

Passage: Talia thought asking for help would prove she was not ready for the advanced art class.

Starting belief: Talia believes independence means never needing help.

Step 2: Identify the Conflicting Perspective

Another character may see the situation differently.

Conflicting perspective: Talia's teacher believes asking for critique is part of serious artistic growth.

The conflict is not just an argument. It is two different beliefs about what growth means.

Step 3: Identify the Change

A coming-of-age moment needs change. Talia changes when she asks for critique and realizes help does not erase skill. It strengthens it.

Changed understanding: Talia learns that maturity includes seeking feedback.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary words and definitions.

Part B: Copy the Talia example.

Part C: Identify the starting belief, conflicting perspective, and changed understanding.
