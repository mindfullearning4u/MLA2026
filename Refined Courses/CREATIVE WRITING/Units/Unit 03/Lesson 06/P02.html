Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
coming-of-age narrativeA story centered on a significant change in maturity, identity, responsibility, or understanding.The coming-of-age narrative follows a teenager learning to accept responsibility.
maturationGrowth in judgment or responsibility produced through experience and reflection.Admitting the harm he caused marks the character’s maturation.
worldviewA person’s organized assumptions about how people and society work.Experience changes her worldview about fairness and power.
perspectiveA position shaped by knowledge, experience, values, and interests.The narrator’s perspective shifts after hearing the opposing account.
conflicting perspectivesDifferent interpretations or priorities that cannot all be satisfied at once.The siblings’ conflicting perspectives create tension about leaving home.
defensible positionA viewpoint supported by understandable evidence or values, even if imperfect.The character’s actions and evidence support a defensible position about his growth.
counterevidenceEvidence that challenges or limits an initial belief.His final selfish decision serves as counterevidence to the claim that he has fully changed.
recognitionA moment when a character understands something previously missed or resisted.The protagonist’s recognition that fear guided her choices begins the climax.
agencyA character’s capacity to make meaningful choices and affect outcomes.Choosing to speak publicly demonstrates agency.
costly choiceA decision that requires a real sacrifice, risk, or loss.Returning the stolen money is a costly choice because it risks punishment.
internal conflictStruggle among competing beliefs, desires, loyalties, or fears within a character.The desire for approval conflicts with personal values, creating internal conflict.
earned changeA believable transformation prepared by pressure, evidence, choice, and consequence.The final decision grows from earlier failures, making the change feel earned.
