Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
author purposeThe result a writer intends to produce for a particular audience.The author’s purpose is to persuade readers to support the library.
audienceThe specific readers or listeners a work addresses or anticipates.A writer adjusts examples for an audience of younger students.
rhetoricThe purposeful use of language, form, evidence, and arrangement to influence understanding or response.The speech uses rhetoric to shape how listeners understand the proposal.
ethosAn appeal based on credibility, character, or trustworthiness.Citing experience as a firefighter builds ethos.
logosAn appeal based on reasons, evidence, patterns, or causal logic.Statistics about response times appeal to logos.
pathosAn appeal to emotion, values, or imagination.A story about a displaced family appeals to pathos.
kairosThe strategic use of timing, urgency, and situation.Delivering the argument before the vote takes advantage of kairos.
rhetorical situationThe relationship among writer, audience, purpose, context, and constraints.The speaker, audience, purpose, and occasion form the rhetorical situation.
intended effectThe response or understanding a writer aims to create.The intended effect is for readers to question the policy.
craft choiceA deliberate decision about structure, viewpoint, diction, syntax, imagery, detail, or pacing.Repeating one sentence at key moments is a deliberate craft choice.
manipulationInfluence that distorts evidence, exploits vulnerability, or blocks informed judgment.Hiding important evidence to provoke fear becomes manipulation.
rhetorical alignmentA clear fit among purpose, audience, evidence, form, and style.Formal language and verified data create rhetorical alignment with a skeptical audience.
