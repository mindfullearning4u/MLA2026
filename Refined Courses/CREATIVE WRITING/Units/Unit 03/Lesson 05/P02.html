Notebook Title

Lesson 5: Author Purpose, Rhetoric, and Craft Choices

Vocabulary

  • Author purpose: The reason a writer creates a passage or makes a craft choice.
  • Rhetoric: Purposeful language choices that shape how readers think, feel, or respond.
  • Reader effect: What a writer wants the reader to notice, feel, infer, question, or believe.
  • Tone: The writer's attitude toward the subject.
  • Craft choice: A specific decision about detail, structure, point of view, dialogue, pacing, imagery, or word choice.
  • Rationale: A clear explanation of how a choice supports purpose.

Step 1: Identify the Purpose

Do not begin with a random detail. First ask what the writer is trying to do.

Passage: The narrator describes the empty chair at dinner three times, but never explains who is missing.

Possible purpose: The writer wants readers to feel absence before they understand it fully.

The repeated empty chair is not just decoration. It creates a question and a mood.

Step 2: Identify the Craft Choice

A craft choice is something the writer controls. In the empty chair example, the craft choice is repetition of a concrete image. The writer repeats the chair instead of immediately explaining the missing person.

Planning sentence: The writer repeats the image of the empty chair to make absence visible and create quiet tension.

Step 3: Explain Reader Effect

Reader effect explains what the choice does. The empty chair makes readers notice loss before the narrator explains it. That delayed explanation creates curiosity and sadness. A strong answer connects purpose, evidence, and effect.

Self-Paced Learning Path

If you need remedial support: Use this frame: The writer uses ____ to make the reader ____.

If you are ready for standard work: Identify purpose, craft choice, evidence, and reader effect.

If you are ready for accelerated work: Compare two possible craft choices and explain which better supports the intended purpose.
