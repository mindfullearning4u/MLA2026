P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Author Purpose, Rhetoric, and Craft Choices

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.WR.02 - Author purpose, rhetoric, and craft choices.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.WR.04 - Rhetoric and craft analysis; MLA.CW.COM.03 - Voice, tone, and purpose.

What You Will Learn

  • How author purpose shapes fiction and creative nonfiction craft choices.
  • How rhetoric means purposeful use of language to influence reader response.
  • How tone, detail, structure, and point of view support purpose.
  • How to explain a writer's craft choice using evidence.

What You Will Do

  • Identify author purpose in short creative passages.
  • Connect rhetorical and craft choices to reader effect.
  • Practice explaining why a writer chooses a specific detail, tone, or structure.
  • Apply purpose-driven choices to your own fiction planning.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery when you can state a writer's purpose, identify a craft choice, cite passage evidence, and explain how the choice shapes reader response.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard means you are learning to ask, "What is the writer trying to make the reader think, feel, notice, or question, and how did the writer make that happen?"

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help

Ask your TOR if you can name a detail but cannot explain why the writer included it.

Student Road Map for This Lesson

Use this overview as a checklist for Author Purpose, Rhetoric, and Craft Choices. Before moving forward, make sure you can say what the lesson standard requires, what product you will create or analyze, and how mastery will be judged. This course is self-paced, so do not wait for a classroom explanation before you work. Read the prompt, model, scenario, or passage on the page first. Then name the exact craft skill from MLA.CW.WR.02, MLA.CW.WR.04, MLA.CW.COM.03, explain what the writer is doing, and connect that choice to reader effect, meaning, voice, mood, structure, or revision purpose. If the task asks you to write, draft in complete sentences and make the craft move visible rather than hidden. If the task asks you to analyze, quote or describe the specific words that prove your answer. Keep your work inside this lesson focus; do not add unrelated literature analysis, research, or personal response unless the task specifically asks for it.
