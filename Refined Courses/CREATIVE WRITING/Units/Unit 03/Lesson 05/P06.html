New Source: The Last Archive

“If we sell the archive,” Devon told the council, “we do not simply lose shelves of paper. We lose the letters that prove who built this town, the flood records that can protect our homes, and the voices our children have not heard yet. I have spent six months cataloging those records with trained historians. Give us one year to turn this room into a public resource before you turn it into storage.”

Author purpose is the effect a writer intends to create. Ethos builds credibility, pathos appeals to emotion, and logos uses reasons and evidence.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Identify Devon’s audience and purpose. Then cite one example of ethos, pathos, or logos and explain how it serves that purpose.
  2. How do the concrete details about letters, flood records, and children strengthen the appeal more than a general claim would?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which statement is better tailored to persuade the council, and why?

A. The archive matters a lot, and everyone should agree with me.
B. The flood records can protect homes, and the letters preserve voices future residents deserve to hear.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write a 400–500 word persuasive monologue spoken by a character facing an important decision.

  1. Opening: Establish the audience, situation, and a clear claim.
  2. Body: Develop the claim with at least two rhetorical appeals and specific evidence.
  3. Counterpoint: Acknowledge one reasonable objection and respond to it.
  4. Conclusion: Synthesize the strongest reason and end with a purposeful call or final image.

Label two sentences to show which rhetorical appeals they use and explain why each fits the audience.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Revise one appeal that is too general. Add a precise detail, reason, or image that better fits the audience and purpose.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
