P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how purpose, rhetoric, and craft connect.

Example 1: Purpose and Tone

Passage: "Of course the award went to the student who arrived late, forgot the speech, and bowed like royalty."

Step 1: Purpose: criticize unfair praise.

Step 2: Craft choice: sarcastic tone.

Step 3: Effect: readers question the fairness of the award.

Example 2: Purpose and Detail

Passage: The narrator mentions the cracked cup every time the family avoids talking about the move.

Step 1: Purpose: show tension without direct explanation.

Step 2: Craft choice: repeated object detail.

Step 3: Effect: the cup becomes a visible sign of family strain.

Example 3: Purpose and Structure

Passage: The scene begins at the locked door, then reveals in flashback why the narrator is afraid to knock.

Step 1: Purpose: create curiosity before explanation.

Step 2: Craft choice: delayed backstory.

Step 3: Effect: readers experience uncertainty before context.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The writer uses details to make the story longer.

This is incorrect because strong details are purposeful, not filler.

Correct: The writer uses repeated details to guide reader attention and develop mood, tension, or theme.
