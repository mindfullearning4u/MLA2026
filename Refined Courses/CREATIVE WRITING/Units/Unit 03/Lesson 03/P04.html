P04 Worked Example

Study the examples to see how adaptation preserves and transforms source elements.

Example 1: A Journey Pattern

Source element: A character leaves home, faces a test, and returns changed.

Step 1: Preserve the journey structure.

Step 2: Transform the setting into a first-generation college visit.

Step 3: Rationale: The adaptation keeps the emotional pattern of leaving and returning with new understanding, but the conflict becomes realistic and modern.

Example 2: A Symbolic Object

Source element: A guide gives the hero a protective object.

Step 1: Preserve the object as a source of courage.

Step 2: Transform the object into a handwritten recipe from a grandparent.

Step 3: Rationale: The adaptation changes protection from physical safety to emotional confidence and family memory.

Example 3: A Pride Conflict

Source element: A character ignores a warning because of pride.

Step 1: Preserve the warning and pride conflict.

Step 2: Transform the setting into a student livestream where a performer refuses to test the equipment.

Step 3: Rationale: The new version updates the conflict while keeping the theme that pride can make a person ignore help.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The adaptation is strong because it uses the same plot, same ending, and same lesson.

This is incorrect because it sounds like copying rather than transforming.

Correct: The adaptation is strong because it keeps the warning-and-pride pattern but changes the setting, stakes, and character perspective.
