Worked Example: Three Adaptation Decisions

Example 1 — Transforming the Midas Pattern

Public-domain pattern: A wish turns everything valuable into unusable gold.

  1. DNA: desire for value → granted power → escalating consequence → recognition.
  2. New context: a designer’s algorithm makes every online post profitable.
  3. Transformation: attention, not gold, consumes private relationships.
  4. New question: What becomes worthless when every interaction becomes content?
  5. Recognition: the causal pattern remains visible.
  6. Independent value: the new system changes stakes and choices.
  7. Attribution: note the adaptation of the Midas tradition and the version consulted.

Example 2 — Changing Viewpoint

Source pattern: A celebrated hero returns home after a long journey.

  1. New focalizer: the sibling who managed the household during the absence.
  2. Retained DNA: return, recognition, restored claim.
  3. Transformation: homecoming becomes a conflict over invisible labor.
  4. New effect: readers question who paid for the hero’s adventure.
  5. Boundary: avoid copying distinctive language or sacred elements from a specific living tradition without context.
  6. Research: identify the exact classical version being engaged.

Example 3 — Responsible Decision Not to Use

A writer finds a ceremonial story summarized on an unsourced website and wants its sacred animal figure for atmosphere.

  1. Problem: no exact source or community context.
  2. Risk: sacred meaning becomes decoration.
  3. Insufficient transformation does not solve missing authority.
  4. Responsible choice: pause, consult reliable community-authored sources, or select a documented public-domain literary source.
  5. Lesson: ethical boundaries are craft decisions because context shapes meaning.
  6. Outcome: “do not use yet” can be the strongest revision.
