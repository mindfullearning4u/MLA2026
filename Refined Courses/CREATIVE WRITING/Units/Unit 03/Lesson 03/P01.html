P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Adaptation of mythic, classical, or religious sources

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.FIC.04 - Adaptation of mythic, classical, or religious sources.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.WR.03 - Paraphrase and interpretation; MLA.CW.WR.08 - Evidence-based craft reasoning.

What You Will Learn

  • How adaptation uses a source as a creative foundation.
  • How to keep a source element while transforming setting, character, conflict, or point of view.
  • How to adapt respectfully and avoid copying or misrepresenting a source.
  • How to explain adaptation choices with evidence.

What You Will Do

  • Identify source elements in short model passages.
  • Choose what to preserve and what to transform.
  • Draft adaptation planning notes.
  • Explain how adaptation changes meaning or reader effect.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery when you can identify a source element, explain how an adaptation transforms it, and support your explanation with details from the adapted scene.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard means you are learning how writers borrow a structure, character type, conflict, image, or theme from older stories and make something new. Adaptation is not copying. Adaptation means making purposeful changes and being able to explain those choices.

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help

Ask your TOR if you are unsure whether your idea is an adaptation, a summary, or a copy of the source.
