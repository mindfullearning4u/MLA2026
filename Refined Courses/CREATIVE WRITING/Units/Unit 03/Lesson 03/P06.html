New Source: Source Pattern: The Ant and the Grasshopper

In a traditional fable, an ant works steadily to store food while a grasshopper spends the warm season singing. When winter arrives, their different choices produce different consequences. Retellings often preserve the contrast between preparation and immediate pleasure but may question what responsibility the characters owe one another.

An adaptation transforms an existing source into a new work. Structural DNA means the recognizable pattern—such as a conflict, role, sequence, or theme—that remains even when setting, character, and outcome change.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Identify two pieces of structural DNA in the source pattern. Explain why each is essential rather than decorative.
  2. How could a writer transform the ending to create a different theme while keeping the source recognizable?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which plan creates a true adaptation rather than a close copy?

A. Rename the ant and grasshopper, but copy the setting, events, and moral.
B. Move the preparation-versus-pleasure conflict to a new setting, reshape the characters’ motives, and change the consequence to develop a fresh theme.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write a 450–600 word original adaptation of the source pattern.

  1. Opening: Establish a distinctly new setting, characters, and stakes.
  2. Development: Preserve at least two pieces of structural DNA while transforming the events.
  3. Turning point: Force a choice connected to preparation, pleasure, or shared responsibility.
  4. Resolution: Create a consequence that develops your own theme.

Add a brief source note naming the pattern you adapted and a 3–4 sentence rationale explaining what you preserved, what you transformed, and why.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Revise one element that feels too close to the source. Make the transformation more original while preserving recognizable structural DNA.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
