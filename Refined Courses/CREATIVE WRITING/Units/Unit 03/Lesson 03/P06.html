Checkpoint: “Emergency Test”

Directions

Source Pattern

Source pattern — “The Boy Who Cried Wolf,” attributed to Aesop: A young shepherd repeatedly raises a false alarm. When danger truly comes, the community no longer believes him.

Adaptation

Adaptation — “Emergency Test”: A building manager sends so many unnecessary emergency alerts that residents begin silencing their phones. When smoke enters the basement, the automated system sends another identical message. A teenager who notices the smell knocks on doors personally, but several neighbors assume she is promoting another drill. The building is evacuated only after she changes the message from an official warning to a specific observation: “Smoke is coming under apartment 1C.”

Analysis and Transfer

  1. Map the source’s structural DNA.
  2. State the source’s likely theme.
  3. Identify retained roles, conflict, turn, and consequence.
  4. Identify three meaningful transformations.
  5. Explain how technology changes responsibility.
  6. Analyze the teenager’s specific observation as the new turn.
  7. State the adaptation’s new thematic question.
  8. Complete the Visual Adaptation Map.
  9. Explain the attribution requirement.
  10. Identify one ethical boundary for this adaptation.
  11. Revise one element so the adaptation questions rather than repeats the source’s lesson.
  12. Write a 350–450 word adaptation of a documented public-domain literary source with a map, attribution note, and 120–150 word rationale.
