Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
source textThe earlier work, tradition, or account an adaptation engages.The original folktale is the source text for the new story.
adaptationA new work that purposefully transforms recognizable elements of a source.A film adaptation changes the setting while preserving the central conflict.
retellingA new presentation that largely preserves the source’s central sequence and meaning.A retelling presents an old tale through a minor character’s viewpoint.
allusionA brief, indirect reference that depends on readers recognizing another work or tradition.The title alludes to a familiar myth without retelling it completely.
structural DNAThe source’s core pattern of roles, conflict, turning point, and consequence.The quest, trial, and return form the source story’s structural DNA.
transformationA meaningful change in context, viewpoint, stakes, genre, structure, or theme.Changing the hero into the antagonist creates a meaningful transformation.
intertextualityThe way a work gains meaning through relationships with other texts.Intertextuality occurs when the new poem answers lines and ideas from an earlier work.
attributionClear acknowledgment of the source or tradition being used.A brief note provides attribution to the traditional story that inspired the piece.
cultural contextThe histories, beliefs, practices, and power relationships shaping a source.Understanding the community’s history provides cultural context for the symbol.
sacred narrativeA story held as spiritually authoritative or significant within a living tradition.The writer treats a sacred narrative with care and consultation.
appropriationUse of cultural material in a way that removes context, exploits unequal power, or causes harm.Using a community’s story as decoration without understanding it risks appropriation.
ethical boundaryA deliberate limit established to avoid distortion, disrespect, or exploitation.The writer sets an ethical boundary by not altering a living group’s ceremonial text.
