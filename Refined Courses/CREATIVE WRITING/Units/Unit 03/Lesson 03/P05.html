Independent Work: Source and Adaptation Study

Directions

Read both texts. Complete all 18 tasks and submit with Page 2 vocabulary.

Source Pattern

Source summary — “The Fisherman and the Little Fish,” attributed to Aesop: A fisherman catches a small fish. The fish asks to be released, promising that if allowed to grow, it will later provide a larger meal. The fisherman refuses because a certain small gain is worth more to him than a possible future gain. Versions differ in wording and emphasis; this summary preserves the core bargaining pattern rather than claiming one definitive original.

Adaptation

Adaptation — “The Last Battery”: During a citywide outage, Jo finds one charged battery in the community supply cabinet. A neighbor asks her to save it for the emergency radio later, when official instructions may arrive. Jo uses it immediately to power a small fan for her feverish brother. Hours later, floodwater reaches the ground floor and the radio remains silent.

Jo expects blame, but the neighbor admits that no future warning could make the earlier choice simple. Together they turn the powerless radio into a hand-cranked signal bell. Its first ring brings residents from the upper floors.

Source and Transformation Analysis

  1. Summarize the source’s structural DNA.
  2. Identify its competing values.
  3. State one likely traditional lesson and qualify it.
  4. Map retained elements in the adaptation.
  5. Map transformed context, roles, stakes, turn, and consequence.
  6. Explain how the outage changes the certainty-versus-future conflict.
  7. Analyze why Jo’s choice cannot be reduced to greed.
  8. Explain how the ending complicates the source pattern.
  9. Identify the adaptation’s new thematic question.
  10. Complete the Visual Adaptation Map.

Craft, Vocabulary, and Ethics

  1. Use context to define hand-cranked as used in the passage.
  2. Explain why the summary names attribution and variation.
  3. Identify one detail that gives the adaptation independent value.
  4. Propose one ethical boundary for adapting a source from a living sacred tradition.
  5. Rewrite the ending to preserve the source’s original certainty-focused lesson.

Original Adaptation

  1. Select a documented public-domain literary source and cite the exact version. Complete source research notes and the four-column map.
  2. Write a 650–800 word adaptation with recognizable DNA, at least two structural transformations, a new controlling effect, and an attribution note.
  3. Write a 200–250 word rationale explaining research, transformation, ethical boundary, and independent value.
