Independent Work: Integrated Fiction Development

Directions

Complete all 18 tasks. Then write the polished story and craft rationale. Use complete sentences and cite precise moments from your draft when explaining decisions. Submit this work with your Page 2 vocabulary notes as Notebook Evidence.

Story Seed: “The Archive Key”

On the morning the town archive is scheduled to close, sixteen-year-old Mara finds a brass key inside a returned library book. A handwritten tag reads, “Room 3—before the bell.” Her older brother insists the archive should close because its records once harmed their family. Mara must decide whether to use the key, return it, or destroy it before the noon hearing. Archive means a place where historical records are preserved.

Part A — Build the Narrative System

  1. State the story’s controlling effect in one precise sentence.
  2. Write a focused premise naming protagonist, desire, opposition, stakes, and pressure.
  3. Explain the central conflict and why it cannot be solved by a simple conversation.
  4. Create an access map showing what Mara, her brother, and the archivist each know, believe, and conceal.
  5. Identify two details that will function as setup and explain their later payoff.
  6. Plan a five-scene sequence: setup, complication, escalation, reversal, and payoff.

Part B — Control Cause, Perspective, and Meaning

  1. For each scene, identify the choice that causes the next event.
  2. Plan one reversal—a discovery or change that forces a new interpretation—without relying on coincidence.
  3. Choose the narrative viewpoint and explain what it reveals and withholds.
  4. Write one recurring image or object motif and trace its changing meaning across three moments.
  5. State the emerging theme as a claim about people or choices, not as a one-word topic.
  6. Plan the ending so it answers the central conflict while leaving one productive question unresolved.

Part C — Draft and Revise

  1. Draft a sustained 900–1,100 word story using the plan.
  2. Mark three transitions where time, place, or focus shifts; revise any unclear movement.
  3. Underline two places where sensory detail creates pressure rather than decoration.
  4. Revise one scene globally by changing its purpose, order, conflict, or outcome.
  5. Revise one paragraph locally for precise diction, sentence rhythm, and continuity.
  6. Write a 220–280 word craft rationale explaining how your throughline, reversal, setup/payoff, viewpoint, and ending create the controlling effect.
