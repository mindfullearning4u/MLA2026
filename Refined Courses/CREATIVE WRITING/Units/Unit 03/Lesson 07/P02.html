Notebook Title

Lesson 7: Integrated Fiction Development

Vocabulary

  • Integrated fiction: Fiction in which theme, conflict, character change, setting, and craft choices support one purpose.
  • Theme pattern: A recurring idea such as courage, belonging, identity, responsibility, or sacrifice.
  • Source influence: A mythic, classical, religious, or traditional element transformed into a new story.
  • Perspective conflict: A disagreement caused by different ways of seeing the same situation.
  • Growth arc: The movement from a starting belief to changed understanding.

Step 1: Choose the Central Idea

Start with one controlling idea. Example: Maturity can mean accepting help instead of pretending to be independent. This idea will guide the character, conflict, and details.

Step 2: Build the Conflict and Perspective

Give the character a starting belief that is incomplete. A character might believe, "If I ask for help, I am weak." A mentor, friend, or sibling may hold the conflicting perspective: "Asking for help is how serious people improve."

Step 3: Add Source Influence or Theme Comparison

You may adapt a source element, such as a guide giving an object before a journey. In a modern scene, a coach might hand the character a marked-up draft before a performance. The source influence should support the same central idea rather than distract from it.

Self-Paced Learning Path

If you need remedial support: Fill in one sentence for central idea, one for starting belief, and one for changed understanding.

If you are ready for standard work: Add a source influence and two supporting details.

If you are ready for accelerated work: Explain how your adaptation choice changes the emotional effect of the theme.
