Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
controlling effectThe primary experience or understanding the writer intends to create.Every scene supports a controlling effect of mounting distrust.
narrative systemThe interacting structure of character, conflict, setting, viewpoint, time, style, and meaning.Character goals, setting pressure, and plot turns operate as a narrative system.
story premiseA concise statement of protagonist, pressure, situation, and stakes.A medic must choose between obeying orders and saving a stranger; that is the story premise.
throughlineThe continuing desire, question, or conflict connecting a narrative’s parts.The protagonist’s effort to uncover the truth forms the throughline.
scene sequenceAn ordered set of scenes in which each changes pressure or knowledge.The scene sequence moves logically from discovery to confrontation to consequence.
setupAn early element that prepares a later development.An early mention of the broken radio provides setup.
payoffA later fulfillment, reversal, or transformation of a setup.The radio’s final transmission delivers the payoff.
escalationAn increase or complication in pressure, cost, or uncertainty.Each obstacle raises the risk, creating escalation.
reversalA development that changes the apparent direction or meaning of events.The trusted guide’s betrayal creates a reversal.
global revisionRevision of purpose, structure, viewpoint, causality, and development.Removing an entire subplot is a global revision.
local revisionRevision of sentences, diction, imagery, rhythm, and conventions.Clarifying punctuation is a local revision.
continuityConsistency of factual, temporal, spatial, and character information.Keeping the scar on the same cheek preserves continuity.
