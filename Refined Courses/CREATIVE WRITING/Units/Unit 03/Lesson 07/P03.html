Direct Teaching: Designing the Fiction System

Nine-Step Manuscript Method

  1. State premise and controlling effect.
  2. Define protagonist desire, worldview, and stakes.
  3. Create a defensible opposing perspective.
  4. Map the evidence chain and thematic question.
  5. Choose viewpoint and access limits.
  6. Sequence scenes so each changes pressure or knowledge.
  7. Plant setups and schedule payoffs.
  8. Draft the decisive scenes first, then connective material.
  9. Revise globally, audit continuity, then revise locally.

Pause and Choose: Strengthen the Scene Throughline

A student discovers altered archive dates, argues with a sibling about privacy, attends a party, and later exposes the records after the curator confesses. Which revision makes the protagonist’s choices drive the sequence?

A. Add vivid party decorations and make the curator’s confession more emotional.
Not the best choice. Stronger description does not connect the party to the evidence chain, and the confession still solves the conflict for the protagonist.
B. Turn the party into a fundraiser where the protagonist recognizes a watermark, then chooses to project that mismatch at the hearing.
✓ Correct—the scene adds knowledge and produces agency. The fundraiser now advances the investigation, plants evidence, and creates a choice that causes the confrontation and payoff.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Global Before Local

  1. Purpose and controlling effect
  2. Character agency and conflict
  3. Causality and scene order
  4. Viewpoint and information
  5. Setup, payoff, and theme
  6. Pacing, tone, and style
  7. Continuity and conventions

Pause and Choose: Revise Globally Before Locally

A manuscript has elegant prose, but its reversal depends on coincidence and two setups never pay off. What should the writer revise first?

A. Replace repeated words and improve punctuation throughout the draft.
Not the best choice. Local polish cannot repair causality or unused setups and may waste effort on scenes that need restructuring or removal.
B. Redesign the reversal so an earlier setup enables a costly protagonist choice, then audit scene order before editing sentences.
✓ Correct—the revision addresses structural dependency. Repairing setup, agency, consequence, and sequence establishes the manuscript the sentence-level work must support.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Common Failures

  • Drafting attractive scenes with no throughline.
  • Escalating volume instead of cost or uncertainty.
  • Solving conflict through coincidence.
  • Planting setups that never pay off.
  • Polishing sentences before repairing structure.

Pause and Try — Diagnose the Weakest Link

Draft sequence: A student discovers altered archive dates, argues with a sibling about family privacy, attends an unrelated party, and then exposes the records when the curator unexpectedly confesses.

Your turn: Identify the weakest link in the narrative system. Name one global revision that would repair the throughline and causality.

Check your thinking with the teacher model

The unrelated party breaks the throughline, and the unexpected confession removes the protagonist’s agency. I would transform the party into a fundraiser where the protagonist recognizes a watermark on the curator’s invitation, then chooses to project the mismatch at the hearing.

Why this works: The revised scene adds knowledge, the choice causes the confrontation, and the setup earns the payoff. This is global revision because it repairs scene purpose and causality before sentence-level polish.
