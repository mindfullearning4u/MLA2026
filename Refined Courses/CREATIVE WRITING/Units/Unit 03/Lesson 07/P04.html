Worked Example: Three System-Level Revisions

Example 1 — Repair the Throughline

A draft follows Nia through school, work, and home but lacks connection.

  1. Premise: Nia wants proof that a closed clinic falsified records.
  2. Throughline: every scene changes access to the records.
  3. School scene supplies a translation skill.
  4. Work scene reveals altered dates.
  5. Home scene creates a conflict over family privacy.
  6. Unrelated friendship scene is removed or tied to the choice.
  7. Result: varied settings support one developing question.

Example 2 — Replace Coincidence

Weak: The missing witness happens to enter the café.

  1. Plant a receipt showing the witness visits weekly.
  2. Make the protagonist choose whether to wait and miss an obligation.
  3. Use a prior favor to explain why the server gives information.
  4. Payoff follows setup and agency.
  5. Consequence: waiting damages trust elsewhere.
  6. Result: discovery feels caused, not delivered.

Example 3 — Align Ending and Theme

A story about community memory ends when a museum accepts one artifact.

  1. Problem: institutional approval solves too much.
  2. Earlier setup: residents disagree over the artifact’s label.
  3. Reversal: acceptance requires a single official interpretation.
  4. Choice: protagonist donates the object with multiple recorded accounts.
  5. Payoff: the display includes disagreement rather than erasing it.
  6. Theme gains qualification: preservation requires room for contested meaning.
