Passage: “The Last Broadcast”

At 11:47 p.m., Imani entered the school radio booth to record the final announcement before the building closed for renovation. Her father’s voice—captured twenty years earlier—waited on an unlabeled tape beside the console. The principal had forbidden unapproved recordings, and the demolition crew would arrive at dawn. When Imani pressed play, she heard not a speech but a rehearsal: her father repeatedly admitting that he had taken credit for a classmate’s winning script. A second voice interrupted him, laughing, “Tell them tomorrow.” The tape snapped before his reply. In the hallway, the motion lights went dark one row at a time. Imani placed the tape beside the microphone, opened the live channel, and watched the red ON AIR sign ignite.

Analysis and Revision

  1. State the passage’s controlling effect and support it with two details.
  2. Identify Imani’s desire, opposition, and stakes.
  3. Explain the throughline from discovery to final choice.
  4. Draw a three-person access map for Imani, her father, and the principal; distinguish known facts from assumptions.
  5. Explain how the countdown and darkening lights control pacing.
  6. Analyze the tape as both plot device and motif.
  7. Define rehearsal from context and explain which clue supports your definition.
  8. Identify the setup and predict two plausible payoffs. Explain which better serves the controlling effect.
  9. Write a 90–120 word continuation that includes a causally earned reversal.
  10. Revise one sentence from your continuation to strengthen diction and rhythm; show both versions.
  11. Create an annotated five-part blueprint for an original 450–550 word scene: premise, escalation, reversal, choice, payoff. Then write the scene.
  12. Write a 150–180 word rationale explaining how viewpoint, causality, motif, and ending integrate to create meaning.

Mastery Check

Before submitting, confirm: every event follows from a choice or established pressure; the reversal changes interpretation; the ending pays off earlier details; the viewpoint controls access; and your rationale names evidence from your writing.
