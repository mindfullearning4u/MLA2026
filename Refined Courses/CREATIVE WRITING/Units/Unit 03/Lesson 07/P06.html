New Source: The Signal Tower Log

At dawn, Keon found a fresh entry in the abandoned signal tower’s logbook: DO NOT LIGHT THE WESTERN LAMP. The handwriting matched his missing sister’s, but the date was tomorrow. Outside, fog erased the harbor, and the ferry carrying two hundred passengers sounded its horn. The western lamp was the only beacon still connected to power.

Integrated fiction development means coordinating character, setting, conflict, point of view, pacing, and theme so each element strengthens the others.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. How do the setting and point of view work together to create conflict and tension? Cite two details.
  2. What two plausible choices does Keon face, and what theme could each consequence develop?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which plan integrates information with pacing more effectively?

A. Explain the tower’s entire history before Keon makes a decision.
B. Reveal only the history Keon needs as the horn, fog, and log entry increase the pressure of his choice.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write a 600–750 word integrated fiction scene using an original situation.

  1. Hook and context: Begin inside a specific setting with an urgent character goal.
  2. Rising conflict: Layer meaningful obstacles and reveal information only when it changes a choice.
  3. Competing choices: Make at least two options understandable and consequential.
  4. Turning point: Use a decisive action that reveals character and develops theme.
  5. Resolution: Show the immediate consequence and close with a resonant image.

Add a 150–200 word craft reflection explaining how point of view, setting, pacing, and one repeated detail work together.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Complete one structural revision and one sentence-level revision. Name both changes and explain the intended effect.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
