Independent Work: Comparative Theme Study

Directions

Read both works. Complete all 18 tasks and submit them with Page 2 vocabulary.

Work A — “Blue Lines”

Each spring, Mara’s family painted one new blue line around the doorway to record another year in the house. When flood insurance became unaffordable, they prepared to leave. Mara wanted to cut the marked frame from the wall. Her mother refused: “A record should remain where it happened.”

On moving day, the new owner arrived early. She measured the doorway and said the frame would be replaced. Mara photographed every line, then painted one final line around both their hands. She left the frame but carried the image.

Work B — “The Snow Post”

At the mountain observatory, Kenji recorded snowfall depth by carving a notch into a cedar post. Cedar is a durable, fragrant wood. When automated sensors replaced the post, the director offered it to him.

Kenji declined. He asked that it remain beside the new equipment. Years later, the sensor failed during a storm. A technician compared the drift against the old notches and reconstructed the missing record. Kenji’s marks had stayed useful because they remained where weather could test them.

Separate Analysis

  1. Summarize Work A in 80–100 words.
  2. State Work A’s qualified theme.
  3. Trace its doorway motif and consequence.
  4. Summarize Work B in 80–100 words.
  5. State Work B’s qualified theme.
  6. Trace its post motif and consequence.

Comparative Analysis

  1. Name the shared subject.
  2. Complete the Visual Comparison Bridge using location as the lens.
  3. Pair two pieces of evidence about preserving records.
  4. Explain one parallel in character motivation.
  5. Explain one divergence in choice.
  6. Analyze how home and observatory contexts change meaning.
  7. Explain why the endings do not support identical themes.
  8. Identify and correct one false equivalence.
  9. Write a comparative claim and synthesis paragraph.

Original Comparative Application

  1. Plan two linked 300–350 word scenes that explore one subject through different contexts, choices, and consequences.
  2. Write both scenes, using a parallel motif but developing distinct thematic variations.
  3. Write a 180–220 word comparative rationale with paired evidence, parallel, divergence, context, and synthesis.
