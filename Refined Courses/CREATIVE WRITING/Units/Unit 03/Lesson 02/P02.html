Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
comparisonAnalysis of meaningful similarities and differences using shared criteria.A comparison examines how two protagonists respond to pressure.
contrastAnalysis of how elements differ and why the difference matters.A contrast shows that one ending offers hope while the other remains unresolved.
comparative claimAn arguable sentence stating a meaningful relationship between two works.A comparative claim explains a meaningful relationship between two texts.
shared subjectA broad concern explored by multiple works.Both poems address the shared subject of home.
thematic variationA distinct insight one work develops about a shared subject.One text presents belonging as comfort; another presents it as obligation, creating thematic variation.
point of comparisonA common analytical category applied to both works.The role of family is one clear point of comparison.
paired evidenceSpecific evidence from each work selected to illuminate the same comparison.A quotation from each story creates paired evidence for the same claim.
contextThe cultural, historical, social, or situational conditions shaping meaning.Historical context explains why the two authors treat authority differently.
parallelA meaningful similarity in structure, image, character role, or development.The stories use parallel journeys that end in opposite choices.
divergenceA point where works move toward different choices, consequences, or meanings.Their treatment of forgiveness marks a major divergence.
synthesisReasoning that combines findings to produce a larger insight.A synthesis combines evidence from both texts into one larger insight.
false equivalenceTreating importantly different situations as if they were the same.Calling two conflicts identical because both involve families creates a false equivalence.
