Notebook Title

Lesson 2: Comparative Universal Themes

Vocabulary

  • Compare: To explain similarities and differences.
  • Shared theme: A theme developed by more than one work.
  • Theme development: The way a writer builds a theme through conflict, character, setting, images, or structure.
  • Similar: Alike in an important way.
  • Different: Not alike in an important way.
  • Comparative claim: A sentence that explains how two works connect or differ.

Step 1: Understand Each Passage Separately

Do not compare too early. First identify the topic, conflict, character choice, and theme in each passage.

Passage A: Mara gave away her extra coat before the first snow, even though she knew the walk home would be colder.

Passage B: Jin kept the lantern burning in the window until the lost hikers found the road, though the oil was almost gone.

Passage A theme: Generosity can require personal sacrifice.

Passage B theme: Helping others can matter more than keeping comfort for yourself.

Step 2: Find the Shared Idea

Both passages explore generosity. Both characters give up comfort to help someone else. This means the passages share a related universal theme.

Shared theme statement: True generosity often means giving something up for another person's need.

Step 3: Compare How the Theme Is Developed

Now ask how the writers build the theme differently. Passage A uses a coat and cold weather to show sacrifice. Passage B uses a lantern, darkness, and limited oil to show a character choosing others' safety over personal comfort. The theme is similar, but the details are different.

Comparative claim: Both passages show generosity through sacrifice, but Passage A focuses on giving away a physical object while Passage B focuses on continuing a helpful action even when resources are low.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary words and definitions.

Part B: Copy Passage A and Passage B.

Part C: Write one theme statement for each passage and one shared theme statement.
