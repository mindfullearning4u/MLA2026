P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how to compare themes across paired passages.

Example 1: Shared Theme

Passage A: Dana admitted she broke the vase before anyone blamed her little brother.

Passage B: Eli returned the answer key even though he had not studied for the test.

Step 1: Passage A shows honesty protecting someone else.

Step 2: Passage B shows honesty despite personal risk.

Step 3: Shared theme: Honesty matters most when lying would be easier.

Example 2: Similar Theme, Different Development

Passage A: Lina kept practicing the speech after everyone else left.

Passage B: Theo asked for help with the speech after three failed attempts.

Step 1: Both passages connect growth to effort.

Step 2: Passage A develops the theme through persistence alone.

Step 3: Passage B develops the theme through humility and help-seeking.

Comparison: Both passages suggest growth requires effort, but one emphasizes independent practice while the other emphasizes accepting support.

Example 3: Avoiding a Weak Comparison

Weak comparison: Both passages are about people doing things.

Step 1: This is too broad.

Step 2: Look for the deeper idea behind the actions.

Step 3: Stronger comparison: Both passages show that difficult choices reveal character, but one uses confession while the other uses perseverance.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Passage A and Passage B are different because the names are different.

This is incorrect because names are surface details. A meaningful comparison focuses on theme, conflict, character choice, or writer craft.

Correct: Passage A and Passage B both explore honesty, but Passage A connects honesty to protecting someone else while Passage B connects honesty to resisting personal temptation.
