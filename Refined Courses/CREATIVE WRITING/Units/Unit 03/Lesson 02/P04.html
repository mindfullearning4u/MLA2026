Worked Example: Three Comparative Theme Models

Example 1 — Same Subject, Different Consequence

Work A: A daughter preserves a family recipe by teaching it openly. Work B: A son protects a family technique by refusing to share it.

  1. Shared subject: inheritance.
  2. A theme: Tradition survives when knowledge becomes relationship rather than possession.
  3. B theme: Protecting inheritance through secrecy may preserve control while endangering continuity.
  4. Lens: transfer of knowledge.
  5. Parallel: both characters fear loss.
  6. Divergence: teaching expands practice; secrecy isolates it.
  7. Synthesis: preservation requires decisions about ownership as well as memory.

Example 2 — Similar Motif, Different Context

Both works repeat a locked door. In one, a refugee character keeps a temporary room secure; in the other, a wealthy heir seals unused rooms.

  1. Do not claim the locks mean the same thing.
  2. Work A: security responds to displacement and uncertainty.
  3. Work B: closure represents avoidance and excess.
  4. Shared lens: access.
  5. Context changes consequence and ethical meaning.
  6. Synthesis: control over space can protect dignity or preserve exclusion depending on who holds power and why.

Example 3 — Endings Complicate Agreement

Two works suggest truth requires courage. Work A ends with public confession and repair. Work B ends when testimony is ignored but recorded.

  1. Parallel: speakers accept personal risk.
  2. Divergence: one receives immediate change; one preserves evidence for later.
  3. Qualified comparison: Both connect truth with risk, whereas only Work A links courage to visible resolution.
  4. Synthesis: the value of testimony cannot be measured only by immediate success.
  5. Craft link: endings alter the scope of hope.
