Worked Example: Three Style Decisions

Example 1

“The alley was thin” becomes “The alley pinched shut.” Denotation stays narrow; connotation, syntax, and hard sounds create pressure.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which explanation best shows how the revision changes style?

A. “The alley pinched shut” is better because it is more vivid.
Weaker response. “More vivid” is a judgment, not an explanation of how diction, sound, and syntax affect the reader.
B. Replacing “was thin” with “pinched shut” turns a static description into forceful action; the hard sounds and compressed syntax make the space feel restrictive.
Stronger response. This response identifies the exact change and traces multiple style features to a precise feeling of pressure.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2

Child narrator: “The residence deteriorated” is precise but unbelievable. “Our house sagged like it was tired” aligns register, image, and voice.

Example 3

A poem repeats clinical words—specimen, incision, sterile—then ends with “home.” The pattern makes the simple final word intimate and resistant.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: Revise “The hallway was long” to create either comfort or pressure. Explain how one word and the sentence shape that effect.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: For pressure: “The hallway narrowed ahead.” “Narrowed” makes the space seem active, and the short sentence keeps attention on the constriction.
