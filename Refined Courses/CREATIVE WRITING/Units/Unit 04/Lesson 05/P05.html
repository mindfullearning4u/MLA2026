Independent Work: Connotation, Denotation, and Style

Purpose

Control denotation and connotation to establish a consistent poetic style.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Make deliberate choices that demonstrate the lesson skill.

Source for Your Work

“Home,” “shelter,” and “refuge” can point to a protected place, but each carries a different emotional atmosphere and stylistic effect.

1. Plan Before You Draft

Poetic DecisionYour Plan
Speaker, situation, and intended effectState each precisely.
Image or language patternList two connected possibilities.
Turn or developmentDescribe what changes and why.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger poetic craft? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. The speaker returned to the residence, a domicile that was adequate and nice.
B. The speaker returned to the refuge, where one lamp held back the winter dark.

3. Apply the Lesson

Write a 16–20 line poem about return, separation, or belonging. Establish a distinct style through a purposeful pattern of diction, syntax, and imagery. Choose at least four words for their connotations.

4. Revise with Purpose

Create a three-word substitution test for one key line. Select the strongest word and explain why its connotation fits the poem better than the alternatives.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your original writing applies the lesson skill and uses the supplied source or model.
  • Your explanation identifies a specific craft choice and its effect.
  • Your revision improves meaning or craft, not only spelling.
