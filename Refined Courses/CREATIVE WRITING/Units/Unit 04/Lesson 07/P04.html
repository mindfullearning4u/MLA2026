Worked Example: Three Integrated Poems

Example 1 — Image Transformation

Opening: a cracked cup signifies family fracture. Pattern: leak, stain, careful hands. Counterpressure: the speaker keeps using it. Turn: a gold repair line appears. Ending: the cup holds tea again. The image transforms damage into visible endurance; it is not merely repeated.

Example 2 — Sound and Lineation

A chase poem begins with short enjambed lines and hard consonants: “gate / clicked / heel struck.” At safety, longer vowel-rich lines slow the breath. Structure and sound enact the movement rather than simply describe fear and relief.

Example 3 — Productive Ambiguity

A speaker leaves a lamp burning for “you.” Concrete clues support both an absent loved one and the speaker’s future self. The turn—“I finally enter by the other door”—does not solve identity; it changes waiting into agency. Both readings support the controlling effect.
