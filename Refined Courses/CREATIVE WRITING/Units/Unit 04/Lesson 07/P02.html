Notebook Title

Lesson 7: Integrated Poem Development

Vocabulary

  • Integrated poem: A poem that combines several craft choices for one intentional effect.
  • Controlling mood: The main feeling the poem is designed to create.
  • Anchor image: The central image that holds the poem together.
  • Layered meaning: Literal meaning and implied meaning working together.
  • Craft explanation: A written explanation of why the writer made specific choices.

Step 1: Choose a Controlling Mood

Begin with one mood before choosing images. Example mood: quiet determination. If you choose the mood first, every image and word can be tested against that goal.

Step 2: Choose an Anchor Image

Model anchor image: a small green shoot pushing through sidewalk concrete. Literally, this is a plant. Implied meaning may include persistence, growth, or hope under pressure.

Step 3: Draft Around Craft Choices

Model lines:
The sidewalk kept its gray fist closed,
but one green word rose through the crack,
small, bright, and stubborn.

The metaphor green word, the personification gray fist, and the diction stubborn all support quiet determination.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary.

Part B: Copy the model lines.

Part C: Create your own poem plan: mood, anchor image, diction set, and figurative language choice.
