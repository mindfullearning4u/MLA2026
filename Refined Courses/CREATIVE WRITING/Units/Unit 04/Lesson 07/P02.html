Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
controlling effectThe dominant experience designed for the reader.A poem may sustain unease from its opening image through its final line.
generative imageA central image capable of producing multiple related developments.A cracked mirror can generate images of division, memory, and self-recognition.
image systemA connected pattern of images.Doors, keys, locks, and thresholds form an image system about access.
poetic architectureThe deliberate organization of a poem.Three sections move from observation to conflict to recognition.
lineationThe intentional shaping and breaking of poetic lines.A break after “never” briefly changes how the promise is understood.
turnA meaningful change in thought, mood, or perspective.The speaker shifts from blame to self-questioning in the final stanza.
compressionDense expression that carries substantial meaning in few words.“Ash remembers flame” compresses loss, history, and transformation.
resonanceMeaning or feeling that continues beyond the line.An unresolved final image lingers after the poem ends.
counterpressureA competing feeling or idea that complicates the poem.Hope creates counterpressure against the poem’s grief.
global revisionChanging purpose, order, image system, or ending.Moving the final stanza to the opening changes the poem’s entire arc.
local revisionImproving words, sounds, lines, and punctuation.Replacing a weak verb strengthens one line without changing the structure.
coherenceThe meaningful fit among the poem’s choices.Image, sound, and ending all reinforce the controlling effect.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which change is a global revision?

A. Replace “walked” with “staggered” in line 6.

Not the best choice. This is a local revision because it improves one word without changing the poem’s overall design.

B. Reorder the stanzas so the realization appears first and becomes the frame for every image.

Best choice. Reordering the poem changes its architecture, sequence of understanding, and likely controlling effect.
