Worked Example: Three Synthesis Repairs

Example 1 — Paraphrase Before Interpretation

Line: “Only the unlocked window kept the winter out.” Accurate paraphrase: The speaker claims, paradoxically, that the open window—not the closed ones—prevented winter’s influence. Interpretation: openness to memory may protect against emotional numbness. The paradox cannot be analyzed accurately if paraphrased as a literal weather claim.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which analysis correctly handles the paradox in the unlocked-window line?

A. The open window literally keeps cold weather outside, so the speaker is describing unusual insulation.
Weaker response. This paraphrase treats the contradiction as ordinary weather information and therefore cannot explain its larger meaning.
B. The line claims that openness prevents winter’s influence; read figuratively, the paradox may suggest that openness to memory protects against emotional numbness.
Stronger response. This response preserves the paradox in the paraphrase and then offers a qualified interpretation supported by the contrast between openness and winter.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Integrate Diction, Sound, and Turn

Draft repeats soft garden words, then turns with “the shovel struck a name.” Hard consonants break the euphony; “struck” changes gardening into discovery. Revising the next line to return to “root” transforms the earlier growth image into ancestry.

Example 3 — Repair an Ending

Opening image: a paper boat carries an unsent apology. Weak ending: “I felt better.” Repair: rain opens the paper and the ink enters the gutter. The transformed image pays off the setup while leaving ambiguous whether release is healing or loss.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: Paraphrase and interpret “The empty chair was the only guest who stayed.” Keep the paradox intact before explaining it.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: Paraphrase: although no person remained, the unoccupied chair continued to represent a presence. Interpretation: the chair may make an absence feel more persistent than the guests who left.
