Lesson: The Poetry Synthesis Audit

Ten-Step Audit

  1. State literal situation.
  2. Paraphrase complex syntax.
  3. Name controlling effect.
  4. Trace image and semantic fields.
  5. Analyze figurative pattern.
  6. Map denotation, connotation, origin, register, and sound.
  7. Locate lineation effects and the volta.
  8. Test ambiguity with two evidence-based readings.
  9. Check architecture and ending payoff.
  10. Revise globally, then locally, and defend the changes.

Pause and Choose: Diagnose the Whole Poem

A poem has striking metaphors and musical lines, but the final image appears only once and does not alter the speaker’s understanding. Which diagnosis is strongest?

A. The poem only needs more advanced vocabulary in its final line.
Weaker choice. More difficult diction would treat a local symptom. The problem is structural: the ending is not prepared by an image pattern and does not deliver a meaningful turn or payoff.
B. The writer should connect the ending to an earlier image and make the return reveal a changed perspective.
Stronger choice. This repairs the global architecture. A transformed return lets imagery, turn, and meaning reinforce one another before local sound and diction are polished.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Pause and Choose: Defend an Interpretation

Which statement best synthesizes literal meaning, craft evidence, and reader effect?

A. “The repeated closed-window image literally limits the speaker’s view; after the volta, the window opens, so the pattern suggests a shift from avoidance toward uncertain engagement.”
Stronger choice. The claim begins with observable details, traces a change across the turn, and qualifies the interpretation. Its reasoning can be checked against the poem.
B. “The window obviously symbolizes freedom, proving that the speaker is completely healed.”
Weaker choice. “Obviously,” “proving,” and “completely” overstate the evidence. It skips the literal pattern, ignores uncertainty, and treats one symbol as a universal code.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Evidence Frame

The detail ___ literally means ___. Through ___, it suggests ___. When it interacts with ___, the reader experiences ___, supporting the interpretation that ___.
