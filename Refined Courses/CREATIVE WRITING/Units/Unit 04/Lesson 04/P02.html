Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
etymologyThe history and development of a word.Knowing that “disaster” once referred to an unfavorable star adds historical texture.
rootThe core element carrying basic meaning.The root “scrib” connects describe, inscription, and manuscript to writing.
prefixA word part added before a root.The prefix “re-” in rewrite signals doing the writing again.
suffixA word part added after a root.The suffix “-less” changes hope into hopeless.
denotationA word’s direct dictionary meaning.The denotation of “home” is the place where someone lives.
connotationFeelings and associations attached to a word.“Home” may suggest safety, belonging, or conflict depending on context.
dictionA writer’s deliberate word choice.Choosing “staggered” instead of “walked” reveals strain and instability.
registerLanguage level suited to audience, purpose, or context.A formal tribute uses a different register from a text message.
lexical fieldA group of words connected by meaning.“Current,” “tide,” “harbor,” and “drift” create a lexical field of water.
euphonyA smooth or pleasing sound effect.“Low leaves lull the lane” uses flowing sounds to create calm.
cacophonyA harsh or jarring sound effect.“Cracked gears grind” creates a rough, clashing sound.
semantic precisionChoosing a word that expresses the exact intended meaning.“Whispered” is more precise than “spoke” when volume and secrecy matter.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which revision demonstrates greater semantic precision?

A. “The cyclist moved down the hill.”

Less precise. “Moved” identifies an action but does not reveal speed, control, or effort.

B. “The cyclist coasted down the hill.”

Best choice. “Coasted” names the exact movement and implies momentum with little pedaling, giving the reader a clearer image.
