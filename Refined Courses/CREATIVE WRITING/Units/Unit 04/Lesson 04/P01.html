P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Word Origins and Poetic Diction

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.WR.06 - Word origins and poetic diction.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.WR.05 - Organization and development of written explanation; MLA.CW.WR.07 - Connotation, denotation, and style.

What You Will Learn

  • How roots, prefixes, and suffixes help writers understand word meaning.
  • How word origin can shape tone, style, and precision.
  • How denotation and connotation affect poetic diction.
  • How to justify a diction choice in original poetry.

What You Will Do

  • Study common word parts used in poetic diction.
  • Compare words with similar denotations but different connotations.
  • Revise lines by choosing more precise words.
  • Explain how a word choice affects mood, image, and meaning.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery when you can choose a precise word for a poem, explain its denotation and connotation, and connect the choice to the mood or meaning you want to create.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard means you are learning that poets do not choose words only because they are correct. They choose words because each word carries history, sound, feeling, and precision. A word can change the whole mood of a line.

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help

Ask your TOR if you can define a word but cannot explain its connotation, or if you are unsure which word best fits the tone of your poem.
