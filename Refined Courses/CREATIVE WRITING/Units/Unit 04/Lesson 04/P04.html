P04 Worked Example

Study how each example uses word meaning and connotation to explain poetic diction.

Example 1: Denotation and Connotation

Line: The child walked through the doorway.

Choice A: stepped. This is neutral.

Choice B: crept. This suggests quiet fear or caution.

Choice C: bounded. This suggests energy or excitement.

Answer: If the poem should feel nervous, crept is strongest because it suggests careful, hesitant movement.

Example 2: Root Meaning

Word set: illuminate, luminous, lightless.

Step 1: The root lum connects to light.

Step 2: Luminous suggests glowing or full of light. Lightless means without light.

Answer: A poet might choose luminous for wonder and lightless for emptiness or fear.

Example 3: Revision for Mood

Basic line: The water moved beside the road.

Calm revision: The water murmured beside the road.

Uneasy revision: The water hissed beside the road.

Answer: Murmured creates a gentle mood, while hissed creates an uneasy mood because the sound is sharper and more threatening.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: Murmured and hissed mean the same thing because both are sounds.

This is incorrect because the words may share a general category, but their connotations are different.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: Murmured suggests a soft, gentle sound, while hissed suggests a sharper sound that can create tension.

This is correct because it compares connotation and explains the effect.
