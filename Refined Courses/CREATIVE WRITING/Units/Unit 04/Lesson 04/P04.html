Worked Example: Three Diction Decisions

Example 1 — Precision

Draft: “The fog went across the field.” Candidates: moved, drifted, prowled. Prowled adds stealth and threat through connotation and hard consonants. Revision: “Fog prowled the field.” The choice creates unease.

Example 2 — Origin and Pattern

“Luminous” derives from Latin lumen, light. In a poem using lamp, ember, and dawn, “luminous” joins the light lexical field while sounding elevated. “Bright” is accurate but less patterned.

Example 3 — Register and Voice

A child speaker says, “Grandma’s room smelled medicinal.” The word is precise but mismatched to voice. “Like the sharp cabinet at the doctor’s” preserves sensory accuracy and believable register.

Revision Rule

Choose the word whose meaning, association, sound, register, and pattern serve the poem—not the longest or rarest word.
