New Source: Two Names for Water

Grandmother says river,
from a word that means to tear away.
I say current,
thinking of motion and messages.
Between us, the water carries
both histories toward the sea.

Etymology is the history and origin of a word. Diction is a writer’s purposeful word choice. Words with similar dictionary meanings can carry different histories, sounds, and associations.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. How do the words “river” and “current” create different associations? Explain how those associations contribute to meaning.
  2. What does the final phrase “both histories” suggest about language, memory, and perspective?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant word, line, or image, and explain how it proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which principle demonstrates purposeful poetic diction?

A. Choose the longest or most unusual synonym because sophisticated poetry always uses difficult words.
B. Choose the word whose origin, sound, and associations best serve the speaker, image, rhythm, and meaning.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write an 18–24 line poem built around three carefully chosen words whose origins or associations matter.

  1. Opening: Introduce a speaker and a concrete situation.
  2. Development: Use each focus word in a context that reveals more than its dictionary meaning.
  3. Turn: Let one word change, complicate, or challenge the speaker’s understanding.
  4. Close: Echo a focus word so its final meaning differs from its first use.

Below the poem, list the three words, briefly note each origin or history, and explain why you selected that word instead of a close synonym.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should experience, how each movement will build that effect, and which words and images will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Replace one vague or decorative word with more purposeful diction. Explain how sound, origin, or connotation improves the line.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
