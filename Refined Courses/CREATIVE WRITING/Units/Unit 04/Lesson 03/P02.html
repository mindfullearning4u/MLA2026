Notebook Title

Lesson 3: Poetic Layers of Meaning and Ambiguity

Vocabulary

  • Literal meaning: What the words directly say.
  • Implied meaning: What the poem suggests without saying directly.
  • Layered meaning: More than one meaning working together.
  • Ambiguity: Intentional openness that allows more than one reasonable interpretation.
  • Symbol: An object, image, or action that represents a larger idea.
  • Inference: A logical conclusion based on evidence.

Step 1: Read for Literal Meaning

Poem Model:
The key stayed under the mat
long after the house was sold.
Rain darkened the brass,
but no one moved it.

Literal meaning: A key remains under a mat after a house is sold. Rain changes the key's color, and no one removes it.

Step 2: Look for Details That Suggest More

The key may suggest access, memory, ownership, or belonging. The house being sold suggests change. The fact that no one moves the key suggests something has been left behind emotionally, not just physically.

This is layered meaning: the poem is about a key, but it may also be about letting go, memory, or a connection that remains after a place is gone.

Step 3: Decide What Is Ambiguous

The poem does not explain who left the key or why. That openness is ambiguity. A weak reader may say, "I do not know, so it means nothing." A stronger reader says, "The missing explanation invites reasonable interpretations, and I can support one with evidence."
