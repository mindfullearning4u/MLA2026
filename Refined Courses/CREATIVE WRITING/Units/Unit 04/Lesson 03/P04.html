Worked Example: Three Layered Readings

Example 1 — Unstable Referent

Line: “I kept your name beneath the plant / where no one could water it.”
Literal: A name is hidden near a plant.
Reading A: The speaker protects a private memory. “Kept” suggests care.
Reading B: The speaker allows the relationship to die. “No one could water it” suggests deliberate deprivation.
Judgment: The care/harm tension is productive because the plant image supports both.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which interpretation best preserves the productive ambiguity in the plant image?

A. The hidden name proves that the speaker wants the relationship to die.
Weaker response. This selects one meaning as certain and ignores the conflicting evidence in the same image.
B. The speaker may be protecting a private memory or deliberately starving the relationship; “kept” suggests care, while “no one could water it” supports harm, so both readings remain plausible.
Stronger response. This response explains two interpretations, assigns evidence to each, and judges the tension as purposeful rather than confusing.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Enjambment

Lines: “She promised she would never / leave the window open.”
First-line reading: “Never” appears to modify leaving entirely.
Completed syntax: It modifies leaving the window open.
Effect: The line break briefly enlarges a practical promise into fear of abandonment, exposing subtext without stating it.

Example 3 — Paradox and Silence

Lines: “After the verdict, the room grew loud / with everything we did not say.”
Paradox: Silence is described as loud.
Reading A: Unspoken anger dominates the room.
Reading B: Shared guilt makes speech impossible.
Evidence need: Surrounding gestures or images must distinguish the readings; without them, the paradox risks vagueness.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: Read “I left the door unlatched so nothing could enter.” Give two plausible interpretations and identify the paradox that supports both.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: The unlatched door may suggest openness, yet the speaker believes openness creates protection. The contradiction could show trust, denial, or a private rule; more context is needed to choose among them.
