P04 Worked Example

Study how each example separates literal meaning from implied meaning.

Example 1: Object as Symbol

Poem: The empty cup stayed warm / between both chairs.

Step 1: Literal meaning: A cup remains warm between two chairs.

Step 2: Possible implied meaning: Someone recently left, or a conversation has just ended.

Step 3: Evidence: empty cup, stayed warm, and both chairs.

Answer: The cup may symbolize a recent presence or relationship because it is empty but still warm.

Example 2: Ambiguous Action

Poem: She folded the map / but kept the corner showing the sea.

Step 1: Literal meaning: A person folds a map but leaves the sea visible.

Step 2: Possible implied meaning: She is trying to move on but still feels drawn to departure or possibility.

Step 3: Evidence: folded suggests closing, while the visible sea suggests longing or openness.

Answer: The poem is ambiguous because the action could show both ending a plan and still wanting what the sea represents.

Example 3: Line Break and Meaning

Poem: I reached for the door
that was already open.

Step 1: Literal meaning: The speaker reaches for an open door.

Step 2: The line break delays the fact that the door is open.

Step 3: Possible implied meaning: The speaker may not realize freedom or opportunity is already available.

Answer: The line break creates layered meaning by making the reader feel the speaker's hesitation before revealing the door is open.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: The open door means the speaker is definitely happy forever.

This overclaims. The poem supports opportunity or freedom, but it does not prove permanent happiness.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The open door may suggest opportunity because the speaker reaches before realizing the way forward is already available.

This is stronger because it uses cautious language and evidence.
