Worked Example: Three Layered Readings

Example 1 — Unstable Referent

Line: “I kept your name beneath the plant / where no one could water it.”
Literal: A name is hidden near a plant.
Reading A: The speaker protects a private memory. “Kept” suggests care.
Reading B: The speaker allows the relationship to die. “No one could water it” suggests deliberate deprivation.
Judgment: The care/harm tension is productive because the plant image supports both.

Example 2 — Enjambment

Lines: “She promised she would never / leave the window open.”
First-line reading: “Never” appears to modify leaving entirely.
Completed syntax: It modifies leaving the window open.
Effect: The line break briefly enlarges a practical promise into fear of abandonment, exposing subtext without stating it.

Example 3 — Paradox and Silence

Lines: “After the verdict, the room grew loud / with everything we did not say.”
Paradox: Silence is described as loud.
Reading A: Unspoken anger dominates the room.
Reading B: Shared guilt makes speech impossible.
Evidence need: Surrounding gestures or images must distinguish the readings; without them, the paradox risks vagueness.
