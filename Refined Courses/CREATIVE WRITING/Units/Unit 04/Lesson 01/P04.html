Worked Example: Three Mood Models

Example 1 — Simile and Sound

Lines: “Rain tapped like careful fingers / along the locked glass.”
Literal: Rain strikes a window.
Device: Simile compares rain to deliberate fingers; repeated soft sounds slow the line.
Association: “Careful” suggests secrecy and restraint; “locked” adds separation.
Mood: Hushed uneasiness.
Evidence chain: The gentle tapping seems controlled rather than random, making the quiet feel watchful.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which analysis most clearly connects the simile to the mood?

A. The rain is compared to fingers, so the line is descriptive and therefore creates a mood.
Weaker response. This identifies the device but does not explain how its precise language shapes the reader’s feeling.
B. Comparing the rain to “careful fingers” makes the tapping seem deliberate, while “locked glass” adds separation; together the details create a hushed, watchful uneasiness.
Stronger response. This response traces two pieces of evidence through association to a precise mood.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Personification and Connotation

Lines: “Morning elbowed through the curtains, / rude with gold.”
Literal: Bright sunlight enters.
Device: Morning is personified as pushing; “elbowed” and “rude” have intrusive connotations.
Mood: Irritated urgency, not peaceful warmth.
Revision test: Replacing “elbowed” with “drifted” changes the mood to calm, proving diction controls the effect.

Example 3 — Symbol and Mood Shift

Lines: “All winter the porch swing wore snow. / In March, one red mitten waited there.”
Literal: Snow covers a swing; a mitten appears.
Pattern: Empty swing suggests absence; the bright mitten becomes a symbol of return or remembered presence.
Shift: Desolation becomes cautious hope.
Nuance: “One” still implies incompleteness, so the hope is not certainty.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: Read “The porch light blinked like a tired eye.” Name the device and explain one mood it could create without inventing a plot.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: The simile compares the light to a tired eye. “Blinked” and “tired” may create a weary or uneasy mood because the light seems weak and watchful, though the line does not establish why.
