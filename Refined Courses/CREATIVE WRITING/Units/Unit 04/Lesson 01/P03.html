P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page continues the lesson by showing how to choose precise mood words and avoid weak explanations.

Step 4: Choose a Mood Word That Matches the Text

Mood words are not random feelings. You prove them with diction and images. Read this model:

Poem Model:
Rain stitched silver lines across the glass,
the room grew soft around the lamp,
and the clock whispered
instead of ticking.

The mood is not scary because nothing threatening appears. The mood is calm, hushed, or reflective because the words soft, lamp, and whispered create quietness.

Step 5: Explain Cause and Effect

A complete explanation names the device, quotes the evidence, names the mood, and explains why the device creates that mood. Use this order: device + evidence + mood + reasoning.

Example: The personification "the clock whispered" creates a hushed mood because the clock is described as quiet and gentle rather than loud or mechanical.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: The poem has a good mood because it uses rain.

This is incorrect because good is not a precise mood word, and the explanation does not identify a poetic device or explain how the language affects the reader.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The metaphor "Rain stitched silver lines across the glass" creates a calm, delicate mood because the word stitched makes the rain seem careful and quiet instead of violent.

This is correct because it identifies figurative language, uses evidence, names a precise mood, and explains the effect.

Notebook Task Part 2
