Notebook Title

Lesson 1: Figurative Language and Mood

Vocabulary

  • Figurative language: Language that means more than its literal words.
  • Mood: The feeling or atmosphere a reader senses from the writing.
  • Simile: A comparison using like or as.
  • Metaphor: A direct comparison that says one thing is another.
  • Personification: Giving human action or qualities to something nonhuman.
  • Imagery: Language that appeals to the senses.
  • Connotation: The feeling or association attached to a word.

Step 1: Read for Literal Meaning First

Before naming a device, ask what is happening on the surface. Read this short poem model:

Poem Model:
The hallway held its breath at dawn,
lockers lined like sleeping guards,
and one gold square of sunlight
opened slowly on the floor.

Literal meaning: A hallway is quiet early in the morning. Lockers are along the wall. Sunlight appears on the floor.

Step 2: Identify the Figurative Language

"The hallway held its breath" is personification because a hallway cannot literally breathe. The poet gives the hallway a human action to make it feel still and tense.

"Lockers lined like sleeping guards" is a simile because it compares lockers to guards using the word like. The image makes the hallway seem watched, still, and quiet.

Step 3: Connect the Device to Mood

A mood word must fit the evidence. This poem does not feel loud, playful, or angry. The words held its breath, sleeping guards, and opened slowly support a quiet, expectant mood. A strong explanation sounds like this: The personification "held its breath" creates an expectant mood because it makes the hallway seem silent and waiting for something to begin.
