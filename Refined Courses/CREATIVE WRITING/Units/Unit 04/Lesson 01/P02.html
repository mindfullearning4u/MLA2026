Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your writer’s notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context.

TermMeaningExample
figurative languageLanguage whose meaning extends beyond the literal words.“The moon watched from the window” communicates more than a literal description.
literal meaningThe direct, dictionary-level meaning.In “the glass broke,” the words describe an actual physical event.
moodThe emotional atmosphere a reader experiences.Dark rooms and uneven footsteps create an uneasy mood.
toneThe speaker’s or writer’s attitude toward the subject.Calling the storm “a welcome interruption” creates an appreciative tone.
simileA comparison using like or as.“The page shook like a trapped wing.”
metaphorA direct comparison stating one thing is another.“Her promise was an anchor.”
personificationGiving human action or qualities to something nonhuman.“The radiator complained all night.”
imageryLanguage appealing to sight, sound, touch, taste, or smell.“Cold rain needled the back of his neck.”
symbolA concrete object or image carrying an additional idea.A repaired bridge may represent renewed trust when the poem supports that meaning.
connotationThe feelings or associations attached to a word.“Home” can suggest safety beyond its dictionary meaning.
denotationA word’s direct dictionary definition.The denotation of “dawn” is the beginning of daylight.
sound deviceA pattern such as repetition, alliteration, consonance, or onomatopoeia that shapes emphasis or mood.Repeated soft s sounds can make a line feel hushed.

Vocabulary in Action

Make the Vocabulary Work

Choose A or B before opening the feedback.

Which explanation correctly separates mood from tone?

A. Mood is the reader’s experienced atmosphere; tone is the attitude communicated by the speaker’s language.
B. Mood and tone are interchangeable names for any emotion mentioned in a poem.
Check your choice and reasoning

A is correct. Mood describes an atmosphere experienced by the reader, while tone describes an attitude conveyed through language. B incorrectly treats the concepts as identical and ignores the evidence needed to support either interpretation.
