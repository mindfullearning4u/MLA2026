Independent Work: Advanced Figurative Language and Mood

Purpose

Layer extended metaphor, personification, and imagery so mood shifts without becoming confusing.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Make deliberate choices that demonstrate the lesson skill.

Source for Your Work

Morning stitched gold along the rooftops, / but beneath the bridge the river / kept its iron voice.

1. Plan Before You Draft

Poetic DecisionYour Plan
Speaker, situation, and intended effectState each precisely.
Image or language patternList two connected possibilities.
Turn or developmentDescribe what changes and why.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger poetic craft? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. Use unrelated comparisons in every line so the poem sounds highly figurative.
B. Develop a connected pattern of images, then alter one key image to signal a controlled change in mood.

3. Apply the Lesson

Create a 16–20 line poem or 300–350 word prose poem with a deliberate mood shift. Build an extended metaphor across at least three connected images and use one contrasting image at the turn.

4. Revise with Purpose

Revise the exact line where the mood changes. Explain how diction, sound, or image makes the turn perceptible.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your original writing applies the lesson skill and uses the supplied source or model.
  • Your explanation identifies a specific craft choice and its effect.
  • Your revision improves meaning or craft, not only spelling.
