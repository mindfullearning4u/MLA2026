Lesson: Trace Patterns, Turns, and Mood

Six-Step Protocol

  1. Establish the baseline: name the opening literal situation and mood.
  2. Group the language: mark semantic fields such as weather, machinery, music, or illness.
  3. Trace the pattern: follow repeated comparison, image, object, or sound.
  4. Locate the turn: identify the word, line break, contrast, or discovery that redirects thought.
  5. Compare before and after: explain what changes and what remains.
  6. State the cumulative effect: connect the pattern and volta to a nuanced mood claim.

Pause and Choose: Locate the Volta

A poem repeats closed-door images, then begins a line with “yet” and introduces light through a window. Which explanation identifies the turn’s function?

A. “The volta occurs because ‘yet’ is an interesting transition word.”
Not the best choice. Naming the signal is only the first step. The answer must explain how the new image redirects the established pattern and mood.
B. “The word ‘yet’ marks a volta because the window light interrupts the closed-door pattern and makes an alternative to isolation visible.”
✓ Correct—the signal and structural change are connected. The response traces the earlier pattern, identifies the turning cue, and explains what the new image adds.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Learning Routes

  • Visual: use two colors for pre- and post-volta patterns.
  • Auditory: read aloud and listen for changed pace, repetition, or sound.
  • Hands-on: cut lines into strips; test where the turn is strongest.

Pause and Choose: State the Cumulative Mood

The closed-door imagery remains in memory after the window light appears. Which claim accounts for both continuity and change?

A. “The mood changes from sad to happy when the light appears.”
Not the best choice. The labels are vague, and a single new image does not erase the earlier guarded pattern.
B. “The mood progresses from guarded isolation to cautious hope because the light offers possibility while the remembered doors keep that hope uncertain.”
✓ Correct—the claim is cumulative and nuanced. It compares before and after, explains what remains, and names a precise mixed effect rather than replacing one mood with its opposite.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Analytical Frame
