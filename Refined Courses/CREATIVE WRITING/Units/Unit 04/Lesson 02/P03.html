P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page shows how to turn evidence into a connected analysis paragraph.

Step 4: Select Evidence That Shows Development

For advanced analysis, choose evidence from more than one part of the poem. If you only quote the first line, you cannot prove a shift. Use one early detail and one later detail.

Early evidence: "the river muttered under ice"

Later evidence: "flinging sunlight from every broken edge"

Step 5: Write the Reasoning

Reasoning explains why the evidence proves the claim. The verb muttered suggests a low, blocked sound, and under ice reinforces a trapped mood. Later, flinging sunlight suggests motion, brightness, and release. Together, the details show the mood changing as the river breaks open.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: The poem uses a river, ice, and sunlight, so the mood is nature.

This is incorrect because nature is a topic, not a mood. The answer lists images but does not analyze the feeling or explain how the mood develops.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The poem shifts from a restrained mood to an energetic mood because "muttered under ice" makes the river seem trapped, while "flinging sunlight" makes it seem bright and released.

This is correct because it names the shift, uses evidence from two parts of the poem, and explains the effect of the language.
