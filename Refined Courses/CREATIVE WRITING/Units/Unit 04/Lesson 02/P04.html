P04 Worked Example

These examples show how to analyze advanced figurative language and mood development.

Example 1: Mood Shift

Poem: The porch light trembled in fog / then morning poured gold over the steps.

Step 1: The early image is uncertain because the light trembled in fog.

Step 2: The later image is warmer because morning poured gold.

Step 3: The mood shifts from uncertain to hopeful.

Answer: The poem develops mood by moving from the fragile image of a trembling light to the warm metaphor of morning pouring gold.

Example 2: Pattern of Images

Poem: Empty chairs listened / dust settled on the piano / the last note folded itself away.

Step 1: Chairs listening and a note folding itself away are personification.

Step 2: Empty chairs, dust, and a final note all suggest absence.

Step 3: The pattern creates a lonely, quiet mood.

Answer: The poem uses a pattern of personification and empty images to create a lonely mood because even the objects seem aware that someone or something is gone.

Example 3: Diction and Metaphor

Poem: Her anger was a locked gate / iron in the rain.

Step 1: The metaphor compares anger to a locked gate.

Step 2: The diction locked, iron, and rain suggests hardness and coldness.

Step 3: The mood is tense and closed-off.

Answer: The metaphor and diction create a closed, tense mood because the anger is shown as something hard, cold, and difficult to pass through.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: The poem has a metaphor and personification.

This is incomplete because naming devices is not analysis. The answer must explain how the devices shape mood.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The poem's metaphor and personification create a quiet, lonely mood because empty objects seem to notice absence.

This answer moves beyond identification and explains the effect on the reader.
