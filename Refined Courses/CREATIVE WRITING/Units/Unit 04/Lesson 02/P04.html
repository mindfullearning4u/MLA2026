Worked Example: Three Advanced Patterns

Example 1 — Extended Metaphor

Lines: “I carried the apology like a sealed jar…At noon the lid began to sing…By evening, rain lived in my hands.”
Trace: The concealed apology is treated as a container under pressure. “Sing” blends sound with tension; the final rain suggests release.
Progression: restrained anxiety → unavoidable pressure → sorrowful relief.
Why it works: Each new image transforms the same conceit rather than adding unrelated decoration.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which explanation best traces the extended metaphor across the whole image?

A. The sealed jar is a creative comparison that makes the apology interesting.
Weaker response. This notices a comparison but does not trace how the conceit develops or changes the emotional progression.
B. The sealed jar turns the apology into contained pressure; the singing lid signals that the pressure can no longer remain hidden, and the rain in the speaker’s hands transforms restraint into sorrowful release.
Stronger response. This answer follows the same conceit through three linked images and explains how each stage changes the feeling.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Juxtaposition and Ambiguity

Lines: “Children chalk suns on the shelter wall / while sirens comb the avenue.”
Contrast: playful creation is placed beside danger.
Two readings: The suns may show resilient hope or innocence that cannot understand the threat.
Judgment: Both readings fit; the unresolved tension creates fragile, uneasy hope rather than uncomplicated optimism.

Example 3 — Pattern Break and Volta

Pattern: Four lines begin “We counted”—steps, coins, breaths, departures.
Break: The final line begins, “Then you stopped counting me.”
Reasoning: Repetition first creates control and shared ritual. The broken syntax turns counting into exclusion.
Cumulative effect: Order collapses into personal abandonment; the mood shifts from tense solidarity to isolation.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: Extend this metaphor by one line: “I carried the secret like a locked room.” Add a new image that develops—not abandons—the comparison, then explain the shift.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: One possible line is “By midnight, the key burned through my pocket.” The key continues the locked-room conceit and changes concealment into pressure to act.
