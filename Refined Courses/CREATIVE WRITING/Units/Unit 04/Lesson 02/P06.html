New Source: After the Sirens

The neighborhood exhales through open windows. Porch lights bloom one by one, small moons returning after the storm. Somewhere, a broom whispers over broken glass, and morning balances on the rooftops—fragile, bright, unwilling to fall.

An extended metaphor develops a comparison across several related details. A motif is a recurring image or idea that helps unify meaning and mood.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. How do the images of breathing, blooming, and balancing work together to create an extended figurative pattern?
  2. Describe the mood shift in the passage. Which image marks the turn, and why is it effective?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant word, line, or image, and explain how it proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which craft plan shows greater control of advanced figurative language?

A. Add many unrelated comparisons so every line sounds poetic.
B. Develop one connected image pattern and vary it at the turn so the figurative language supports a controlled mood shift.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write an 18–24 line poem that develops an extended metaphor and a deliberate mood shift.

  1. Opening: Establish a concrete setting and the first part of the central comparison.
  2. Pattern: Extend the comparison through at least three related images without overexplaining it.
  3. Turn: Change the mood through a new perception, contrast, or reversal.
  4. Closing: Transform or echo an earlier image to leave a layered final impression.

Add a 100–150 word craft note explaining the metaphor, the mood before and after the turn, and the image that controls the shift.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the reader should experience, how each movement will build that effect, and which words and images will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Remove one image that competes with the central pattern, then strengthen a related image so the poem becomes more unified.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise the organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the writing becomes clearer and more effective.
