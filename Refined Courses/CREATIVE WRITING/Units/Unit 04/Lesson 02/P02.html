Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your writer’s notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context.

TermMeaningExample
extended metaphorA comparison developed across multiple lines or sections.A poem treats grief as winter through images of frost, bare branches, and thaw.
conceitAn elaborate, often surprising comparison that organizes a poem.A poem compares a crowded schedule to a constellation the speaker must navigate.
semantic fieldA group of words connected by a shared area of meaning.“Tide,” “shore,” “current,” and “anchor” create a semantic field of the sea.
juxtapositionPlacing contrasting images or ideas together to reveal a relationship.A warm kitchen is placed beside an empty, cold chair.
ambiguityLanguage supporting more than one reasonable interpretation.“I left the light on” may suggest welcome, fear, or unfinished hope.
synesthesiaImagery that blends two or more senses.“The trumpet’s bright note tasted like silver.”
motifA recurring image, phrase, sound, or idea.A cracked cup returns whenever the speaker remembers home.
voltaA meaningful turn in thought, mood, argument, or perspective.After describing loss, “But morning came” turns the poem toward possibility.
mood progressionThe way atmosphere develops across a text.Playful opening images gradually darken into an uneasy final scene.
tonal complexityThe coexistence or tension of more than one attitude.The speaker sounds affectionate about home while also criticizing its limits.
pattern breakA deliberate disruption that draws attention or signals change.A repeated refrain suddenly disappears in the final stanza.
cumulative effectThe combined impact produced by multiple related choices.Short lines, repeated hard sounds, and dark imagery together create pressure.

Vocabulary in Action

Match each meaning to the correct lesson term. Choose Check, review the correct matches, and retry. This is ungraded practice; no screenshot or extra submission is required.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Choose A or B before opening the feedback.

Which example is an extended metaphor?

A. “The moon was a coin.”

B. Across the poem, grief becomes winter through frost, bare branches, frozen ground, and an eventual thaw.

Check your choice and reasoning

B is correct. Its comparison develops through several connected images. A is a metaphor, but it appears only once and is not extended.
