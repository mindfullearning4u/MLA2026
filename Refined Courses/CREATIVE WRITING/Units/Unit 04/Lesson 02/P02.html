Notebook Title

Lesson 2: Advanced Figurative Language and Mood

Vocabulary

  • Analysis: Explanation of how parts of a text work and why they matter.
  • Mood shift: A change in feeling or atmosphere.
  • Pattern: A repeated or connected set of choices.
  • Diction: A writer's word choice.
  • Claim: A clear statement that answers the question.
  • Reasoning: The explanation that connects evidence to the claim.

Step 1: Read the Whole Poem for Movement

Poem Model:
At first the river muttered under ice,
a gray rope pulled through stone.
By noon it shattered into mirrors,
flinging sunlight from every broken edge.

Do not stop after the first device. Read all four lines and ask whether the mood stays the same or changes.

Step 2: Track the Pattern of Language

The first half uses muttered, ice, gray, rope, and stone. These words feel heavy, cold, and restrained. The second half uses shattered, mirrors, flinging, sunlight, and broken edge. These words feel brighter and more energetic.

Step 3: Build an Analytical Claim

A strong claim explains the overall effect, not just one device. Claim: The poem shifts from a trapped, muted mood to a vivid, released mood as the river changes from ice to reflected sunlight.

This claim works because it names both moods and explains the movement between them.
