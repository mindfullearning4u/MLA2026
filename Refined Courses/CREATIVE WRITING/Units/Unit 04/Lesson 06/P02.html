Notebook Title

Lesson 6: Paraphrase and Interpretation of Poetic Models

Vocabulary

  • Paraphrase: Restating meaning in your own words without adding interpretation.
  • Interpretation: Explaining what a poem suggests or means beyond the literal wording.
  • Model: A text studied as an example for craft.
  • Preserve: Keep the important meaning or detail.
  • Overinterpret: Claim more than the evidence supports.
  • Craft choice: A deliberate decision about words, images, line breaks, or structure.

Step 1: Read the Poem Model Literally

Poem Model:
The last bus sighed away,
leaving its yellow square of light
on the wet street
for one breath more.

First, ask what happens. A bus leaves. Its light remains briefly on a wet street.

Step 2: Paraphrase Without Explaining Yet

Paraphrase: The final bus drove away, and its yellow light stayed on the wet street for a short moment.

This paraphrase is accurate because it keeps the main details but uses clearer everyday language. It does not yet explain symbolism, mood, or theme.

Step 3: Interpret After Paraphrasing

Interpretation: The poem may suggest a brief feeling of loneliness after departure because the last bus leaves and only a small piece of light remains.

Notice the difference: paraphrase restates what happens; interpretation explains what it may mean.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary.

Part B: Copy the poem model.

Part C: Write one paraphrase and one interpretation.
