Checkpoint: Integrated Author Portfolio Preparation

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: The Coherent Author

A portfolio moves from a restrained poem about departure to a tense short story about returning, then to a monologue spoken by someone who refuses to leave. The forms differ, yet repeated doorway images and increasingly complex choices create a coherent conversation across the works.

An integrated portfolio creates intentional relationships among selection, sequence, revision, design, author statement, and presentation. Coherence does not require sameness; it requires meaningful connection.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. How do form, sequence, repeated imagery, and character choices create coherence without making the pieces repetitive?
  2. What evidence should the author statement and presentation highlight to help readers recognize the portfolio’s design?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which approach produces mature portfolio coherence?

A. Make every piece use the same narrator, setting, and ending so the collection cannot feel inconsistent.
B. Preserve meaningful range while using sequence, recurring concerns, craft development, and contextual notes to create connection.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Assemble an integrated author portfolio draft with at least four polished works in two or more forms.

  1. Curate: Select for quality, range, growth, and an identifiable throughline.
  2. Revise: Complete and document one major revision for every piece.
  3. Sequence: Arrange the collection to create an intentional reader journey.
  4. Frame: Add a polished author statement and 100–150 word context note for each work.
  5. Prepare: Apply the publication system and draft the author presentation.

Write a 250–300 word integration rationale explaining how selection, sequence, revision, design, and presentation operate as one project.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Audit the portfolio as a whole. Make one revision to sequence, one to framing, and one to visual consistency; explain the effect of each.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.
