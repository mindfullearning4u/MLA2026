Lesson: Integrate, Audit, and Defend the Portfolio

Step 1 — Freeze the Inventory

List every required component, its final file name, version date, standard demonstrated, and current status. Work only from this inventory so an outdated draft cannot replace an approved artifact.

Step 2 — Build the Alignment Matrix

Pause and Choose: Detect an Alignment Gap

The selection rationale claims a story demonstrates controlled point of view, but its evidence discusses only punctuation corrections. What should the writer do?

A. Replace the evidence with a specific point-of-view choice or revise the claim so it accurately matches what the artifact demonstrates.
Stronger choice. Portfolio coherence requires a traceable relationship between criterion, claim, and evidence. Either side may change, but they must align.
B. Keep both because any revision evidence supports any craft claim.
Weaker choice. Unrelated evidence does not verify the claim. It creates the appearance of support without demonstrating the named skill.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Step 3 — Run Gap Analysis

  1. Highlight every rubric criterion.
  2. Point to exact evidence satisfying it.
  3. Mark unsupported claims and duplicated evidence.
  4. Find missing range, growth, access, rights, or presentation evidence.
  5. Assign a corrective action, owner, and deadline—even if you are all three.

Step 4 — Audit in Independent Passes

  • Craft: quality, purpose, and range.
  • Revision: before/after accuracy and reasoning.
  • Coherence: titles, claims, cross-references, and order.
  • Publication: metadata, files, links, rights, and privacy.
  • Accessibility: contrast, headings, descriptions, captions, transcripts.
  • Presentation: excerpt, timing, visuals, and rehearsal.

Step 5 — Create the Release Candidate

Export the exact files a reader will receive. Open them outside the editing environment, follow every navigation path, and compare displayed content to the inventory.

Pause and Choose: Defend Readiness

Which final judgment is supported by a genuine quality-assurance argument?

A. “The portfolio is ready because everything looks good to me.”
Weaker choice. The statement gives no criterion, evidence, verification method, or acknowledged limitation.
B. “The portfolio meets the growth criterion through two verified before-and-after pairs; all release files passed link and display tests, though the poetry sample still shows a deliberate narrow range of forms.”
Stronger choice. It names criteria, points to evidence, reports verification, acknowledges a limitation, and reaches a proportionate judgment.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Step 6 — Defend Readiness

Use Criterion → Evidence → Verification → Limitation → Final judgment. “Everything looks good” is not a quality-assurance argument.
