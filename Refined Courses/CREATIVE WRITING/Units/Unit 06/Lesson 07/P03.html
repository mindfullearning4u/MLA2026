P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continue your notebook notes. This page teaches how to connect the portfolio, author statement, and presentation plan.

Step 4: Connect the Author Statement to the Portfolio

The author statement should not feel separate from the portfolio. It should prepare the reader to notice your choices and growth. Read your statement beside the selected pieces. Then ask: Does the statement name the skills the reader can actually see in the portfolio?

If your statement says you improved dialogue, the portfolio should include a piece where dialogue matters. If your statement says you developed imagery, the portfolio should include writing where imagery is visible. The statement and portfolio should support each other.

Step 5: Connect the Presentation Plan to the Portfolio

Your presentation plan should point to a specific portfolio piece or excerpt. It should not be a general speech about the whole course. Choose one excerpt that helps the audience understand the larger portfolio.

  1. Name the piece and genre.
  2. Give brief context.
  3. Tell the audience what craft choice to notice.
  4. Explain how that craft choice connects to your growth as a writer.

This creates a clear bridge between publication and presentation: the portfolio shows the work, and the presentation explains how the work was made.

Common Mistake: Collecting Without Connecting

Incorrect: A student submits several pieces, an author statement, and presentation notes, but none of the parts refer to the same craft growth. The statement discusses imagery, the selected excerpt shows dialogue, and the portfolio order is random.

Why this is incorrect: A portfolio is not just a folder of files. It should show intentional choices. The reader should understand why each part is included and how the parts work together.

Correct Approach

Correct: A student selects a revised story and poem that both show stronger voice, explains voice development in the author statement, and plans a presentation excerpt where sentence rhythm reveals the speaker's personality.

Why this is correct: The portfolio, statement, and presentation all point to the same author growth. The reader sees the evidence, and the writer explains it clearly.
