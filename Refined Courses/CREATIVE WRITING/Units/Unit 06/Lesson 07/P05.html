Independent Work: Integrated Author Portfolio Preparation

Purpose

Integrate selection, revision, author reflection, publication design, and presentation into a unified author portfolio.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Support decisions with precise evidence from your portfolio work.

Source for Your Work

Portfolio audit: Strong pieces alone do not guarantee a strong portfolio. Selection, sequence, framing, consistency, accessibility, and reflection must create a coherent experience for the reader.

1. Plan Before You Work

Portfolio DecisionEvidence and Plan
Purpose and intended audienceState each precisely.
Strongest current evidenceName the piece, passage, or feature.
Most important improvementDescribe the needed change and intended effect.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger author decision-making? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. Finish each component separately and place everything together at the end without reconsidering the reader’s path.
B. Develop a controlling portfolio purpose, test how every component supports it, and revise transitions and framing so the collection functions as a whole.

3. Apply the Lesson

Assemble a complete portfolio prototype containing three polished works, an author statement, craft rationales, contents/navigation, publication formatting, and a presentation plan. Include an evidence-based readiness audit for each component.

4. Revise with Purpose

Ask what a first-time reader would understand about you as an author. Revise one sequencing choice and one framing element to make that answer clearer.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your work applies the lesson skill to specific portfolio evidence.
  • Your explanation identifies a decision and its effect on the audience.
  • Your revision meaningfully improves quality, coherence, or readiness.
