P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Integrated Author Portfolio Preparation Notes

Vocabulary

  • Integrated portfolio: A final collection in which the pieces, reflection, revision evidence, and presentation plan work together.
  • Portfolio order: The sequence of pieces and materials that guides the reader through the collection.
  • Readiness pass: A final review for completeness, readability, formatting, and submission requirements.
  • Author rationale: A short explanation of why a piece belongs in the portfolio and what craft growth it shows.

Step 1: Identify the Required Portfolio Parts

Before judging quality, check completeness. A strong portfolio cannot be ready if a required part is missing. In your notebook, make a checklist with these categories: selected creative pieces, revised final versions, author statement, publication formatting, presentation plan, and submission requirements.

For each category, write present, missing, or needs revision. This keeps the task concrete. Do not assume you will remember later; write the status now.

Step 2: Check Each Creative Piece

Review one piece at a time. Do not try to check the whole portfolio at once. For each piece, answer four questions:

  1. Does this piece represent a meaningful craft skill or growth area?
  2. Has it been revised from feedback or self-review?
  3. Is it formatted for its genre and easy to read?
  4. Can I explain why this piece belongs in the portfolio?

If the answer to any question is no, mark the exact problem. A vague note such as "fix this" is not useful. Write the action, such as "add paragraph breaks," "clarify author rationale," or "remove draft comments."

Step 3: Build a Portfolio Order

Portfolio order shapes the reader's experience. Put pieces in an order that makes sense. You might begin with the strongest piece, show growth from early to later work, group similar genres, or move from shorter to longer pieces.

After choosing an order, write one sentence explaining the reason. Example: I placed the revised story before the poem because the story introduces the voice and the poem shows how that voice changes in a different form.
