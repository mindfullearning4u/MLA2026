Independent Work: Author Statement Voice and Tone

Purpose

Craft an author statement with a credible voice, purposeful tone, and specific insight into artistic growth.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Support decisions with precise evidence from your portfolio work.

Source for Your Work

Opening A: “This portfolio contains assignments I completed during the course.” Opening B: “I began this course trying to control every sentence; the pieces in this portfolio show how I learned when to control and when to let uncertainty work.”

1. Plan Before You Work

Portfolio DecisionEvidence and Plan
Purpose and intended audienceState each precisely.
Strongest current evidenceName the piece, passage, or feature.
Most important improvementDescribe the needed change and intended effect.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger author decision-making? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. List each assignment and praise the portfolio in broad terms so the statement sounds confident.
B. Establish an authentic perspective, trace meaningful growth, and support claims with specific choices from the work.

3. Apply the Lesson

Write a 350–450 word author statement introducing your portfolio. Establish a consistent voice and tone, explain your artistic priorities, cite at least three specific craft decisions, and identify one continuing goal.

4. Revise with Purpose

Revise the most general paragraph by replacing broad claims with concrete evidence and reflection. Explain how the new version sounds more authentic.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your work applies the lesson skill to specific portfolio evidence.
  • Your explanation identifies a decision and its effect on the audience.
  • Your revision meaningfully improves quality, coherence, or readiness.
