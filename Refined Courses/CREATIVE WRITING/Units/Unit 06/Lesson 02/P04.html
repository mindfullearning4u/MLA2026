Worked Example: Three Voice-and-Tone Revisions

Example 1 — From Vague to Specific

Weak: “I worked hard on this story and used a lot of imagery to make it better.”

  1. Problem: effort and “better” are unmeasurable; imagery is unnamed.
  2. Evidence: the final draft repeats rust-colored water at each failed escape.
  3. Revision: “I developed the rust-colored water into a recurring image, so the setting records each failed escape without an explanatory narrator.”
  4. Effect: the voice sounds credible because it names a decision and consequence.

Example 2 — From Casual to Reflective

Weak: “The ending was confusing, so I fixed it and now it hits harder.”

  1. Translate casual language without removing personality.
  2. Identify the diagnosis: the sister’s choice lacked setup.
  3. Name the revision: an earlier scene plants her fear of public testimony.
  4. Revision: “Readers could not connect the sister’s final testimony to her earlier behavior, so I added a restrained scene that establishes what speaking publicly costs her.”

Example 3 — From Inflated to Qualified

Weak: “My poem gives everyone a completely new understanding of grief.”

  1. Problem: “everyone” and “completely new” exceed the evidence.
  2. Preserve ambition but qualify the claim.
  3. Revision: “I use interrupted lists and unfinished comparisons to invite readers to experience grief as an incomplete act of naming.”
  4. Result: the statement proposes an intended experience rather than claiming control over every reader.

Adaptation Example

Portfolio: emphasize growth and revision evidence. Publication bio note: condense to purpose and one craft choice. Oral introduction: use shorter sentences and mark pauses. The core truth remains; emphasis and register change.
