P04 Worked Example

Study how a weak author statement becomes publication-ready.

Worked Example 1: Identify the Tone Problem

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Weak statement: I guess this poem is fine, and people can think whatever.

Step 1: Identify tone: uncertain and dismissive.

Step 2: Identify missing content: purpose, craft choice, and reader effect.

Step 3: Decide the target tone: reflective and confident.

Worked Example 2: Revise for Voice and Specificity

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Revision: In this poem, I wanted the speaker's uncertainty to feel quiet rather than dramatic. I used short lines and the repeated image of the porch light to slow the reader down.

Why it works: The author still sounds like a student writer, but the tone is clear, reflective, and craft-focused.

Worked Example 3: Final Author Statement

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

In this poem, I wanted the speaker's uncertainty to feel quiet rather than dramatic. I used short lines and the repeated image of the porch light to slow the reader down. After feedback, I removed one sentence that explained the feeling too directly. The final version asks the reader to notice hesitation through image and pacing.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: My poem is amazing and nobody should question any of it.

This is incorrect because the tone is exaggerated and defensive. It does not explain craft reasoning.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I used short lines and repeated imagery to create hesitation, and I revised the ending so the reader feels uncertainty through pacing.

This is correct because the tone is confident but not arrogant, and it explains a craft effect.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
