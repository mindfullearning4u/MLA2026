Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
author statementA concise explanation that frames a creative work, its purposes, craft choices, and development.The statement explains the work’s central concern and revision path.
voiceThe distinctive presence or personality conveyed through language and perspective.Compressed sentences and dry humor create a recognizable voice.
toneThe attitude a writer communicates toward the subject and audience.Precise, restrained language creates a reflective tone.
registerA level and style of language suited to a context.The presentation uses a professional but accessible register.
credibilityThe quality of being trustworthy, knowledgeable, and honest.Acknowledging uncertainty strengthens the author’s credibility.
specificityThe use of exact, concrete details rather than broad generalities.Naming the repeated image gives the statement specificity.
rhetorical situationThe relationship among purpose, audience, context, message, and form.The author adjusts the statement for a public reading audience.
qualificationA limit or condition added to make a claim more accurate.“In the final two scenes” qualifies the claim about pacing.
curatorial noteA short text explaining why a work was selected or arranged.The note connects the piece to the portfolio’s theme of belonging.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which sentence adds useful specificity to an author statement?

A. “I made several choices to improve the story.”

This is too general to show what changed or why.

B. “I shortened the final three paragraphs so the repeated door image arrives sooner and increases urgency.”

Correct. It identifies the exact revision, location, craft pattern, and intended effect.
