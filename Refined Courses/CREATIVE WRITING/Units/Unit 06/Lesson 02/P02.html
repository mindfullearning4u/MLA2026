P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

This lesson teaches how voice and tone shape an author statement.

Notebook Title

Author Statement Voice and Tone

Vocabulary

Author statement: A short explanation that introduces the writer's purpose, craft choices, and growth.

Voice: The writer's personality or presence in the statement.

Tone: The attitude the statement creates for the reader, such as reflective, confident, respectful, or defensive.

Publication context: The setting where the statement will appear, such as a class portfolio, digital publication, or presentation.

Step 1: Understand the Purpose of an Author Statement

An author statement prepares readers to understand your creative work and your decisions as a writer. It should not summarize every event. It should explain purpose, craft choices, revision, and growth.

Weak: This story is about stuff that happened.

Stronger: This story explores how a friendship changes when one character chooses honesty over comfort.

Step 2: Choose a Tone That Fits the Context

For a portfolio or publication, the tone should usually be reflective, clear, and confident. Avoid sounding careless, defensive, or exaggerated.

Too casual: I just wrote this and it came out pretty good.

Better: I revised this piece to make the speaker's uncertainty clearer through repeated images.

Step 3: Keep Your Voice Authentic

Professional tone does not mean sounding like someone else. You can use your own voice while still being specific and respectful. The key is to explain your choices clearly.

Authentic and clear: I wanted the ending to feel quiet, so I removed the extra explanation and let the final image do more work.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Define author statement, voice, tone, and publication context.

Part B: Copy one weak statement and one stronger statement from this page.

Part C: Write one sentence explaining the tone your author statement should create.
