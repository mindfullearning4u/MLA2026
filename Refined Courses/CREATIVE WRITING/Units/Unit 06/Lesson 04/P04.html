P04 Worked Example

Study how a poem is revised for stronger style and portfolio readiness.

Worked Example 1: Image Revision

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Before: I felt sad at the window.

Revision: Rain gathered on the sill until the glass forgot the street.

Why it works: The revised image lets the reader feel sadness through setting.

Worked Example 2: Line Break Revision

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Before: I almost knocked but my hand stayed still.

Revision: I almost knocked
but my hand
stayed still.

Why it works: The breaks slow the action and emphasize hesitation.

Worked Example 3: Portfolio Explanation

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

I revised the poem's final image so the reader could feel sadness through setting instead of being told the emotion directly. I also changed the line breaks to slow the final action. This belongs in my portfolio because it shows growth in imagery and pacing.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: I replaced every simple word with a longer word so the poem sounds smarter.

This is incorrect because stronger style is about precision and effect, not longer words.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I replaced a general emotion word with a concrete image so the reader experiences the feeling through the scene.

This is correct because the style revision improves image and reader effect.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
