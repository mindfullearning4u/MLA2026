Worked Example: Three Style Revisions

Example 1 — Image Instead of Label

Draft: “I was lonely after everyone left.”

  1. Purpose: convey abandonment without naming it.
  2. Observation: “lonely” tells the conclusion.
  3. Revision: “Four cups cooled around the table. Mine kept its ring of steam.”
  4. Effect: the group’s absence becomes visible; the remaining steam isolates the speaker in time.
  5. Tradeoff: the revision is less direct but more interpretively active.

Example 2 — Line Break and Enjambment

Version A:
I kept the door closed.
The winter waited outside.

Version B:
I kept the door
closed against the winter
waiting outside.

  1. A produces two complete, stable statements.
  2. B isolates “door,” delays “closed,” and lets “waiting” modify the winter more actively.
  3. Choose A for control and finality; choose B for pressure and permeability.

Example 3 — Syntax and Sound

Draft: “The train moved quickly past the town, and I remembered leaving.”

Revision: “Track-click, glass-flash—the town broke backward. I left again.”

  1. Compound sounds imitate mechanical rhythm without copying an actual train.
  2. The dash accelerates the visual shift.
  3. The short final sentence breaks the pattern and turns travel into memory.
  4. The revision compresses explanation but heightens embodied experience.

Transfer Pattern

Purpose → Version A → Version B → read aloud → name effect → choose and justify.
