Independent Work: Poetry and Style Portfolio Revision

Purpose

Revise poetry by coordinating image, diction, sound, lineation, structure, and thematic development.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Support decisions with precise evidence from your portfolio work.

Source for Your Work

Revision problem: A poem uses strong individual images, but shifting diction, inconsistent line breaks, and an unclear turn prevent the images from building a unified effect.

1. Plan Before You Work

Portfolio DecisionEvidence and Plan
Purpose and intended audienceState each precisely.
Strongest current evidenceName the piece, passage, or feature.
Most important improvementDescribe the needed change and intended effect.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger author decision-making? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. Correct punctuation and add rhyme to make the poem feel complete.
B. Identify the poem’s central movement, retain the strongest images, and revise language and structure so every choice supports that movement.

3. Apply the Lesson

Select one poem of at least 20 lines. Create an intention statement, map its image and sound patterns, and revise the poem for coherence, precision, lineation, and a purposeful turn.

4. Revise with Purpose

Annotate four before-and-after lines representing image, diction, sound, and structure. Explain how the revisions work together rather than as isolated fixes.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your work applies the lesson skill to specific portfolio evidence.
  • Your explanation identifies a decision and its effect on the audience.
  • Your revision meaningfully improves quality, coherence, or readiness.
