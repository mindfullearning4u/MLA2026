Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
line breakThe point where one poetic line ends and another begins.Breaking after “alone” isolates the word and deepens its emphasis.
enjambmentA sentence or phrase continuing beyond a line break without terminal punctuation.The sentence rushes across two lines through enjambment.
end-stopped lineA poetic line ending with a grammatical pause or punctuation.A period makes the final line end-stopped and decisive.
caesuraA strong pause within a poetic line.The dash creates a caesura between hope and doubt.
prosodyThe patterned rhythm, stress, pace, and sound of language.Heavy stresses slow the line and make it sound burdened.
consonanceRepetition of consonant sounds within or at the ends of nearby words.The repeated k sounds in “dark rock” create consonance.
image systemA connected pattern of sensory details that develops meaning across a work.Windows, mirrors, and glass form an image system about perception.
figurative languageLanguage creating meaning through comparison, association, or nonliteral expression.“The city swallowed the moon” personifies the city.
syntactic variationPurposeful changes in sentence length, structure, or order.A long flowing sentence followed by a fragment changes the pace.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which line uses a caesura to create a meaningful turn?

A. “The rain kept falling softly through the night.”

The line is continuous and contains no strong internal pause.

B. “I called it freedom—until the door locked.”

Correct. The dash creates an internal pause that divides expectation from reversal.
