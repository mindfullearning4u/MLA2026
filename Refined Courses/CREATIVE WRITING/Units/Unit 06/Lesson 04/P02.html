Notebook Title

Poetry and Style Revision Diagnosis

Vocabulary

Image: Language that helps the reader picture, hear, feel, taste, or smell something.

Line break: The place where a poetic line ends and creates pause or emphasis.

Sound device: A technique such as repetition, alliteration, rhyme, or rhythm.

Style: The writer's pattern of word choice, sentence rhythm, tone, and voice.

Step 1: Identify the Intended Effect

Before revising a poem or style piece, decide what the language should make the reader feel or notice. The intended effect may be quiet grief, tension, humor, wonder, uncertainty, or speed.

Example: This poem should make the reader feel hesitation before the final choice.

Step 2: Diagnose the Language Problem

Ask which craft feature needs attention. Is the image too general? Are line breaks random? Is repetition helpful at first but distracting later? Is the rhythm too flat for the intended mood? Is the figurative language unclear?

Step 3: Match Revision to Effect

If the poem needs stronger imagery, replace abstract feeling with a concrete image. If the poem needs slower pacing, revise line breaks. If the style sounds flat, vary sentence length or strengthen word choice. Every revision should support the intended effect.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Define image, line break, sound device, and style.

Part B: Write the intended effect of one poem or style piece.

Part C: Diagnose one language problem and name the matching revision type.
