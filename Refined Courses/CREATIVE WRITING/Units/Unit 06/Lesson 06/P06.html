New Source: A Presentation Without a Throughline

A writer plans to read three complete pieces in ten minutes, explain every revision, show twelve slides, and answer questions. The excerpts are strong, but the plan has no central message and leaves no time for transitions or audience response.

A presentation throughline is the central idea connecting every section. Audience cueing prepares listeners to understand why a detail or excerpt matters.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Identify three problems that would reduce audience understanding or engagement.
  2. What throughline could organize the presentation, and which material should be cut or shortened to serve it?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which plan demonstrates stronger author presentation design?

A. Speak faster and keep all material so the audience receives the maximum amount of information.
B. Choose one throughline, select brief representative excerpts, build purposeful transitions, and protect time for reflection and questions.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Create a 6–8 minute portfolio presentation plan and full script.

  1. Opening: Use a hook and state the throughline connecting your work.
  2. Portfolio context: Briefly identify the audience, forms, and organizing purpose.
  3. Excerpts: Select two contrasting excerpts; introduce each with a listening purpose and annotate delivery.
  4. Craft reflection: Explain how one technique and one major revision developed across the portfolio.
  5. Closing: Synthesize your growth and next goal, then invite one focused question.

Include a timed outline, any essential visual, and an accessibility plan for the audience.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Rehearse with a timer and record the actual length. Cut or reshape one section to strengthen the throughline and revise two delivery cues.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.
