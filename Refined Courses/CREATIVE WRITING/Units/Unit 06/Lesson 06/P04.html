Worked Example: Three Presentation Plans

Example 1 — Fiction Excerpt Choice

Options: an exciting climax requiring extensive context, or a quieter 180-word scene where a character repeatedly mislabels boxes while avoiding a farewell.

  1. Purpose: explain indirect characterization.
  2. The quieter scene is more representative and discussable.
  3. Frame with one sentence about the departure.
  4. Analyze repeated mislabeling, withheld dialogue, and pacing.
  5. Revision evidence: an early draft directly stated that the character feared leaving.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which excerpt is better for a short presentation on indirect characterization?

A. Use the exciting climax even though it needs several minutes of plot explanation.
Weaker response. The climax may be strong writing, but extensive context would consume the presentation and obscure the stated craft focus.
B. Use the 180-word box-labeling scene because repeated mislabeling, withheld dialogue, and pacing reveal avoidance with little setup.
Stronger response. This selection is representative, manageable, and rich in precise evidence that the presenter can explain within the available time.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Poetry Delivery Map

Excerpt: a 16-line poem whose sentence crosses three stanzas before ending with “home.”

  1. Mark breaths that preserve syntax without erasing line breaks.
  2. Slow slightly at stanza boundaries; do not stop automatically.
  3. Emphasize repeated directional verbs, not every image.
  4. Display the poem in high contrast with the syntactic path labeled.
  5. Explain how delayed completion makes “home” both destination and release.

Example 3 — Time-Cut Decision

Problem: A six-minute draft must fit four minutes.

  1. Protect the 60-second excerpt and two evidence-based craft claims.
  2. Cut plot summary from 90 to 20 seconds.
  3. Combine publication context with the opening.
  4. Reduce three revision examples to the most consequential one.
  5. Keep the reader invitation because it gives the ending purpose.

Principle: Cut redundancy before cutting evidence.

Transfer Grid
