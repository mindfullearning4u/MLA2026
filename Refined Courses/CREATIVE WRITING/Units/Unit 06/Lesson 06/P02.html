P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Author Presentation Planning Notes

Vocabulary

  • Author presentation: A planned explanation and reading of creative work by the writer.
  • Audience frame: The context the audience needs before hearing or reading the work.
  • Excerpt: A selected part of a longer piece used for presentation.
  • Craft reasoning: An explanation of how a writer's choices shape meaning, tone, pacing, voice, or reader effect.

Step 1: Identify the Presentation Purpose and Audience

A presentation begins before the reading. First decide what the audience should understand by the end. Your purpose might be to show growth, explain a craft choice, introduce a genre experiment, or highlight a revised portfolio piece.

Write this sentence frame in your notebook: My audience should understand ___ about my creative work.

Then identify who the audience is. A class audience may need a short reminder of the assignment. A public audience may need more context about genre, speaker, or theme.

Step 2: Choose the Right Excerpt

An excerpt should be short enough to present clearly and strong enough to represent the work. Do not choose a section only because it is first. Choose the section that helps the audience notice your craft.

  1. Find a section with a clear example of voice, image, conflict, dialogue, line break, structure, or revision.
  2. Check that the audience can understand the excerpt with brief context.
  3. Avoid an excerpt that requires a long plot explanation before it makes sense.
  4. Mark the exact beginning and ending so your presentation stays focused.

Step 3: Plan the Craft Points

A craft point is not "this is what happens." A craft point explains how the writing works. Plan two or three points only. Too many points can make the presentation unfocused.

Use this order for each point:

  1. Name the craft choice.
  2. Point to where it appears in the work.
  3. Explain what the choice does for the reader or listener.

Example: "I used short sentence fragments during the chase scene. They make the pace feel faster and show the character's panic."
