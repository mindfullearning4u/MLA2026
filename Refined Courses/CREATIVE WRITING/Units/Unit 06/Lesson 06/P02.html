Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
presentation arcThe purposeful sequence that guides an audience through a presentation.The speaker moves from context to excerpt to reflection.
representative excerptA selected passage that accurately demonstrates important features of a larger work.The excerpt displays the story’s voice, conflict, and pacing.
signpostingBrief language that shows where a presentation is going or how ideas connect.“First, notice the repeated image” directs attention.
annotationA note marking evidence, delivery, timing, or interpretation.The script marks where to pause before the final line.
cadenceThe rhythmic rise, fall, stress, and timing of spoken language.Varied cadence prevents the reading from sounding mechanical.
vocal emphasisPurposeful stress placed on a word or phrase during speech.Emphasizing “still” reveals the character’s persistence.
rehearsal protocolA planned method for practicing, timing, observing, and revising delivery.The writer records one run, reviews it, and retests changes.
visual supportA chart, slide, image, or text display that clarifies spoken reasoning.One annotated image supports the explanation without replacing it.
audience calibrationAdjustment of context, language, and pacing to what listeners need.The speaker defines a specialized term for unfamiliar listeners.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which opening shows effective signposting?

A. “Here is my story.”

This announces the work but does not guide listeners toward a purpose or feature.

B. “Before I read, notice how the repeated train image changes from escape to obligation.”

Correct. It tells listeners what to attend to and prepares the later craft explanation.
