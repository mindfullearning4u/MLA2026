Lesson: Engineer the Presentation Before Rehearsing

Step 1 — Define Audience, Purpose, and Constraint

Complete one sentence: “For an audience of ___, I will use ___ minutes to present ___ so listeners can understand/question/notice ___.” Time is a design constraint, not merely a stopwatch problem.

Step 2 — Select the Excerpt

CriterionTest
RepresentativeDoes it reveal a central craft pattern?
Self-containedCan listeners follow with 1–2 context sentences?
DiscussableDoes it support precise craft reasoning?
SpeakableCan it be delivered clearly within the allotted time?

Step 3 — Build the Five-Part Arc

  1. Frame: title, genre, central question, minimum context.
  2. Read: deliver the excerpt with intentional pace and emphasis.
  3. Analyze: name two exact craft choices and their effects.
  4. Revise: compare one meaningful before-and-after decision.
  5. Invite: offer an unresolved question or listening lens.

Step 4 — Budget Time

For a five-minute presentation, a useful starting allocation is 0:30 Frame | 1:30 Read | 1:45 Analyze | 0:50 Revise | 0:25 Invite. Adjust by purpose, then time every section separately.

Step 5 — Design Visual Support

  • Display only text or images the audience must examine.
  • Use large readable type and high contrast.
  • Highlight evidence with labels, not color alone.
  • Keep one idea per visual.
  • Provide alternative text or spoken description for meaningful images.

Step 6 — Rehearse Diagnostically

  1. Record a full timed run.
  2. Mark where pace changes, filler appears, or evidence becomes vague.
  3. Ask a listener to name the purpose and two craft choices.
  4. Revise content before polishing gestures.
  5. Repeat until time, clarity, and delivery are stable.
