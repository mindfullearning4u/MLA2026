Checkpoint: Putting It All Together: Unit 6 Synthesis

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: Final Editorial Review

Hours before a public portfolio showcase, an author discovers that one selected piece no longer represents the collection’s strongest work, the author statement describes an earlier sequence, a credited image is missing permission documentation, and the presentation link opens an outdated file.

A capstone synthesis demonstrates independent control by coordinating selection, revision, reflection, publication, ethics, accessibility, and presentation into a finished body of work.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Prioritize the four problems and justify the order using ethics, audience access, coherence, and quality.
  2. Explain what evidence would prove that the final portfolio represents both polished achievement and genuine growth.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which response demonstrates responsible capstone decision-making?

A. Keep the portfolio unchanged because last-minute consistency is more important than accuracy or ethics.
B. Resolve permission and file-authority risks, update the selection and framing together, then complete a documented final audience check.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Submit a complete Creative Writing Capstone Portfolio and Presentation.

  1. Portfolio: Include at least five polished works in multiple forms, selected for quality, range, risk, and growth.
  2. Revision evidence: Provide a concise change record and before-and-after evidence for every work.
  3. Author statement: Write 500–700 words with a clear throughline, specific craft evidence, growth analysis, and next direction.
  4. Publication: Apply a professional, accessible design with verified navigation, attribution, permissions, and authoritative file versions.
  5. Presentation: Deliver or submit an 8–10 minute script with hook, throughline, selected excerpts, craft analysis, revision evidence, synthesis, and audience question.

Add a 300–400 word final reflection explaining how your strongest choices across the course work together and identifying one specific future goal.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Conduct three final audits—content and coherence, ethics and accessibility, and technical delivery. Correct every issue found and document the three highest-impact changes.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.

Before You Submit

  • Every numbered item is complete.
  • Analysis includes specific evidence and reasoning.
  • The creative work follows the required organization and length.
  • The A/B response is recorded and explained.
  • The revision is visible and its purpose is clear.
