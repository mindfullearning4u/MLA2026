P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue your synthesis review. This page teaches how to answer Unit 6 assessment questions step by step.

Step 4: Identify the Skill Being Tested

Read the question and name the skill before choosing an answer. If the question mentions selecting pieces, think portfolio evidence. If it mentions feedback, think revision. If it mentions spacing or file preview, think publication formatting. If it mentions speaking to an audience, think author presentation.

Step 5: Choose the Answer with Evidence and Effect

The strongest answer usually does three things: it names a specific action, connects that action to a writing or publication purpose, and explains reader or audience effect. Avoid answers that are vague, random, decorative, summary-only, or based only on personal preference.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Choosing the answer that sounds positive but does not name a specific craft, revision, publication, or presentation action.

Why this is incorrect: Unit 6 mastery requires evidence. A vague answer such as "make it better" or "choose the favorite part" does not show control of the standard.

Correct Approach

Correct: Choose the answer that names the exact issue and explains effect. For example, "revise the dialogue so readers infer tension" is stronger than "make the scene better."

Why this is correct: The answer shows a specific writer action and connects it to reader understanding.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
