Lesson: Make Defensible Decisions Across the Whole Portfolio

1. Establish the Nonnegotiables

Separate requirements (standards, rights, privacy, accessibility, file function) from preferences (color, ordering options, decorative style). Requirements set the quality threshold.

2. Rank Constraints and Dependencies

PriorityExamplesReason
1 Safety/ethicsConsent, privacy, rightsMay block publication entirely
2 Meaning/alignmentCorrect versions and evidenceDetermines validity
3 Access/functionContrast, transcripts, linksDetermines usability
4 PolishConsistency and surface errorsRefines an already valid product

Evidence Chain

Claim: what the portfolio demonstrates.
Artifact: which work carries the claim.
Exact evidence: where the feature appears.
Reasoning: how the feature supports purpose or growth.
Verification: how you confirmed the final reader receives the correct version.

4. Resolve Feedback Conflicts

  1. Name the shared reader observation beneath conflicting advice.
  2. Return to purpose, audience, and evidence.
  3. Generate at least two revision options.
  4. Test both against the intended effect.
  5. Choose, adapt, or reject feedback with a documented rationale.

5. Apply a Release Decision

Choose Ready, Ready after named corrections, or Not ready. Support the judgment with evidence from craft, revision, alignment, publication, accessibility, rights, and presentation.

Assessment Strategy

On a new scenario, do not choose the most polished-sounding answer automatically. Identify the highest-level problem, eliminate choices that ignore evidence or ethics, then select the action with the strongest causal connection to improvement.
