Worked Example: Three Narrative Revisions

Example 1 — Repairing Motivation and Causality

Draft: Lio suddenly enters a burning archive to recover a stranger’s notebook.

  1. Symptom: the action feels unbelievable.
  2. Diagnosis: no established desire connects Lio to the risk.
  3. Revision: earlier, Lio learns the notebook contains testimony that could clear his mother’s name.
  4. Causal chain: desire for truth → fire blocks access → choice to enter → injury and recovered evidence.
  5. Result: the same action becomes character-driven rather than arbitrary.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which revision most directly repairs Lio’s unbelievable decision to enter the burning archive?

A. Add more flames and smoke so the scene feels dangerous enough to justify his action.
Weaker response. Increasing danger raises the stakes but does not supply the missing motivation connecting Lio to the choice.
B. Establish earlier that the notebook contains testimony that could clear his mother’s name, creating a desire strong enough to explain the risk.
Stronger response. This revision repairs causality by linking a prior desire to the obstacle, decision, consequence, and recovered evidence.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Controlling Point of View

Draft: “Mara wondered whether Theo lied. Across town, Theo hid the letter and regretted everything.”

  1. Problem: the scene begins in Mara’s consciousness and enters Theo’s without a signal.
  2. Option A: remain with Mara and infer Theo’s lie through behavior.
  3. Option B: insert a scene break and establish Theo as the new focal character.
  4. Decision depends on purpose: uncertainty favors A; dramatic irony may favor B.

Example 3 — Expanding the Right Moment

Draft: “After months of searching, Nia found the missing ledger. She read the final name and decided not to publish it.”

  1. Diagnosis: two consequential moments are compressed.
  2. Revision: summarize months of routine searching, then dramatize the discovery.
  3. Slow time with sensory detail, selected entries, and Nia’s physical response.
  4. Give the decision an immediate cost or consequence.
  5. Result: pacing teaches the reader what matters.

Transfer Test

For each revision, state: symptom → diagnosis → structural change → textual evidence → reader effect.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: A character suddenly lies to protect a stranger. Add one earlier detail that makes the action character-driven without explaining everything.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: Earlier, the character notices the stranger wearing the same emergency-service badge once carried by a missing sibling. That evidence creates a personal reason to protect the stranger while leaving room for uncertainty.
