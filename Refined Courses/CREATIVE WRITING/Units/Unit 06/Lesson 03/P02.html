P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

This page teaches how to diagnose a narrative or fiction draft before revising it for a portfolio.

Notebook Title

Narrative and Fiction Revision Diagnosis

Vocabulary

Conflict: The problem, pressure, or choice that drives a story.

Motivation: The reason a character acts.

Pacing: How quickly or slowly a scene moves for the reader.

Point of view: The position from which the story is told.

Step 1: Identify the Story Job

Before revising, decide what the story or scene is supposed to do. It may reveal a character choice, build suspense, show a relationship changing, or prepare a turning point. If you do not know the story job, your revision may become random.

Example: This scene should show that Mara wants to apologize but is afraid Ellis will reject her.

Step 2: Diagnose the Craft Problem

Ask one question at a time. Is the conflict clear? Does the reader know what the character wants? Is the point of view consistent? Does the scene move too quickly or too slowly? Does the ending follow from the character's choice?

Step 3: Match the Revision to the Problem

If motivation is unclear, add a thought, action, or dialogue line that reveals what the character wants. If pacing is too fast, slow the moment with sensory detail or action beats. If point of view shifts accidentally, revise pronouns, thoughts, and narration so the reader knows whose experience controls the scene.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Define conflict, motivation, pacing, and point of view.

Part B: Write the story job for one narrative or fiction piece.

Part C: Diagnose one craft problem and name the matching revision type.
