Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
narrative arcThe shaped progression of conflict, choices, consequences, and change.The arc moves from concealment to discovery to a costly decision.
causalityThe relationship in which one event or choice meaningfully produces another.Because Mara hides the letter, her friend makes the wrong choice.
character motivationThe desire, fear, belief, or need that explains a character’s actions.Fear of repeating her father’s mistake motivates Lena to leave.
sceneA dramatized unit of action occurring in a particular time and place.The confrontation unfolds moment by moment in the kitchen.
summaryCompressed narration that covers time or information without dramatizing every moment.One paragraph summarizes the quiet months between conflicts.
narrative distanceHow close the narration feels to a character’s immediate thoughts and perceptions.Sensory detail and fragments reduce the distance during the panic.
focalizationThe perspective through which narrative information is perceived or filtered.The scene is focalized through a child who misreads the adults.
turning pointA choice, discovery, or event that changes the direction or stakes of a narrative.Finding the second key forces the narrator to reconsider the accusation.
earned endingA conclusion prepared by earlier choices and patterns while still allowing surprise.The ending surprises readers but resolves the repeated promise motif.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which sequence has the strongest causality?

A. A storm begins. Later, the character remembers a song. Then a key appears.

The events are adjacent, but no clear choice or consequence connects them.

B. The character hides the warning, so the crew leaves; the resulting accident forces her to confess.

Correct. Each choice produces a consequence that changes the next event and the character’s situation.
