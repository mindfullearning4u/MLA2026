Checkpoint: Diagnose a New Fiction Draft

Directions

Complete this distinct checkpoint independently. Analyze the new case and answer all 12 questions in complete sentences. A score of at least 80% is required.

Draft Case

In a near-future story, Asha repairs illegal memory devices. The opening establishes that she refuses to alter childhood memories after a past mistake. Midway, a wealthy client asks her to erase evidence of a crime. Asha agrees immediately. The next three pages summarize the repair process. In the climax, the device reveals that the victim was Asha’s brother, a fact never suggested earlier. The narration has stayed close to Asha, but the final paragraph reports the client’s private plan across the city.

Near-future fiction imagines plausible change a short distance beyond the present. A revelation is newly disclosed information that changes understanding.

Diagnosis

  1. State Asha’s established desire or principle.
  2. Explain why her immediate agreement creates a motivation problem.
  3. Write one plausible obstacle or pressure that could make the choice believable.
  4. Map the revised causal chain through the climax.
  5. Explain why the brother revelation feels unearned.
  6. Propose two earlier details that prepare the revelation without exposing it.

Point of View and Pacing

  1. Diagnose the final paragraph’s focalization problem.
  2. Propose two valid ways to revise that paragraph.
  3. Identify which summarized material should remain compressed.
  4. Identify one consequential moment that should become a full scene.
  5. Write a 120–160-word revised passage addressing motivation, focalization, or pacing.
  6. Explain how your revision improves reader understanding while preserving tension.

80% Mastery Requirement

If you score below 80%, review feedback, correct the identified weaknesses, and resubmit according to your TOR’s directions.
