Checkpoint: Narrative and Fiction Portfolio Revision

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: Revision Crossroads

In the first draft, a character decides to leave home after one short argument. The setting is vivid, but the decision feels unearned. Peer feedback asks for more history; the writer worries that added explanation will slow the pace.

A global revision changes structure, development, motivation, pacing, or meaning. A local revision improves sentences, diction, clarity, or conventions.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. What global problem prevents the turning point from feeling earned? Propose two ways to address it without adding a long explanation.
  2. Which existing strength should the writer preserve while revising, and how can it support the new development?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which revision plan improves motivation while protecting pacing?

A. Add a paragraph that explains the entire family history immediately before the argument.
B. Plant two earlier details or actions that reveal the history gradually, then let the argument activate those established pressures.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Complete a substantial revision of one 700–1,000 word narrative or fiction piece.

  1. Diagnose: State the intended effect and identify the highest-impact global problem.
  2. Plan: Map the opening, development, turning point, and resolution; note what must move, expand, cut, or transform.
  3. Revise: Strengthen character motivation, conflict, pacing, point of view, and thematic consequence.
  4. Edit: Improve paragraphing, sentence control, diction, dialogue, and conventions.
  5. Reflection: Provide two before-and-after excerpts and explain how the revisions improve the whole piece.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Perform a reverse outline of the revised piece. Change one scene or paragraph whose function is unclear, then explain the structural improvement.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.

Before You Submit

  • The creative work follows the required organization and length.
  • The A/B response is recorded and explained.
  • The revision is visible and its purpose is clear.
