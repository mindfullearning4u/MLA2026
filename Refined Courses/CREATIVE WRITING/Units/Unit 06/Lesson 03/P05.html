Independent Work: Narrative and Fiction Portfolio Revision

Purpose

Revise narrative and fiction at the structural, scene, and sentence levels while preserving the work’s central purpose.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Support decisions with precise evidence from your portfolio work.

Source for Your Work

Revision problem: A story has a compelling ending, but the protagonist’s decisive action depends on information introduced only in the final paragraph.

1. Plan Before You Work

Portfolio DecisionEvidence and Plan
Purpose and intended audienceState each precisely.
Strongest current evidenceName the piece, passage, or feature.
Most important improvementDescribe the needed change and intended effect.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger author decision-making? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. Add a sentence near the beginning that directly announces the ending so readers are prepared.
B. Plant meaningful evidence earlier, develop the character’s motivation through scenes, and preserve discovery while making the ending earned.

3. Apply the Lesson

Select one narrative or fiction piece of at least 500 words. Complete a three-level revision plan—development, organization, and language—then produce a revised version with at least one consequential change at each level.

4. Revise with Purpose

Present three before-and-after excerpts and explain how each revision improves coherence, characterization, pacing, or impact.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your work applies the lesson skill to specific portfolio evidence.
  • Your explanation identifies a decision and its effect on the audience.
  • Your revision meaningfully improves quality, coherence, or readiness.
