Continue your notebook notes. This page teaches the final publication pass: conventions, file readiness, and common formatting mistakes.

Step 4: Complete the Final Conventions Pass

A conventions pass is not a full rewrite. It is a careful check for errors that distract readers from the piece. Read one sentence, line, or script beat at a time. Do not rush.

  1. Check capitalization at the beginning of sentences, names, places, and titles.
  2. Check punctuation that guides meaning, especially dialogue punctuation and end marks.
  3. Check spelling for words that spellcheck may miss, such as form/from or there/their.
  4. Check paragraphing, stanzaing, or script spacing after the conventions pass so corrections did not disturb the format.

The goal is not to make every creative choice look the same. The goal is to remove accidental errors while preserving intentional style.

Step 5: Create a Submission-Ready Version

A submission-ready version is the version a reader, reviewer, or publication space can use without needing extra explanation. Prepare it in this order:

  1. Name the file clearly. Use the required course naming pattern if one is given. If not, use a clear name such as Unit6_PortfolioPiece_Title.
  2. Check the order. If the piece is part of a portfolio, make sure it appears in the correct sequence.
  3. Remove drafting marks. Delete comments, highlighted reminders, bracketed notes, and unfinished labels unless the assignment specifically asks for them.
  4. Preview the file. Open or view it the way a reader will see it. A preview often reveals spacing problems that are hidden while editing.
  5. Submit only the final version. Do not include extra drafts unless the assignment asks for process evidence.

Common Mistake: Decorating Instead of Formatting

Incorrect: A student adds random fonts, extra symbols, uneven spacing, and bright colors to make the piece look published, but the title is unclear and the paragraphs are hard to read.

Why this is incorrect: Decoration does not equal publication quality. If visual choices make the piece harder to read, they work against the standard. Formatting should support genre, clarity, and audience access.

Correct Approach

Correct: A student keeps the title clear, uses consistent paragraph spacing, preserves purposeful line breaks, removes draft notes, checks conventions, previews the file, and writes a short note explaining how the format helps the reader.

Why this is correct: This approach treats publication as reader preparation. The final version looks finished because every visible choice helps the reader enter, follow, and understand the piece.
