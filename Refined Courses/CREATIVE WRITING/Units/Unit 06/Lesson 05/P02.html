P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Publication Formatting and Digital Readiness Notes

Vocabulary

  • Digital publication: A finished piece prepared to be read in an online or electronic space.
  • Formatting: The visual arrangement of the writing, including title, spacing, paragraph breaks, line breaks, labels, and order.
  • Readability: How easily a reader can follow the piece without confusion or distraction.
  • File readiness: The final check that the piece is named, organized, complete, and ready to submit.

Step 1: Identify the Publication Format

Before you change spacing or appearance, identify what kind of writing you are preparing. A poem, short story, script, memoir excerpt, and author statement do not use the same format. The format should help the reader understand the genre.

Write this sentence frame in your notebook: The piece I am preparing is a ___, so the reader needs to see ___ clearly.

  • If the piece is a poem, the reader needs clear title, line breaks, stanza breaks, and intentional white space.
  • If the piece is a story or memoir, the reader needs clear paragraphing, dialogue formatting, and scene flow.
  • If the piece is a script, the reader needs character labels, stage directions, and readable spacing.
  • If the piece is an author statement, the reader needs a direct explanation of purpose, craft choices, and growth.

Step 2: Prepare the Reader Before the First Line

A published piece should not begin in confusion. The reader should know what they are reading and how to enter it. This does not mean explaining everything. It means giving the piece a clean entry point.

  1. Check that the title is present and easy to find.
  2. Check that your name or author label is included if the assignment requires it.
  3. Check whether the piece needs a short author note, content context, or portfolio label.
  4. Remove temporary draft labels such as "draft," "final maybe," or "fix later."

A strong title and clean opening tell the reader the piece is finished. A missing title or leftover draft note tells the reader the piece has not been prepared carefully.

Step 3: Check Digital Readability

Digital readability means the piece works on a screen. A reader should not have to guess where a paragraph begins, where a poem pauses, who is speaking, or whether a blank space was accidental.

Use this check in order:

  1. Scan from far away. Notice whether the piece looks crowded, scattered, or uneven.
  2. Read the first screen slowly. Check whether the opening is easy to follow.
  3. Check each break. Every paragraph break, line break, stanza break, or script spacing choice should help meaning, pacing, speaker, or clarity.
  4. Check consistency. Similar elements should look similar. For example, all character names in a script should follow the same style.
