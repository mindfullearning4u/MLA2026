P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page teaches how to write a complete craft rationale.

Step 4: Use a Three-Part Rationale

A complete craft rationale has three parts: selection claim, evidence, and reader effect.

Selection claim: I selected this poem because it shows stronger control of imagery.

Evidence: In revision, I replaced "I was lonely" with the image of an empty bus stop.

Reader effect: The image lets the reader feel loneliness without being told directly.

Step 5: Avoid Vague Praise

Vague praise sounds positive, but it does not prove anything. Words like good, nice, interesting, and strong need evidence. Instead of saying My poem is good, say The repeated image of the bus stop is effective because it shows the speaker waiting even after the chance to leave has passed.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: I picked this story because I like it and it is good.

This is incorrect because it gives an opinion but no evidence, craft choice, revision detail, or reader effect.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I selected this story because the revised dialogue shows clearer character conflict. I changed the apology scene so each character's word choice reveals what they want, which helps the reader understand the tension.

This is correct because it makes a selection claim, uses evidence, and explains reader effect.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Copy the three parts of a complete craft rationale.

Part B: Revise the sentence "This piece is good" into a claim with evidence.

Part C: Write a three-part craft rationale for one piece you may include in your portfolio.
