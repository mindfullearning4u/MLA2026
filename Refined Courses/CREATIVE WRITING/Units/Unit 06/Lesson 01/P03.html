Lesson: Select with Evidence, Explain with Precision

Step 1 — Define the Portfolio Purpose

Name what the portfolio must demonstrate: strongest current craft, growth across time, range, readiness for publication, or a combination. Selection criteria must follow that purpose.

Step 2 — Build an Evidence Matrix

CriterionEvidenceQuestion
Craft controlDiction, imagery, syntax, pacing, formWhere is the choice sustained?
GrowthBefore/after passage and feedbackWhat problem did revision solve?
RangeDifferent risks or techniquesWhat does this add that others do not?

Step 3 — Separate Preference from Judgment

Preference: “This is my favorite because it is personal.” Judgment: “This belongs because its recurring harbor image develops the speaker’s divided loyalty, and revision made that pattern structurally coherent.” Personal meaning may matter, but it is not sufficient evidence.

Step 4 — Write the Rationale

  1. Claim: name why the piece belongs.
  2. Craft evidence: quote or describe an exact decision.
  3. Revision evidence: compare a meaningful before-and-after change.
  4. Effect: explain what readers can perceive or experience.
  5. Portfolio contribution: show what the work adds to the collection.
  6. Limitation: acknowledge one remaining weakness or tradeoff.

Quality Test

Remove the title and ask whether the rationale could describe any piece. If yes, it is too vague. Add exact evidence until the reasoning is unmistakably tied to this artifact.
