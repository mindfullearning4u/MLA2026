Worked Example: Three Selection Decisions

Example 1 — Strongest Piece vs. Strongest Growth

Poem A: polished imagery, little revision evidence. Poem B: slightly less polished, but feedback led the writer to replace abstract grief statements with a controlled pattern of empty-chair images.

  1. Purpose: demonstrate growth and craft reasoning.
  2. Evidence: B contains a traceable shift from abstraction to concrete motif.
  3. Effect: readers infer grief through pattern rather than explanation.
  4. Decision: select B, while acknowledging A may better represent current surface polish.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which portfolio choice best demonstrates growth and craft reasoning?

A. Select Poem A because the most polished piece always provides the strongest evidence of learning.
Weaker response. This assumes current polish is the only criterion and ignores the portfolio’s stated purpose of demonstrating growth.
B. Select Poem B because its revision trail shows abstract grief statements transformed into a controlled empty-chair motif, while acknowledging that Poem A has stronger surface polish.
Stronger response. This choice applies the purpose, cites traceable revision evidence, explains the reader effect, and acknowledges a legitimate tradeoff.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Avoiding Redundancy

Story C and Story D both use first-person narrators and withheld information. C controls revelation through scene order; D relies mainly on an unexplained final twist.

  1. Compare contribution: C demonstrates structural control; D repeats the same category less successfully.
  2. Select C for evidence of sustained technique.
  3. If D remains, revise it to demonstrate a genuinely different skill or genre.
  4. Rationale: selection must improve the portfolio as a system, not reward every acceptable piece.

Example 3 — Full Craft Rationale

Claim: “The Salt Line” belongs because it shows my growth in using setting as pressure rather than decoration.

Evidence: An early draft described the flooded road in one paragraph. The final version returns to the rising salt stain at each decision point.

Effect: The repeated measurement makes time and danger visible without a narrator explaining urgency.

Portfolio contribution: Unlike my dialogue-centered pieces, this story demonstrates structural imagery.

Limitation: The final scene still compresses the sibling’s reaction, so emotional consequence remains a next goal.

Transfer Formula

Portfolio purpose + exact craft evidence + revision evidence + reader effect + unique contribution + honest limitation = defensible selection.
