Checkpoint: Portfolio Selection and Craft Rationale

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: The Selection Problem

A writer has room for three portfolio pieces: a technically clean early poem, a risky but uneven recent story, a revised scene that shows major growth, and a favorite monologue that repeats skills already shown elsewhere.

A craft rationale explains why a work belongs in a portfolio using evidence about purpose, technique, growth, and audience. Curation is purposeful selection and arrangement.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Which three pieces would create the strongest evidence of range and growth? Defend each selection with a different criterion.
  2. What revision would the risky recent story need before it could strengthen rather than weaken the collection?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which approach produces a defensible portfolio selection?

A. Select the three pieces the writer likes most and explain that each is meaningful.
B. Select pieces that collectively show quality, range, growth, and purposeful risk; support every claim with a specific feature of the work.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Create a three-piece portfolio proposal using your own work.

  1. Portfolio purpose: State the audience and the impression the collection should create.
  2. Selection: Name three pieces in at least two forms and summarize each.
  3. Evidence: Cite one specific passage or craft feature from each piece.
  4. Rationale: Write 150–200 words per piece explaining quality, range, growth, and contribution to the whole.
  5. Sequence: Arrange the pieces and explain how the order guides the reader.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Replace one preference-based claim such as “I like this” with a precise claim supported by craft evidence.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.
