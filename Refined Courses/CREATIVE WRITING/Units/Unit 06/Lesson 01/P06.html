Checkpoint: Selection Judgment in a New Portfolio

Directions

Complete this distinct checkpoint independently. Analyze the new case and answer all 12 questions in complete sentences. A score of at least 80% is required.

Candidate Portfolio

Rina must select one anchor piece. Piece A is a technically polished sonnet that imitates a familiar model but shows little revision. Piece B is a braided essay combining a bus timetable, family dialogue, and reflection; drafts show that Rina reorganized the strands after readers lost the chronology. Piece C is an experimental monologue with striking voice, but its final two pages shift perspective without a clear purpose. Rina says, “A is safest, B taught me the most, and C sounds most like me.”

A braided essay interweaves two or more narrative or reflective strands. A sonnet is a fourteen-line poem shaped by an established pattern or variation.

Analysis

  1. State two reasonable portfolio purposes that could produce different selections.
  2. Identify the strongest craft evidence in each piece.
  3. Distinguish Rina’s preference statements from evidence-based judgments.
  4. Evaluate which piece best demonstrates revision growth.
  5. Evaluate which piece best demonstrates distinctive voice.
  6. Explain the main risk of selecting A.

Decision and Defense

  1. Select an anchor piece for a portfolio emphasizing growth and justify it.
  2. Select an anchor piece for a portfolio emphasizing experimental voice and justify it.
  3. For C, propose a test to determine whether the perspective shift is purposeful.
  4. Write a 120–160-word craft rationale for one selection.
  5. Name one limitation your rationale must acknowledge.
  6. Explain why a different reasonable selection could still be defensible.

80% Mastery Requirement

If you score below 80%, review feedback, correct the identified weaknesses, and resubmit according to your TOR’s directions.
