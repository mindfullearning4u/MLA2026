Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
craft rationaleAn evidence-based explanation of why a writer made or preserved particular artistic choices.The rationale connects the fragmented structure to the narrator’s uncertainty.
selection criterionA stated standard used to evaluate whether an item belongs in a collection.Range and revision depth are two portfolio selection criteria.
representativeAccurately showing an important quality, skill, or pattern.The scene is representative of the writer’s strongest dialogue.
rangeMeaningful variety in genre, purpose, technique, voice, or challenge.The portfolio shows range through poetry, fiction, and memoir.
artifactA work or piece of evidence included in a portfolio.A final story and its marked draft are separate artifacts.
revision evidenceSpecific before-and-after material showing how and why a work changed.Two excerpts document the pacing change and its effect.
metacognitionAwareness and analysis of one’s own thinking and decision-making.The reflection explains how the writer’s priorities changed.
tradeoffA gain in one quality that may require giving up or limiting another.Cutting a scene improves pace but reduces background detail.
defensible claimA conclusion supported by relevant reasons and evidence.The claim that the piece shows growth is supported by paired drafts.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which statement is a defensible claim about portfolio selection?

A. “This is my best piece because I like it most.”

This states a preference without a criterion or evidence.

B. “This piece belongs because its two drafts show substantial pacing revisions and the final version demonstrates controlled tension.”

Correct. It applies criteria and supports the conclusion with revision evidence.
