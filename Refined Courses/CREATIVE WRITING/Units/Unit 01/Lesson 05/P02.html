Notebook Title

Lesson 5: Inference and Writer Choices

Vocabulary

  • Inference: A reasonable conclusion based on evidence and thinking.
  • Evidence: A specific word, phrase, detail, action, or image from the writing.
  • Imply: To suggest meaning without stating it directly.
  • Subtext: Meaning beneath the surface of what is said or shown.
  • Reader effect: What a craft choice causes the reader to notice, feel, infer, or question.

How Inference Works in Creative Writing

A writer can state, "The character felt guilty." That is clear, but it may not be the strongest creative choice. A writer can also show guilt through a character avoiding eye contact, hiding an object, or returning to a place after everyone leaves.

Step 1: Identify the detail the writer gives.

Step 2: Ask what the detail suggests.

Step 3: Check that the inference is supported by the detail.

Step 4: Explain why the writer may have chosen to imply instead of directly state.

Embedded Craft Model

Model: "Mara set the unopened apology card on the shelf, behind the clock, where no one would see it unless they already knew to look."

Literal Detail: Mara hides an unopened apology card behind a clock.

Inference: Mara may not be ready to forgive, respond, or face the apology.

Craft Reasoning: The writer uses the hidden card to imply conflict without explaining the entire backstory.
