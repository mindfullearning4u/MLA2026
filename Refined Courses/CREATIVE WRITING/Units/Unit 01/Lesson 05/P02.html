Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

Submit the vocabulary with Page 5 Tasks 1–4 as Notebook Evidence.

TermMeaningExample
observationA directly verifiable detail stated in the text without an added interpretation.The text states that Kit places one script on the desk.
inferenceA conclusion supported by textual observations and logical reasoning.The empty chair and changed routine suggest someone may be absent.
speculationA possibility that goes beyond what available evidence reasonably supports.Claiming the missing partner quit after an argument invents unsupported history.
evidence convergenceSeveral different, relevant details pointing toward the same conclusion.The empty chair, single script, and stored badge all suggest a change in attendance.
evidence hierarchyA ranking of details by how specifically and consistently they support a particular claim.A repeated broken promise may support a claim more strongly than the weather outside.
calibrated claimAn interpretation whose degree of certainty matches the strength and limits of its evidence.The wording “may be worried” matches limited evidence better than “is terrified.”
contradictionA conflict between statements or details that may reveal tension, concealment, or change.A character says she is calm, but her hands shake.
omissionInformation left unstated; its absence can shape reader questions or meaning.The writer never explains why the second chair is empty.
intentional ambiguityTwo or more supported meanings deliberately left open while the situation remains understandable.The final image may suggest either relief or regret, and both readings fit the evidence.
overdeterminationIn this lesson, so much explicit support or explanation that an intended inference leaves little meaningful thinking for the reader.Explaining every motive directly would remove the reader’s opportunity to infer.
