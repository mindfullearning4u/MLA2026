P04 Worked Example

Study how details create reasonable inferences.

Worked Example 1: Object Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Model: "The medal stayed in its box, still wrapped in tissue."

Step 1: The medal has not been displayed.

Step 2: The tissue suggests the medal has not been fully accepted or celebrated.

Step 3: The reader may infer mixed feelings about winning.

Worked Example 2: Action Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Model: "Lena answered yes, but her hand covered the key in her pocket."

Step 1: Lena says yes.

Step 2: Her hand hides or protects the key.

Step 3: The reader may infer hesitation or a plan she has not shared.

Worked Example 3: Setting Detail

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Model: "The party music shook the windows, but Malik sat on the back steps counting porch cracks."

Step 1: The party is loud and active.

Step 2: Malik is outside, focused on cracks instead of the celebration.

Step 3: The reader may infer isolation or discomfort.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Malik hates everyone at the party."

Why this is weak: The passage supports discomfort or isolation, but it does not prove hatred.

Correct: "Malik may feel isolated because he sits outside while the party continues inside."

Why this works: The inference is reasonable and supported by the contrast in the model.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
