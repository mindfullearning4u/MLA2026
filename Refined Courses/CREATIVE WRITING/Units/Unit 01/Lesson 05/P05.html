Independent Work: “The Second Signature”

Directions

Read the complete passage. Answer all 16 questions in complete sentences using precise evidence. Submit these responses with Page 2 vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

The Second Signature

The museum received the painting on a Thursday, wrapped in brown paper and tied with blue cotton string. No return address appeared on the package. Inside, a note in block letters read: FOR THE WINTER EXHIBIT. YOU WILL KNOW WHERE IT BELONGS.

Elena, the collections assistant, recognized the harbor immediately. Her grandfather had painted the same view for forty years: breakwater, red buoy, narrow stripe of sky. This canvas carried his familiar gray-green water, but the clouds were violet, a color he had called dishonest.

She checked the lower corner. His signature curved above the date—1998. Beneath it, visible only when she tilted the canvas, another name had been scraped away.

The director wanted the painting catalogued before lunch. “Anonymous gift, authenticated family work,” he dictated.

Elena did not type. In the conservation room, she compared the canvas with a photograph from her grandfather’s studio. The photograph showed the same harbor painting unfinished. Beside it stood her grandmother, brush in hand, violet paint bright on the palette.

At noon, Elena called her grandmother. The line clicked once, then went quiet.

“Did you send it?” Elena asked.

“Your grandfather signed everything,” her grandmother said.

“That is not what I asked.”

Outside the conservation room, the director’s shoes stopped at the door. Elena watched the scraped space beneath the signature catch the overhead light.

Her grandmother exhaled. “Some names made buyers nervous.”

Elena opened the catalogue entry. She deleted authenticated family work and typed attribution under review. Then she placed the blue string in an evidence envelope instead of the trash.

Part A — Evidence and Calibration

  1. State six observations without interpretation.
  2. Write a 90–120 word accurate paraphrase.
  3. Which three details form the strongest evidence convergence? Explain.
  4. What does the passage establish about authorship, and what remains uncertain?

Part B — Writer Choices

  1. Analyze the contradiction between the violet clouds and the grandfather’s stated opinion.
  2. Explain how the scraped signature functions as both physical evidence and omission.
  3. How does the grandmother’s first answer evade Elena’s question?
  4. Analyze the director’s shoes stopping outside the door. What does this selected detail add?
  5. Explain why Elena preserves the blue string.
  6. How does the sequence of discoveries control reader certainty?

Part C — Rival Inferences

  1. Make the strongest calibrated claim about who painted the canvas.
  2. Develop a plausible rival inference and identify its best supporting detail.
  3. Explain why your preferred claim accounts for more evidence.
  4. Identify one additional fact that would resolve the ambiguity. Explain how it would change the passage.

Part D — Revision and Transfer

  1. Rewrite the grandmother’s final line in a way that overdetermines the meaning. Then explain why the original is stronger.
  2. Write a 160–200 word original scene in which four converging clues suggest a concealed truth, one contradictory clue preserves uncertainty, and the ending changes the evidence rather than explaining it. Identify all five clues and justify your final level of ambiguity.
