Independent Work: Build a Fair Inference

Purpose and Directions

Separate evidence from assumption, compare two interpretations, and design an original scene that guides a reader without explaining everything. Complete every numbered task and submit this work with your vocabulary notes in Notebook Evidence Submission.

Draft for Workshop: “The Spare Key”

A spare key rested on the table beside Lena’s untouched breakfast. Her brother reached for it, stopped, and pushed it back toward her. “You may need this,” he said. Lena studied the new blue tag tied around its ring. The old tag had carried their apartment number in their mother’s handwriting. Outside, a taxi sounded its horn. Her brother lifted one suitcase but left the smaller one beside the door.

1. Evidence, Inference, and Limit

Complete three rows. Each inference must be reasonable but not presented as a confirmed fact.

Exact evidenceReasonable inferenceWhat remains unknown
Record three evidence-based rows in your submission.

2. Choose the Stronger Interpretation

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice is better calibrated to the passage. The answer is not revealed here.

A. Lena’s brother is moving away forever because he has a suitcase and gives her the key.

B. The suitcase, waiting taxi, and transferred key suggest a departure, but the untouched smaller suitcase and unexplained new tag keep its length and purpose uncertain.

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a six-to-eight-sentence scene that implies a change in a relationship without directly naming the change. Use two clues that support the inference and one detail that leaves a believable alternative. State the inference you want readers to make before drafting.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one clue so it guides the reader more precisely without turning the inference into a stated fact. Show the original and revision, then explain what the evidence supports and what uncertainty you intentionally preserved.

Before You Submit

  • I separated facts, inferences, and unknowns.
  • I explained my A/B choice with multiple details.
  • My scene guides an inference without explaining everything.
  • I revised one clue and preserved meaningful uncertainty.
