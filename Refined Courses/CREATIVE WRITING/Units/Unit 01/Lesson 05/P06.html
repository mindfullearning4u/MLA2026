The Unsent Forecast

At 5:40 each morning, Dev recorded the river level and emailed the number to the county office. On Tuesday, the gauge read 14.2 feet. He entered 13.8, hovered over Send, and closed the laptop.

Rain struck the station roof hard enough to blur the radio. The emergency map above his desk marked fourteen feet in yellow and fifteen in red. Dev turned the map facedown.

At six, his sister texted: Road still open. We can move Dad after breakfast.

Dev reopened the report. He changed 13.8 to 14.2 but did not send it. Instead, he called the nursing home and asked how long evacuation took.

When the county supervisor arrived, the laptop displayed the correct number. The unsent message waited beneath a gray button.

“Connection trouble?” she asked.

Dev looked at the rain, then at the road camera showing his sister’s car beside the nursing-home entrance. “Not exactly.”

He pressed Send. The road-closure alert sounded before the message left the outbox.

Analysis

  1. List five direct observations.
  2. Write a 70–90 word accurate paraphrase.
  3. What inference does the false 13.8 entry initially invite?
  4. How does the sister’s text refine that inference?
  5. Analyze the facedown map as action, symbol, and attempted control.
  6. Which detail most strongly establishes Dev’s conflict? Defend your ranking.
  7. Explain the effect of the supervisor’s question and Dev’s calibrated reply.
  8. How does the final sequence complicate responsibility and outcome?

Evaluation and Transfer

  1. Make a calibrated claim about Dev’s motive, distinguishing what is established from what is probable.
  2. Present and evaluate one rival inference.
  3. Add one sentence that would overdetermine the passage. Explain why removing it improves the story.
  4. Write a 140–170 word original scene using three converging clues, one contradiction, and a delayed action to imply a difficult motive. Identify the clues and explain how you calibrated reader certainty.
