Notebook Title

Lesson 4: Reading Complex Craft Models

Vocabulary

  • Craft model: A short text studied for the writer's choices and techniques.
  • Paraphrase: A restatement of meaning in your own words without changing the meaning.
  • Inference: A reasonable conclusion based on evidence and thinking.
  • Compression: A craft move that says a lot in a small space.
  • Subtext: Meaning suggested beneath the surface of the words.

How to Read a Craft Model

Step 1: Read for basic meaning. Before discussing craft, make sure you understand what is happening.

Step 2: Paraphrase the passage accurately. This keeps your craft thinking grounded in the text.

Step 3: Identify a craft choice. Look for diction, imagery, syntax, point of view, pacing, repetition, or contrast.

Step 4: Infer why the writer made that choice. Ask what the choice makes the reader feel, notice, or wonder.

Step 5: Decide what you could try in your own writing.

Embedded Mentor Text

Craft Model: "At the curb, the suitcase waited beside her like a quiet accusation. Nina kept one hand on the handle and one eye on the upstairs window. No curtain moved. No voice called her back."

Accurate Paraphrase: Nina stands by the curb with a suitcase, watching a window and waiting for someone to respond, but no one does.

Craft Note: The suitcase is compared to "a quiet accusation," which suggests conflict without explaining it directly.
