Vocabulary and Definitions

TermMeaningExample
craft modelA text studied not only for what it says but for how deliberate choices create meaning and reader effect.A published scene demonstrates how dialogue can reveal tension.
literal situationThe observable action, setting, or circumstance stated directly in a passage.Two siblings clean an attic while preparing to move.
subtextMeaning suggested beneath the literal words through implication, omission, contrast, or pattern.A character says “I’m fine” while hiding a trembling hand.
compressionThe concentration of substantial meaning into few words or carefully selected details.A writer condenses five years into one reflective paragraph.
patternA meaningful recurrence of words, images, structures, sounds, or ideas.Repeated door images connect moments of change.
contrastThe emphasis on differences between elements, such as actions, images, or situations, to sharpen meaning or tension.A bright kitchen is set against a silent, dark hallway.
juxtapositionThe deliberate side-by-side placement of elements so their relationship produces meaning.A child’s laughter appears beside an empty hospital chair.
craft interactionThe way two or more choices reinforce, complicate, or counteract one another.Short fragments and repeated sounds work together to increase urgency.
interpretive claimA defensible statement about meaning or purpose supported by specific textual evidence.The repeated locked doors suggest the narrator feels trapped.
transferAdapting a craft principle to a new subject, situation, and wording in original writing.A student uses the model’s pattern in an original scene.

Pause and Try

Can the invitation and damaged-bicycle photograph be both juxtaposition and contrast? Explain before checking.

Pause: say or jot your response before opening the explanation. This is optional, ungraded practice, not a submission.

Check your thinking

Yes. Juxtaposition names the side-by-side placement; contrast names the highlighted difference between an opportunity and an obstacle. They can work together. Name the relationship and explain its effect rather than assuming the words are exact synonyms.

Reference Glossary — Understand, Do Not Copy

TermMeaning here
mentor text / paraphraseA mentor text is a craft model. A paraphrase restates essential meaning in genuinely new wording and sentence structure while preserving important relationships.
inference / omissionAn inference is a reasonable conclusion based on evidence. Omission means leaving something out; its significance depends on the surrounding text.
pattern break / time markerA pattern break interrupts an established recurrence. A time marker identifies when something occurs, such as “at six” or “on Friday.”
eviction notice / understatedAn eviction notice is a formal notice concerning removal from a home or property; in the model it signals a housing threat. Understated language presents something less dramatically than its circumstances might suggest.
subtext versus backstorySubtext is implied meaning. Backstory is earlier history behind the present action; do not invent that history and present it as a fact from the model.
