Independent Work: “The Unclaimed Key”

The Unclaimed Key

For eleven years, the brass key hung behind the counter of Sato’s Hardware, looped through a tag that read APARTMENT 3B. Mr. Sato polished it every Monday although the building across the street had been empty since the fire.

Customers asked about the key when rain darkened the store windows and made the abandoned apartments look almost occupied. Mr. Sato always gave the same answer: “Locks outlive explanations.” Then he measured rope or counted washers until the question disappeared.

On the first Monday in June, demolition fencing surrounded the building. A crew painted orange numbers across its doors. Mr. Sato polished the key, returned it to its hook, and placed a small paper bag beneath it.

His daughter Emi found the bag after closing. Inside were three blue tiles, their edges blackened, and a photograph of two boys on a narrow balcony. One boy was her father. The other had been cut from the picture so carefully that the balcony rail continued in a straight white line through the empty space.

“They start tomorrow,” Emi said.

Mr. Sato looked past her toward the numbered doors. “Then they will need good hinges.”

Emi waited. The store clock clicked seven times. Her father removed the key from its hook, but instead of putting it in the bag, he pressed it into her palm.

“It does not open anything now,” he said.

Across the street, wind lifted a corner of the demolition notice. Emi closed her fingers around the key. It was warmer than the coins in the register and heavier than it looked.

Part A — Literal Accuracy and Paraphrase

  1. State the literal situation in two precise sentences without interpretation.
  2. Write a 75–100 word paraphrase that preserves the sequence and essential relationships.
  3. Identify one detail your paraphrase must retain and explain why omitting it would distort meaning.

Part B — Pattern and Craft Interaction

  1. Trace the key pattern from the first paragraph to the final paragraph. How does its function change?
  2. Explain how the recurring Monday routine creates compression.
  3. Analyze the photograph’s cut-out space. How do visual detail and omission interact?
  4. Explain the effect of Mr. Sato’s two evasive statements.
  5. How does the clock’s seven clicks affect pacing and tension?
  6. Analyze the juxtaposition of the key’s warmth and the register coins.

Part C — Interpretation and Evaluation

  1. Develop an interpretive claim about why Mr. Sato gives Emi the key.
  2. Offer one plausible alternative interpretation, then explain why your claim better accounts for the evidence.
  3. Which single sentence carries the greatest compressed meaning? Defend your choice.
  4. Evaluate the title. Explain how “unclaimed” can apply to more than the physical key.

Part D — Transfer

  1. Write a 140–180 word original scene using an ordinary object, a repeated routine, and a pattern break to imply an unstated relationship. Do not copy this passage’s setting, key, fire, or family situation.
  2. Identify four specific craft choices in your scene and explain how at least two interact to create subtext.
