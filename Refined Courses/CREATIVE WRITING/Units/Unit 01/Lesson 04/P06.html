Platform Nine

The station announcement apologized for the delay in three languages. Above Platform Nine, the clock had stopped at 4:17, though phones and wristwatches continued toward evening.

Jonah sat beneath the broken clock with his violin case between his shoes. Each time a train entered, he stood. Each time the doors opened, he touched the silver latch but did not lift the case.

A cleaner swept around him twice. On the third pass she pointed to the departures board. “Your city came and went.”

“I know,” Jonah said.

The next train was not his either. He stood anyway. This time he opened the case. Beneath the violin lay a cream envelope addressed in his own handwriting. He placed it on the bench, tuned one string, and closed the lid again.

When the platform emptied, the stopped clock reflected in the polished case: 4:17 reversed and trembling. Jonah carried the case toward the exit. The envelope remained beneath the clock, bright against the dark wood.

Analysis

  1. State the literal situation without inferring Jonah’s backstory.
  2. Write a 60–80 word accurate paraphrase.
  3. Trace the pattern of Jonah standing when trains arrive. What changes on the final occurrence?
  4. Analyze the stopped clock beside moving personal timepieces.
  5. Explain how repetition and pattern break work together to control pacing.
  6. What subtext is created by the self-addressed envelope?
  7. Analyze the cleaner’s statement and Jonah’s reply as compression.
  8. Explain the effect of “4:17 reversed and trembling.”

Interpretation and Transfer

  1. Make a defensible claim about what Jonah decides by the end. Cite three details.
  2. Present one competing interpretation and explain why it is less convincing.
  3. Write a 120–150 word original scene that uses a repeated action, a time marker, and an intentionally abandoned object to imply a decision. Change the setting and situation completely.
  4. Name four craft choices in your scene and explain how two of them interact to create the intended subtext.
