Checkpoint: Reading Complex Craft Models

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply reading complex craft models in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Clockmaker’s Window”

At noon, the clocks in Mr. Vale’s window disagreed by seven minutes. Children called it a mistake. Adults hurried past as if accuracy were contagious. Only Lena noticed that every clock reached twelve when the train appeared beyond the rooftops.

Term support: A craft model is a text studied for how its choices create meaning—not a pattern to copy word for word.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Trace how the clock details develop from apparent error to possible meaning. Use two exact details.
  2. Explain how the contrast between children, adults, and Lena shapes the reader’s interpretation of Mr. Vale or the clocks.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Copy the clock image, the three groups of observers, and the train ending because those choices made the model effective.
B. Study how repeated details change meaning, then create a different pattern of your own that produces a new effect.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 300–400 word scene inspired by the model’s craft—not its content. Use a recurring detail that changes meaning at least twice and three perspectives that guide interpretation.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—introduce the original setting and recurring detail through action.
  2. Development—show two contrasting interpretations without explaining which is correct.
  3. Turning point—add new information that changes how the recurring detail is understood.
  4. Closing—return to the detail with altered meaning and leave the reader with a supported inference.

4. Revise and Explain

Name the model’s craft move and your original transformation. Revise one moment so your work is clearly independent rather than a close imitation.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
