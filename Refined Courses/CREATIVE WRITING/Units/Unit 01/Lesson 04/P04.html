P04 Worked Example

Study each craft model step by step. The goal is to understand meaning, identify craft, and transfer a technique.

Example 1: Repetition

Craft Model: "No letter. No call. No light in the upstairs room."

Step 1: The passage shows absence.

Step 2: The repeated "No" creates rhythm and emphasis.

Step 3: The reader infers disappointment or abandonment.

Transfer: A writer can repeat a word to intensify a feeling.

Example 2: Object Detail

Craft Model: "His chair was pushed back from the table, but the napkin still sat folded beside the plate."

Step 1: Someone has left or is missing.

Step 2: The chair and napkin are object details.

Step 3: The reader infers an interrupted meal or unresolved moment.

Transfer: A writer can use objects to suggest what happened before the scene began.

Example 3: Contrast

Craft Model: "Outside, the parade drums kept shouting. Inside, Elena folded the program into smaller and smaller squares."

Step 1: The outside world is loud and public, while Elena is quiet and private.

Step 2: The writer uses contrast.

Step 3: The reader infers that Elena's feelings do not match the celebration.

Transfer: A writer can contrast setting and character action to reveal emotion.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The model has words and a character, so the craft is good."

Why this is weak: It does not paraphrase meaning, identify a craft choice, or infer purpose.

Correct: "The model contrasts loud parade drums with Elena's quiet folding action, which suggests she feels separate from the celebration."

Why this works: It names contrast, uses evidence, and explains the inferred effect.
