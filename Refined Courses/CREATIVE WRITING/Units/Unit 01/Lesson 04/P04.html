Worked Example: Three Models, Three Original Transfers

Follow the Whole Thinking Process

Each example includes a complete short model, a literal reading, an evidence-based interpretation, a check on uncertainty, and a finished original transfer. Notice where I revise a claim that goes beyond the evidence. The goal is a method you can use—not a single interpretation to memorize.

Example 1 — Objects Compress an Unstated Situation

Model: “Mara erased the grocery list but left birthday candles on the board. At six, she turned off the porch light without opening the cake box.”

1. Establish and paraphrase. Mara removes most of a list but leaves an item connected to a birthday. Later she switches off the porch light and leaves a cake unopened. I have preserved the contrast without explaining why.

2. Trace two choices. The candles remain on the board; the cake remains unused. These selected details belong to a possible celebration. Switching off the light adds an action that changes the scene’s readiness for a visitor.

3. Test the interpretation. A canceled or postponed celebration is plausible. Disappointment may explain the unused objects, but the passage does not prove bereavement, a missed birthday, or that nobody will ever arrive. Six o’clock is a time marker, not automatic proof of finality.

4. Complete model analysis. The retained candle entry and unopened cake concentrate an expected celebration into two objects. The later action of turning off the porch light complicates that expectation, suggesting a change of plans or disappointment. The interaction creates restrained uncertainty without naming its cause.

5. Transfer the principle. Principle: objects prepared for an event remain unused, implying an altered expectation. New context: a robotics team’s demonstration.

Original transfer scene: “Nico lined up three visitor badges beside the robot. When the hall lights dimmed, he stacked the unused badges beneath the sign that said TRY IT YOURSELF. He ran the demonstration once more, facing the empty chairs.”

Craft rationale: Unused badges and empty chairs reinforce a gap between preparation and attendance. Repeating the demonstration suggests persistence or disappointment without copying candles, a cake, or the original situation.

Example 2 — Side-by-Side Documents Create Tension

Model: “The acceptance letter rested beneath the eviction notice. Luis read the first twice, folded both into the same envelope, and told his sister the mail was mostly advertisements.” An eviction notice is a formal notice concerning removal from a home or property; here it signals a threat to housing.

1. Paraphrase the relationships. Luis handles news of acceptance and a housing threat together, then minimizes what the mail contains when speaking to his sister. The sentence does not identify the acceptance program or explain the housing situation.

2. Name the interaction. The juxtaposition places opportunity beside instability. Folding both documents into one envelope keeps the two kinds of news physically joined. His understated reply adds a mismatch between what he has read and what he says.

3. Compare possibilities. He may be protecting his sister, feeling ashamed, avoiding questions, or not knowing how to explain. Concealment is well supported; a protective motive is possible, not proven more strongly merely because it sounds generous.

4. Complete model analysis. The acceptance letter and eviction notice are juxtaposed so that achievement cannot be read apart from immediate uncertainty. Folding them together reinforces that tension. Luis’s minimizing reply creates subtext about concealment, while leaving its motive unresolved.

5. Transfer the principle. Principle: place evidence of an opportunity beside a practical barrier, then let a response imply the conflict.

Original transfer scene: “Ari pinned the race invitation beside the photograph of her bicycle’s cracked frame. Her teammate asked whether she had chosen a start time. ‘The route looks good,’ Ari said, smoothing the invitation until it covered one wheel.”

Craft rationale: The invitation and damaged bicycle establish opportunity and obstacle. The indirect answer and covering gesture suggest avoidance without announcing what Ari will decide. The new scene transfers the relationship among choices rather than the documents’ exact wording.

Example 3 — A Routine Makes Its Interruption Meaningful

Model: “Every Friday, Grandmother labeled one jar and placed it on the top shelf: peaches, beans, tomatoes, pears. In October she wrote no label. She set the empty jar beside the telephone.”

1. Establish the literal situation. A recurring labeling routine changes: an empty, unlabeled jar goes beside the telephone rather than onto the shelf. We do not know who might call or why.

2. Trace the pattern. “Every Friday” and the list establish recurrence. The absent label, emptiness, and new location interrupt more than one part of that pattern. The break matters because the opening supplies an expectation.

3. Test the inference. Waiting for news is plausible because of the telephone. A practical interruption or distraction is also possible. The jar does not prove a particular illness, death, or family event.

4. Complete model analysis. The repeated named jars make the unlabeled, empty jar stand out. Its placement beside the telephone connects the pattern break with a possible expectation of contact. The model compresses a disruption into a changed object and location, leaving the cause open.

5. Transfer the principle. Principle: establish a routine, interrupt it, and position the changed object near something that suggests a possible next action.

Original transfer scene: “After every practice, Tessa signed the attendance sheet and slid it into the drawer. Today she left her signature line blank. She carried the sheet to the coach’s office and set it beside the closed door, keeping the pen.”

Craft rationale: Filing the signed sheet creates an expected ending. The blank line and move toward the office interrupt closure, while keeping the pen suggests an unfinished decision. It does not prove that Tessa plans to leave the team.
