Checkpoint: Evidence-Based Craft Reasoning

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply evidence-based craft reasoning in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “After the Announcement”

The principal’s voice faded from the speaker. Around Tessa, chairs scraped and backpacks zipped. She remained seated, smoothing the same crease in the permission form until the paper began to shine. “You coming?” Malik asked. Tessa folded the form along a different line.

Term support: Craft reasoning connects exact evidence to a named writing choice, its reader effect, and the writer’s purpose.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Build an evidence-to-effect chain for two exact details: evidence → craft choice → reader effect → possible purpose.
  2. Compare the two folds in the final sentences. Explain how the change functions in the scene without inventing unsupported history.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. The paper shows Tessa is anxious, so the writer should state the reason for her anxiety immediately.
B. The repeated smoothing and changed fold make the decision visible; the writer could test one additional response if the intended effect needs sharpening.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 325–425 word scene in which a repeated physical action reveals a character’s changing decision. Follow it with one evidence-based craft paragraph about your own work.

Required organization:

  1. Scene opening—begin with an event that creates pressure and introduce the repeated action.
  2. Scene development—repeat the action with variation while using dialogue or setting to complicate the choice.
  3. Scene turning point and closing—change the action when the character decides, then show the immediate effect.
  4. Craft paragraph—begin with a clear claim, quote one exact detail, name the craft choice, explain the reader effect, and connect it to your purpose.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise either the repeated action or the craft paragraph so the evidence-reasoning chain is complete. Copy both versions and explain the improvement.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
