Margin Notes

The donated textbook contained comments in two inks. Blue ink corrected dates and underlined definitions. Green ink argued with the author: Who benefits from calling this progress? and What evidence is missing?

Ravi recognized the green handwriting before he recognized the name inside the cover. His mother had used the book twenty-five years earlier, before she left school to work at the textile plant.

He photographed three pages and sent them to her. She replied with a thumbs-up, then deleted the message.

For his history presentation, Ravi projected the printed paragraph but not the green questions. He explained the factory’s expansion, wages, and production records. When his teacher asked whether those numbers told the whole story, Ravi felt the hidden photographs warm in his pocket.

That evening, he reopened the slides. On the final one, he placed the author’s paragraph beside his mother’s question. Beneath both he wrote: Evidence can measure growth without measuring cost.

He did not add her name. He added an empty citation line.

Analysis

  1. Write an 80–100 word accurate paraphrase.
  2. State a focused craft claim about the two ink colors.
  3. Select two details and write the warrant connecting them to the claim.
  4. Explain how the deleted message functions as counterevidence to a simple pride interpretation.
  5. Analyze the hidden photographs as both physical detail and structural delay.
  6. Evaluate the claim that the teacher’s question causes Ravi’s revision.
  7. How does the final empty citation line complicate the ending?
  8. Qualify a claim about whether Ravi is protecting or erasing his mother.

Revision and Transfer

  1. Identify one isolated detail that would be weak evidence by itself and explain why.
  2. Propose one revision that would strengthen the passage’s tension without naming Ravi’s motive. Write a revision rationale and success criterion.
  3. Write a 150–180 word original scene in which a character responds to incomplete or conflicting evidence by revising a public statement.
  4. Analyze your scene with a claim, three pieces of evidence, a warrant, counterevidence, and a qualified conclusion.
