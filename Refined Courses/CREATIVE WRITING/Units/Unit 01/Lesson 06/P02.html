Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
craft claimA focused, arguable statement about how a writer’s choice contributes to an effect.The repeated pauses create a craft claim about the narrator’s hesitation.
evidenceExact language, a recurring pattern, or a structural feature that a reader can verify in the text.Three clipped sentences provide evidence of a rushed pace.
warrantThe reasoning that explains how and why the evidence supports the claim.The warrant explains that the fragments imitate interrupted thought.
relevanceHow directly evidence addresses the particular claim being made.A description of sentence length is relevant to a claim about pacing.
sufficiencyWhether the amount and range of evidence justify the scope of the claim.One example may be insufficient for a claim about the entire story.
representativenessHow fairly selected evidence reflects the passage or the specific portion named in the claim.Evidence from the opening and ending better represents the whole passage.
counterevidenceA detail or pattern that challenges, limits, or complicates a claim.A calm final sentence challenges the claim that the tone is entirely frantic.
qualificationLanguage that narrows a claim to match the strength and limits of its evidence.The phrase “in this scene” qualifies a claim that would otherwise be too broad.
causal claimA statement that a choice produces or contributes to an effect; comparison can support it but does not prove every reader will respond alike.The delayed reveal contributes to the reader’s surprise.
revision rationaleAn evidence-based explanation of a change, its intended effect, its trade-off, and how its success will be checked.The writer explains why a revised ending improves tension but reduces ambiguity.

Pause and Try

A response quotes three lines and says, “This proves the writing is good.” What is missing?

Pause: say or jot your reasoning before opening the explanation. This is optional, ungraded practice—not a submission.

Check your thinking

A focused craft claim and a warrant. Three quotations are not automatically sufficient or representative. Explain the exact choice, how it contributes to an effect, and any detail that limits the claim.

Reference Glossary — Understand, Do Not Copy

Syntax: the arrangement of words and clauses. Finite verb: a verb showing tense, such as “clicked.” A fragment lacks a complete independent clause; a short sentence is not automatically a fragment. Mechanism: how a choice works. Trade-off: what a change gains and may lose. Success criterion: a specific sign used to judge whether a goal was met. Coincidence: events occurring together without an established causal connection.
