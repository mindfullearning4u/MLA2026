Notebook Title

Lesson 6: Evidence-Based Craft Reasoning

Vocabulary

  • Claim: A statement about a writer's craft choice or effect.
  • Evidence: Exact words, details, actions, or images that support a claim.
  • Reasoning: The explanation of how the evidence proves the claim.
  • Craft effect: The result a writer's choice creates for the reader.
  • Workshop feedback: A specific response that helps a writer revise.

Claim, Evidence, Reasoning

Step 1: Make a craft claim. Name what the writer is doing.

Step 2: Choose evidence. Use exact words or details from the writing.

Step 3: Explain reasoning. Tell how the evidence creates the effect.

Step 4: Connect to revision or writer purpose.

Embedded Model

Passage: "The bus pulled away before Ana raised her hand. She kept waving anyway, long after the taillights blurred."

Claim: The writer creates a feeling of missed opportunity.

Evidence: "before Ana raised her hand" and "kept waving anyway."

Reasoning: The bus leaving before Ana acts shows the chance is already gone, and her continued waving shows she has not accepted that loss yet.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary words and definitions.

Part B: Copy the embedded model.

Part C: Write one additional claim about the passage and identify evidence that supports it.

Notebook Teaching Support

Use your notebook to build the skill slowly for Evidence-Based Craft Reasoning. First, copy the key vocabulary. Next, define each term in student-friendly language. Then write one sentence that tells how the term changes a draft or changes the reader's understanding. This course is self-paced, so do not wait for a classroom explanation before you work. Read the prompt, model, scenario, or passage on the page first. Then name the exact craft skill from MLA.CW.WR.08, MLA.CW.WR.09, MLA.CW.COM.02, explain what the writer is doing, and connect that choice to reader effect, meaning, voice, mood, structure, or revision purpose. If the task asks you to write, draft in complete sentences and make the craft move visible rather than hidden. If the task asks you to analyze, quote or describe the specific words that prove your answer. A strong notebook entry should help you answer a question later without guessing.
