Lesson Overview: Creative Writing Identity and Workshop Norms

Your Starting Point as a Writer

You do not need an extraordinary life or a perfect first sentence to begin. Start with something you notice: a worn object, a repeated family phrase, or a moment when someone’s words do not match their actions. Your writing identity is the developing pattern of interests, experiences, questions, and choices that shapes what and how you write. It can change as you experiment.

In this lesson, I will show you how a small observation becomes a notebook experiment. Then we will examine how a thoughtful reader can help a writer revise without taking over. You may invent situations; you never need to share private experiences to succeed.

Lesson Focus

Lesson 1: Creative Writing Identity and Workshop Norms

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.COM.02 — Peer Feedback and Collaborative Techniques.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.COM.04 — Academic Language for ELLs; MLA.CW.COM.05 — Social and Instructional Communication for ELLs.

What You Will Learn

  • Explain how creative writers collect, develop, test, and revise ideas.
  • Use a writer’s notebook as evidence of observation, experimentation, and reflection.
  • Distinguish useful craft feedback from praise, judgment, commands, and vague advice.
  • Use specific evidence and respectful academic language during a workshop.

Why This Matters

Writers rarely produce strong work in one attempt. A trustworthy workshop helps a writer see how a draft affects a real reader while protecting the writer’s ownership of the piece. Learning to name a craft choice, explain its effect, and propose a focused option prepares you to revise your own work and respond responsibly to others.

What You Will Do

  • Record lesson vocabulary and definitions in your writer’s notebook.
  • Study a four-step method for giving evidence-based feedback.
  • Analyze feedback across narrative, poetry, and dialogue examples.
  • Complete a substantial Independent Work analysis and writing task.
  • Complete a different Checkpoint task independently.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Complete Pages 1–4 in order.
  2. Complete Page 5 and submit it with Page 2 vocabulary in Notebook Evidence Submission.
  3. Complete Page 6 and submit it separately in Checkpoint Submission.
  4. Use TOR feedback to correct and resubmit work scoring below 80%.
  5. Complete the five-question lesson quiz; at least 80% is required.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Identify exact craft evidence instead of making general judgments.
  • Explain how a choice affects meaning, voice, mood, clarity, or reader response.
  • Offer a focused revision possibility without rewriting the piece for its author.
  • Use complete sentences and respectful workshop language.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can listen actively and give specific, respectful feedback that helps a writer make an informed revision while keeping ownership of the writing.
