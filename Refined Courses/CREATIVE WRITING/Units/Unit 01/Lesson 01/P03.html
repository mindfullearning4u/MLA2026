P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue your notebook work by learning how feedback works in a Creative Writing workshop.

How Workshop Feedback Works

Workshop feedback is not the same as saying whether you liked a piece of writing. A helpful workshop response teaches the writer something about the writing. It points to a specific place, names the craft choice, explains the effect, and gives a possible next step.

Step 1: Start with the writing, not the writer. Say "The opening image creates curiosity" instead of "You are good at this."

Step 2: Name the craft choice. Identify a word, image, line, detail, structure, voice, or tone choice.

Step 3: Explain the effect on the reader. Tell what the choice makes the reader notice, feel, question, or understand.

Step 4: Offer one clear next move. Suggest a specific revision action the writer could try.

Feedback Sentence Frames

  • I noticed... Use this to point to a specific craft choice.
  • This creates... Use this to explain the effect of the choice.
  • I wondered... Use this to ask a question that could guide revision.
  • One place to develop is... Use this to identify a focused next step.
  • You might try... Use this to offer a possible revision without taking control away from the writer.

Common Mistake

Situation: A classmate shares a scene about a character entering a silent kitchen after an argument.

Incorrect: "This is good. I liked it."

Why this is not enough: The comment is friendly, but it does not tell the writer what is working or what to revise. The writer cannot use it to make a stronger draft.

Correct: "I noticed the silent kitchen setting after the argument. This creates tension because the reader can feel that something important just happened. You might add one object in the kitchen that shows what the character is feeling."

Why this works: The comment names a specific craft choice, explains the reader effect, and gives one focused revision move while respecting the writer's control over the draft.

Notebook Task Part 2

Write the incorrect and correct feedback examples in your notebook. Then write two original feedback sentence frames you could use in a workshop. Each frame must include a place for a specific craft choice and a place for a revision suggestion.
