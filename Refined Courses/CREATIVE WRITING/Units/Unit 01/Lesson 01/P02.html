Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
creative writingOriginal writing shaped through deliberate choices in language, image, structure, voice, character, and form to create an intended effect.A writer reshapes a true memory into a scene that emphasizes uncertainty.
writer’s notebookA working record of observations, questions, experiments, drafts, feedback, and revision plans—not merely a collection of finished work.You collect a line of dialogue, a sensory detail, and two possible openings.
workshopA structured exchange in which writers share drafts, listen actively, discuss craft evidence, and use feedback to make their own revision decisions.A group names what a draft does, asks questions, and offers revision possibilities.
craft choiceA deliberate decision about diction, imagery, detail, structure, voice, tone, pacing, character, dialogue, or form.The writer replaces “walked” with “stumbled” to suggest exhaustion.
feedbackA specific response that identifies draft evidence, explains its effect, and offers a focused possibility for revision.“The abrupt final sentence creates surprise; consider echoing its image earlier.”
active listeningAttentive listening that seeks to understand the writer’s purpose before responding, interrupting, or proposing changes.A reader first asks, “What effect did you want here?”
reader effectThe thought, feeling, question, expectation, or interpretation produced by a particular craft choice.A repeated ticking sound makes the reader expect time to run out.
revisionPurposeful re-seeing and reshaping of a draft to strengthen meaning, effect, structure, voice, or clarity; it is more than correcting errors.The writer moves the reveal, cuts an explanation, and strengthens a recurring image.

Craft Glossary — Use It While You Read

You are not expected to arrive knowing all these terms. First notice the words or action; then use this glossary to find an accurate name. A plain-language observation with evidence is more useful than an impressive label used incorrectly.

Craft terms used in this lesson
TermMeaning and example
writing identityYour developing interests, experiences, questions, and choices as a writer; not a fixed label or a measure of talent.
evidence and inferenceEvidence is what the text actually says or shows. An inference is a reasoned interpretation based on that evidence; it is not automatically a proven fact.
dictionWord choice. “Gripped” suggests a different action or feeling from “held.”
imagery and sensory detailLanguage that evokes sight, sound, touch, taste, or smell. “The rough paper scratched her thumb” uses touch.
personificationGiving a human action or quality to something nonhuman: “the hallway swallowed everyone.”
metaphor and simileA metaphor describes one thing as another without “like” or “as”: light “stitched gold.” A simile makes a comparison using “like” or “as”: sugar “like a small hill.”
contrastA meaningful difference placed side by side, such as a rushing crowd beside a still character.
pacingHow quickly or slowly the reader experiences events. Describing small actions can stretch a brief moment.
dialogue and characterizationDialogue is a character’s spoken words. Characterization is how writing reveals a character through words, actions, thoughts, and details.
subtextMeaning implied beneath the spoken words. A character may say “Fine” while actions suggest discomfort.
ambiguityMore than one plausible meaning or interpretation. Productive ambiguity gives enough evidence to consider alternatives; it is different from missing basic information.
symbolismUsing a concrete object, image, or action to suggest a larger idea. Its meaning must be supported by the passage, not assumed from a universal symbol list.
mood, tone, and voiceMood is the atmosphere or feeling a passage creates for a reader. Tone is the attitude conveyed toward the subject. Voice is the distinctive way a narrator, speaker, or writer sounds.
tension and conflictTension is a sense of uncertainty or pressure. Conflict is a struggle between competing wants, people, or forces; it can happen within a character.
speaker and narrativeThe speaker is the voice speaking in a poem, not necessarily its author. A narrative tells a sequence of connected events.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Choose A or B before opening the feedback.

Which response uses evidence and inference accurately?

A. “She gripped the ticket” is evidence. It may suggest fear, but nearby details are needed to support that inference.
B. “She is afraid of leaving” is evidence because gripping always proves fear.
Check your choice and reasoning

A is stronger. It separates the exact words in the sentence from a possible interpretation. B confuses an inference with evidence and treats one action as proof of only one emotion; gripping could also suggest anger, excitement, or urgency.
