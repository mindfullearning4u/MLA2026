Notebook Title

Lesson 1: Creative Writing Identity and Workshop Norms

Vocabulary

  • Creative writing: Original writing that uses language, structure, image, voice, and craft choices to create an effect for a reader.
  • Writer's notebook: A working space for ideas, observations, drafts, questions, craft notes, feedback, and revision plans.
  • Workshop: A writing routine where writers share work, listen actively, discuss craft, and use feedback to revise.
  • Craft choice: A decision a writer makes about words, details, structure, voice, tone, image, character, or form.
  • Feedback: A specific response that helps a writer understand what is working and what could be strengthened.

What Creative Writers Actually Do

Creative writers do not wait for perfect ideas. They collect small observations, try lines, test voices, revise weak spots, and learn from other writers. The first job in this course is to build habits that help you notice and shape material.

Think of your writer's notebook as a studio. A studio is not only for finished art. It holds sketches, experiments, labels, plans, and notes about what to try next. Your notebook works the same way for writing.

Step 1: Notice something specific. A writer might notice the sound of shoes in a hallway, the color of afternoon light, or the way a person avoids answering a question.

Step 2: Record the detail before it disappears. Write it as a phrase, sentence, list, image, or question.

Step 3: Ask what the detail could become. It might become a character trait, a setting detail, a poem image, a story conflict, or a line of dialogue.

Model Notebook Entry

Observation: A student keeps smoothing the corner of a folded note until the paper becomes soft.

Possible Use: This could show nervousness without saying "the student was nervous."

Craft Note: A small action can reveal emotion. The writer can let the reader infer the feeling through behavior.

Next Draft Move: Place the character in a scene where the note matters, then show the hand movement before the character speaks.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Create the lesson title in your notebook.

Part B: Copy the vocabulary words and definitions.

Part C: Write one original observation from your own surroundings. Then write one sentence explaining how that observation could become part of a poem, story, scene, or character sketch.

Need help? Ask your Teacher of Record if you cannot identify a specific observation or if your notebook entry only gives a general opinion instead of a usable writing detail.
