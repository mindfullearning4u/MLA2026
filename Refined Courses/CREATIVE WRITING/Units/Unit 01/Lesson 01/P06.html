New Source: “The Last Chair”

Writer’s goal: Make the reader sense that Eli is delaying an important decision without explaining the decision yet.

The custodian had stacked every chair except one. Eli sat in it, turning a bus token between his fingers. The metal had warmed against his palm, but the number stamped across its face still looked unfamiliar.

“Building closes in five,” the custodian called.

Eli nodded as if five minutes were an answer. Beyond the glass doors, the Route 6 bus sighed at the curb. He slid the token into his pocket, removed it, and placed it on the empty desk beside him. When the bus pulled away, he did not watch it go. He watched the token stop spinning.

Bus token: a small coin-like object once used to pay for a bus ride.

3. Create an Organized Scene

Writing situation: A character waits outside a closed shop while holding an unopened envelope. The character must decide whether to leave it, open it, or take it away.

Write a 250–350 word original scene. Follow this structure:

  1. Opening paragraph—hook and context: Begin with a concrete action, image, sound, or line of dialogue. Establish the place, the envelope, and the character’s immediate situation. Do not explain the entire history.
  2. Development paragraph—build tension: Use at least two sensory details and one repeated action or image. Let those details reveal hesitation and guide the reader’s attention.
  3. Turning-point paragraph—force a choice: Introduce a new event, discovery, person, or time pressure. Show the character thinking through action, dialogue, or subtext rather than a long explanation.
  4. Closing paragraph—resolve and suggest meaning: Show the character’s decision and immediate consequence. End with an image or action that helps the reader infer why the choice matters.

Keep writer ownership: The structure guides your organization, but the character, circumstances, voice, and meaning must be your own.

4. Revise and Explain

  1. Reread your scene and underline one place where you explained an emotion or motive directly.
  2. Revise that place using an action, sensory detail, dialogue, or subtext.
  3. Copy the original and revised versions. In 3–4 sentences, explain how the revision better supports your purpose and affects the reader.
