Checkpoint: Putting It All Together: Unit 1 Synthesis

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply putting it all together: unit 1 synthesis in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Locked Greenhouse”

At sunrise, Ava found the greenhouse door chained from inside. Her grandmother’s notebook lay open on the wet step, one sentence underlined twice: “A seed remembers the season it was promised.” Through the fogged glass, a figure moved between the empty planting beds. Ava raised her hand to knock, then noticed the key tied around her own wrist.

Term support: Synthesis combines multiple skills into a coherent understanding or product; it is more than listing separate techniques.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Use exact evidence to analyze how voice, tone, imagery, inference, and symbolism work together in the passage.
  2. Explain two plausible interpretations of the key or underlined sentence. Identify the evidence and limitations of each.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Explain the greenhouse mystery immediately so the reader cannot form an incorrect interpretation.
B. Control the clues so readers can form supported inferences while preserving purposeful uncertainty.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a polished 500–650 word scene using the source above as a starting point or an equally complex original premise. Synthesize Unit 1 skills into one coherent piece.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—use a purposeful hook, establish voice and tone, and introduce a concrete question or tension.
  2. Development—build the situation through precise diction, imagery, subtext, and connected evidence rather than explanation.
  3. Turning point—reframe an earlier detail and force a meaningful character choice.
  4. Closing—show the consequence, return to a key image or symbol with changed meaning, and create a resonant final effect.
  5. Craft reflection—write 150–200 words with a central claim, two quoted details, analysis of reader effect, and synthesis of how the choices serve your purpose.

4. Revise and Explain

Complete two revision passes: one for development/organization and one for language/craft. Cite one consequential change from each pass and explain how the revisions work together.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.

80% Mastery Requirement
