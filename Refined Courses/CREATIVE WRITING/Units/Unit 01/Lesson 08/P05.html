Unit Synthesis Model: “After the Storm”

By morning, the storm had rearranged the neighborhood. Branches blocked the road, a blue chair sat in the fountain, and Mrs. Alvarez’s recipe cards clung to the fence like small wet flags. Theo began collecting them without being asked. At the final card, he paused. On the back, beneath a faded list of ingredients, someone had written: Make enough for whoever arrives. He carried the stack toward the community room, where the lights were already coming on.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice makes the stronger evidence-to-effect connection. The answer is not revealed here.

A. The passage is about a storm, and the recipe cards symbolize community because recipes bring people together.

B. The scattered cards first appear as storm debris, but the inclusive note and Theo’s movement toward the lit community room transform them into a prompt for shared action.

3. Create a Polished Scene

Write an eight-to-ten-sentence scene that moves from disruption toward a meaningful choice. State your audience, purpose, and intended tone. Coordinate at least three craft choices drawn from Unit 1, such as voice, diction, syntax, imagery, structure, or selected detail. Do not simply label the choices inside the scene.

4. Revise and Defend

Revise one sentence or short passage to strengthen the relationship among your three craft choices. Show the before-and-after version, then write a three-to-four-sentence craft rationale that quotes evidence, names the choices accurately, and explains how they work together for your audience and purpose.
