Vocabulary and Definitions: Unit 1 Review

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
audienceThe readers a writer anticipates; their knowledge and needs influence the choices.A writer explains unfamiliar context for readers new to the setting.
purposeWhat the writer intends the text to do, such as reveal, question, unsettle, or entertain.The scene is designed to unsettle readers before revealing the truth.
voiceThe distinctive presence created by patterns of diction, syntax, detail, perspective, and attitude.Recurring precise verbs create the narrator’s controlled voice.
toneThe attitude expressed toward the subject or audience; it may shift within a consistent voice.Warm descriptions shift to a suspicious tone when the stranger arrives.
craft interactionThe way two or more writing choices reinforce, complicate, or counteract one another.Sparse dialogue and withheld details work together to create tension.
subtextMeaning implied beneath the literal action or language.A character’s polite words conceal resentment beneath the surface.
calibrated inferenceA conclusion whose certainty and scope match the evidence and its limits.The phrase “may distrust him” matches evidence that is suggestive but incomplete.
warrantThe reasoning that explains why evidence supports a claim.The warrant connects the repeated pauses to the character’s reluctance.
counterevidenceA detail that challenges, limits, or complicates an interpretation.A final generous action complicates the claim that the character is entirely selfish.
goal-aligned feedbackSpecific feedback evaluated against the writer’s intended effect rather than personal preference.A reviewer connects advice to the writer’s goal of creating uncertainty.
revision rationaleAn evidence-based explanation of what changed, why it changed, its trade-off, and how success will be judged.The writer explains that cutting a paragraph quickens pacing but removes context.
synthesisCombining skills or ideas so they work together in a coherent understanding or product.The final draft combines voice, structure, evidence, and revision into one coherent piece.

Reference Glossary — Understand, Do Not Copy

Diction: word choice. Syntax: sentence arrangement. Register: language style for a context. Referent: the person or thing a word points to. Revision hypothesis: a prediction about what a particular change may do. Trade-off: what a choice gains and may lose. Success criterion: observable evidence that a choice serves its purpose.
