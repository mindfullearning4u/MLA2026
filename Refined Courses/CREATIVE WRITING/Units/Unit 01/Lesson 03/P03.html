Direct Teaching: From Evidence to Craft Explanation

The Four-Part Explanation

  1. Evidence: Quote or precisely identify the choice.
  2. Term: Name the most accurate craft term; name a second term when choices overlap.
  3. Effect: Explain what the choice makes the reader notice, infer, feel, or expect.
  4. Purpose: Connect the effect to tone, voice, meaning, pacing, characterization, or structure.

Craft Choices Often Overlap

In “The hallway exhaled the last students into the rain,” exhaled is diction with a tired connotation, personification, and part of the sentence’s imagery. Strong analysis chooses the most relevant terms and explains how the choices work together rather than listing labels.

Denotation, Connotation, and Precision

Walked, stalked, drifted, and lurched can describe movement, but their connotations produce different character inferences. A dictionary can confirm denotation; the surrounding context determines which associations are active.

Syntax and Structure

Short fragments may slow attention, quicken action, imitate thought, or create emphasis. Long cumulative sentences may build pressure, abundance, reflection, or confusion. The term alone does not prove the effect; explain how the pattern functions in that specific passage.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

  • Label without analysis: “This is imagery.” Correct: quote it and explain the sensory and thematic effect.
  • Vague praise: “The detail makes it better.” Correct: identify the detail and the exact reader effect.
  • Term stacking: listing diction, imagery, and syntax without relationships. Correct: explain how the choices reinforce or complicate one another.
  • Assuming connotation is universal: Correct: use context to justify the association.
  • Confusing editing with revision: Correct: revise meaning and effect before proofreading mechanics.

Analysis Sentence Frame

“The writer’s use of [quoted evidence] is [craft term] that suggests [connotation or inference]. Together with [second choice], it creates [reader effect] and supports the purpose of [purpose].”
