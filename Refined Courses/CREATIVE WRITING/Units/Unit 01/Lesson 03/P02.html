Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
dictionDeliberate word choice, including precision, formality, sound, and association.The writer replaces “walked” with “crept” to create tension.
denotationA word’s literal dictionary meaning.The denotation of “storm” is severe weather.
connotationEmotional, cultural, or personal associations carried beyond literal meaning.“Home” can suggest comfort beyond its literal meaning.
imageryLanguage that creates sensory experience through sight, sound, touch, taste, smell, or movement.Cold rain tapped the window and soaked her sleeves.
syntaxThe arrangement and rhythm of words, phrases, clauses, and sentences.One knock. Silence. The short syntax slows the moment.
figurative languageNonliteral language—such as metaphor, simile, or personification—used to create comparison, emphasis, or layered meaning.The floorboards groaned beneath his steps.
detailA selected fact, action, object, or observation that develops character, setting, tone, conflict, or meaning.A cracked photograph hints at the family’s history.
structureThe organization and sequence of parts in a sentence, passage, scene, poem, or complete work.The letter’s contents are withheld until the final paragraph.
styleA recognizable pattern formed by recurring choices in diction, syntax, imagery, detail, voice, and structure.Brief sentences and precise images create a spare style.
reader effectThe thought, feeling, inference, expectation, or interpretation produced by a craft choice or pattern.The delayed answer makes readers anticipate what comes next.

Pause and Try

Can “The floorboards groaned” demonstrate both diction and figurative language? Explain before opening the feedback.

Check your thinking

Yes. “Groaned” is a chosen word, and it gives floorboards a human-like vocal action: personification, a form of figurative language. Choose the term most useful to your explanation, then show its effect in context. You do not need to list every possible label.

Reference Glossary — Understand, Do Not Copy

TermMeaning here
simile / metaphor / personificationA simile compares using like or as; a metaphor presents one thing as another; personification gives a nonhuman thing a human quality or action.
fragment / clauseA fragment is an incomplete sentence used deliberately in some creative writing. A clause contains a subject and verb.
pacing / inferencePacing is how quickly or slowly events seem to unfold. An inference is a reasonable conclusion from evidence, not a confirmed fact.
tone / purposeTone is the attitude communicated by language. Purpose is what the writer intends readers to understand, feel, question, or do.
sill / cumulative / spareA sill is a window ledge. Cumulative means building by adding parts. A spare style uses relatively few, carefully chosen words.
