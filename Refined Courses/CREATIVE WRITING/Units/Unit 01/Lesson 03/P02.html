Notebook Title

Lesson 3: Academic and Craft Vocabulary

Vocabulary

  • Diction: A writer's word choice.
  • Connotation: The feeling or association a word carries beyond its literal meaning.
  • Denotation: The literal dictionary meaning of a word.
  • Imagery: Language that appeals to the senses.
  • Syntax: The arrangement and rhythm of words in a sentence.
  • Style: The recognizable pattern of choices that makes writing sound and feel a certain way.

Why Craft Vocabulary Matters

A writer needs vocabulary for the same reason a musician needs the names of notes and rhythms. The terms do not replace creativity. They help you notice, explain, and revise creative choices more clearly.

Step 1: Read the sentence or passage carefully.

Step 2: Identify the exact word, image, sentence pattern, or detail that creates an effect.

Step 3: Name the craft choice using vocabulary.

Step 4: Explain how the choice affects voice, tone, image, or meaning.

Model Example

Sentence: "The candle trembled in the window."

Craft Term: Diction.

Explanation: The word "trembled" has a nervous connotation. The candle is not just moving; it seems uneasy. That diction helps create a fragile, uncertain tone.
