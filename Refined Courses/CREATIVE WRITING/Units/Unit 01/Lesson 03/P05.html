Independent Work: Craft Vocabulary in Context

Directions

Read closely and answer all thirteen requirements in complete sentences. Quote evidence precisely. Submit this work with Page 2 vocabulary as Notebook Evidence.

Passage: “After Closing”

The library lights went dark one row at a time, leaving the reference desk in a small island of yellow. Mara slid the returned books into a cart. Most landed with a soft thud. The final book refused to close.

A pressed fern lay between pages 214 and 215, thin as a shadow. Beneath it, someone had written in pencil: Not everything lost wants to be found.

Mara looked toward the locked front doors. Rain combed the glass. She should have placed the fern in the lost-and-found drawer. Instead, she reopened the book and read the sentence again.

Part A — Terminology and Analysis

  1. State the passage’s situation and dominant tone.
  2. Analyze “an island of yellow” using imagery and figurative-language terminology.
  3. Explain the diction and connotation of “refused.”
  4. Analyze the simile “thin as a shadow.”
  5. Explain how the sentence fragments and paragraph breaks influence pacing.
  6. Analyze “Rain combed the glass” and explain how it shapes setting or mood.
  7. Identify one structural contrast and explain its reader effect.
  8. Explain how the final action develops characterization without direct explanation.

Part B — Revision and Evaluation

  1. Replace “soft thud” with two alternatives: one that makes the library comforting and one that makes it threatening. Explain each connotation.
  2. Rewrite the second paragraph using different syntax to create urgency while preserving its information.
  3. Identify three craft terms that best explain your revision and justify why they are more precise than two other possible terms.
  4. Write a 100–140 word continuation that sustains or deliberately shifts the tone.
  5. Explain four craft choices in your continuation and how they interact to serve your purpose.
