Checkpoint: Academic and Craft Vocabulary

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply academic and craft vocabulary in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “Under the Awning”

The awning clicked in the wind. Imani watched the empty intersection, rehearsing the apology without speaking it. When the signal changed, she stayed on the curb. The red hand vanished; the white figure appeared; still, her shoes held their place.

Term support: An awning is a covering that extends above a doorway or window. Subtext is meaning suggested beneath spoken words or visible actions.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Identify one example of imagery and one example of subtext. Quote the exact evidence and explain how each functions.
  2. Explain how the repeated traffic signal affects pacing and meaning. Use the terms evidence, craft choice, and reader effect accurately.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. The symbolism proves Imani committed a serious crime and is afraid of punishment.
B. The repeated signal may symbolize opportunities to act, but the passage does not establish why Imani is apologizing.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 275–375 word scene in which a character hesitates at a familiar boundary. Correctly apply at least four lesson terms through the writing and analysis.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—begin with a concrete image that establishes the boundary and situation.
  2. Development—use imagery and one additional craft technique to build subtext.
  3. Turning point—change the pacing when something forces the character to respond.
  4. Closing—end with a symbolic action or object whose meaning is supported by the scene.

4. Revise and Explain

Annotate four craft terms beside exact passages in your scene. Revise one passage where the named term is inaccurate or ineffective, then explain the correction.

Before You Submit

  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
