P04 Worked Example

Study how precise vocabulary helps a writer explain craft choices step by step.

Example 1: Diction

Line: "The dog crept toward the porch."

Step 1: Identify the word creating the effect: "crept."

Step 2: Name the craft term: diction.

Step 3: Explain the connotation: "crept" suggests quiet, cautious movement.

Step 4: Explain the effect: the dog seems uncertain or afraid.

Example 2: Imagery

Line: "Orange dust clung to the windowsill."

Step 1: Identify the sensory detail: orange dust.

Step 2: Name the craft term: imagery.

Step 3: Explain the effect: the visual detail makes the setting feel dry, old, or neglected.

Step 4: Connect to purpose: the image can prepare the reader for a forgotten place.

Example 3: Syntax

Line: "One step. Then another. Then the door."

Step 1: Notice the sentence pattern: short fragments.

Step 2: Name the craft term: syntax.

Step 3: Explain the effect: the short rhythm slows the moment and creates suspense.

Step 4: Connect to purpose: the writer wants the reader to feel each movement.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The author uses detail to make it better."

Why this is weak: "Detail" and "better" are too general. The response does not explain what kind of detail or what effect it creates.

Correct: "The visual imagery of 'orange dust' makes the room feel abandoned and dry, which supports a lonely tone."

Why this works: The corrected response uses the terms imagery and tone, points to exact words, and explains reader effect.
