Worked Example: Precise, Layered Craft Analysis

Example 1 — Diction and Character

Line: “The dog crept toward the porch, stopping whenever the screen door sighed.”

  1. Evidence: “crept,” “stopping,” and “sighed.”
  2. Terms: diction, detail, and personification.
  3. Reasoning: “Crept” suggests caution; repeated stopping confirms uncertainty; the sigh makes the house seem watchful or tired.
  4. Purpose: Build unease while characterizing the dog indirectly.

Model analysis: The cautious connotation of “crept” is reinforced by the dog’s repeated stopping. Personifying the door as something that “sighed” turns an ordinary sound into a possible warning, creating an uneasy reader effect.

Example 2 — Imagery and Setting

Line: “Orange dust clung to the sill, and every footprint ended before the locked room.”

  1. Evidence: color, texture, footprints, and the abrupt endpoint.
  2. Terms: visual imagery, selected detail, and structure.
  3. Reasoning: The dust suggests neglect, while the footprints introduce recent human presence.
  4. Purpose: Make the setting feel abandoned and mysteriously active at once.

Model analysis: The visual imagery of “orange dust” establishes age and neglect, but the footprints complicate that impression. Ending the sentence at “locked room” directs attention toward a boundary and creates suspense.

Example 3 — Syntax and Pacing

Lines: “One step. Then another. Then the door. Behind it, the radio continued playing to an empty room.”

  1. Evidence: three fragments followed by one longer sentence.
  2. Terms: syntax, repetition, structural contrast, and auditory imagery.
  3. Reasoning: Fragments isolate each movement; the longer sentence releases information but deepens the mystery.
  4. Purpose: Slow the approach and make the discovery unsettling.

Model analysis: The repeated fragments force the reader to experience each step separately. When the syntax expands, the radio’s sound reaches an “empty room,” creating a contradiction that intensifies suspense rather than resolving it.

Comparison

All three examples move beyond naming a device. Each explanation quotes evidence, selects accurate terminology, reasons through relationships among choices, and connects the pattern to a purpose. That visible reasoning is the standard for your own analysis.
