P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue by studying how purpose controls voice, tone, and point of view.

Purpose Comes Before the Craft Choice

A writer chooses voice and tone because the writing needs to do something. If the purpose is to make a reader laugh, the diction and rhythm may be playful. If the purpose is to create suspense, the writer may use shorter sentences, unanswered questions, and uneasy details.

Step 1: Decide what the writing should make the reader experience.

Step 2: Choose a speaker or narrator who can create that experience.

Step 3: Select diction and details that match the intended tone.

Step 4: Check whether the tone stays consistent or shifts on purpose.

Embedded Craft Model

Draft: "The box sat on the porch. Nobody had written a name on it. Nobody had knocked. Still, Mara knew it was meant for her."

Voice: The voice is controlled and observant. It does not explain too much.

Tone: The tone is mysterious and tense.

Purpose: The writer wants the reader to feel curiosity and unease.

Craft Choices: Short sentences, repeated "Nobody," and the final sentence create suspense.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The tone is good because the box is interesting."

Why this is not enough: The explanation gives an opinion but does not name a tone or explain which craft choices create it.

Correct: "The tone is mysterious because the writer uses short sentences, the repeated word 'Nobody,' and the unexplained box to make the reader wonder what Mara knows."

Why this works: The response names the tone, points to specific evidence, and explains the reader effect.

Notebook Task Part 2

Write a three-sentence scene about an object arriving unexpectedly. Choose either a mysterious, humorous, or serious tone. Under the scene, identify your intended tone and explain two craft choices that create it.
