P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 2: Voice, Tone, and Writer Purpose

In this lesson, you will learn how writers shape voice and tone so a reader can hear a speaker, sense an attitude, and understand the purpose behind a creative choice.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.COM.03 - Voice and Tone.

Support Standards: MLA.CW.WR.05 - Academic and Craft Vocabulary; MLA.CW.NAR.01 - Narrative Techniques and Point of View.

What You Will Learn

  • How voice shows the personality or presence behind the writing.
  • How tone shows the writer's or speaker's attitude toward the subject.
  • How purpose guides choices in diction, detail, sentence rhythm, and point of view.
  • How to discuss voice and tone using accurate craft vocabulary.

What You Will Do

  • Study short embedded voice and tone models.
  • Identify diction, detail, and point of view choices that create voice.
  • Rewrite short lines to shift tone for a different purpose.
  • Complete a checkpoint explaining your own voice and tone choices.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Your notebook explains the difference between voice and tone.
  • Your examples use craft vocabulary such as diction, tone, point of view, and purpose.
  • Your checkpoint includes an original passage and an explanation of the voice and tone choices you made.
  • Your quiz responses show at least 80% mastery of voice, tone, and writer purpose.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

MLA.CW.COM.03 in student language: I can choose a voice and tone that fit my writing purpose and audience.

Voice and tone matter because readers do not only receive information. They hear attitude, personality, distance, emotion, confidence, uncertainty, humor, and seriousness through the choices a writer makes.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help: Ask for help if you cannot tell whether a passage sounds playful, serious, uncertain, confident, distant, or personal.
