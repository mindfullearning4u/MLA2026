Lesson Overview: Voice, Tone, and Writer Purpose

Lesson Focus

Lesson 2: Voice, Tone, and Writer Purpose

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.COM.03 — Voice and Tone.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.WR.05 — Academic and Craft Vocabulary; MLA.CW.NAR.01 — Narrative Techniques and Point of View.

What You Will Learn

  • Distinguish a writer’s or narrator’s voice from the tone created in a particular moment.
  • Analyze how diction, syntax, detail, imagery, and point of view shape reader response.
  • Match voice and tone to purpose, audience, genre, and situation.
  • Revise a passage so its tone changes while its basic event remains recognizable.

Why This Matters

Readers interpret more than a writer’s literal meaning. They hear confidence, distance, humor, urgency, tenderness, doubt, or resentment through craft choices. Controlling voice and tone allows a writer to guide interpretation deliberately instead of producing an accidental effect.

What You Will Do

  • Record and apply lesson vocabulary.
  • Study a step-by-step method for analyzing voice, tone, and purpose.
  • Compare three fully modeled passages in different tones.
  • Analyze and revise a substantial passage in Independent Work.
  • Create and defend independent craft choices in the Checkpoint.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Complete Pages 1–4 in order.
  2. Submit Page 2 vocabulary and Page 5 work together as Notebook Evidence.
  3. Submit Page 6 separately in Checkpoint Submission.
  4. Correct and resubmit work below 80% using TOR feedback.
  5. Complete the five-question quiz with at least 80% mastery.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Support tone and voice claims with exact textual evidence.
  • Explain how at least three craft choices work together.
  • Revise deliberately for a new purpose without merely replacing emotion words.
  • Use accurate terminology and complete sentences.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can create and explain a voice and tone that fit my purpose, audience, and genre.
