Checkpoint: Voice, Tone, and Writer Purpose

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply voice, tone, and writer purpose in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: “The Returned Map”

After three years away, Sora unfolded the town map on the café table. Someone had circled the old footbridge in blue ink. “You kept it,” her brother said. Sora flattened the torn corner before answering. “I kept the parts that still knew the way.” Outside, rain softened the streets until every direction looked possible.

Term support: Tone is the writer’s or speaker’s attitude toward a subject or audience.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Identify two exact language choices that establish the narrator’s voice or tone. Explain the effect of each.
  2. Infer Sora’s purpose in saying she kept “the parts that still knew the way.” Distinguish what the words support from what remains uncertain.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Replace the final line with “Sora felt hopeful” so readers know the intended tone.
B. Keep the softened streets and open directions, then strengthen one image if the writer wants the hopeful tone to be clearer.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: A character returns an object that has been kept for years. Write a 275–375 word scene with a clear purpose and a consistent voice and tone.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—hook the reader with an action, image, or line of dialogue; establish the object and relationship.
  2. Development—use diction, syntax, and two sensory details to reveal the character’s attitude without labeling it.
  3. Turning point—introduce a response or discovery that complicates the purpose of returning the object.
  4. Closing—show the decision or consequence and end with an image that reinforces or subtly shifts the tone.

4. Revise and Explain

Revise one paragraph by changing at least three words or one sentence pattern. Copy both versions and explain how the revision better matches the intended tone and purpose.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.
