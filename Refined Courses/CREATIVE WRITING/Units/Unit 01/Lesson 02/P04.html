P04 Worked Example

Study how the same event changes when voice, tone, and purpose change.

Example 1: Humorous Voice

Event: A character spills water before an important meeting.

Draft: "Naturally, the water chose the exact moment before my interview to leap from the cup and introduce itself to my shirt."

Step 1: The purpose is to make the problem feel embarrassing but funny.

Step 2: The voice sounds witty because the water is treated like it made a choice.

Step 3: The tone is humorous, not tragic, because the diction is playful.

Example 2: Suspenseful Tone

Event: A character hears a sound outside a window.

Draft: "The tap came again. Not loud. Not hurried. Just patient enough to make Lena stop breathing."

Step 1: The purpose is to create suspense.

Step 2: Short fragments slow the reader down.

Step 3: The detail "patient enough" makes the sound feel intentional, which increases tension.

Example 3: Reflective Voice

Event: A character returns to an old playground.

Draft: "The swings were smaller than I remembered, or maybe I had finally grown into the person who could leave them behind."

Step 1: The purpose is to show memory and growth.

Step 2: The voice is reflective because the narrator thinks beyond the object.

Step 3: The tone is thoughtful because the sentence connects the setting to personal change.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The voice is sad because something happens."

Why this is weak: It does not identify the speaker's sound, the diction, or the sentence choices that create sadness.

Correct: "The voice is reflective and sad because the narrator compares the smaller swings to growing up and leaving childhood behind."

Why this works: It names the voice, points to the comparison, and explains how the craft choice creates meaning.
