Checkpoint Passage: “The Key”

The key arrived in a plain envelope with no return address. Darius held it to the lamp and laughed once, because mystery was apparently cheaper than postage.

He nearly dropped it into the junk drawer. Then he noticed the number stamped beneath the teeth: 214. The laughter left him. Room 214 had belonged to his grandfather’s repair shop before the building was sold.

Darius set the key beside his coffee. By morning, the coffee was cold and the key had not moved.

Checkpoint Requirements

  1. State the passage’s likely purpose and initial tone.
  2. Describe the narrator’s voice using one precise adjective and evidence.
  3. Identify two examples of diction or syntax that create the initial tone.
  4. Locate the tone shift and explain exactly what triggers it.
  5. Name the later tone and support it with two details.
  6. Explain the effect of the final sentence’s parallel structure.
  7. Analyze why the writer withholds the key’s purpose.
  8. Rewrite the final paragraph in 80–110 words to create one different but plausible tone.
  9. Identify three coordinated craft changes in your revision.
  10. Explain in four to five sentences how your revision serves a new purpose while remaining consistent with the established event.
