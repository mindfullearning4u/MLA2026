Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

Submit your vocabulary evidence with Page 5 as Notebook Evidence.

TermMeaningExample
voiceThe distinctive verbal personality of a writer, narrator, or speaker, built through recurring language choices.A narrator repeatedly notices small absurdities and speaks with dry humor.
toneThe attitude toward a subject, person, situation, or audience communicated by the language in a particular passage.The same humorous voice becomes tender when describing a friend.
purposeWhat the writer intends the reader to understand, feel, question, or do.The writer wants readers to reconsider a quick judgment.
dictionThe writer’s choice of words, including their precision, formality, and associations.“Crept” makes an arrival feel different from “strode.”
syntaxThe arrangement of words and sentences, including sentence length, fragments, repetition, and order.“The door opened. Nobody moved.” uses short sentences to pause the action.
point of viewThe perspective from which a story is told and the limits of what the narrator can reveal.First-person “I” gives one narrator’s perspective, not access to everyone’s thoughts.
connotationThe feelings or associations a word suggests beyond its basic dictionary meaning.“Thrifty” and “stingy” both concern spending little, but suggest different judgments.
tone shiftA change in the attitude communicated by a passage, usually prompted by new information or a change in understanding.A joking description becomes respectful when the narrator learns what an object means.

Keep These Ideas Separate

Question to askConcept
Whose recognizable verbal personality do I hear?Voice
What attitude does this language communicate now?Tone
What atmosphere or feeling might the reader experience?Mood
What result is the writer trying to achieve?Purpose

Mood is the atmosphere or feeling created for the reader. A narrator can describe danger in a calm tone while the reader feels an uneasy mood. Readers may respond differently; support your interpretation with evidence.

Reference Glossary — Understand, Do Not Copy

TermMeaning here
audience / genreAudience: intended readers. Genre: a kind of writing, such as a story, poem, or personal reflection.
imagery / personificationImagery uses sensory detail. Personification gives a nonhuman thing a human action or quality.
understatement / mock-formalUnderstatement describes something as less serious or extreme than expected. Mock-formal language uses exaggerated official-sounding words for humorous effect.
reflective / introspective / candidReflective: thoughtfully considering meaning. Introspective: examining one’s own thoughts. Candid: direct and honest.
wry / sardonic / nostalgicWry: quietly humorous about difficulty. Sardonic: mocking, often bitter humor. Nostalgic: looking back with longing or affection.
restrained / defensive / reverentRestrained: holding emotion back. Defensive: protecting oneself from criticism. Reverent: showing deep respect.
parallel structure / contrastParallel structure repeats a grammatical pattern. Contrast places differences together to make them noticeable.

Pause and Try

Which concept names the feeling a reader experiences: tone or mood? Explain the difference in one sentence.

Pause: say or jot your answer before opening the explanation. This is ungraded practice, not a submission.

Check your thinking

Mood names the atmosphere or feeling experienced by the reader; tone names the attitude communicated by the writer or speaker. They can relate, but they are not the same thing.
