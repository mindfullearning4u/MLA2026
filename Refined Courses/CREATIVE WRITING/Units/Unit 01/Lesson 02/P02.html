Notebook Title

Lesson 2: Voice, Tone, and Writer Purpose

Vocabulary

  • Voice: The personality, presence, or sound of the speaker or narrator in the writing.
  • Tone: The attitude the writing communicates toward the subject, character, situation, or reader.
  • Purpose: The reason a writer creates a piece, scene, line, or craft choice.
  • Diction: The writer's word choice.
  • Point of view: The position or perspective from which a story or scene is told.

Step 1: Voice Is the Sound of the Speaker

Voice answers the question, "Who does this sound like?" A voice may sound curious, sarcastic, formal, nervous, playful, direct, dreamy, confident, or guarded. Voice is built through diction, sentence length, rhythm, point of view, and the kinds of details the speaker notices.

Model A: "I walked into the room and saw the broken lamp."

Model B: "So there it was: the lamp, dramatic as ever, lying on the carpet like it had personally lost a battle."

Both models describe a broken lamp, but Model B has a stronger voice. The speaker sounds humorous and opinionated because of words such as "dramatic," "personally," and "lost a battle."

Step 2: Tone Is the Attitude

Tone answers the question, "What attitude does the writing create?" A writer can describe the same event with different tones.

Playful tone: "The rain tapped the windows like it wanted to join the conversation."

Serious tone: "The rain pressed against the windows, steady and gray, while no one at the table spoke."

The subject is rain in both examples. The playful tone uses a friendly comparison. The serious tone uses quiet pressure and silence.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Copy the vocabulary words and definitions.

Part B: Write one sentence in a playful voice and one sentence in a serious voice about the same object.

Part C: Under each sentence, explain one diction choice that helped create the voice or tone.
