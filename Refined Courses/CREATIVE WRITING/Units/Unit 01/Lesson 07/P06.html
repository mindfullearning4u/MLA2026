Checkpoint: Inclusive Academic and Social Communication

Your Purpose

Independently analyze a new source and apply inclusive academic and social communication in organized original writing.

Complete all four parts. A score of at least 80% is required.

New Source: Workshop Exchange

Writer: “My goal is to show that Sam feels caught between family expectations and a new opportunity.” Reader 1: “People from families like that never understand ambition.” Reader 2: “When Sam hides the application beneath the family calendar, I sense competing loyalties. Could another family detail deepen that tension without making the family seem identical?”

Term support: Inclusive communication discusses the text and its effect without stereotyping, attacking identity, or assuming private experience.

1. Analyze the Source

  1. Compare the two responses. Identify specific language that makes one response harmful or useful.
  2. Explain how the stronger response uses evidence, acknowledges complexity, and protects writer ownership.

2. Choose and Defend

Record A or B, then defend your choice in 3–4 sentences using source evidence and a lesson concept. Explain why the other response is weaker.

A. Remove the family conflict because culturally sensitive writing should avoid disagreement within families.
B. Develop the conflict with specific, individual details while avoiding claims that one character represents an entire group.

No answer is revealed before submission. Your choice and reasoning are graded.

3. Create and Organize

Task: Write a 325–425 word scene involving two characters with different perspectives on a meaningful decision. Then write a respectful 100–125 word workshop response to your own scene.

Required organization:

  1. Opening—establish the decision and each character’s relationship to it without reducing either person to a label.
  2. Development—use distinct dialogue, actions, and specific context to make both perspectives understandable.
  3. Turning point and closing—let new information or a choice shift the conversation; end without erasing the disagreement.
  4. Workshop response—state the writer’s goal, cite exact evidence, explain the reader effect, and offer one optional test using respectful language.

4. Revise and Explain

Review for stereotypes, assumptions, or unequal complexity. Revise one line or detail and explain how the change makes the communication more precise and inclusive.

Before You Submit

  • Both analysis questions use exact source evidence.
  • Your A/B defense applies the lesson concept and addresses both choices.
  • Your original writing follows the required organization and demonstrates the lesson skill.
  • Your revision is meaningful and your explanation identifies its effect.
  • Your work is original, complete, and carefully proofread.

80% Mastery Requirement

If your score is below 80%, review the identified weakness and follow your TOR’s directions for correction and resubmission.
