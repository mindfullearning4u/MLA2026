Checkpoint: Revision Conference

Scenario

Mateo’s poem alternates formal English with lines shaped by the Spanish expressions his grandmother uses. His goal is to show how two registers coexist in one memory. During an online conference, a reviewer writes:

“The switching is distracting. Pick one language style and make the poem consistent. Also, the line ‘the house remembers us backward’ does not make sense.”

Mateo replies: “Consistency is not my goal, but I do want the reader to follow the emotional movement.”

The reviewer answers: “I’m only trying to make it clearer.”

Analysis and Repair

  1. Restate Mateo’s goal precisely.
  2. Identify two assumptions embedded in the original feedback.
  3. Distinguish a legitimate access concern from pressure to erase linguistic voice.
  4. Explain why “I’m only trying to make it clearer” does not repair the exchange.
  5. Write a non-blaming statement that names the communication gap.
  6. Paraphrase what the reviewer may be experiencing as a reader.
  7. Write one focused confirmation question for Mateo.
  8. Identify the exact evidence that should anchor further feedback.

Options and Transfer

  1. Offer two revision options that preserve code-meshing while improving access. Explain each tradeoff.
  2. Write a complete revised feedback message using appropriate tone markers and returning ownership to Mateo.
  3. Rewrite “Analyze how code-meshing destabilizes monolingual audience expectations” as an accessible three-step prompt without reducing rigor.
  4. Write a 140–170 word reflection explaining how your response preserves academic precision, linguistic assets, dignity, and writer ownership.
