Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task

Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.

TermMeaningExample
registerThe level and style of language chosen for an audience, purpose, and situation; it is not a measure of intelligence.A writer uses formal language in a grant letter and conversational language in dialogue.
linguistic assetA language, dialect, expression, or communication strategy that adds to a person’s expressive resources.A bilingual expression gives the narrator a precise emotional nuance.
clarificationMaking meaning or a process easier to follow while preserving essential ideas and intellectual demand.Breaking a complex direction into numbered steps clarifies it without lowering rigor.
simplificationReducing complexity; it helps when unnecessary difficulty is removed, but weakens a task if essential reasoning is lost.Removing the analysis requirement would oversimplify the task.
confirmation checkA focused question or paraphrase that invites someone to confirm or correct your understanding.“Do you mean the scene should begin later?” checks the reviewer’s meaning.
repair moveAn action that addresses a communication gap without blame, such as acknowledging a mismatch and asking a focused question.“I may have misunderstood; can you point to the confusing sentence?” repairs a gap respectfully.
goal-aligned feedbackSpecific feedback evaluated against the writer’s stated purpose rather than the reviewer’s preference.“Your ending supports your goal of uncertainty” connects feedback to purpose.
specificityThe use of exact wording, location, and effect instead of vague praise or criticism.“In paragraph two, the repeated adjective weakens the contrast” is specific feedback.
tone markerLanguage that signals a stance, such as “I may be missing…” for uncertainty or “Could we test…” for a collaborative suggestion.“I may be missing something” marks uncertainty and keeps the exchange collaborative.
writer ownershipThe writer’s authority to use, adapt, or decline feedback with reasons, while still meeting the task’s requirements.The writer keeps a deliberate fragment after explaining how it serves the intended voice.

Drag and Drop — Identify the Communication Move

Apply each term to what a writer or reviewer is doing. Drag a word, or click it and then its destination. Select Check, read the feedback, and Retry as needed. Optional, ungraded practice; it does not block progress.

Pause and Try

A reviewer says, “I may be wrong, but you must remove all family expressions.” Which part is a tone marker, and why does it not make the whole comment useful?

Pause: say or jot your reasoning before opening the explanation. This is optional, ungraded practice—not a submission.

Check your thinking

“I may be wrong” marks uncertainty, but the command still ignores the writer’s goal and gives no specific evidence. Tone markers support a helpful message; they cannot replace goal alignment, reasoning, or ownership.

Reference Glossary — Understand, Do Not Copy

Audience: intended readers or listeners. Dialect: a systematic language variety associated with a community; it is not a measure of intelligence. Paraphrase: restate meaning in new wording. Ambiguity: openness to more than one interpretation. Trade-off: what an option gains and may lose. Patronizing: appearing helpful while treating another person as less capable. Multilingual: using or knowing more than one language; needs and preferences vary among individuals.
