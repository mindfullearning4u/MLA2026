Direct Teaching: Inclusive Workshop Communication

The Six-Part Protocol

  1. Ask the writer’s goal: “What effect or question should guide my response?”
  2. Observe before judging: identify exact language or structure.
  3. Paraphrase meaning: confirm what you understand without claiming authority over the writer’s intent.
  4. Connect evidence to effect: explain how the choice currently works for you as a reader.
  5. Offer options: provide more than one possible revision and name tradeoffs.
  6. Return ownership: invite the writer to decide which option fits the purpose.

Clarify Without Lowering Rigor

Accessible language is not less intelligent language. Define essential terms, break multi-step directions into visible stages, provide an example and non-example, and preserve the analytical demand. Replacing “Analyze the interaction of syntax and pacing” with “Underline fragments; describe their rhythm; explain how that rhythm changes the scene’s speed” clarifies the pathway without removing the reasoning.

Treat Language Difference as Information

A nonstandard phrase may be accidental, purposeful, dialect-based, translated, or voice-driven. Ask before correcting: “Is this phrasing part of the narrator’s voice?” Focus feedback on reader effect and context. Do not label a person, language, or dialect as deficient.

Use Communication Repair

When misunderstanding occurs:

  1. Name the gap without blame: “I may be reading this differently.”
  2. Paraphrase your current understanding.
  3. Ask one focused question.
  4. Offer a concrete example.
  5. Confirm the revised understanding.

Repair is evidence of careful collaboration, not failure.

Control Register and Tone

Academic register supports precise analysis. Peer register can be warmer and more conversational. Supportive register slows the exchange and makes next steps explicit. Effective communicators adapt tone without becoming patronizing, vague, or artificially formal.

Feedback Boundaries

  • Critique the text, not the writer’s intelligence or identity.
  • Do not demand “standard” language where voice, dialogue, or context justifies variation.
  • Distinguish comprehension barriers from personal preference.
  • Preserve writer ownership; feedback is evidence, not command.

Inclusive Feedback Frame

Your goal is ____. In “____,” I notice ____. I currently understand this to mean ____. Is that accurate? This choice makes me ____. One option is ____; another is ____. The tradeoff is ____. Which option better matches the voice and effect you want?
