Lesson Overview: Inclusive Academic and Social Communication

Lesson Focus

Lesson 7: Inclusive Academic and Social Communication

Primary Standards: MLA.CW.COM.04 — use accessible academic language with multilingual learners; MLA.CW.COM.05 — communicate effectively in social and instructional contexts.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.COM.02 — use collaborative feedback techniques; MLA.CW.COM.03 — control voice and tone.

What You Will Learn

  • Make complex craft discussion precise without making it needlessly inaccessible.
  • Use questions, paraphrase, examples, and confirmation checks to repair misunderstanding.
  • Give feedback that respects linguistic difference while addressing the writer’s stated goals.
  • Adapt register and tone across academic, peer, and supportive contexts.

Why This Matters

Creative-writing communities depend on language, but participants do not all process, speak, or write in identical ways. Inclusive communication treats linguistic variety as an asset, makes expectations visible, and keeps feedback focused on craft rather than identity.

What You Will Do

  • Record communication vocabulary.
  • Learn a six-part inclusive workshop protocol.
  • Study three complete communication examples and revisions.
  • Analyze a complex workshop transcript and produce accessible feedback.
  • Complete a separate checkpoint involving misunderstanding and repair.

Lesson Workflow

  1. Complete Pages 1–4 in order.
  2. Submit Page 2 vocabulary and Page 5 responses together in Notebook Evidence.
  3. Submit Page 6 responses separately in Checkpoint Submission.
  4. Review TOR feedback and resubmit work below 80%.
  5. Complete the five-question randomized quiz with at least 80%.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Distinguish simplification from clarification.
  • Give evidence-based, goal-aligned feedback.
  • Identify exclusionary assumptions and revise them.
  • Repair communication while preserving dignity and writer ownership.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can discuss writing clearly and respectfully, adapt language to the situation, and repair misunderstandings without lowering expectations or erasing another writer’s voice.
