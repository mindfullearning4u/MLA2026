Independent Work: Communicate with Care

Purpose and Directions

Apply the lesson skill through evidence-based reasoning, focused original writing, and purposeful revision. Complete every numbered task and submit this work with your vocabulary notes in Notebook Evidence Submission. Quality matters more than length.

Workshop Scenario

A peer writes: “The new student stood outside the club room, rereading the handwritten sign. Inside, everyone was already laughing at a joke she had not heard. She adjusted the sleeve covering her name tag and knocked once.” Your job is to respond to the writing without judging the writer or assuming the character’s identity, background, or motive.

1. Observe Before Advising

Complete three rows: quote an exact detail, describe a possible reader effect, and write one respectful question or suggestion. Use language such as “may,” “could,” or “I wondered” when the evidence allows more than one interpretation.

Record three evidence-based rows.

2. Choose the Stronger Reasoning

Record A or B, then explain in two or three sentences why your choice makes the stronger evidence-to-effect connection. The answer is not revealed here.

A. This character is obviously shy. You should explain her culture and why she is afraid so readers understand.

B. The covered name tag and unheard joke may make the character seem uncertain or excluded. Could one additional sensory detail clarify what she notices without assigning her a background or fixed motive?

3. Apply the Skill in Your Writing

Write a five-to-seven-sentence scene in which a character enters an unfamiliar group. Give readers enough context to follow the moment while allowing the character complexity. Then write a two-to-three-sentence workshop response to your own scene that names one strength and one optional, evidence-based revision.

4. Revise for Clarity and Respect

Make the suggested revision or choose a stronger one. Show the original and revised sentence, then explain how the change improves clarity, inclusion, or reader access without flattening the character.

  • I made and explained one purposeful revision.
