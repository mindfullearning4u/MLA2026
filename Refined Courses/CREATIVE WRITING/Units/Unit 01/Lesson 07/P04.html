P04 Worked Example

Study how to make feedback clear, respectful, and useful.

Example 1: Clarify Vocabulary

Feedback: "By tone, I mean the attitude the scene creates. The repeated short sentences create a tense tone."

Step 1: Define the term.

Step 2: Point to evidence.

Step 3: Explain the effect.

Example 2: Ask a Useful Question

Feedback: "I understand the character is leaving, but I wondered what object they choose to take with them. Could one object show what matters most?"

Step 1: State what is understood.

Step 2: Ask a focused craft question.

Step 3: Keep control with the writer.

Example 3: Respect Tone

Feedback: "The serious tone fits the scene. If you want the ending to feel hopeful, you might shift one image from dark to light."

Step 1: Identify current tone.

Step 2: Connect revision to writer purpose.

Step 3: Suggest one option.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Just write it better."

Correct: "The middle scene has strong dialogue, but I could not tell where the characters are standing. You might add one setting detail before the second line of dialogue."

Why this works: The corrected feedback is specific, respectful, and actionable.
