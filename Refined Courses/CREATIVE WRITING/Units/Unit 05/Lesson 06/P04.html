Worked Example: Three Author Presentations

Example 1 — Image and Restraint

Draft excerpt: “The hospital corridor was very quiet and scary, and Mara felt afraid.”

Revision: “The corridor swallowed the squeak of Mara’s shoes.”

  1. Focus: The scene presents fear through restrained description.
  2. Evidence: The verb swallowed personifies the corridor; the single sound makes the silence concrete.
  3. Effect: Readers infer Mara’s fear rather than being told.
  4. Revision rationale: The revision replaces labels with sensory evidence and trusts the reader.

Model presentation line: “I removed the emotional labels so one image could carry both silence and threat.”

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which presentation statement most clearly explains the revision’s craft and effect?

A. I made the sentence better by adding stronger imagery.
Weaker response. This evaluates the revision without identifying what changed or how the reader’s experience changed.
B. I removed the emotional labels and personified the corridor as swallowing one small sound, allowing readers to infer both silence and threat.
Stronger response. This response names the deletion, the new device and evidence, and the intended inferential effect.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Pacing Through Syntax

Excerpt: “He found the key. Turned it. Waited. Nothing.”

  1. Focus: A character expects a consequence that does not arrive.
  2. Evidence: Fragments and one-word sentences compress time, then the final word creates a stop.
  3. Effect: The audience experiences each action and the uneasy pause.
  4. Delivery choice: Read the first three units steadily, pause, then lower volume on “Nothing.”

Model presentation line: “I broke grammatical completeness on purpose because the character’s attention has narrowed to separate beats.”

Example 3 — Symbol and Revision

Early version: A cracked teacup appears once on a kitchen counter.

Final version: The cup appears at the opening, is repaired with visible gold resin midway, and is offered to an estranged sister at the close.

  1. Focus: The story explores repair that does not erase damage.
  2. Evidence: Repetition turns the cup into a motif; the visible seam parallels the sisters’ relationship.
  3. Effect: The final gesture suggests cautious reconciliation without claiming everything is fixed.
  4. Revision rationale: Repetition and placement transform a prop into structural meaning.

Model presentation line: “I revised the cup from decoration into a recurring motif, so its changed condition tracks the relationship.”

Transfer Pattern

For your own work, complete this chain: Exact choice → textual evidence → intended effect → revision reason. If any link is missing, the explanation is incomplete.
