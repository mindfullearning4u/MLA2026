Lesson: Build an Author Presentation

The Core Principle

An author presentation is not a plot summary and not a defense of every sentence. It is a curated window into intention and revision. Curated means carefully selected for a purpose.

The FRAME Process

  1. F — Focus: Name the work, genre, and central creative aim in one sentence.
  2. R — Read: Choose a brief excerpt that reveals a significant craft decision.
  3. A — Analyze: Point to exact words, structure, image, or rhythm in the excerpt.
  4. M — Make the connection: Explain how the choice supports purpose and audience effect.
  5. E — Evaluate revision: Compare an earlier decision with the final one and name what improved.

Common Mistakes

  • Summary trap: retelling events instead of explaining a decision.
  • Intent without evidence: saying “I wanted it to feel tense” without showing how language creates tension.
  • Empty praise: calling a choice “effective” without naming an audience effect.
  • Revision list: naming changes without explaining why the final choice is stronger.
