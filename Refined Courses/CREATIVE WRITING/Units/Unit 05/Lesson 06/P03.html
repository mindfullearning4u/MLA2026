P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page teaches how to build a clear author presentation sequence.

Step 4: Use a Simple Presentation Order

Use this order so the audience can follow your thinking:

  1. Name the title and form.
  2. State the purpose or intended effect.
  3. Explain one or two craft choices.
  4. Describe one revision decision.
  5. Invite the audience to notice something specific as they read or listen.

Step 5: Keep the Creative Work Central

The presentation should help the audience enter the piece, but it should not replace the piece. Avoid explaining every symbol or telling the audience exactly what to think. Instead, point them toward a craft feature they can notice.

Strong audience frame: As you read, notice how the repeated image of the umbrella changes from protection to hesitation.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: My presentation tells the whole plot, explains every line, and leaves no reason to read the piece.

This is incorrect because an author presentation should guide the audience, not replace the creative work.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: My presentation names the purpose, explains two craft choices, and asks the audience to notice the repeated umbrella image.

This is correct because it prepares the audience while keeping the creative work central.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Copy the five-part presentation order.

Part B: Write one audience frame beginning with "As you read, notice..."

Part C: Explain why an author presentation should not summarize every part of the creative work.
