Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
author statementA brief explanation that frames a creative work and identifies purposeful craft decisions.The author statement explains why the story uses an unreliable narrator.
framingThe context an author gives an audience before presenting a work.The speaker introduces the setting before reading the excerpt.
craft choiceA deliberate decision about language, structure, imagery, voice, pacing, or form.Repeating the river image is a craft choice that unifies the poem.
audience effectThe response, question, feeling, or understanding a writer intends to create.The abrupt ending is meant to leave the audience unsettled.
rationaleA clear explanation of why a decision was made.The writer gives a rationale for selecting the opening scene.
excerptA short selected passage from a longer work.The presenter reads a two-paragraph excerpt from the memoir.
deliveryThe use of pace, volume, emphasis, posture, and eye contact while presenting.A slower pace and pause emphasize the final line.
reflectionCareful thinking about a process, its results, and possible next steps.The reflection names what improved and what the writer will revise next.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which author-statement sentence gives a clear rationale?

A. “I chose this excerpt because it is my favorite.”

This gives a preference but does not connect the choice to craft or audience effect.

B. “I chose the opening excerpt because its repeated river image introduces the conflict and prepares listeners for the ending.”

Correct. It explains the craft-based reason and the intended audience effect.
