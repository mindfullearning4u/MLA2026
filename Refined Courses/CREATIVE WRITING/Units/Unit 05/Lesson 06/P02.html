P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

This lesson teaches how to prepare an author presentation that helps the audience understand your creative work.

Notebook Title

Author Presentation Plan

Vocabulary

Author presentation: A short explanation or delivery of creative work that introduces purpose, craft choices, and revision decisions.

Craft reasoning: Explaining why a writer made a choice and how that choice affects the reader.

Reflection: Thinking about what changed during the writing process and why it matters.

Audience framing: Preparing listeners or readers to understand the piece without explaining every detail for them.

Step 1: Start with Purpose, Not Summary

A weak presentation retells every event. A strong presentation begins by explaining what the writer wanted the piece to do. Purpose might include creating suspense, showing a relationship, exploring memory, making a reader laugh, or revealing a change in perspective.

Weak: My story is about a student who walks home and talks to a friend.

Stronger: I wanted the story to show how a small conversation can reveal a friendship changing.

Step 2: Choose Two Craft Choices

Do not try to explain every choice in the piece. Choose two important craft choices. You might explain imagery, dialogue, line breaks, point of view, pacing, title, setting, repetition, or structure.

For each craft choice, use this sentence frame: I used _____ so the reader would _____.

Step 3: Connect Revision to Improvement

An author presentation should show that revision was purposeful. Name one change you made and explain why it improved the piece.

Example: I shortened the final paragraph because feedback showed that the ending explained too much. The shorter ending lets the final image carry the emotion.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Define author presentation and craft reasoning.

Part B: Write the purpose of one creative piece in one sentence.

Part C: Choose two craft choices you could explain in a presentation.
