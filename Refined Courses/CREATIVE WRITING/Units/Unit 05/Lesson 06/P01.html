P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 6: Author presentation of creative work

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.CW.COM.01 - Author presentation of creative work.

Supporting Standards: MLA.CW.WR.08 - Reflection on creative process and product; MLA.CW.COM.03 - Communication of craft reasoning.

What You Will Learn

  • How to introduce creative work clearly to an audience.
  • How to explain craft choices without summarizing the whole piece.
  • How to connect revision decisions to purpose and reader effect.
  • How to prepare a short author presentation or statement.

What You Will Do

  • Study author statements and presentation moves.
  • Identify purpose, audience, and craft choices.
  • Practice explaining revision decisions.
  • Create a short presentation plan for one creative piece.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery when you can present a creative piece by naming its purpose, explaining selected craft choices, and reflecting on revision decisions that improved the final work.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This standard means you are learning how to speak or write as the author of your work. You will explain what you made, why you made key choices, and how revision helped the piece become stronger.

Ask Your TOR (Teacher of Record) for Help

Ask your TOR if your presentation becomes a plot summary, if you cannot explain a craft choice, or if you are unsure how to connect feedback and revision to the final piece.

Student Road Map for This Lesson

Use this overview as a checklist for Author Presentation of Creative Work. Before moving forward, make sure you can say what the lesson standard requires, what product you will create or analyze, and how mastery will be judged. This course is self-paced, so do not wait for a classroom explanation before you work. Read the prompt, model, scenario, or passage on the page first. Then name the exact craft skill from MLA.CW.COM.01, MLA.CW.WR.08, MLA.CW.COM.03, explain what the writer is doing, and connect that choice to reader effect, meaning, voice, mood, structure, or revision purpose. If the task asks you to write, draft in complete sentences and make the craft move visible rather than hidden. If the task asks you to analyze, quote or describe the specific words that prove your answer. Keep your work inside this lesson focus; do not add unrelated literature analysis, research, or personal response unless the task specifically asks for it.
