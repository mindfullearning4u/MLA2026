Checkpoint: Clarity, Cohesion, and Conventions

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: The Missing Connection

Jules found the unsigned letter beneath the register. The café was closing. Rain covered the windows. His sister had not called in six months. He turned the sign to CLOSED. The handwriting leaned sharply to the left.

Clarity makes meaning easy to follow. Cohesion connects ideas and details into a unified flow. Conventions are agreed rules of grammar, punctuation, spelling, and usage.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. What relationship among the details is implied but not yet clear? Cite two details that need a stronger connection.
  2. Where would a transition, sentence combination, or reordered detail improve cohesion? Explain the effect.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which sequence produces a clearer, more cohesive revision?

A. Correct spelling and punctuation first; if every sentence is correct, the passage is automatically cohesive.
B. Clarify the relationship among ideas and improve their sequence first, then edit conventions so the final language is accurate.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Transform the source into a 450–550 word complete scene.

  1. Opening: Establish Jules’s immediate situation and the letter’s significance.
  2. Development: Connect the café, sister, and handwriting through purposeful transitions and a logical sequence.
  3. Turning point: Reveal a decision or discovery.
  4. Resolution: End with a consequence that follows clearly from the choice.

After drafting, complete three passes: (1) clarity of meaning, (2) cohesion and transitions, and (3) conventions. List one change from each pass and explain its purpose.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Choose one choppy section and revise its sentence boundaries or order. Read it aloud and make one additional change for flow.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.
