P04 Worked Example

Study each worked example. Notice the order: clarity, cohesion, then conventions.

Worked Example 1: Clarity

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Unclear: Jordan told Sam that he should leave because he was angry.

Problem: The reader cannot tell who is angry.

Revision: Jordan told Sam to leave because Jordan was too angry to keep talking.

Why it works: The revision names the person and removes pronoun confusion.

Worked Example 2: Cohesion

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Choppy: The rain stopped. I found the letter. The house was quiet.

Revision: After the rain stopped, I found the letter in the quiet house.

Why it works: The transition After the rain stopped connects time, action, and setting.

Worked Example 3: Conventions

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Error: i opened the door it was snowing

Revision: I opened the door. It was snowing.

Why it works: Capitalization and sentence boundaries help the reader process the action clearly.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: Only spelling matters during revision.

This is incorrect because revision also improves meaning, flow, structure, and reader understanding.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: Strong revision improves clarity, cohesion, and conventions so the reader can understand the creative work.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
