Worked Example: Three Editorial Repairs

Example 1 — Reference and Spatial Logic

Draft: “Maya told Lena she left it beside her chair. When she returned, it was gone.” The pronouns allow several readings. Revision: “Maya told Lena, ‘I left the portfolio beside your chair.’ When Maya returned, the portfolio was gone.” The change clarifies speaker, owner, object, and return.

Example 2 — Cohesion Without Overexplaining

Draft jumps from an argument to a train platform. Revision adds a causal action: “Because he could not answer her accusation, Devon grabbed the envelope and ran for the last train.” The transition works because character choice—not “later”—connects scenes.

Example 3 — Intentional Fragment

Draft: “The alarm sounded. Loud. Red. Unavoidable.” The fragments create impact but “red” lacks a clear referent. Revision: “The alarm sounded. Loud. The red panel pulsed—unavoidable.” Two fragments remain purposeful; the visual relationship becomes legible.

Edit Log

Record issue → reader risk → revision → preserved effect. This proves that an edit supports meaning instead of merely obeying a rule.
