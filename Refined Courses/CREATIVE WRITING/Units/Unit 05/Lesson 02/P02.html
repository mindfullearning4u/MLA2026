Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
clarityEase with which the intended meaning can be understood.A clear revision names which character “she” refers to.
cohesionConnections that make the parts of a text function together.Repeated images of frost link the opening, conflict, and ending.
coherenceThe overall logical and meaningful unity of a text.Every scene advances the same central conflict, so the story feels coherent.
antecedentThe noun to which a pronoun refers.In “Mara lifted the box before she opened it,” Mara is the antecedent of she.
chronologyThe sequence of events in time.Dates at each scene break help readers follow the chronology.
transitionA signal showing relationship or movement.“Three weeks earlier” marks a transition into a flashback.
lexical chainRelated words repeated or varied across a passage.Tide, current, harbor, and drift form a lexical chain.
given-new flowSentence movement from known information toward new information.The second sentence begins with the key named in the first, then reveals what it opens.
sentence boundaryThe point where one complete sentence ends and another begins.A period repairs the run-on by marking a sentence boundary.
intentional fragmentAn incomplete sentence used deliberately for effect.“Not this time.” is an intentional fragment that sharpens resolve.
usageConventional ways words and structures are used.The editor checks whether the verb agrees with its subject.
house styleA publication’s consistent editorial rules.The journal’s house style requires numerals for dates.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which revision most improves both clarity and cohesion?

A. Replace every short sentence with a longer one.

Not the strongest choice. Sentence length alone does not clarify references or connect ideas.

B. Name the unclear antecedent and add a transition that shows when the scene shifts.

Correct. Naming the antecedent improves clarity, while the transition strengthens cohesion by showing the relationship between scenes.
