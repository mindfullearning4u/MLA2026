Notebook Title

Lesson 2: Clarity, Cohesion, and Conventions

Vocabulary

  • Clarity: How easy the writing is to understand.
  • Cohesion: How well ideas, sentences, scenes, or lines connect.
  • Conventions: Grammar, punctuation, capitalization, spelling, and formatting rules.
  • Transition: A word, phrase, sentence, or detail that connects one part to another.
  • Pronoun reference: The noun a pronoun such as he, she, they, or it refers to.
  • Publication-ready: Revised and edited enough for an audience beyond the writer.

Step 1: Revise for Clarity First

Unclear draft: Mara handed Lena the box after she dropped it, and it made her upset.

The reader cannot tell who dropped the box, what it refers to, or who is upset. A clear revision names the people and objects: After Lena dropped the box, Mara handed it back. Lena looked upset because the lid had cracked.

Step 2: Revise for Cohesion

Cohesion helps the reader move through the draft. If a scene jumps from morning to night without a signal, add a transition. Example: By sunset, the cracked lid still sat between them on the kitchen table. This sentence connects time, image, and conflict.

Step 3: Edit Conventions Last

Conventions matter, but editing them too early can distract from bigger meaning problems. First fix unclear meaning and weak connections. Then check punctuation, capitalization, spelling, and sentence boundaries. Editing last helps the final draft look ready for readers.
