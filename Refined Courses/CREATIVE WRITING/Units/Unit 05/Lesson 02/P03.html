Lesson: The Four-Pass Editorial Protocol

Pass 1 — Reader Orientation

Track who speaks or acts, where the reader is, when events occur, and what each pronoun refers to. Mark every point where a reader could form the wrong mental picture.

Pass 2 — Cohesion and Coherence

Map cause, contrast, sequence, comparison, and return. Use transitions only where relationships are not otherwise visible. Build lexical chains and place known information before new information when clarity requires it.

Pass 3 — Sentence Control

Check boundaries, coordination, subordination, modifier placement, parallelism, tense, and agreement. Preserve a fragment or unusual syntax only when its effect is identifiable and worth the cost.

Pass 4 — Publication Proof

Read aloud; inspect punctuation, capitalization, spelling, dialogue format, paragraphing, and house style. Compare the final version with the original purpose so correctness does not erase voice.

Pause and Choose: Repair Reader Orientation

Draft: “Maya handed Lena the folder after she finished the interview.” The context does not reveal who finished. Which revision is clearest?

A. “After Lena finished the interview, Maya handed her the folder.”
Stronger choice. Naming Lena at the decision point removes the ambiguous antecedent while preserving the original sequence.
B. “After she finished it, she handed her the folder.”
Weaker choice. More pronouns increase ambiguity. A reader cannot build an accurate mental picture when the actors and referents remain hidden.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Pause and Choose: Correct Without Erasing Voice

A narrator’s fragment—“No door. No window. Just the hum.”—intentionally creates panic. What should an editor do?

A. Combine every fragment into one conventional sentence because fragments are always errors.
Weaker choice. Mechanical correctness is not the only criterion. The fragments have a clear rhythmic and psychological effect, so automatic correction could flatten the voice.
B. Preserve the fragments if the surrounding passage makes their meaning clear and their urgency supports the purpose.
Stronger choice. The editor identifies the deviation, tests its effect, and keeps it deliberately. Control means knowing when a convention serves the reader and when a purposeful exception serves the piece.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.
