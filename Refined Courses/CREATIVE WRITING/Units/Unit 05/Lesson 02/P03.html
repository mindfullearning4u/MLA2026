This page continues the lesson by showing how clarity, cohesion, and conventions work together.

Step 4: Do Not Confuse Creative Ambiguity with Confusion

Creative ambiguity is intentional and still supported by evidence. Confusion happens when the reader cannot tell basic information. If the reader cannot tell who is speaking, where the scene is, or what action happened, revise for clarity.

Step 5: Explain Convention Choices

Some creative writing uses fragments, line breaks, or unusual punctuation. These choices still need purpose. A fragment can create emphasis, but repeated accidental fragments can confuse readers. A strong writer can explain when a convention choice is intentional.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: It is creative writing, so conventions do not matter.

This is incorrect because readers still need enough control to understand the work. Creative choices should be purposeful, not accidental.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: Creative writing can bend conventions when the choice supports meaning, but the draft still needs enough clarity and cohesion for readers to follow it.

This is correct because it protects both creative voice and reader understanding.
