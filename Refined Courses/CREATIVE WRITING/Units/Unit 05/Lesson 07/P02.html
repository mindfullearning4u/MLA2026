P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

This page teaches how to select and diagnose a piece before final portfolio preparation.

Notebook Title

Portfolio-Ready Preparation Plan

Vocabulary

Portfolio-ready: Revised, formatted, checked, and supported by evidence of craft decision-making.

Selection rationale: The reason a writer chooses a piece for a portfolio.

Revision evidence: Proof that feedback, rereading, or craft goals led to meaningful changes.

Final polish: The last check for clarity, cohesion, conventions, and format.

Step 1: Select with a Reason

Choose a piece because it shows growth, strong craft, or clear publication potential. Do not choose only because it was easy or because it was the first draft you found.

Weak selection reason: I chose this because it is short.

Strong selection reason: I chose this poem because feedback helped me revise the ending, and the final version shows stronger imagery and pacing.

Step 2: Diagnose the Draft Before Polishing

Before final editing, ask what kind of revision the piece still needs. Check in this order: purpose, feedback, structure or format, clarity, cohesion, conventions, and presentation. If the purpose is unclear, fix that before correcting commas. If the format is confusing, fix that before writing the final author note.

Step 3: Match Evidence to the Final Piece

A portfolio-ready piece should have evidence. Evidence might include a feedback note, a before-and-after sentence, a format change, a revised ending, or an explanation of a craft choice. Evidence shows that the final version did not happen by accident.

Self-Paced Learning Path

If you need support: Choose one piece and name one reason it belongs in a portfolio.

If you are on pace: Choose one piece, diagnose its needs, and list revision evidence.

If you are ready for a challenge: Compare two pieces and explain which one better shows growth and publication readiness.
