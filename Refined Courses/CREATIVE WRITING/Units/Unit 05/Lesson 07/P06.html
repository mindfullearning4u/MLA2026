Checkpoint: Integrated Publication-Ready Portfolio Preparation

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: The Uneven Portfolio

A writer’s portfolio contains a polished poem, an unrevised first-draft story, a strong script with inconsistent formatting, and an author statement that lists assignments without explaining growth. The pieces are individually promising, but the collection does not yet communicate a coherent identity or meet publication standards.

A portfolio is a deliberately selected and organized body of work. Publication-ready means content, conventions, formatting, attribution, accessibility, and presentation have been purposefully completed for an audience.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Identify the three highest-priority revisions and justify the order in which the writer should complete them.
  2. What unifying idea could the author statement develop without pretending that every piece is the same?

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which approach creates a more purposeful publication-ready portfolio?

A. Include every draft in chronological order because a larger portfolio always appears more accomplished.
B. Select strong evidence of range and growth, revise each piece to a common quality threshold, and organize the collection around a clear rationale.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Prepare an integrated mini-portfolio containing three original works in at least two formats.

  1. Selection: Choose pieces that demonstrate range, growth, and intentional craft.
  2. Revision: Complete a substantive revision and final conventions pass on each piece.
  3. Organization: Create a title page, table of contents, purposeful sequence, and consistent visual system.
  4. Context notes: Write 100–150 words per piece explaining purpose, audience, craft, feedback used, and key revision.
  5. Quality control: Verify format conventions, attribution, accessibility, and working navigation.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Conduct a portfolio-level consistency pass. Correct one inconsistency in design or labeling and one weakness in the sequence; explain the reader effect.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.

Before You Submit

  • Every numbered item is complete.
  • Analysis includes specific evidence and reasoning.
  • The creative work follows the required organization and length.
  • The A/B response is recorded and explained.
  • The revision is visible and its purpose is clear.

Mastery Target
