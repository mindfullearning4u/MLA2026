Lesson: The Purpose-Centered Workshop Cycle

Before Workshop

Writer supplies genre, audience, purpose, two questions, and boundaries. Readers preview the draft and mark exact places where their experience changes.

During Workshop: Six Moves

  1. Writer reads the purpose statement; listeners do not interrupt.
  2. Reader describes one exact observation.
  3. Reader explains the resulting interpretation or experience.
  4. Reader asks an open craft question.
  5. Reader offers feedforward tied to a criterion—not a command.
  6. Writer paraphrases what was heard and asks clarifying questions before deciding.

Pause and Choose: Give Usable Feedforward

A draft’s opening makes the reader curious, but the central conflict does not become clear until page three. Which workshop comment is most useful?

A. “The beginning is confusing. You should rewrite it.”
Weaker choice. It labels the draft and commands a solution without pointing to an exact place, describing the reader effect, or connecting advice to a criterion.
B. “I became curious about the locked box in paragraph one, but I could not identify what the character risked until page three. Could an earlier consequence clarify the central conflict while preserving mystery?”
Stronger choice. It gives a textual observation, names the reader’s experience, and asks a purpose-centered craft question. The writer retains authority over the solution.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

After Workshop

Writer clusters feedback, distinguishes pattern from preference, chooses accept/adapt/test/set aside, and writes a revision plan. Thanking a reader does not require obeying every suggestion.

Visual Feedback Ladder

TEXTUAL OBSERVATION → READER EFFECT → CRAFT QUESTION → CRITERION-BASED FEEDFORWARD → WRITER DECISION

Pause and Choose: Respond as the Writer

Three readers report losing track of the timeline, but they suggest different fixes. What is the strongest next move?

A. Mark the exact confusion points, identify the shared pattern, and test a revision that clarifies sequence without automatically adopting any one suggestion.
Stronger choice. Repeated reader experience is strong evidence of a problem, while conflicting solutions remain options. The writer diagnoses first and preserves purposeful control.
B. Choose the suggestion from the most confident reader and apply it everywhere.
Weaker choice. Confidence is not a craft criterion. This approach skips evidence, purpose, and comparison of effects.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Learning Routes and Safety

Use color-coded comment types, rehearse responses aloud, or sort feedback cards. Critique the work, not the person; do not pressure disclosure; protect private drafts; pause and contact the TOR if norms are violated.
