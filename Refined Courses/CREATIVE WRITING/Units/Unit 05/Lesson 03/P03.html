Lesson: The Purpose-Centered Workshop Cycle

Before Workshop

Writer supplies genre, audience, purpose, two questions, and boundaries. Readers preview the draft and mark exact places where their experience changes.

During Workshop: Six Moves

  1. Writer reads the purpose statement; listeners do not interrupt.
  2. Reader describes one exact observation.
  3. Reader explains the resulting interpretation or experience.
  4. Reader asks an open craft question.
  5. Reader offers feedforward tied to a criterion—not a command.
  6. Writer paraphrases what was heard and asks clarifying questions before deciding.

After Workshop

Writer clusters feedback, distinguishes pattern from preference, chooses accept/adapt/test/set aside, and writes a revision plan. Thanking a reader does not require obeying every suggestion.

Visual Feedback Ladder

TEXTUAL OBSERVATION → READER EFFECT → CRAFT QUESTION → CRITERION-BASED FEEDFORWARD → WRITER DECISION

Learning Routes and Safety

Use color-coded comment types, rehearse responses aloud, or sort feedback cards. Critique the work, not the person; do not pressure disclosure; protect private drafts; pause and contact the TOR if norms are violated.
