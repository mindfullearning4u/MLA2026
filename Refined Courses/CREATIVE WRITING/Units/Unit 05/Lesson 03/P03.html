P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page shows how to give revision suggestions without taking control of another writer's draft.

Step 4: Give a Specific Suggestion

A workshop suggestion should point to a craft move, not command the writer to make the story yours. Helpful suggestion: Consider adding one sensory detail after the light clicks so readers know what the narrator notices next.

Step 5: Receive Feedback Actively

When you receive feedback, do not argue immediately. First, write down what the reader noticed. Then decide whether to use, adapt, or set aside the suggestion. Active listening does not mean obeying every comment; it means understanding the comment before deciding.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: Your story should be about a dragon instead.

This is incorrect because it replaces the writer's purpose with the reader's idea. It is not craft-focused feedback.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: If you want the scene to stay tense, consider adding one detail that shows what the narrator hears after the light clicks.

This is correct because it respects the writer's purpose and gives a specific revision path.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Copy the incorrect and correct feedback examples.

Part B: Explain why the correct feedback is more useful.

Part C: Write one craft-focused suggestion for the draft excerpt.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
