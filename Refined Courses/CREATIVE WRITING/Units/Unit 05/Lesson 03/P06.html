Checkpoint: Workshop Collaboration and Active Listening

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: A Workshop Exchange

Writer: “I want the ending to feel uneasy, but readers say it is confusing.”

Reader 1: “The final image is strong. I lost track of why the character left the key behind.”

Reader 2: “I would make the character go back and explain everything.”

Writer: “So you both want a happier ending?”

Active listening means accurately receiving, clarifying, and responding to another person’s ideas. A useful workshop response separates observation, question, and suggestion.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Where does the writer misinterpret the feedback? Restate what each reader actually communicated.
  2. Write one clarifying question the writer could ask before deciding how to revise.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which response demonstrates productive workshop collaboration?

A. Tell the writer exactly how you would rewrite the ending so the problem disappears.
B. Describe the reading experience, point to a specific place, ask a clarifying question, and offer options that preserve the writer’s ownership.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Write a 350–450 word scene in which a character must make a difficult choice, then conduct a simulated workshop.

  1. Draft: Organize the scene with context, development, turning point, and consequence.
  2. Reader response: Write two specific observations, two questions, and one suggestion tied to the intended effect.
  3. Writer response: Paraphrase the feedback accurately and identify one point that requires clarification.
  4. Revision decision: Adopt, adapt, or decline at least two comments and explain why.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Revise the scene using one workshop decision. Quote the before-and-after passage and explain how the change improves the intended effect.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.

Before You Submit

  • Every numbered item is complete.
  • Analysis includes specific evidence and reasoning.
  • The creative work follows the required organization and length.
  • The A/B response is recorded and explained.
  • The revision is visible and its purpose is clear.
