P04 Worked Example

Study how each example uses active listening and craft-focused feedback.

Example 1: Restating Purpose

Writer says: I want the poem to feel hopeful but not overly cheerful.

Step 1: Listen for purpose.

Step 2: Restate it: You want hope with a quiet tone.

Step 3: Give feedback that protects that goal.

Example 2: Strength and Question

Draft image: One porch light held its breath.

Strength: The personification creates quiet tension.

Question: What does the speaker notice after the porch light appears?

Why it works: The comment names a craft strength and asks a question that can guide revision.

Example 3: Revision Suggestion

Suggestion: Consider adding one sound after the porch light image so the quiet mood becomes more specific.

Why it works: The suggestion is specific, optional, and connected to the writer's mood goal.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: I do not like poems about porch lights.

This is not helpful workshop feedback because it centers the reader's preference instead of the writer's craft goal.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The porch light image supports your quiet hopeful mood; consider adding one sound to deepen the scene.

This is useful because it names a strength and offers a focused revision option.
