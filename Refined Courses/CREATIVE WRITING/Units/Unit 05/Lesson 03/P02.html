Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
workshopA structured conversation for studying and improving drafts.Writers use a workshop to notice patterns before choosing revisions.
active listeningAttending, paraphrasing, questioning, and confirming before responding.A listener restates the writer’s goal before offering feedback.
writer’s purposeThe intended audience, meaning, and effect of a piece.The writer’s purpose is to leave readers uneasy about easy answers.
observationA neutral description of what appears in the draft.“The scene contains three shifts in time” is an observation.
interpretationAn explanation of how a reader understands a choice.A reader interprets the repeated bell as a warning.
open questionA question that invites explanation rather than yes or no.“What should readers understand at this turning point?” is open.
craft feedbackEvidence-based response about technique and effect.The feedback links an abrupt transition to reader confusion.
feedforwardGuidance focused on a useful next action.The reader suggests testing one clearer time marker.
writer agencyThe writer’s authority over final decisions.The writer considers advice but chooses the revision that serves the story.
discussion normAn agreed rule supporting respectful participation.One norm requires listeners to discuss the draft, not the writer.
uptakeHow a listener shows understanding and uses what was heard.The writer’s summary of two repeated concerns demonstrates uptake.
metacognitionAwareness and explanation of one’s own thinking.A reflection explains why the writer accepted one suggestion and rejected another.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which comment is craft feedback rather than a personal preference?

A. “I do not like stories that end quietly.”

This is a preference. It names the reader’s taste but gives no evidence about technique or effect.

B. “The final image echoes the opening, but the abrupt time shift makes the character’s decision hard to follow.”

Correct. It identifies craft choices, describes their effects, and gives the writer evidence to evaluate.
