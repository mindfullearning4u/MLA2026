Notebook Title

Lesson 3: Workshop Collaboration and Active Listening

Vocabulary

  • Workshop: A structured process where writers share work and receive craft-focused feedback.
  • Active listening: Listening to understand the writer's purpose before responding.
  • Craft feedback: Feedback about choices such as image, structure, voice, pacing, clarity, or style.
  • Clarifying question: A question that helps the reader understand the writer's intention.
  • Revision suggestion: A specific idea for improving the draft.

Step 1: Listen for the Writer's Purpose

Writer statement: I want this scene to feel tense but not confusing.

Before giving advice, repeat the purpose in your own words: You want readers to feel tension while still understanding what happens. This proves you heard the goal.

Step 2: Name One Strength

Draft excerpt: The hallway light clicked once, went dark, and clicked again behind me.

Strength: The repeated clicking sound creates tension because the reader hears the setting become unstable.

Step 3: Ask One Useful Question

A useful question helps the writer revise. Weak question: Why is this weird? Useful question: What does the narrator think is behind them when the light clicks? That question points to a place where the writer may clarify tension.
