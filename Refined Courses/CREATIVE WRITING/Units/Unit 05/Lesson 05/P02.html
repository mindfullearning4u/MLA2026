P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

This lesson teaches the first steps of preparing creative writing for online collaborative publication.

Notebook Title

Online Collaborative Publication Checklist

Vocabulary

Publication: Sharing writing with an intended audience in a finished or near-finished form.

Collaborative publication: A publication process where writers, reviewers, editors, and format checkers work together.

Publication-ready: Revised, formatted, checked, and prepared for readers.

Author note: A brief statement that explains the writer's purpose, context, or craft choices.

Step 1: Understand the Difference Between Posting and Publishing

Posting means putting writing somewhere others can see it. Publishing means preparing writing so readers can understand and experience it as intended. In this course, publication requires revision, format control, appropriate content, and a final quality check.

Not ready: A rough draft copied into an online space without revision or formatting.

Publication-ready: A revised piece with a clear title, correct format, clean conventions, and a short author note if needed.

Step 2: Know the Collaborative Roles

Online publication often uses roles. A writer protects the purpose and voice of the piece. A peer reviewer gives feedback about meaning, clarity, and reader effect. A format checker makes sure line breaks, paragraphs, dialogue, titles, and spacing fit the chosen form. A final reviewer checks readiness before submission.

You may complete more than one role, but you should still think through each role separately.

Step 3: Use the First Publication Check

Before a piece moves toward publication, ask four questions in order:

  1. Is the purpose clear?
  2. Has feedback been considered?
  3. Does the format fit the genre?
  4. Is the piece appropriate for the intended audience and online space?

If the answer to one question is no, revise before publishing.

Notebook Task Part 1

Part A: Define publication-ready in your own words.
