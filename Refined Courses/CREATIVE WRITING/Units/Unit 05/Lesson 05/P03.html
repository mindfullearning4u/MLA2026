P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page teaches how to make collaborative publication decisions without losing the writer's voice.

Step 4: Protect the Writer's Purpose

Collaborative publication does not mean every reviewer gets to redesign the piece. The writer's purpose comes first. If a peer suggests a change, ask: Does this change help the piece do what the writer wants it to do?

For example, if a poem is meant to feel quiet, a suggestion to add loud dramatic language may not fit. A better suggestion might be to strengthen one quiet image.

Step 5: Prepare the Online Reader

Online readers need clear entry points. A strong publication entry usually includes a title, the creative work, and sometimes an author note. The title helps the reader enter the piece. The format helps the reader follow it. The author note can explain context or craft choices without replacing the writing itself.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: I accepted every suggested change because collaboration means everyone else decides what my piece should become.

This is incorrect because collaboration supports the writer's purpose. Feedback should be considered carefully, not accepted automatically.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I considered the peer suggestion, kept the part that improved clarity, and rejected the part that changed the quiet tone of the poem.

This is correct because the writer uses collaboration while still protecting the intended effect.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Explain why a writer should not accept every suggestion automatically.

Part B: Write one sentence that explains how a title helps an online reader.

Part C: Write one sentence that explains how an author note can help without replacing the creative work.
