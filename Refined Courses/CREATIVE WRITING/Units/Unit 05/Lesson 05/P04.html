P04 Worked Example

Study how a draft moves toward online collaborative publication.

Example 1: Purpose Check

Draft purpose: The writer wants a short poem to feel calm after a difficult day.

Step 1: Name the purpose: calm after difficulty.

Step 2: Check whether feedback supports that purpose.

Step 3: Keep feedback that strengthens calm images, and question feedback that makes the poem loud or dramatic.

Example 2: Format Check

Publication entry: A poem has a title, stanza breaks, and an author note.

Step 1: Check title: Does it prepare the reader?

Step 2: Check stanza breaks: Do they support pauses and meaning?

Step 3: Check author note: Does it explain context without summarizing every line?

Example 3: Collaboration Decision

Peer suggestion: Add a louder ending so the poem feels exciting.

Writer purpose: The poem should feel calm and reflective.

Decision: Do not use the louder ending. Instead, revise the final image so the calm tone is clearer.

Why it works: The writer considers feedback but chooses the revision that fits the purpose.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: I posted the draft as soon as I finished typing it.

This is incorrect because online publication requires review, revision, formatting, and readiness checks.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I revised from feedback, checked the format, added a clear title, and made sure the piece fits the online publication space.

This is correct because the writer follows a publication workflow instead of treating the rough draft as final.
