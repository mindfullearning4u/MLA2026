Worked Example: Three Publication Decisions

Example 1 — Protect Writer Agency

A copy editor rewrites a dialect passage into Standard English. The writer explains that code-switching is central to voice. The team restores the original pattern, corrects only accidental inconsistencies, and records the decision. Quality improves without erasing identity.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which editorial decision best protects writer agency while improving consistency?

A. Replace all dialect and code-switching with Standard English so every reader encounters the same grammar.
Weaker response. This confuses standardization with quality and erases a purposeful language choice without consulting the writer.
B. Preserve the deliberate code-switching that creates voice, correct only accidental inconsistencies, and record the agreed editorial boundary.
Stronger response. This decision distinguishes intentional voice from error, improves consistency, and documents consent and responsibility.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Rights and Attribution

A poem includes four lines from a song and an uncredited web photograph. The team cannot assume educational use permits public posting. It replaces the lyrics with an original allusion, uses a properly licensed image, records creator/license information, and adds attribution.

Example 3 — Accessibility and Rendered QA

A digital story uses pale text over a moving image and audio without captions. Production adds sufficient contrast, a pause control, transcript/captions, descriptive alt text, and a text-only route. Mobile testing catches a title covering the first paragraph.

Approval Record

The final checklist names the exact version, permissions, unresolved risks, reviewers, writer approval, approver, and release destination. Silence is not consent.
