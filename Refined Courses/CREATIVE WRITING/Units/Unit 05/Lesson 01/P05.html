Independent Work: Revision from Adult, Peer, and Online Feedback

Purpose

Evaluate feedback and make revision decisions that strengthen the writer’s purpose without surrendering ownership.

Complete each numbered part in your notebook. Use precise evidence from your own work and the source provided.

Source for Your Work

Draft line: “The room was tense when I entered.” Feedback A: Add a concrete detail that lets readers feel the tension. Feedback B: Change the narrator to third person. Feedback C: The opening needs more energy.

2. Choose and Explain

Which approach demonstrates stronger decision-making? Record A or B, then explain your choice in 2–3 sentences using a lesson concept.

A. Accept every suggestion because feedback is automatically correct.
B. Compare each suggestion with the purpose, identify the underlying concern, and adopt, adapt, or decline it with a reason.

3. Apply the Lesson

Use a 250–350 word excerpt from your own earlier work or the draft seed above. Create a feedback log for the three comments, label each Adopt, Adapt, or Decline, explain why, and revise the excerpt using the two most useful ideas.

4. Revise with Purpose

Compare one before-and-after passage. Explain exactly how the revision improves clarity, effect, or reader experience.

Before You Submit

  • All four numbered parts are complete.
  • Your work applies the lesson skill to a specific creative product.
  • Your explanation identifies a decision and its effect on readers.
  • Your revision meaningfully improves the work, not only its appearance.
