P04 Worked Example

Study how each example turns feedback into a targeted revision.

Example 1: Adult Feedback

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Draft: The poem is about missing my old neighborhood.

Adult feedback: Add one concrete image from the neighborhood so the feeling is grounded.

Revision: The poem now includes the cracked blue mailbox and the corner store bell.

Why it works: The revision uses specific images to support memory and loss.

Example 2: Peer Feedback

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Draft issue: The ending feels sudden.

Peer feedback: Add one sentence before the final line that shows the character deciding to leave.

Revision: The writer adds, "I folded the note twice before I opened the door."

Why it works: The added action creates a transition into the ending.

Example 3: Online Feedback

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Online tool suggestion: Sentence may be unclear: "It was there and it made me think."

Revision decision: The writer does not accept a random replacement. The writer rewrites for clarity: "The empty chair reminded me of the argument we never finished."

Why it works: The revision clarifies the pronouns and strengthens meaning.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: Always accept online suggestions automatically.

This is incorrect because online tools may miss tone, purpose, voice, or creative effect.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: Consider online suggestions carefully and accept only the changes that improve clarity while preserving the writer's purpose and voice.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
