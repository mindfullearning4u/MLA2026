Checkpoint: Revision From Adult, Peer, and Online Feedback

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: Three Notes on One Draft

Draft line: “Mina entered the empty gym and felt nervous about the audition.”

Adult feedback: Clarify what Mina may lose if the audition goes badly.
Peer feedback: Let us hear or see the gym instead of naming the emotion.
Online comment: “Make it more exciting!!!”

Actionable feedback identifies a specific problem and suggests a useful direction. Revision changes meaning, structure, development, or effect—not merely surface errors.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Which two comments are most actionable? Explain what each helps the writer improve.
  2. How should the writer evaluate the online comment before using it? Name one useful question the writer should ask.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which revision approach protects the writer’s purpose while using feedback responsibly?

A. Accept every suggestion so the revised draft pleases all readers.
B. Compare each suggestion with the purpose, audience, and evidence in the draft; adopt, adapt, or decline it for a stated reason.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Revise the following seed into a 400–500 word scene: “Mina entered the empty gym and felt nervous about the audition.”

  1. Plan: State the scene’s purpose and intended effect.
  2. Opening: Establish setting, stakes, and character goal without naming the emotion directly.
  3. Development: Use sensory detail and action to build tension.
  4. Turning point: Make Mina choose whether and how to begin.
  5. Resolution: Show the immediate consequence.

Then create a feedback log with the three comments above. Label each adopt, adapt, or decline and explain your reasoning. Make at least two substantive revisions.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Highlight one revision that changes meaning or effect and one edit that corrects a surface issue. Explain the difference.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.
