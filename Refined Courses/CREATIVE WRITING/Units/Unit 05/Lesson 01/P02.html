Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
feedbackInformation about how a reader experiences a draft.A reader reports where tension faded and where curiosity increased.
reader responseA description of a reader’s reaction or understanding.“I became confused when the narrator entered the station” is a reader response.
diagnosisAn explanation of the underlying craft problem.The confusion comes from an unclear time shift, not the setting itself.
suggestionA proposed way to revise.Add a transition before the flashback is a specific suggestion.
criterionA standard used to judge effectiveness.Clarity of cause and effect is one criterion for evaluating the scene.
authorial purposeThe writer’s intended meaning or effect.The authorial purpose is to make the reader distrust the narrator.
triageSorting feedback by urgency, value, and scope.A missing climax receives attention before a single weak adjective.
feedback patternA concern repeated by independent readers.Three readers missing the same motivation signals a feedback pattern.
conflicting feedbackRecommendations pointing toward incompatible revisions.One reader asks for more explanation while another prefers greater ambiguity.
revision hypothesisA testable prediction about how a change will improve the draft.If the clue appears earlier, readers should understand the final decision without added exposition.
change logA record of feedback, decisions, revisions, and results.The log connects each major change to its source and outcome.
source credibilityThe trustworthiness and relevant expertise of a feedback source.A genre-aware peer may offer credible advice about mystery pacing.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which response is a diagnosis rather than only a suggestion?

A. “Move the explanation to the opening paragraph.”

Not the best choice. This proposes an action, but it does not identify the craft problem the change is meant to solve.

B. “Readers cannot connect the final decision to the conflict because the character’s motivation appears after the climax.”

Best choice. It identifies the underlying problem and explains how placement affects comprehension, providing a basis for a targeted revision.
