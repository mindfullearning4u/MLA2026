Notebook Title

Lesson 1: Revision from Adult, Peer, and Online Feedback

Vocabulary

  • Revision: Changing a draft to improve meaning, purpose, clarity, structure, or style.
  • Feedback: Information from a reader or tool about how a draft is working.
  • Adult feedback: Guidance from a teacher, TOR, mentor, or other adult reader.
  • Peer feedback: Reader response from a classmate or writing partner.
  • Online feedback: Suggestions from a digital editing or review tool.
  • Revision goal: The specific improvement the writer is trying to make.

Step 1: Read Feedback for the Problem It Names

Draft excerpt: I walked into the room. It was weird. Something felt different. I did not know why.

Peer feedback: I understand the speaker feels uneasy, but I cannot picture the room.

This feedback names a real problem: the mood is stated, but the setting does not show it. The writer should revise by adding sensory details that create unease.

Step 2: Sort Feedback Before Revising

Do not accept every suggestion automatically. Sort feedback into three groups: use now, adapt, and set aside. Use feedback now if it matches your writing purpose and identifies a clear issue. Adapt feedback if the idea is useful but the exact suggestion does not fit. Set aside feedback if it changes the purpose or is too vague to act on.

Step 3: Turn Feedback into a Revision Goal

Feedback: I cannot picture the room.

Revision goal: Add two sensory details that make the room feel uneasy.

Revision: I walked into the room. The curtains hung crooked, and the air smelled like wet cardboard. The lamp clicked twice before it stayed on.
