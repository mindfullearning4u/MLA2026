Lesson: The Integrated Editorial Decision Cycle

1. Establish Purpose and Constraints

Before revising, name the intended audience, desired effect, genre expectations, publication format, and nonnegotiable constraints. A change is “better” only in relation to these goals.

2. Diagnose Before Editing

SymptomPossible diagnosisTest
Reader is boredWeak stakes, repetitive scene, or flat pacingAsk where attention dropped and why
Ending feels suddenMissing setup or compressed consequenceTrace promises planted earlier
Voice is unclearInconsistent diction, perspective, or distanceCompare key passages aloud

3. Revise in the Right Order

  1. Meaning and purpose: central effect, stakes, theme, ethical representation.
  2. Structure: order, pacing, development, transitions.
  3. Scene and sentence craft: imagery, dialogue, syntax, diction, sound.
  4. Correctness and format: grammar, mechanics, consistency, accessibility.

4. Test the Revision

Do not ask only, “Do you like it?” Ask targeted questions: What did you expect? Where did your interpretation change? Which detail shaped that conclusion? Then compare feedback to the intended effect.

5. Publish and Present Responsibly

  • Verify ownership, permissions, and privacy.
  • Match formatting to genre and platform.
  • Provide accessible headings, links, images, audio, and contrast.
  • Prepare a concise author statement grounded in exact craft evidence.

Decision Rule

Preserve what works. Revise what blocks purpose. Reject feedback that conflicts with evidence or goals—but explain why.
