P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

This page reviews common assessment mistakes and how to correct them.

Step 4: Match the Evidence to the Standard

If a question asks for revision evidence, choose the answer that names what changed and why it improved the piece. If a question asks for workshop feedback, choose the answer that is respectful, specific, and connected to the writer's purpose. If a question asks about author presentation, choose the answer that explains purpose and craft rather than retelling the plot.

Step 5: Check Reader Effect

Many Unit 5 questions ask what a choice does for the reader. A strong answer explains the effect: clearer meaning, stronger pacing, better speaker clarity, more useful feedback, stronger publication readiness, or a more focused author presentation.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: The best answer is the one that changes the most words.

This is incorrect because revision quality is not measured by the number of words changed. A small revision can be strongest if it improves purpose, clarity, format, or reader effect.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: The best answer is the one that solves the identified writing problem while protecting purpose, voice, format, and reader understanding.

This is correct because it follows the Unit 5 workflow and evaluates revision by effect.

Notebook Task Part 2

Part A: Explain why changing more words does not always mean stronger revision.

Part B: Write one sentence explaining how reader effect helps you choose the best answer.

Part C: Write one test-taking reminder for Unit 5 assessment questions.
