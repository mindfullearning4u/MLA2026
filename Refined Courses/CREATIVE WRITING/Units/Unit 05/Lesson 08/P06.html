Checkpoint: Putting It All Together: Unit 5 Synthesis

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: The Launch Decision

A student editorial team must publish its annual collection tomorrow. The strongest story has an unresolved permissions question. The most polished poem does not fit the collection’s theme. Two collaborators disagree about the order, and the author scheduled to present the work has practiced an outdated draft.

Publication synthesis coordinates revision, feedback, collaboration, format, digital responsibility, presentation, and quality control into one defensible process.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Rank the team’s four problems from most urgent to least urgent and justify the order using audience, ethics, and publication quality.
  2. Explain how the team can preserve author ownership while still making a unified final product.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which plan demonstrates responsible publication synthesis?

A. Fix whichever problem is quickest, publish on time, and explain any remaining errors afterward.
B. Resolve legal and ethical risks first, confirm the authoritative versions, address audience-facing quality, document decisions, and publish only after a final check.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Create a complete Unit 5 publication dossier for a polished original work or coordinated mini-collection.

  1. Editorial purpose: Define audience, purpose, format, and quality criteria.
  2. Revision record: Document feedback from at least two perspectives and justify adopt/adapt/decline decisions.
  3. Final publication: Present a polished 700–1,000 word work, equivalent multimodal piece, or three-piece mini-collection.
  4. Collaboration and ethics: Include roles, approvals, version record, attribution, permissions, privacy, and accessibility checks.
  5. Author presentation: Provide a 4–6 minute script with context, excerpt, craft reflection, and closing.

Add a 250–300 word editorial rationale explaining how at least five Unit 5 skills work together.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Complete three final revisions: one content or structure change, one audience or format change, and one conventions or accessibility change. Explain the effect of each.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.
