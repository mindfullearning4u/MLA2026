Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
synthesisThe integration of several ideas or skills into a new, coherent understanding or product.The final portfolio combines drafting, feedback, revision, and presentation.
diagnosisA reasoned identification of the underlying cause of a writing problem.The writer traces reader confusion to a missing scene transition.
editorial judgmentA decision about what to revise, preserve, cut, or present based on purpose and evidence.The editor preserves the unusual ending because it serves the theme.
revision priorityA ranked change chosen for its likely effect on meaning and readers.Clarifying the central conflict comes before correcting commas.
feedback loopA cycle of drafting, response, revision, testing, and further response.The writer revises, asks a new reader, and evaluates the result.
transferThe application of a learned skill to a new situation.The writer applies pacing techniques from fiction to a memoir scene.
publication readinessThe condition of being complete, polished, accessible, ethical, and usable by the intended audience.Final checks confirm permissions, formatting, and accessibility.
defensibleSupported by clear reasons and relevant evidence.The selection is defensible because it demonstrates both range and growth.
constraintA limit or requirement that shapes a creative decision.A five-minute presentation limit determines the excerpt length.

Which decision shows strong editorial judgment?

A. Correct every punctuation mark before deciding whether the central conflict is understandable.

B. Diagnose the unclear conflict, revise it first, then proofread after the meaning is stable.
