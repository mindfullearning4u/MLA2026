Independent Work: Unit 5 Editorial Defense

Directions

Analyze the new draft packet below and produce an 800–1,000-word editorial defense plus a visual decision map. Submit it with your Page 2 vocabulary notes as Notebook Evidence.

Draft Packet

A writer’s 900-word speculative story follows Imani, who receives weather reports from one day in the future. Early scenes carefully establish that each prediction carries a personal cost. In the climax, Imani prevents a bridge collapse, but the story introduces a previously unmentioned device that solves the problem instantly. Workshop readers praise the unsettling weather imagery, disagree about whether Imani is selfish, and consistently report that the ending feels unearned. The draft also includes a copyrighted news photograph, three unlabeled links, and pale-yellow text on white.

Speculative fiction imagines realities altered by scientific, social, supernatural, or other “what if” conditions.

Required Tasks

  1. State the intended audience and central effect you infer from the packet.
  2. Distinguish feedback consensus from individual interpretation.
  3. Diagnose the ending problem and identify the highest revision priority.
  4. Propose two structurally different solutions; evaluate their tradeoffs.
  5. Choose one solution and draft a 150–250-word replacement or outline.
  6. Identify two strengths that should be preserved.
  7. Explain how you would test whether the revision works.
  8. Address the photograph, links, and contrast problems.
  9. Create a visual map: Goal → Diagnosis → Priority → Revision → Test → Publish.
  10. Conclude with a defensible editorial judgment connecting at least four Unit 5 standards.

Quality Check

  • Uses exact case evidence
  • Explains cause, not only symptoms
  • Considers alternatives and tradeoffs
  • Connects revision to audience effect
  • Includes ethical and accessible publication decisions
