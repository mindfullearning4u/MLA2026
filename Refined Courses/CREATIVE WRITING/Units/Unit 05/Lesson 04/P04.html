P04 Worked Example

Study how each example makes format choices that improve publication quality.

Example 1: Poem Format

Draft line: I stood outside the door not ready to knock.

Step 1: Identify the format: poem.

Step 2: Decide what the reader should feel: hesitation.

Step 3: Use line breaks to slow the moment:

I stood outside the door
not ready
to knock.

Why it works: The format creates pauses that match the speaker's hesitation.

Example 2: Story Format

Draft: "Come in" Mara said but she did not open the door.

Step 1: Identify the format: prose story.

Step 2: Check sentence-level clarity.

Step 3: Add punctuation and paragraph control: "Come in," Mara said, but she did not open the door.

Why it works: The corrected format helps the reader understand who speaks and what happens next.

Example 3: Script Format

Draft idea: Mara tells Ellis not to enter, but she sounds uncertain.

Step 1: Identify the format: script.

Step 2: Use character labels so the reader can follow speakers.

Step 3: Use a stage direction only when it helps performance:

MARA (keeping one hand on the door): Do not come in yet.

Why it works: The label shows who speaks, and the stage direction reveals tension without adding a long explanation.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: I changed the spacing because it looked more creative.

This is incorrect because publication quality is not random decoration. The format should help the reader understand the genre, meaning, pacing, or speaker movement.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I changed the spacing so the final action appears after a pause, which makes the speaker's hesitation clearer.

This is correct because it names the format choice and explains how the choice improves the reader's experience.
