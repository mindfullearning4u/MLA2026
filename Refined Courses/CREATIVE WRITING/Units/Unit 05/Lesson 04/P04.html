Worked Example: Three Format Transformations

Example 1 — Prose to Poem

Prose sentence: “At dawn, the empty school hallway seemed to wait for the first student.” Poem formatting breaks after “wait” to suspend the action and isolates “the first student” for emphasis. The words remain similar, but lineation changes pacing and attention.

Pause and Choose: A or B

Which explanation best describes how lineation changes the prose sentence?

A. The poem is stronger because poems use shorter lines than prose.
Weaker response. This names a surface difference between formats without explaining the effect of the actual line breaks.
B. Breaking after “wait” suspends the action, and isolating “the first student” directs attention to the anticipated arrival; the words stay similar while pacing and emphasis change.
Stronger response. This response identifies the precise breaks and traces each formatting decision to reader attention and pacing.

Retry: Close your first choice and open the other. Compare both explanations before continuing.

Example 2 — Prose to Script

Narration: “Mina hides the letter when her brother enters.” Script:
INT. KITCHEN—NIGHT
MINA folds the letter beneath a towel.
JON enters.
JON
You’re still awake.
Action becomes visible and playable; no narrator explains motive.

Example 3 — Accessibility Repair

Weak portfolio page uses pale gray text, colored labels with no words, and “click here.” Repair increases contrast, adds written labels, uses descriptive link text (“Open audio performance”), preserves heading order, and supplies a transcript. Access improves without changing the creative work.

Verification Step

Always inspect the exported or uploaded version. A correct editor view can still produce broken page breaks, missing fonts, or inaccessible media.

Your Turn Before Independent Work

Pause and try: Reformat “The last bus crossed the bridge before the lights went out” as a short poem. Explain one line break.

Check your thinking — open after you try
Teacher model: One option is “The last bus crossed / the bridge / before the lights went out.” Isolating “the bridge” holds the reader at the crossing before the final darkness arrives.
