Notebook Title

Lesson 4: Format-Specific Quality Work

Vocabulary

  • Format: The way a piece is arranged for its genre or purpose.
  • Publication-ready: Clear, revised, formatted, and ready for an audience.
  • Line break: Where a poem line ends.
  • Paragraphing: How prose is divided into blocks of related ideas or action.
  • Script format: A format using character names, dialogue, and stage or performance directions.
  • Quality check: A final review for format, clarity, conventions, and reader access.

Step 1: Identify the Format

Before revising formatting, identify the type of piece. A poem uses line breaks and stanza choices. A short story uses paragraphs, dialogue punctuation, and scene breaks. A script uses speaker names and performance directions. A reflection uses organized paragraphs that explain thinking.

Step 2: Match Format to Reader Need

Unformatted poem draft: The porch light waited in rain and I stood across the street not ready to knock.

Poem format:
The porch light waited
in rain,
and I stood across the street,
not ready to knock.

The line breaks slow the moment and help the reader feel hesitation.

Step 3: Check Quality Within the Format

Do not use formatting only for decoration. Ask: Does this format make the work easier to read? Does it match the genre? Does it support meaning? Does it follow enough conventions for readers to understand?
