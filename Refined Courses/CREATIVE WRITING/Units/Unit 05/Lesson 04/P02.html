Vocabulary and Definitions

Notebook Task: Record every core term and its complete meaning in your notebook. Submit these vocabulary notes with your Independent Work as Notebook Evidence. Use the examples to see how each term works in context. Reference glossary terms do not need to be copied.
TermMeaningExample
formatThe structural and visual form used to present a work.A script uses character labels and stage directions as part of its format.
genre conventionA common expectation associated with a type of writing.Line breaks are a familiar poetry convention.
manuscriptA draft prepared for editing, submission, or publication.The author formats the manuscript before sending it to an editor.
visual hierarchyDesign cues showing the order and importance of information.A title, headings, and spacing guide the reader’s attention.
white spaceUnprinted area used to separate and organize content.Space between stanzas lets each image stand apart.
lineationThe intentional shaping of poetic lines.Breaking after “alone” emphasizes the word through lineation.
paragraphingThe division of prose into meaningful units.A new paragraph marks a change in speaker.
dialogue tagWords identifying a speaker or manner of speech.“she whispered” is a dialogue tag.
stage directionA script instruction for action, setting, or delivery.[He turns away.] tells the actor what to do.
consistencyReliable use of the same formatting rules.All scene headings follow the same capitalization.
accessibilityDesign enabling people with varied needs to use the work.Readable contrast and descriptive text improve accessibility.
submission specificationA required technical or formatting condition.The journal requires a PDF under five megabytes.

Make the Vocabulary Work

Which choice best uses visual hierarchy to help a reader?

A. Give every line the same size, weight, and spacing.

This removes signals that help readers recognize order and importance.

B. Use a clear title, consistent headings, and white space between sections.

Correct. These cues organize information and direct attention without changing the writing itself.
