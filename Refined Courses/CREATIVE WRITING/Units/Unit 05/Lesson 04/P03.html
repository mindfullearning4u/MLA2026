Lesson: The Format-Fit Audit

Six-Step Audit

  1. Name destination: identify genre, audience, platform, and submission specifications.
  2. Separate fixed from flexible: mark required conventions and optional design choices.
  3. Build hierarchy: title, headings, speaker labels, stanza/scene divisions, and page flow.
  4. Control genre movement: line breaks for poetry, paragraphing/dialogue for prose, cues/directions for scripts.
  5. Test access: readable type, contrast, descriptive link text, logical order, and no meaning conveyed by color alone.
  6. Proof technically: file type, name, margins, page numbers, embedded media, and final rendered view.

Pause and Choose: Match Format to Genre

A student submits a stage scene in dense prose paragraphs with no speaker labels. Which revision best serves the genre and reader?

A. Add consistent speaker labels, separate dialogue turns, and include only necessary stage directions.
Stronger choice. These conventions reveal who speaks and what must happen onstage. The format supports performance without over-directing every gesture.
B. Keep the prose format but change the title to “Stage Play.”
Weaker choice. A label does not repair navigation. Performers still cannot quickly identify dialogue, cues, or actions.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Learning Routes

Compare rendered examples visually; read layouts aloud as navigation instructions; physically reformat a mixed-up page.

Pause and Choose: Test Accessibility

A portfolio uses pale gray text on white and color alone to distinguish drafts from final pieces. Which change is strongest?

A. Keep the design because it looks elegant on the writer’s screen.
Weaker choice. Personal appearance does not prove readability across readers and devices. Low contrast and color-only meaning create avoidable access barriers.
B. Increase contrast and add text labels such as “Draft” and “Final” alongside any color coding.
Stronger choice. The design remains organized while meaning becomes available without relying on color perception or one display setting.

Retry: If your first choice is weaker, close it and select the other choice before continuing. Compare both explanations so you can name why the stronger answer works.

Quality Principle

Formatting should reveal structure, not decorate randomly. A convention may be broken only when the reader can still navigate and the effect is intentional.
