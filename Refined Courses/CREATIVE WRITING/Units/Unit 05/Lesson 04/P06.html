Checkpoint: Format-Specific Quality Work

Put the lesson together through analysis, decision-making, writing, and purposeful revision.

Purpose

Show that you can understand the lesson concept, apply it in original work, and explain your decisions. Complete every numbered item and submit your responses with your Checkpoint work.

New Source: One Event, Two Formats

Prose: The elevator stopped between floors. Rae pressed the alarm, counted three breaths, and heard someone laugh softly in the dark.

Script:
BLACKOUT.
RAE: Who’s there?
A laugh. Metal CREAKS above.
RAE reaches for the alarm.

A format is the structure and conventions of a type of writing. Format-specific quality means using the strengths and expectations of the chosen form intentionally.

Part 1: Analyze the Source

  1. Compare how the prose and script versions control pacing and reveal sensory information.
  2. Identify one convention each format requires for the event to remain clear to its audience.

Use this response pattern: Make a precise claim, cite the relevant detail, and explain how the detail proves the claim.

Part 2: Choose A or B

3. Which approach creates authentic format-specific quality?

A. Write the same sentences in every format and change only the line breaks or labels.
B. Reimagine how information is delivered so narration, dialogue, stage directions, panels, or lineation serve the selected format.

Record A or B in your submission and explain your choice in 2–3 sentences. The answer is not revealed here because this item is part of your graded written work.

Part 3: Create and Apply

4. Develop one original dramatic event in two different formats.

  1. Version 1: Write 300–400 words of prose with narration, interior perspective, and paragraph control.
  2. Version 2: Recreate the event as a 2–3 page script, comic script, or 24–32 line narrative poem using the conventions of that form.
  3. Comparison: Write 150–200 words explaining three choices you changed because of format and how each affects the audience.

Both versions must include an opening situation, development, turning point, and consequence.

Plan before drafting: Decide what the audience should understand, how each section will build that understanding, and which details will do the most work.

Part 4: Revise With Purpose

5. Revise one moment that still feels transferred rather than redesigned. Use a feature unique to the second format and explain its effect.

Do more than correct spelling. Revise organization, meaning, evidence, or craft so the work becomes clearer and more effective.

Before You Submit

  • Every numbered item is complete.
  • Analysis includes specific evidence and reasoning.
  • The creative work follows the required organization and length.
  • The A/B response is recorded and explained.
  • The revision is visible and its purpose is clear.
