# Phase 3A-A: Speech Communication Mapping Framework

## Executive Summary

This mapping framework defines how Speech Communication standards are distributed across the six-unit, eight-lesson MLA production structure. It preserves the English I-IV architecture format while adapting the progression to oral communication, speech organization, listening, rhetoric, research-supported speaking, digital presentation, and professional communication.

This document is mapping only. It does not create lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, GIFT files, quiz JSON files, lesson JSON files, LMS pages, or student-facing instructional content.

## Mapping Principles

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| Standards-first architecture | All unit and lesson placements are derived from the verified CPALMS Speech 1 standard list. |
| Mastery model | Standards are introduced, practiced, reinforced, and synthesized across units. |
| Lesson 8 control | Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together, contains Unit Assessment only, and introduces no new primary standard. |
| Speech-course identity | Writing and reading standards support speech planning, critique, source use, audience awareness, and delivery. |
| Vertical progression | Speech 1 foundations prepare for Speech 2 rigor without importing Grade 10 benchmark wording. |

## Unit Progression Model

| Unit | Unit Title | Primary Domains | Progression Function |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Communication, Audience, and Listening | COM, LST | Establishes norms, audience awareness, active listening, voice/tone, academic language, and rhetorical awareness. |
| 2 | Speech Structure, Organization, and Informative Speaking | SPK, COM, LST, PRES | Builds logically organized informative communication with coherent focus, paraphrase, format, and expository support. |
| 3 | Rhetoric, Argument, and Persuasive Speaking | COM, LST, RES, SPK | Moves from rhetorical analysis into argument structure, evidence, claims, counterclaims, and persuasive speaking. |
| 4 | Research-Supported Speaking and Source Use | RES, LST, COM | Develops inquiry, reliable sources, complex text comprehension, inference, evidence, and source-supported speech development. |
| 5 | Delivery, Revision, Digital Presentation, and Publication | COM, PRES, DIG, SPK | Builds revision, conventions, vocabulary precision, digital presentation support, and online collaboration. |
| 6 | Professional, Multimedia, and Integrated Communication | COM, SPK, PRES, RES, DIG | Synthesizes the full course into professional, multimedia, collaborative, and audience-aware communication. |

## Workload Controls

| Risk Area | Control |
|---|---|
| Unit 3 persuasive speaking overload | Argument writing, rhetorical evaluation, opposing claims, and oral presentation are sequenced across seven lessons. |
| Unit 4 research overload | Research is staged from question to source evaluation, comprehension, inference, evidence, and speech integration. |
| Unit 5 production overload | Digital presentation, publication, revision, conventions, and vocabulary are connected to previously planned speeches. |
| Unit 6 new-instruction risk | Unit 6 uses synthesis and transfer only; standards have prior instruction in Units 1-5. |
