# Speech Communication Final Architecture Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit reviewed the full Speech Communication curriculum architecture package before lesson production. The audit compared the package against the approved English I, English II, English III, and English IV architecture frameworks; verified the applicable Florida course standards through the CPALMS public course record for `Speech 1 (#1007300)`; validated MLA Speech standard numbering and domain assignment; checked crosswalk, unit-level mapping, and lesson-level mapping coverage; audited all 48 lesson mapping rows; and confirmed that the course preserves the required six-unit, eight-lesson structure.

Initial audit status: `PASS`

Final audit status: `PASS`

The package uses the verified CPALMS Speech 1 standard list as the primary source. CPALMS lists 25 standards for the current Speech 1 course version. The course identity is correctly framed as high-school oral communications, not as an English literature course. Speech 2 was reviewed only as a progression reference and was not used to replace the Speech 1 benchmark wording.

## 3. Strengths

- The architecture preserves the approved MLA mastery model.
- The package uses an official CPALMS course record rather than assuming a generic public speaking standard set.
- All 25 CPALMS-listed standards are included exactly once in the standard inventory and crosswalk.
- Speech-specific domains make the course easier to audit while preserving MLA numbering rules.
- The unit sequence moves from foundations to informative speaking, persuasive speaking, research-supported speaking, digital presentation, and professional/multimedia synthesis.
- Lesson 8 remains Putting It All Together in every unit.
- Lesson 8 contains Unit Assessment only and introduces no new primary standards.
- Reading and writing benchmarks are interpreted appropriately as speech planning, critique, rhetoric, source use, and audience-aware communication supports.

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Speech 1 includes Grade 9 ELA benchmarks and K-12 expectations rather than a separate speech-only standards family. | Medium | Architecture explicitly cites the CPALMS Speech 1 course record and maps each listed standard to speech-course functions. |
| Unit 3 can become overloaded because rhetoric, argument, opposing claims, counterclaims, and evidence converge. | Medium | Seven-lesson sequence separates rhetorical analysis, argument structure, rebuttal, opposing arguments, evidence, and integrated persuasive planning. |
| Unit 4 can become overloaded if research is treated as a full research paper rather than speech support. | Medium | Mapping keeps research tied to source-supported speaking and source evaluation. |
| Unit 5 can become production-heavy if digital work becomes a separate multimedia project. | Medium | Mapping connects digital presentation and online publication to prior speech products. |
| Unit 6 could drift into new instruction. | Low | Unit 6 uses previously taught standards only and functions as synthesis and transfer. |

## 6. Recommended Improvements

These recommendations do not block certification.

- During future lesson production, build a speech-task bank before drafting lessons so informative, persuasive, research-supported, digital, and professional communication products remain distinct.
- Use a source-selection audit before Unit 4 production so sources are appropriate for speech support rather than essay-only analysis.
- Preserve the Speech 1 to Speech 2 progression by keeping Speech Communication foundational while preparing students for the more formal, synthesized, and multimedia expectations in Speech 2.
- Keep delivery and confidence-building activities aligned to the verified standards without creating unsupported performance requirements.
