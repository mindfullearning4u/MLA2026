P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Bringing Communication Choices Together

Vocabulary

  • Integrated communication: using several communication skills together in one situation.
  • Audience-aware response: a response shaped for the listener and situation.
  • Communication choice: a decision about words, tone, evidence, or response.
  • Synthesis: combining several ideas into one clear response.

Step 1: Identify the Whole Situation

Before you respond, identify the audience, purpose, and communication problem. Do not choose tone first. Tone should be based on the audience and purpose.

Ask: Who is listening? What needs to happen? What response would help the situation?

Step 2: Build a Complete Response

  1. Paraphrase or identify the main idea.
  2. Choose a respectful tone.
  3. Use precise academic language.
  4. Connect your response to audience or purpose.
  5. Give a clear next step when needed.

Communication Example

Situation: A group member says, "Our speech should start with a funny story."

Integrated response: "You want the opening to get attention. Since our audience needs to take the safety topic seriously, we could start with a short question instead of a joke."

This response paraphrases, uses respectful tone, considers audience, and suggests a clear communication choice.

Notebook Directions and Model Example

Copy the vocabulary. Then explain why the integrated response is stronger than simply saying, "No, that is wrong."

Model answer: The integrated response explains the reason, respects the group member, and connects the choice to audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Integrated audience-aware communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.09, MLA.SPEECH.COM.08

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
