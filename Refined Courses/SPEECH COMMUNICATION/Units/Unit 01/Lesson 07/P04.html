P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Audience-Aware Tone

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Situation: You are speaking to younger students about hallway rules.

Best choice: Use a friendly but firm tone.

  1. The audience is younger students.
  2. The purpose is safety.
  3. The tone must be clear without sounding harsh.

Example 2: Listening Before Disagreeing

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speaker: "We should start with a joke."

Response: "You want to get attention. Since the topic is serious, a question may fit the audience better."

  1. The response paraphrases first.
  2. It disagrees respectfully.
  3. It gives a reason based on audience and tone.

Example 3: Rhetorical Choice

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech excerpt: "If everyone practices once today, every group will know what to fix tomorrow."

Explanation: This uses logic because it gives a reason for practicing today.

  1. The speaker suggests an action.
  2. The speaker explains the result.
  3. The choice supports the purpose of persuading groups to practice.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "Pick the answer that sounds strongest."

Strong is not enough. A communication choice must fit audience, purpose, and situation.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "Pick the response that fits the audience, supports the purpose, and keeps communication respectful."

This checks the full communication situation.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated audience-aware communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.09, MLA.SPEECH.COM.08

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
