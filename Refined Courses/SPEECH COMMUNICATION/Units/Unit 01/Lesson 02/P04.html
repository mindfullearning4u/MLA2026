P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Choosing Tone for a Teacher

Situation: You need to ask your Teacher of Record for help understanding the checkpoint.

Best message: "Hello, I am having trouble understanding what the checkpoint is asking me to explain. Could you please help me review the directions?"

  1. The audience is a teacher, so the language is respectful.
  2. The purpose is to ask for help, so the message is clear and specific.
  3. The tone is polite and responsible.

Example 2: Adjusting Tone for Classmates

Situation: Your group needs to practice more before presenting.

Best message: "I think one more practice round would help us sound more confident. Could we each rehearse our part once?"

  1. The audience is classmates, so the tone can be friendly but still focused.
  2. The purpose is to suggest practice, not blame anyone.
  3. The speaker gives a reason and a clear next step.

Example 3: Matching Tone to Purpose

Purpose: persuade classmates to support a cleanup project.

Best message: "If each of us helps for twenty minutes, we can make the courtyard cleaner and safer for everyone who uses it."

  1. The tone is encouraging and confident.
  2. The message connects the action to a benefit for the audience.
  3. The voice sounds responsible instead of demanding.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "You all need to help because this place is gross."

This tone is negative and demanding. It may push the audience away instead of encouraging them to help.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "Together, we can make the courtyard cleaner and more welcoming for everyone."

This tone is positive and audience-aware. It explains the benefit and invites participation.
