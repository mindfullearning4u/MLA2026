P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued notebook application: Explaining rhetorical choices.

Deeper Communication Thinking

To explain rhetoric, do not only name the appeal. Explain how the speaker's choice supports the purpose. Use this sentence frame when needed:

The speaker uses ___ to help the audience ___ because ___.

Embedded Example

Speech excerpt: "Imagine walking into a clean courtyard where every student has a place to sit, talk, and feel welcome."

Explanation: The speaker uses an appeal to emotion to help the audience picture a positive result because the speaker wants students to care about improving the courtyard.

This explanation names the appeal, names the audience effect, and connects the choice to purpose.

Notebook Directions

Speech excerpt: "Our survey showed that 72 students want more club options, so adding one new club each semester would respond to student interest."

Identify the appeal and explain how it supports the speaker's purpose.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "The speaker uses rhetoric because it sounds good."

This is too vague. It does not identify the rhetorical choice, audience effect, or speaker's purpose.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "The speaker uses an appeal to logic by including survey results. This supports the purpose because the numbers give the audience a reason to believe students want more clubs."

This explanation is stronger because it names the appeal, uses evidence from the excerpt, and explains the purpose.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Rhetoric as a communication concept

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.LST.06

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Identify the claim first. A persuasive speech needs a clear position before evidence or rhetoric can be evaluated.
  2. Connect reasons and evidence directly to the claim. Evidence should prove, clarify, or justify the position.
  3. Address opposing views fairly. A strong rebuttal explains why the speaker's position remains stronger.
  4. Evaluate audience effect. Rhetorical choices should strengthen credibility, emotion, logic, or urgency without weakening fairness.
