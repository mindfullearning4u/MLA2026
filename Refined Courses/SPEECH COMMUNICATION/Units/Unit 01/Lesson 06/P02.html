Notebook Title: Understanding Rhetoric in Speech Communication

Vocabulary

  • Rhetoric: the choices a speaker or writer makes to affect an audience.
  • Purpose: the reason for communicating, such as to inform, persuade, or motivate.
  • Appeal to trust: showing the audience that the speaker is believable or responsible.
  • Appeal to emotion: using feelings to help the audience care about the message.
  • Appeal to logic: using reasons or evidence to support the message.

Step 1: Start with Purpose and Audience

Rhetoric is not just decoration. It is a set of communication choices. A speaker chooses examples, tone, organization, and appeals based on the audience and purpose.

Thinking rule: To understand rhetoric, ask: Who is the audience? What does the speaker want the audience to think, feel, or do?

Step 2: Identify the Appeal

  1. If the speaker builds credibility, it may be an appeal to trust.
  2. If the speaker creates concern, hope, pride, or urgency, it may be an appeal to emotion.
  3. If the speaker uses reasons, facts, or cause-and-effect thinking, it may be an appeal to logic.

Communication Example

Speech excerpt: "As your class representative, I have listened to your concerns about lunch lines. If we create two lines instead of one, students can get food faster and have more time to eat."

This example uses trust because the speaker identifies a responsible role. It also uses logic because the speaker explains how two lines could solve the problem.

  3. Which appeal appears in the excerpt?
