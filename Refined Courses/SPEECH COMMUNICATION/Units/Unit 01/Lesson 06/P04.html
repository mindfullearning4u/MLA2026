P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Appeal to Trust

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech excerpt: "As the student council treasurer, I have reviewed the club budget carefully."

Explanation: The speaker uses an appeal to trust by naming a responsible role and showing careful review.

  1. The speaker's role builds credibility.
  2. The phrase "reviewed carefully" suggests responsibility.
  3. The audience may be more willing to listen.

Worked Example 2: Appeal to Emotion

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech excerpt: "Every student deserves to feel welcome when they walk into our classroom."

Explanation: The speaker uses an appeal to emotion by focusing on belonging and fairness.

  1. The word "deserves" creates a feeling of fairness.
  2. The phrase "feel welcome" helps the audience care.
  3. The emotional appeal supports a purpose of motivating action.

Worked Example 3: Appeal to Logic

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech excerpt: "If each group practices twice, every speaker will have a chance to fix timing before presenting."

Explanation: The speaker uses an appeal to logic by giving a reason and showing a cause-and-effect relationship.

  1. The speaker explains a practical action.
  2. The speaker connects that action to a clear result.
  3. The audience can follow the reasoning.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "This is logic because the speaker wants people to care."

Wanting people to care usually points toward emotion. Logic depends on reasons, evidence, or cause-and-effect thinking.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "This is an appeal to emotion because the speaker uses language that helps the audience care about the issue."

This is stronger because it names the appeal and explains how the language affects the audience.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Rhetoric as a communication concept

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.LST.06

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Identify the claim first. A persuasive speech needs a clear position before evidence or rhetoric can be evaluated.
  2. Connect reasons and evidence directly to the claim. Evidence should prove, clarify, or justify the position.
  3. Address opposing views fairly. A strong rebuttal explains why the speaker's position remains stronger.
  4. Evaluate audience effect. Rhetorical choices should strengthen credibility, emotion, logic, or urgency without weakening fairness.
