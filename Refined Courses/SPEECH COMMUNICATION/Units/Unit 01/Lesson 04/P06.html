P06 Independent Work

Complete the following work independently in the same notebook used for your lesson notes.

Show your thinking clearly. Answer in complete sentences whenever an explanation is required.

Important Important:

The work completed below should be written in the same notebook as your lesson notes. Both your notes and your Independent Work will be included together when you submit your Notebook Evidence assignment.

Instructions

Complete Parts A, B, and C in your notebook. Your responses should show that you can use academic language clearly in oral communication situations.

Part A

Write three vague words or phrases that speakers should avoid when giving feedback.

Next to each one, write a more precise academic word or phrase that could replace it.

Part B

Revise this feedback so it uses academic language: "Your speech part was good, but the middle was kind of confusing."

Your revision should name the speech section, the communication issue, and one improvement.

Part C

Write two sentences explaining why precise academic vocabulary helps an audience understand a spoken message.

Use at least two lesson vocabulary words in your explanation.

Need help? Contact your Teacher of Record if you need help making your academic feedback more precise and useful.

Independent Work Planning Support

Lesson focus: Academic language in oral communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.10

Use this checklist before completing Parts A, B, and C so your independent work stays aligned to the lesson standard.

  1. Identify the exact communication purpose before beginning the task.
  2. Choose evidence, wording, and organization that match the audience and setting.
  3. Explain your communication choices so the reason behind each choice is clear.
  4. Review the final response for clarity, respect, accuracy, and audience awareness.

P06 independent work quality check: Before you submit, make sure Part A shows the basic skill, Part B applies the skill to a communication situation, and Part C explains your reasoning. A strong response does not only complete the task; it shows why your choices fit the audience, purpose, evidence, and communication goal.

  • Use complete, specific responses instead of one-word answers.
  • Connect each answer to the lesson focus and mapped standard.
  • Reread your work as if an audience, listener, or collaborator will use it.
