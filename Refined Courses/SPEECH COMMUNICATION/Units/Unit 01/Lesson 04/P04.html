P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Replacing a Vague Word

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Vague statement: "Your speech had good stuff."

Academic revision: "Your speech used clear evidence to support the main claim."

  1. The revision replaces "stuff" with "evidence."
  2. The revision names the communication purpose of the evidence.
  3. The listener now knows exactly what worked.

Worked Example 2: Giving Academic Feedback

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Weak feedback: "The middle was weird."

Academic feedback: "The body of the speech needs a clearer transition between the first reason and the second reason."

  1. The feedback names the speech section.
  2. The feedback identifies the communication issue.
  3. The feedback gives the speaker a specific place to revise.

Worked Example 3: Explaining Audience Impact

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Prompt: Explain why a speaker should define a difficult term.

Academic response: "Defining the term helps the audience understand the claim because listeners may not know the vocabulary yet."

  1. The response names the academic action: defining the term.
  2. The response connects the action to the audience.
  3. The response explains why the action supports understanding.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "Academic language means using big words."

This is incorrect because academic language is not about making speech sound difficult. It is about making meaning precise and clear.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "Academic language means using precise words that help the audience understand the idea."

This is correct because the goal is clear communication, not complicated wording.
