Notebook Title: Speaking with Precise Academic Language

Vocabulary

  • Academic language: clear and precise language used for learning, explaining, and discussing ideas.
  • Precise vocabulary: words that name exactly what the speaker means.
  • Claim: a main idea or position a speaker explains or supports.
  • Evidence: information used to support a claim or explanation.
  • Transition: a word or phrase that connects one idea to the next.
  • Feedback: a response that helps someone understand what is working and what can improve.

Step 1: Replace Vague Words with Precise Words

In speech communication, vague words make the audience work too hard. Words like "stuff," "thing," "bad," and "good" do not explain the idea clearly. Academic language names the idea directly.

Vague: "The thing in your speech was good."

Precise: "Your opening question helped the audience focus on the main claim."

Step 2: Use Academic Language to Explain Your Thinking

  1. Name the communication idea: audience, purpose, claim, evidence, tone, transition, or response.
  2. Explain what the speaker did.
  3. Explain why it helped or did not help the audience.
  4. Give a clear next step if improvement is needed.

Communication Example

Situation: A classmate asks for feedback after practicing an introduction.

Weak feedback: "It was nice."

Academic feedback: "Your introduction was effective because the opening question connected to the audience and introduced the main claim clearly."

Notebook Directions and Model Example

In your notebook, copy the vocabulary and answer the prompts.

  1. Why is "It was nice" not strong academic feedback?
  2. Which academic words make the stronger feedback clearer?
  3. Write one sentence of academic feedback for a classmate's conclusion.

Model answer: "It was nice" is too vague. The stronger feedback uses introduction, opening question, audience, and main claim.
