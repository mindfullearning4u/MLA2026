P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued notebook application: Explaining oral communication with academic vocabulary.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Academic language should make speaking clearer, not more confusing. A strong academic response uses precise words and then explains them in a way the audience can understand.

For example, if you say a speaker needs a stronger transition, you should also explain what the transition should connect.

Embedded Example

Situation: A classmate's speech jumps from the introduction to the first reason.

Academic response: "Your main claim is clear. A transition before the first reason would help the audience follow how the evidence connects to your point."

This response names the communication issue, explains the audience benefit, and gives a specific improvement.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, revise this vague feedback into academic feedback: "Your speech was confusing."

  1. Name the communication issue.
  2. Use at least one academic word from the lesson.
  3. Explain how the speaker can improve.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "Use better words so it sounds smarter."

This feedback is vague and focuses on sounding smart instead of helping the audience understand the message.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "Use precise vocabulary to name your claim and explain how your evidence supports it."

This feedback is clear because it names the academic words and explains what the speaker should do with them.
