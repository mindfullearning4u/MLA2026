P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Paraphrasing a Speaker

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speaker: "The audience may get confused if we give all three examples before explaining the claim."

Paraphrase: "You think we should explain the claim before listing all the examples so the audience can follow our point."

  1. The paraphrase keeps the same meaning.
  2. It uses new words.
  3. It shows the listener understood before responding.

Worked Example 2: Asking a Clarifying Question

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speaker: "The tone needs to be different."

Clarifying response: "When you say the tone needs to be different, do you mean it should sound more serious or more encouraging?"

  1. The listener identifies the unclear word: different.
  2. The question gives two possible meanings.
  3. The tone stays respectful and focused.

Worked Example 3: Respectful Disagreement

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speaker: "We should make the opening funny even though the topic is serious."

Response: "You want the audience to pay attention. I think a serious opening may fit the topic better because the issue affects student safety."

  1. The listener recognizes the speaker's goal.
  2. The disagreement focuses on the communication choice.
  3. The response gives a reason based on audience and tone.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "I disagree because I just do."

This response does not show listening and does not give a reason. The discussion cannot improve if the listener gives no explanation.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "I understand your idea, but I see it differently because the audience may need a serious tone for this topic."

This response shows understanding, uses respectful tone, and gives a reason connected to audience awareness.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Active listening and discussion response

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.08

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
