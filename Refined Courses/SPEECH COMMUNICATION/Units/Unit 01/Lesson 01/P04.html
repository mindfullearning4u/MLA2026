P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Identifying Active Listening

Scenario: A speaker says, "I am worried our presentation has too many facts and not enough explanation." A listener replies, "You are saying we need to explain the facts more clearly, not just list them. Which fact should we explain first?"

  1. The listener restates the speaker's concern in new words.
  2. The listener checks meaning instead of assuming.
  3. The listener asks a useful next-step question.
  4. This is active listening because the response proves attention and helps the group continue.

Example 2: Improving a Weak Response

Weak response: "No, that will not work."

Improved response: "I see why that idea could make the opening stronger. I am concerned it may take too much time. Could we use a shorter example instead?"

  1. The improved response begins by acknowledging the idea.
  2. It gives a reason for concern.
  3. It offers a possible adjustment.
  4. This keeps disagreement respectful and useful.

Example 3: Choosing the Best Collaborative Move

Scenario: Three students are planning a speech. One student has not spoken yet.

Best move: "Ari, we have shared a few ideas. What do you think would help our audience understand the topic?"

  1. The speaker notices that one group member has not contributed.
  2. The question invites participation without embarrassing the person.
  3. The question is tied to the shared goal: helping the audience understand.
  4. This is collaborative because it makes room for another voice.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "You are wrong. My idea is better."

This focuses on winning instead of collaborating. It attacks the person and gives no reason, question, or path forward.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "I see your point. I have a different idea because our audience may need more background first. Can we compare both openings?"

This response disagrees respectfully, gives a reason, and invites comparison. It keeps the discussion focused on the speech, not on personal conflict.
