P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now you will go deeper. Collaborative communication is not only being polite. It means your words help the discussion become more useful, accurate, and respectful.

A strong response usually does one of four things: it adds information, asks for clarification, connects ideas, or respectfully challenges an idea with a reason. A weak response often changes the subject, attacks the person, repeats without adding anything, or shuts down the conversation.

When you read or hear a communication scenario, ask: "What is the speaker trying to do, what does the listener need to understand, and what response would help the group make progress?"

Step-by-Step Thinking

  1. Name the speaker's idea.
  2. Decide whether the listener understands the idea.
  3. Choose the communication purpose: clarify, add, connect, or disagree respectfully.
  4. Write a response that begins with the idea, not the person.
  5. Check whether the response would help the discussion continue.

Embedded Example

Scenario: Priya says, "Our group should divide the presentation into three parts so each person has a clear role." Eli says, "So you mean each person would be responsible for one section and we would still practice together?"

Modeled thinking: Eli paraphrases the idea to check understanding. He does not take over, ignore Priya, or criticize her. His response helps the group confirm the plan.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect response: "That is obvious. Everyone already knows we need parts."

This response attacks the usefulness of the speaker's contribution and does not move the discussion forward. Even if the listener thinks the idea is simple, the response should still address the idea respectfully.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct response: "Dividing the speech into three parts could help us stay organized. Can we decide which part each person will explain?"

This response acknowledges the idea, explains why it could help, and asks a next-step question. It is respectful and collaborative.

Notebook Task

Write one original group discussion scenario. Then write one weak response and one improved collaborative response. Explain why the improved response helps the group.
