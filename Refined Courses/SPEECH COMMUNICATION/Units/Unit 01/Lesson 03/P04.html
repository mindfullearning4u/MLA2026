P04 Worked Example

Example 1: Asking for Clarification

Situation: You understand that a checkpoint is due, but you do not understand what evidence to include.

Best message: "I understand the checkpoint is due today, but I am not sure what evidence I should include. Could you please clarify that part?"

  1. The message names what is understood.
  2. The message names what is unclear.
  3. The question is respectful and specific.

Example 2: Helping a Classmate Understand Directions

Situation: A classmate asks what Part B requires.

Best response: "Part B asks you to write two sentences explaining how the audience affects your word choice."

  1. The response names the exact part of the task.
  2. The response explains what the student must do.
  3. The response keeps the focus on the assignment instead of judging the classmate.

Example 3: Joining a Group Discussion

Situation: You want to add an idea after a classmate finishes speaking.

Best response: "I want to build on that idea. If our audience is new to the topic, we may need one simple example before the main point."

  1. The speaker waits for the classmate to finish.
  2. The speaker connects to the previous idea.
  3. The speaker adds a useful reason related to audience understanding.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "I do not get it. This makes no sense."

This tells someone there is confusion, but it does not say what part is confusing. The listener cannot easily help because the question is too general.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "I understand that I need to respond, but I do not understand whether my answer should be spoken or written. Could you clarify that?"

This version gives the listener enough information to help because it names both the clear part and the unclear part.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Social and instructional communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.11

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.
