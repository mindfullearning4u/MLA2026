P01 Lesson Overview

Lesson Title

Lesson 3: Social and Instructional Communication

In this lesson, you will learn how to communicate clearly and respectfully in school situations, group tasks, class discussions, and teacher directions.

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.SPEECH.COM.11 - Social and Instructional Communication for ELLs.

English language learners communicate for social and instructional purposes within the school setting.

Support Standards: MLA.SPEECH.COM.08 supports collaborative communication, and MLA.SPEECH.COM.10 supports academic language for school communication.

What You Will Learn

  • How social communication helps people interact respectfully.
  • How instructional communication helps students understand directions and expectations.
  • How to ask for clarification when directions are unclear.
  • How to respond in a way that supports collaboration and school success.

What You Will Do

  • Study examples of school communication situations.
  • Practice turning unclear or unhelpful responses into clear responses.
  • Identify when to ask a question, restate a direction, or offer respectful help.
  • Complete notebook tasks, guided practice, independent work, checkpoint, and quiz.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • Explain the difference between social and instructional communication.
  • Choose clear school-appropriate responses for different situations.
  • Use respectful language when asking for help or clarifying expectations.
  • Show mastery in your Guided Practice, Independent Work, Checkpoint, and Quiz.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

In student language: I can communicate clearly and respectfully in school situations, especially when I need to understand directions, work with classmates, or ask for help.

This matters because school communication is not only about what you say. It is also about helping others understand you and helping yourself understand what is expected.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help
