P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued notebook application: Choosing clear responses for school communication.

Deeper Communication Thinking

A strong school response usually does three things: it is respectful, it is specific, and it helps the next step happen. If one of those pieces is missing, the response may not support learning.

  1. Respectful: It does not insult or blame.
  2. Specific: It names the question, direction, or task.
  3. Helpful: It gives a next step or asks for one.

Embedded Example

Situation: Your group is unsure who should speak first in a presentation practice.

Strong response: "The directions say each person should practice one section. Could we start with the introduction and then move in order?"

This response is strong because it refers to the directions, asks a clear question, and gives the group an organized next step.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, write a response for this situation: A classmate says, "I do not know what part I am supposed to practice."

  1. Start with a respectful phrase.
  2. Name the task or direction.
  3. Give or ask for a clear next step.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: "You should already know what to do."

This response is not helpful because it blames the classmate and does not explain the direction or next step.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: "The directions say each person practices one section. Which section are you assigned, or should we check the list together?"

This response is respectful, specific, and helpful. It points back to the directions and offers a clear way to solve the problem.
