P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Communicating Clearly in School Situations

Vocabulary

  • Social communication: communication used to interact respectfully with others.
  • Instructional communication: communication used to understand or explain directions, tasks, and expectations.
  • Clarify: to make something easier to understand.
  • Expectation: what someone is supposed to do or show.
  • Respectful response: a response that addresses the situation without insulting or dismissing another person.

Step 1: Understand the Situation Before Responding

In school, communication often has a purpose. Sometimes the purpose is social, such as greeting a group member or inviting someone to share. Sometimes the purpose is instructional, such as asking what to submit, checking the order of steps, or explaining a direction to a classmate.

Before responding, ask yourself: What is happening? What does the other person need? What do I need to understand or explain?

This step matters because a quick response may sound rude or unclear if you do not first understand the situation.

Step 2: Use a Clear School Communication Process

  1. Name the task or situation.
  2. State what is clear and what is unclear.
  3. Ask a specific question or give a specific explanation.
  4. Use respectful language so the conversation can continue.
  5. Check whether the next step is understood.

Communication Example

Situation: A classmate asks, "What are we supposed to submit for the checkpoint?"

Unclear response: "Just do the thing on the page."

Clear response: "The checkpoint asks us to submit a short response that explains the situation, the audience, and the respectful wording we would use."

Notebook Directions and Model Example

In your notebook, copy the vocabulary and answer the prompts below.

  1. What is the difference between social communication and instructional communication?
  2. Why is "Just do the thing" unclear?
  3. What makes the clear response better?

Model answer: The clear response is better because it names the assignment and explains what information must be included.

Need help? Contact your Teacher of Record if you need help turning an unclear response into a clear instructional response.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Social and instructional communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.11

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.
