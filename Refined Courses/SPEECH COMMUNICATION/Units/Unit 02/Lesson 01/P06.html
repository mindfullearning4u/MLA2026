Show your thinking clearly. Answer in complete sentences whenever an explanation is required.

Important Important:

The work completed below should be written in the same notebook as your lesson notes. Both your notes and your Independent Work will be included together when you submit your Notebook Evidence assignment.

Instructions

Use complete sentences. Keep your work focused on speech organization, not writing a full essay. Your goal is to show that you can plan a short speech with one clear focus and logical order.

Part A: Identify the Focus

Choose one topic: school clubs, phone use in class, preparing for a test, or helping a new student. Write one focused sentence that tells what your speech will explain or prove.

Check yourself: A focused sentence should be specific enough that every main point can connect back to it.

Part B: Organize Main Points

Write three main points for your speech. Place them in the order that would be easiest for an audience to follow. After each point, write one sentence explaining why that point belongs in that order.

Part C: Add Transitions and Closing

Write one transition between Point 1 and Point 2. Write one transition between Point 2 and Point 3. Then write a one-sentence conclusion that reminds the audience of the speech focus.

Independent Work Planning Support

Lesson focus: Logical speech organization and coherent focus

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.SPK.01

Use this checklist before completing Parts A, B, and C so your independent work stays aligned to the lesson standard.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.

P06 independent work quality check: Before you submit, make sure Part A shows the basic skill, Part B applies the skill to a communication situation, and Part C explains your reasoning. A strong response does not only complete the task; it shows why your choices fit the audience, purpose, evidence, and communication goal.

  • Use complete, specific responses instead of one-word answers.
  • Connect each answer to the lesson focus and mapped standard.
  • Reread your work as if an audience, listener, or collaborator will use it.
