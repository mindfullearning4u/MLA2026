P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how format expectations create a clear speech product.

Example 1: Check Required Parts

Speech product: Introduction, focus, Point 1, Point 2, Point 3, conclusion.

Step 1: Look for the required speech sections.

Step 2: Notice what is missing: transitions and support.

Revision: Add support under each point and transitions between points.

Example 2: Check Note Card Quality

Weak note: "Say everything about study skills."

Step 1: Ask whether the note helps the speaker know what to say.

Step 2: Make the note specific.

Improved note: "Point 1: planner shows deadlines. Evidence: assignments listed by date. Reasoning: student can plan work earlier."

Example 3: Check Standard English

Draft: "my speech explain three ways planning help students"

Step 1: Check capitalization: My.

Step 2: Check subject-verb agreement: speech explains, planning helps.

Revised: "My speech explains three ways planning helps students."

Common Mistake

Incorrect: If the speech idea is good, format and conventions do not matter.

This is incorrect because format and conventions help the audience and evaluator understand the speaker's work clearly.

Correct Example

Correct: A strong speech idea should be placed in the required format and checked for clear conventions before submission.

This is correct because quality speech work includes both content and presentation readiness.
