P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Integrated Informative Speech Planning

Vocabulary

Integrated plan: A speech plan that combines focus, organization, support, reasoning, transitions, and format.

Informative focus: The central idea the speech explains.

Paraphrased support: Source information restated accurately in the speaker's own words.

Purposeful transition: A phrase that shows how one idea connects to the next.

Speech-ready format: A format that helps the speaker present clearly.

Step 1: Start with a Clear Focus

Every integrated speech plan begins with one informative focus. The focus tells the audience what they will understand by the end of the speech.

Example focus: Organized preparation helps students deliver clearer presentations.

This focus is strong because each main point can explain a part of organized preparation.

Step 2: Build Main Points

A strong informative speech plan uses main points that support the same focus.

Point 1: Planning the order of ideas helps the audience follow.

Point 2: Paraphrased support helps the speaker explain borrowed information clearly.

Point 3: Rehearsal and note cards help the speaker deliver the plan smoothly.

Notebook Directions

Write one informative focus about speech preparation. Then write three main points that all support that focus.

Model Example: Focus: Preparation improves presentation clarity. Points: organize ideas, paraphrase support, rehearse delivery.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Integrated informative speech planning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.SPK.01, MLA.SPEECH.COM.02

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.
