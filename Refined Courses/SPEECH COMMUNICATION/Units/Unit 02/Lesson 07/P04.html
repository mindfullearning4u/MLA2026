P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how a speech plan becomes complete and presentation-ready.

Example 1: Build the Focus and Points

Focus: Organized preparation improves presentation clarity.

Point 1: Planning the order helps the audience follow.

Point 2: Paraphrased support helps explain sources.

Point 3: Rehearsal helps the speaker deliver ideas smoothly.

Example 2: Add Support and Reasoning

Point: Planning the order helps the audience follow.

Support: A chronological outline shows what the speaker will explain first, next, and last.

Reasoning: This helps the audience understand the process without getting lost.

Example 3: Add Format and Transition

Speech note: Point 1: order of ideas; support: chronological outline; reasoning: audience follows process.

Transition: After the ideas are in order, the speaker needs support that explains each point clearly.

Reasoning: The note is speech-ready because it cues the speaker without requiring a full script.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "I can use a full paragraph as a note card because it has all the words."

A full paragraph may make the speaker read instead of present. Speech notes should guide delivery.

Correct Example

Correct: "I should use short speech notes that show my point, support, and reasoning without forcing me to read every word."

This is correct because it follows speech format and supports natural delivery.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated informative speech planning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.SPK.01, MLA.SPEECH.COM.02

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.
