P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

After you choose main points, each point needs support and reasoning. Support gives the audience something specific to understand. Reasoning explains why the support matters.

Example: Point: Paraphrased support helps explain sources. Support: A speaker can restate a source sentence in simpler speech notes. Reasoning: This helps the audience understand the idea without hearing copied source wording.

Add Transitions

Transitions should connect the parts of your plan. A transition such as "After the ideas are organized, the speaker needs clear support" shows a relationship. A transition such as "Next thing" does not explain the relationship.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "My speech plan is complete because it has three topics."

This is incorrect because topics alone do not create an integrated speech plan. The plan also needs focus, support, reasoning, transitions, and format.

Correct Example

Correct: "My speech plan is complete when each main point supports the focus, includes support and reasoning, and connects to the next idea with a transition."

This is correct because it checks all major Unit 2 planning skills.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated informative speech planning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.SPK.01, MLA.SPEECH.COM.02

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.
