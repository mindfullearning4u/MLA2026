P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how support and transitions strengthen informative speaking.

Worked Example 1: Add Support

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Point: Note cards help speakers stay organized.

Support: A note card can list each main point in order.

Reasoning: This helps the speaker remember what comes next without reading a full script.

Worked Example 2: Choose a Transition

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Point 1: Rehearsal improves timing.

Point 2: Organized notes improve delivery.

Transition: After rehearsal helps control timing, organized notes help the speaker deliver each section clearly.

Reasoning: The transition connects preparation skills instead of simply saying "next."

Worked Example 3: Revise Weak Support

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Weak support: Visual aids are good.

Revised support: A simple chart can show changes over time, which helps the audience see a pattern quickly.

Reasoning: The revised support gives a specific example and explains why it helps.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A transition only needs to say "next."

This is weak because it does not show how the ideas connect.

Correct Example

Correct: A purposeful transition shows the relationship between ideas, such as cause, contrast, or sequence.

This helps the audience follow the speech without guessing the connection.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Informative speech support and transitions

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.02

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.
