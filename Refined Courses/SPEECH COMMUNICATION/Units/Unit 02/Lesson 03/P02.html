P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Paraphrasing Source Information for Speech Notes

Vocabulary

Paraphrase: A restatement of source information in your own words while keeping the same meaning.

Summary: A shorter version that includes only the main idea and most important details.

Source meaning: The idea the original text communicates.

Academic vocabulary: Precise school or subject-area words that may need to stay in a paraphrase.

Speech notes: Short notes a speaker uses to remember and explain important ideas during a presentation.

Step 1: Read for Meaning First

Before you paraphrase, do not start by changing one word at a time. First ask, "What is this sentence really saying?" A paraphrase begins with understanding.

Source sentence: Students who rehearse a presentation several times are more likely to notice unclear sections before speaking to an audience.

Meaning: Practice helps speakers find confusing parts before the real presentation.

Step 2: Restate the Idea for Speech Notes

Once you understand the meaning, write the idea in a new sentence. You may keep necessary academic words like presentation, audience, or rehearse because those words are part of the communication topic.

Paraphrase: Practicing a speech more than once can help a speaker find parts that need to be clearer before presenting.

This paraphrase works because it keeps the meaning but changes the wording and sentence pattern.

Notebook Directions

Copy the source sentence above. Under it, write the meaning in your own words. Then write a paraphrase that could be used as a speech note.

Model Example: Source: A speaker's eye contact helps listeners feel included. Meaning: Looking at the audience helps them feel involved. Paraphrase: Eye contact can make listeners feel like the speaker is communicating with them directly.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help

Need help? Contact your Teacher of Record if your paraphrase looks too close to the source sentence.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: If this skill feels new, begin by defining the key term, naming the speaker or audience, and finding one clear detail that connects to the task.

Standard path: Complete the task in order: identify the communication choice, connect it to the audience or purpose, and explain why it strengthens or weakens the message.

Accelerated extension: After your answer is complete, strengthen it by using a more precise communication term, comparing two choices, or explaining how the choice affects credibility, clarity, or audience response.
