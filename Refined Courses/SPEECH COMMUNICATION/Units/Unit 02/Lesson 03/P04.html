🗣 SPEECH | Unit 02 | Lesson 03

P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how a speaker turns source information into accurate speech notes.

Example 1: Identify the Meaning

Source: Speakers who organize notes by main point can locate information more quickly during a presentation.

Step 1: Find the key idea: organized notes help speakers find information.

Step 2: Keep needed academic words: speakers, notes, presentation.

Paraphrase: When presentation notes are grouped by main point, a speaker can find needed details faster.

Example 2: Avoid Copying Sentence Structure

Source: An audience may lose track of a speech when the speaker jumps from one idea to another without a transition.

Step 1: Meaning: missing transitions can confuse the audience.

Step 2: Change the structure.

Paraphrase: Without transitions, listeners can become confused because the speech moves between ideas too suddenly.

Example 3: Make a Speech Note

Source: A concise note can remind the speaker of the idea without forcing the speaker to read a full sentence aloud.

Step 1: Paraphrase the idea: Short notes can help a speaker remember ideas without reading word for word.

Step 2: Turn it into a speech note.

Speech note: Use short notes to remember ideas; avoid reading full sentences.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Copying the source and replacing one word with a similar word is always a paraphrase.

This is incorrect because paraphrasing requires restating the idea in your own wording and structure, not swapping a few words.

Correct Example

Correct: A paraphrase keeps the source meaning but uses a new sentence pattern and wording that the speaker understands.

This is correct because the speaker must understand and accurately restate the information before using it in speech notes.
