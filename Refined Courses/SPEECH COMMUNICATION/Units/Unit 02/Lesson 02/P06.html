🗣 SPEECH | Unit 02 | Lesson 02

P06 Independent Work

Complete each part in your notebook before moving to the checkpoint.

Instructions

Write clearly and label each part. You are developing a section of an informative speech, so every answer should help an audience understand a topic.

Part A: Write an Informative Focus

Choose one school skill: taking notes, studying for a quiz, managing time, or preparing for a presentation. Write one sentence that explains what your audience will learn about that skill.

Part B: Add Evidence and Reasoning

Write one speech point that supports your focus. Then write one piece of evidence and one reasoning sentence that explains how the evidence supports the point.

Part C: Add a Transition

Write one transition that would move from your first point to a second point. The transition should show the connection between the ideas, not just announce that another point is coming.

Ask Your Teacher of Record for Help

Need help? Contact your Teacher of Record if your evidence does not clearly connect to your point.

Independent Work Planning Support

Lesson focus: Informative and expository speech development

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.02

Use this checklist before completing Parts A, B, and C so your independent work stays aligned to the lesson standard.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.

P06 independent work quality check: Before you submit, make sure Part A shows the basic skill, Part B applies the skill to a communication situation, and Part C explains your reasoning. A strong response does not only complete the task; it shows why your choices fit the audience, purpose, evidence, and communication goal.

  • Use complete, specific responses instead of one-word answers.
  • Connect each answer to the lesson focus and mapped standard.
  • Reread your work as if an audience, listener, or collaborator will use it.
