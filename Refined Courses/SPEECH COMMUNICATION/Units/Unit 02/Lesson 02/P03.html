P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now connect your evidence to your explanation. Many speakers include evidence but forget to tell the audience why the evidence matters. In an informative speech, the audience should not have to guess the connection.

Step 1: State the point clearly.

Step 2: Give evidence that supports the point.

Step 3: Explain the evidence in your own words.

Step 4: Use a transition to move to the next idea.

Deeper Communication Thinking

A good informative speaker thinks like a guide. The speaker does not just place facts in front of the audience. The speaker walks the audience through the facts so the information becomes understandable.

Embedded Example: If the point is "Practice improves delivery," evidence might be "A speaker who rehearses can notice where to pause." The reasoning is "This matters because pauses make the speech easier for the audience to follow."

Common Mistake

Incorrect: My point is that planning helps. Evidence: Planning is good.

This is weak because the evidence simply repeats the point. It does not give the audience a concrete detail to understand.

Correct Example

Correct: My point is that planning helps students remember assignments. Evidence: A student who writes due dates in one planner can check the planner before leaving school. Reasoning: This helps because the student can see what materials to bring home and which assignments need attention first.

This is stronger because the evidence is specific and the reasoning explains why it matters.
