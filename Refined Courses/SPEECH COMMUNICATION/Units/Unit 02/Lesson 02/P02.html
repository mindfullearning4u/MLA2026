P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Developing an Informative Speech with Evidence

Vocabulary

Informative speech: A speech that teaches, explains, or clarifies information for an audience.

Expository communication: Communication that explains and analyzes information in an organized way.

Evidence: Information, facts, examples, or details used to support a point.

Reasoning: The explanation of how evidence proves or supports a point.

Purposeful transition: A word or phrase that shows how one idea connects to the next.

Step 1: Understand the Purpose

An informative speech is not mainly about persuading the audience to agree with your opinion. Its purpose is to help the audience understand something more clearly.

Example focus: Daily planning helps students manage homework more effectively.

This focus tells the audience what they will learn. The speaker can explain what daily planning means, why it helps, and how students can use it.

Step 2: Develop a Point with Evidence

A strong informative point needs more than a claim. It needs support. First state the point. Then give evidence. Then explain how the evidence helps the audience understand the point.

Point: Writing assignments in a planner can reduce missed deadlines.

Evidence: A student who checks a planner each afternoon can see which assignments are due the next day.

Reasoning: This evidence shows that planning makes deadlines visible, so the student has a better chance to complete work on time.

Notebook Directions

Copy the vocabulary. Then write one informative speech focus about a skill students can use in school. Under it, write one point, one piece of evidence, and one reasoning sentence.

Model Example: Focus: Organized notes help students review information. Point: Headings make notes easier to study. Evidence: A heading tells the reader what each section is about. Reasoning: This helps students find the information they need when preparing for a quiz.
