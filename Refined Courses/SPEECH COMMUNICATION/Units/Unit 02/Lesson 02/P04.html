P04 Worked Example

Study each example to see how an informative speaker develops information for an audience.

Example 1: Turn a Topic into an Informative Focus

Topic: note-taking

Step 1: Ask what the audience should learn about the topic.

Step 2: Make the focus specific.

Result: Organized notes help students review important information more efficiently.

Modeled thinking: This focus is informative because it explains a learning strategy instead of arguing about whether students must take notes.

Example 2: Match Evidence to a Point

Point: Headings make notes easier to review.

Step 1: Choose evidence that shows how headings work.

Evidence: A heading such as "Causes" or "Examples" tells the student what kind of information is in that section.

Step 2: Explain the evidence.

Reasoning: This helps because the student can find specific information without rereading every line.

Example 3: Add a Purposeful Transition

Point 1: Headings organize notes.

Point 2: Bullet points make details easier to scan.

Step 1: Notice how the ideas connect. Both are note-taking strategies.

Step 2: Write a transition that names the connection.

Transition: After using headings to separate major ideas, bullet points can help organize the details under each heading.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Notes are useful because they are useful, and students should use them.

This does not teach the audience anything specific. It repeats the idea without evidence or reasoning.

Correct Example

Correct: Notes are useful because headings separate major ideas, and this helps students find the exact section they need when studying.

This is stronger because it gives a specific feature, explains the effect, and helps the audience understand why the feature matters.
