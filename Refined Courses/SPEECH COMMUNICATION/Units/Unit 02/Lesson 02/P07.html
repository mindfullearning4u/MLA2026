Checkpoint Task

Submit one developed informative speech section. Include a focus sentence, one speech point, one specific piece of evidence, one reasoning sentence, and one transition to the next idea.

Do not submit a full speech. This checkpoint measures whether you can develop information clearly and support it with evidence.

Mastery Criteria

  • The focus is informative and clear.
  • The point supports the focus.
  • The evidence is specific and relevant.
  • The reasoning explains why the evidence matters.
  • The transition logically connects to a next idea.

Checkpoint Mastery Support

Lesson focus: Informative and expository speech development

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.02

Use this checklist before submitting your checkpoint. Your work should show both the communication choice and the reason it meets the lesson goal.

  1. Begin with the central focus of the speech. The audience should know what the speech explains and why it matters.
  2. Organize information in a logical order such as problem-solution, cause-effect, chronological order, or main-point order.
  3. Use transitions to show how ideas connect. Listeners need verbal signposts because they cannot reread a speech.
  4. Check whether each section supports the speech purpose instead of adding unrelated details.

P07 checkpoint quality check: Your checkpoint should be clear enough for your Teacher of Record to see what communication skill you practiced, what choice you made, and why that choice meets the lesson goal. Before submitting, confirm that your response is complete, specific, audience-aware, and connected to the mapped standard.

  • Name the communication decision or skill you used.
  • Explain why your work fits the purpose and audience.
  • Revise any vague wording before checkpoint submission.
