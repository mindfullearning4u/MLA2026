P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Using Feedback to Revise Speech Drafts

Vocabulary

  • Revision: improving content, organization, clarity, or delivery choices.
  • Feedback: information someone gives to help improve the work.
  • Peer feedback: suggestions from a classmate or collaborator.
  • Actionable feedback: feedback that tells the speaker what can be improved.

Feedback Scenario

Speech draft line: "Recycling is important and everyone should do it."

Teacher feedback: "Explain why recycling matters for this audience and add one specific example."

Step-by-Step Teaching

Start by reading the feedback carefully. Do not revise randomly. First identify what the feedback asks for. Then decide what part of the speech needs work. Finally, revise in a way that keeps the purpose but makes the idea clearer.

  1. Name the feedback focus.
  2. Find the sentence or section that needs revision.
  3. Add, remove, reorder, or clarify information.
  4. Check that the revision helps the audience understand.
  5. Keep the speaker purpose intact.

Model Example

Revised line: Recycling matters at school because it reduces waste from lunch containers and gives students a simple way to protect shared spaces.

This revision responds to feedback by adding audience connection and a specific example.
