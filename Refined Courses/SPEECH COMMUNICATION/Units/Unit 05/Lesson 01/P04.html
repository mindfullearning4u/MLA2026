Task: Revise from online feedback.

Draft: "This topic is very very useful."

  3. Reasoning: the revision removes repetition and adds meaning.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Feedback says add detail, so I will add a random fact.

Correct: Feedback says add detail, so I will add a detail that explains my speech point for the audience.

Revision should strengthen the speech purpose, not simply make the draft longer.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Revision from adult, peer, and online feedback

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.03

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Read the speech or notes as a listener would hear them. Mark any wording that may confuse the audience.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: clarity, organization, vocabulary, transitions, tone, and conventions.
  3. Choose words for audience impact. Denotation gives meaning; connotation shapes how the audience feels or responds.
  4. Check that revised wording is precise, natural to say aloud, and appropriate for the speaking situation.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
