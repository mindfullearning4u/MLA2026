Worked Example 1

Task: Replace vague wording.

Draft: "The speech needs better parts."

  2. Precise revision: The speech needs clearer transitions between main points.
  3. Reasoning: transitions names the exact speech feature.

Term: cohesion.

  1. Definition: Cohesion means ideas connect smoothly.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Use academic vocabulary in context.

Draft: "The audience gets it."

  1. Academic revision: The audience comprehends the claim because the speaker explains the evidence clearly.
  2. Term: comprehends.
  3. Reasoning: the sentence shows what the audience understands and why.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The speech is enhanced by multisyllabic terminology.

Correct: The speech is clearer when the speaker uses precise vocabulary and explains important terms.

The incorrect example is unnecessarily complicated. The correct example is precise and understandable.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Academic vocabulary in speeches

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.05

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Read the speech or notes as a listener would hear them. Mark any wording that may confuse the audience.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: clarity, organization, vocabulary, transitions, tone, and conventions.
  3. Choose words for audience impact. Denotation gives meaning; connotation shapes how the audience feels or responds.
  4. Check that revised wording is precise, natural to say aloud, and appropriate for the speaking situation.
