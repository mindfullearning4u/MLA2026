P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

A speaker should not use academic words just to sound impressive. The word must fit the idea and help the audience understand. If a term is important but unfamiliar, define it briefly before using it in a larger point.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Precise vocabulary can also change tone. Saying "revise" is more specific than saying "fix." Saying "evidence" is more specific than saying "stuff." The more exact word helps the audience know what the speaker means.

Embedded Example

Vague: The speaker should fix the speech.

Precise: The speaker should revise the introduction so the claim and purpose are clear.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will use hard words so the speech sounds advanced.

Correct: I will use precise words and explain them when they help the audience understand my message.

The incorrect approach values difficulty over clarity. The correct approach values precision and audience understanding.

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Read the speech or notes as a listener would hear them. Mark any wording that may confuse the audience.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: clarity, organization, vocabulary, transitions, tone, and conventions.
  3. Choose words for audience impact. Denotation gives meaning; connotation shapes how the audience feels or responds.
  4. Check that revised wording is precise, natural to say aloud, and appropriate for the speaking situation.
