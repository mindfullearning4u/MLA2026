P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Publishing a Collaborative Speech Product Online

Vocabulary

  • Online publication: sharing a communication product digitally for an audience.
  • Collaborative role: a responsibility assigned to a group member.
  • Publication-ready: clear, revised, organized, and appropriate for the audience.
  • Version control: tracking changes so the group knows which draft is current.

Collaboration Scenario

Project: A group is creating a digital slide presentation and speaker notes about presentation preparation.

Roles: one student checks slide order, one reviews speaker notes, one checks visual clarity, and one confirms final formatting.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Online collaborative publication requires planning. A group should not simply post the first draft. First, clarify the audience and purpose. Next, divide revision roles. Then review the digital product for clarity, organization, tone, accessibility, and consistency. Finally, confirm which version is final before submitting or publishing.

  1. Clarify audience and purpose.
  2. Assign roles.
  3. Revise content and design.
  4. Check final formatting and source information.
  5. Submit the final version, not an unfinished draft.

Model Example

Revision role: slide order checker.

Task: confirm that notes, practice, visuals, and conclusion appear in a logical sequence.

Why it matters: the audience can follow the presentation more easily.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Online collaborative publication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.DIG.01

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.
