P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Choosing Words for Audience Impact

Vocabulary

  • Denotation: the direct dictionary meaning of a word.
  • Connotation: the feeling, attitude, or association a word suggests.
  • Tone: the speaker attitude created by word choice and delivery.
  • Audience impact: how listeners may respond to a communication choice.

Speech Example

Version A: Students should stop wasting time during group work.

Version B: Students should use group work time more purposefully.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Both versions address time use, but they create different audience reactions. The phrase wasting time may sound blaming. The phrase more purposefully still communicates improvement, but it sounds more respectful. A speaker should choose words that match the goal and audience.

  1. Identify the direct meaning.
  2. Notice the feeling the word creates.
  3. Decide whether that feeling fits the purpose.
  4. Revise words that create the wrong tone.
  5. Check how the audience may hear the message.

Model Example

Direct meaning: Both versions say group time should be used better.

Connotation: Version B sounds more constructive.

Speech choice: Version B is stronger for persuading classmates respectfully.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Connotation, denotation, and audience impact

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.07

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
