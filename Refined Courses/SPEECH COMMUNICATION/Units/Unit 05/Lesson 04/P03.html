P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Word parts are clues, not automatic answers. A root can guide your thinking, but context confirms meaning. In a speech, you should explain the word as it is used in the topic, not just list a dictionary-style definition.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Understanding derivations helps speakers use related words correctly. For example, revise, revision, and revised are connected, but they fit different sentence roles. Choosing the right form helps the speech sound clear and polished.

Embedded Example

Word family: revise, revision, revised.

Speech use: Revision is the process of improving a draft based on feedback.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I know one related word, so every word in the family means exactly the same thing.

Correct: Related words share meaning, but each form must fit the sentence and speech context.

The incorrect approach ignores usage. The correct approach uses word relationships and context together.

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Read the speech or notes as a listener would hear them. Mark any wording that may confuse the audience.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: clarity, organization, vocabulary, transitions, tone, and conventions.
  3. Choose words for audience impact. Denotation gives meaning; connotation shapes how the audience feels or responds.
  4. Check that revised wording is precise, natural to say aloud, and appropriate for the speaking situation.
