P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Slides should not contain the entire speech. If a slide has too much text, the audience may read instead of listening. The speaker should use slides as guideposts and explain the meaning aloud.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Coherence means the audience can see how each slide connects to the next. If slide 1 is about notes and slide 2 suddenly shows an unrelated picture, the audience may lose the speech thread.

Embedded Example

Weak slide: a full paragraph copied from the speech.

Stronger slide: a title, two keywords, and one image that supports the spoken point.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will put every sentence on the slide so I do not forget what to say.

Correct: I will use short slide cues and speaker notes so the audience listens to my explanation.

The incorrect approach makes slides compete with the speaker. The correct approach lets slides support the speech.

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Identify what the audience should understand after viewing the digital or multimedia product.
  2. Choose visuals, layout, audio, or media only when they clarify the message or improve audience understanding.
  3. Make sure digital elements support the speech rather than distract from the speaker's purpose.
  4. Review the final product for accuracy, organization, accessibility, and publication readiness.
