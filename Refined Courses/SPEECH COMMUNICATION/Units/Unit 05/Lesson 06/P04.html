Task: Improve slide order.

Original order: visuals, conclusion, practice, notes.

  1. Problem: the sequence is confusing.
  2. Better order: notes, practice, visuals, conclusion.
  3. Reasoning: the order follows preparation steps.

Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Choose visual support.

Speech point: timed rehearsal helps pacing.

  1. Useful visual: a simple timer image or timeline.
  2. Weak visual: a random decorative background.
  3. Reasoning: the timer visual clarifies the point.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A slide is better if it has more colors, images, and text.

Correct: A slide is better when its design helps the audience understand the speaker point.

Visual design should clarify the message, not distract from it.

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Identify what the audience should understand after viewing the digital or multimedia product.
  2. Choose visuals, layout, audio, or media only when they clarify the message or improve audience understanding.
  3. Make sure digital elements support the speech rather than distract from the speaker's purpose.
  4. Review the final product for accuracy, organization, accessibility, and publication readiness.
