P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Clear and Connected Speech Language

Vocabulary

  • Clarity: how easy the message is to understand.
  • Cohesion: how well ideas connect.
  • Transition: a word or phrase that shows how ideas relate.
  • Standard English: language that follows accepted grammar and usage for school and professional communication.

Speech Draft Example

Draft: "Practice helps. Notes matter. The audience listens better maybe."

Step-by-Step Teaching

Clear speech language tells the audience exactly what the speaker means. Cohesive language connects ideas so listeners do not feel like the speech jumps from one thought to another. Standard English helps the speaker sound prepared and credible in school and professional settings.

  1. Find unclear wording.
  2. Decide how the ideas connect.
  3. Add a transition when needed.
  4. Use complete, standard sentences.
  5. Read the revision as if an audience is listening.

Model Example

Revision: Practice helps speakers improve because organized notes keep the message focused, and a focused message is easier for the audience to follow.

This revision is clearer and more cohesive because it explains the relationship among practice, notes, message, and audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Clarity, cohesion, and standard English

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.04

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Read the speech or notes as a listener would hear them. Mark any wording that may confuse the audience.
  2. Revise one layer at a time: clarity, organization, vocabulary, transitions, tone, and conventions.
  3. Choose words for audience impact. Denotation gives meaning; connotation shapes how the audience feels or responds.
  4. Check that revised wording is precise, natural to say aloud, and appropriate for the speaking situation.
