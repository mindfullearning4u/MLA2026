P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Turn a broad topic into a research question.

  1. Broad topic: student clubs.
  2. Focused topic: helping new students join clubs.
  3. Research question: What club information helps new students choose a group that fits their interests?

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Check whether a question is answerable.

  1. Question: Why are clubs the best?
  2. Problem: The question is opinion-based and unclear.
  3. Revision: How can club descriptions help students compare meeting times, activities, and goals?

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Connect the question to speech purpose.

  1. Purpose: explain how students can choose a club.
  2. Audience: students who are unsure where to start.
  3. Research question: What details should students compare before joining a club?

Common Mistake

Incorrect: What about clubs?

Correct: What details should students compare before joining a club?

The incorrect question gives the speaker no direction. The correct question tells the speaker what information to research and explain.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Research questions for speeches

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.RES.01

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
