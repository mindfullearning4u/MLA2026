P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Research Questions for Speeches

Vocabulary

  • Research question: a focused question that guides what information a speaker needs to find.
  • Topic: the general subject of a speech.
  • Focused topic: a narrowed version of the topic that is specific enough for a speech.
  • Audience need: what listeners need to understand, know, or decide after the speech.

Step-by-Step Teaching

A speech topic is usually too broad at first. For example, the topic school safety could include hallway rules, digital safety, emergency drills, transportation, or classroom routines. A speaker cannot explain everything well in one speech. A research question narrows the topic and tells the speaker what information to look for.

  1. Start with a broad topic.
  2. Decide what part of the topic matters for the audience.
  3. Turn that focus into a question.
  4. Check whether the question can be answered with evidence.
  5. Use the question to guide speech research.

Model Example

Broad topic: school safety

Focused topic: safe hallway movement during class changes

Research question: What hallway routines help students move safely and arrive to class on time?

This question is useful because it is specific, speech-focused, and answerable with examples, school procedures, or observations.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
