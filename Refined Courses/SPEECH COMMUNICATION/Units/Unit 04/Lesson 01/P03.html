P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Research questions should help a speaker make decisions. A weak question may be too broad, too opinion-based, or too simple. A strong research question helps the speaker find evidence and explain the topic clearly to the audience.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Before writing a research question, ask who will hear the speech. A question for elementary students may need simple examples. A question for school leaders may need data, policy details, or proposed solutions. The audience changes what information is useful.

Embedded Example

Speech idea: improving lunchtime routines.

Audience: students and cafeteria staff.

Research question: What lunchroom procedures could reduce waiting time while keeping the cafeteria organized?

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Is lunch good?

Correct: What lunchroom procedures could reduce waiting time while keeping the cafeteria organized?

The incorrect question is too vague and mostly asks for an opinion. The correct question focuses the speech and points the speaker toward useful evidence.

Deeper Learning Support

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
