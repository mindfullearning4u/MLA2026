P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Separate direct detail from inference.

Source: "Speakers who practice with a timer can notice whether they need to shorten or expand parts of the speech."

  1. Direct detail: speakers practice with a timer.
  2. Direct detail: timing shows whether parts are too short or too long.
  3. Inference: timed practice helps speakers revise before presenting.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Support an inference.

Inference: Timed practice can improve organization.

  1. Support: the source says speakers can shorten or expand parts.
  2. Reasoning: changing section length helps the speech fit the time limit.
  3. Speech use: this supports advice about rehearsal.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Avoid unsupported inference.

Source: "Practice with a timer can help speakers adjust length."

  1. Unsupported inference: timers make every speech interesting.
  2. Problem: the source does not discuss audience interest.
  3. Supported inference: timers help speakers manage speech length.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The timer automatically makes the speech excellent.

Correct: The timer helps the speaker notice whether sections need to be shortened or expanded.

The incorrect version adds a claim the source does not support. The correct version stays connected to the evidence.
