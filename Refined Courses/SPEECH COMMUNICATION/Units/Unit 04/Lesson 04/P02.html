Notebook Title

Making Supported Inferences from Sources

Vocabulary

  • Inference: a reasonable conclusion based on evidence and thinking.
  • Direct statement: information the source says clearly.
  • Support: source details that prove an inference is reasonable.
  • Central idea: the most important idea a source communicates.

Source Excerpt

Source: "Students who preview a presentation space before speaking often feel more prepared. Seeing the room, checking where to stand, and noticing where the audience will sit can reduce last-minute confusion."

Step-by-Step Teaching

An inference is not a wild guess. It must connect to what the source actually says. First, identify the direct details. Then ask what those details suggest. Finally, explain the inference using the source.

  1. Direct detail: students preview the room.
  2. Direct detail: students check where to stand and where the audience sits.
  3. Inference: previewing the room may help speakers feel more confident and organized.
  4. Support: the source says previewing reduces last-minute confusion.

Model Example

Inference: Speakers can prepare better when they understand the physical setting before presenting.

This inference is supported because the source connects room previewing with reduced confusion.
