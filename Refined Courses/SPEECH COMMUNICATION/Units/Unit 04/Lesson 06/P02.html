P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Finding Central Idea and Support

Vocabulary

  • Central idea: the main idea the source develops.
  • Supporting detail: information that explains, proves, or develops the central idea.
  • Relevant evidence: evidence that directly fits the idea being explained.
  • Expository support: facts, examples, or explanation used to clarify an idea.

Source Excerpt

Source: "Student speakers improve when they practice explaining one main point at a time. When a speech jumps between unrelated ideas, listeners may miss the purpose. Clear supporting details help the audience follow the speaker from one idea to the next."

Step-by-Step Teaching

The central idea is not just one interesting word. It is the main message the source develops. After finding the central idea, look for details that explain or prove it. A detail about an unrelated topic should not be used as support.

  1. Ask what the source is mostly about.
  2. Write the central idea in one sentence.
  3. Find details that explain or prove that idea.
  4. Reject details that are interesting but unrelated.
  5. Explain how the support helps the speech audience.

Model Example

Central idea: Clear supporting details help listeners follow a speech.

Supporting detail: When a speech jumps between unrelated ideas, listeners may miss the purpose.

This detail supports the central idea because it explains what can happen when support is not clear.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.

  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
