P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Identify central idea.

Source: "Visual aids work best when they clarify the speaker message instead of distracting from it."

  1. Topic: visual aids.
  2. Central idea: visual aids should clarify the speaker message.
  3. Speech use: support a point about purposeful visuals.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Choose strong support.

Source detail: "A chart can help the audience compare information quickly."

  1. This supports the central idea because it shows how a visual can clarify.
  2. It is stronger than a detail about slide colors alone.
  3. The speaker can explain why the chart helps listeners.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Reject weak support.

Detail: "Many presenters use slides."

  1. This detail is related to presentations.
  2. It does not explain how visuals clarify meaning.
  3. It is weak support for the central idea.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Any detail about slides supports the central idea.

Correct: A detail supports the central idea only if it explains how the visual helps the audience understand the message.

Related is not the same as supportive. A useful detail must develop the central idea.

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
