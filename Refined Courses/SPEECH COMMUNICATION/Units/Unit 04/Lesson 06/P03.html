Speech Communication | Unit 04

P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

When using source support in a speech, do not drop in a detail without explaining it. The audience needs to know how the detail supports the central idea. Your job as speaker is to make the connection visible.

Deeper Communication Thinking

A source may include several details. Some details support the central idea strongly. Others may be background information. Strong speakers choose details that help the audience understand the main idea, not details that simply sound interesting.

Embedded Example

Central idea: Practice helps speakers communicate clearly.

Strong support: Practicing one main point at a time helps speakers avoid jumping between unrelated ideas.

Weak support: Some speeches happen in classrooms.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will use any sentence from the source because it is all about speeches.

Correct: I will use the sentence that explains how clear supporting details help listeners follow the speech.

The incorrect version treats all details as equal. The correct version chooses support that connects directly to the central idea.

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
