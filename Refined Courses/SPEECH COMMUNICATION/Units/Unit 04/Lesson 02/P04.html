P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Evaluate source reliability.

  1. Source A names the organization, explains the topic, and gives current information.
  2. Source B gives an anonymous opinion with no evidence.
  3. Decision: Source A is more reliable because the creator and information are clearer.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Evaluate source validity.

  1. Research question: How can students prepare for a school presentation?
  2. Source: an article about organizing presentation notes and practicing aloud.
  3. Decision: The source is valid because it directly answers the research question.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Reject a weak source.

  1. Research question: What makes peer feedback useful before a speech?
  2. Weak source: a comment saying feedback is boring.
  3. Reason: It is an unsupported opinion and does not explain usefulness.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: This source is long, so it must be reliable.

Correct: This source is reliable because it identifies the creator, gives evidence, and fits the research question.

Length alone does not prove reliability. A speaker must check the creator, evidence, purpose, and connection to the speech.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Reliable and valid source selection

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.RES.01

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
