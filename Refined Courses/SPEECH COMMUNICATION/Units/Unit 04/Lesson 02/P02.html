P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Reliable and Valid Source Selection

Vocabulary

  • Reliable source: a source that can be trusted because it has accurate information and a responsible creator.
  • Valid source: a source that truly supports the research question and speech purpose.
  • Relevance: how closely information connects to the topic or question.
  • Bias: a one-sided viewpoint that may leave out important information.

Step-by-Step Teaching

A source is not strong just because it appears online or sounds confident. A speaker must ask whether the source can be trusted and whether it actually answers the research question. Reliable sources usually identify the author or organization, give current and accurate information, and explain facts clearly. Valid sources fit the speech purpose and support the point the speaker is making.

  1. Identify who created the source.
  2. Check why the source was created.
  3. Look for evidence, facts, or clear explanations.
  4. Check whether the information is current enough for the topic.
  5. Decide whether the source answers the research question.

Model Example

Research question: What lunchroom procedures could reduce waiting time?

Stronger source: a school operations article explaining lunch line organization and student flow.

Weaker source: one student comment saying the line is annoying.

The stronger source is more useful because it explains procedures and gives information that can support a speech.
