P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now connect the source to the speech plan. A source detail is useful only when it helps answer the research question or develop the central idea. A speaker should explain why the source matters instead of assuming the audience will make the connection.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Research-supported speaking requires accuracy and judgment. Accuracy means you do not change what the source says. Judgment means you choose the source details that best support your speech purpose.

Embedded Example

Source detail: previewing the speaking space can reduce confusion.

Supported inference: seeing the room before presenting may help speakers feel more prepared.

Speech use: this can support a point about preparing for delivery conditions.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The source proves that every student who previews the room will give a perfect speech.

Correct: The source suggests that previewing the room can reduce confusion and help students prepare for the speaking space.

The incorrect version exaggerates the source. The correct version uses a careful, supported inference.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated research-supported speech planning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.RES.01, MLA.SPEECH.LST.02

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
