P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Building a Research-Supported Speech Plan

Vocabulary

  • Research-supported speech: a speech that uses reliable source information to explain or support ideas.
  • Research question: a focused question that guides source selection.
  • Central idea: the main message the speech develops.
  • Source support: source evidence that explains, proves, or clarifies the central idea.

Planning Scenario

Speech topic: helping students prepare stronger presentations.

Research question: What preparation habits help students present clearly and confidently?

Source excerpt: "Students who organize notes by main idea, rehearse with a timer, and preview the speaking space can reduce confusion before presenting."

Step-by-Step Teaching

A research-supported speech plan should start with a focused research question. Then the speaker chooses source information that answers the question. After that, the speaker explains how the source supports the central idea. Do not simply list source details. Explain them so the audience understands why they matter.

  1. Write the research question.
  2. Identify the source central idea.
  3. Select evidence that supports the speech purpose.
  4. Make a supported inference when the source suggests an idea.
  5. Explain the evidence in audience-friendly language.

Model Example

Central idea: Careful preparation helps students present more clearly.

Evidence: Organizing notes by main idea and rehearsing with a timer can reduce confusion.

Explanation: This evidence supports the speech because it gives practical preparation habits the audience can use.
