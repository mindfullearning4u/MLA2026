P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Match research question and evidence.

Research question: How can students prepare clearer presentations?

  1. Useful evidence: organize notes by main idea.
  2. Why it fits: the evidence directly answers the question.
  3. Speech explanation: main-idea notes help the speaker stay focused.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Use central idea support.

Central idea: Rehearsal helps students improve presentation timing.

  1. Source detail: rehearsing with a timer shows whether sections are too long or too short.
  2. Support: the detail explains how rehearsal improves timing.
  3. Audience connection: listeners can apply the strategy before presenting.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Use an inference carefully.

Source: Previewing the room can reduce last-minute confusion.

  1. Inference: knowing the room may help speakers feel more prepared.
  2. Support: the source connects room previewing with less confusion.
  3. Speech use: the speaker can recommend checking the space before presenting.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I found one source detail, so my whole speech is supported.

Correct: I need source details that answer the research question, support the central idea, and are explained for the audience.

One detail is not automatically enough. The speaker must show how evidence fits the research-supported speech plan.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated research-supported speech planning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.RES.01, MLA.SPEECH.LST.02

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Start with the speech question or information need. Research should answer a purpose, not collect random facts.
  2. Check source reliability, relevance, and usefulness before adding information to speech notes.
  3. Paraphrase source information accurately so the speech sounds natural and avoids copying.
  4. Explain how the source evidence supports the speech point, central idea, or inference.
