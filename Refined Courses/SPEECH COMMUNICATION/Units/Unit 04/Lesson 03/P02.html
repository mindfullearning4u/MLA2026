P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Reading Sources Before Speaking

Vocabulary

  • Complex text: a source that may include several ideas, details, or academic words.
  • Comprehension: understanding what the source says and means.
  • Paraphrase: restating an idea in your own words without changing the meaning.
  • Source detail: a fact, example, or explanation from a source.

Source Excerpt

Source: A school wellness newsletter states, "Students who plan short movement breaks during long study sessions may return to their work with better focus. Movement breaks should be brief, purposeful, and scheduled so they do not replace the study task."

Step-by-Step Teaching

Before using a source in a speech, slow down and understand the source. First, identify the topic. Next, find the main point. Then look for details that explain or limit the main point. Finally, decide how the information helps your speech audience.

  1. Topic: movement breaks during study.
  2. Main point: short movement breaks may help students return with better focus.
  3. Important limit: breaks should be brief and should not replace studying.
  4. Speech use: this source could support a speech about healthy study routines.

Model Example

Paraphrase: The newsletter suggests that brief, planned movement breaks can support focus during long study sessions, but the breaks should not become a substitute for studying.

This paraphrase keeps the meaning and does not exaggerate the source.
