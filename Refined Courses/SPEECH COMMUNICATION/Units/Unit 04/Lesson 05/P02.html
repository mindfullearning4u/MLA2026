P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Reading Source Structure and Purpose

Vocabulary

  • Source structure: the way information is organized.
  • Text feature: a heading, list, chart, example, or section that helps a reader understand information.
  • Author purpose: why the source was created.
  • Speech purpose: what the speaker wants the audience to understand or do.

Source Excerpt

Source: A handout titled "Three Steps for Safer Online Group Work" lists: 1. Use shared documents with clear names. 2. Keep comments focused on the project. 3. Check final slides before presenting.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Before using a source, notice how it is built. A numbered list usually gives steps or procedures. A cause-and-effect structure explains why something happens. A comparison structure shows similarities and differences. Source structure helps a speaker decide how to use the information.

  1. Identify the source topic.
  2. Notice the structure or feature.
  3. Ask why the source was created.
  4. Connect the source purpose to the speech purpose.
  5. Choose details that help the audience follow the speech.

Model Example

The handout uses a numbered list. Its purpose is to give practical steps. A speaker could use it in a speech about digital collaboration because the structure makes the advice easy to explain.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Structures, features, and source purpose

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.LST.01

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
