P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Source features are not decoration. A heading may show the topic. A list may show order. A chart may show comparison. A speaker should use these clues to understand what the source is doing before selecting evidence.

Deeper Communication Thinking

When source purpose and speech purpose match, evidence is easier to explain. If a source is designed to persuade, it may use strong language. If a source is designed to inform, it may define terms or explain steps. The speaker must decide whether that purpose fits the audience.

Embedded Example

Source feature: numbered steps for group work.

Purpose: to instruct students how to collaborate online.

Speech use: the speaker can explain each step as part of a speech about better group presentations.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will use this source because it has a list, and lists are always strong evidence.

Correct: I will use this source because the numbered list gives clear steps that match my speech purpose about online group work.

The incorrect version notices a feature but does not explain why it matters. The correct version connects structure, purpose, and speech use.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Structures, features, and source purpose

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.LST.01

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
