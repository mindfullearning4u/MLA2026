Notebook Title

Comparing Opposing Arguments Notes

Vocabulary

  • Opposing argument: an argument that disagrees with another position.
  • Effectiveness: how well an argument supports its claim for an audience.
  • Validity: whether reasoning is logical and supported.
  • Central idea: the main point an argument develops.

Detailed Teaching

Step 1: Identify each claim. Do not compare details until you know each side’s main point.

Step 2: Look at evidence. Ask whether each side uses specific, credible, and relevant support.

Step 3: Examine reasoning. Strong reasoning explains how the evidence proves the claim.

Step 4: Compare effectiveness. The stronger argument is usually clearer, better supported, and more logical.

Model Example

Argument A: Students should rehearse alone because it feels less stressful.

Argument B: Students should rehearse with a peer because peer feedback can reveal unclear parts before the final speech.

Model thinking: Argument B is stronger because it gives a specific benefit and explains how feedback improves the speech.

Notebook Directions

  3. Write one explanation sentence in your own words.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Comparing opposing arguments

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.LST.04

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the claim first. A persuasive speech needs a clear position before evidence or rhetoric can be evaluated.
  2. Connect reasons and evidence directly to the claim. Evidence should prove, clarify, or justify the position.
  3. Address opposing views fairly. A strong rebuttal explains why the speaker's position remains stronger.
  4. Evaluate audience effect. Rhetorical choices should strengthen credibility, emotion, logic, or urgency without weakening fairness.
