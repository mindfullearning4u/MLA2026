P04 Worked Example

Each worked example shows how to identify a rhetorical appeal and explain the speaker’s purpose.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech line: "I have coached three debate teams, and I have seen preparation change nervous speakers into clear speakers."

Step 1: The speaker mentions experience.

Step 2: Experience builds credibility.

Answer: This is ethos because the speaker shows trustworthiness.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech line: "Imagine standing in front of the room with your hands shaking, then realizing your practice has prepared you."

Step 1: The speaker asks the audience to imagine a feeling.

Step 2: The feeling connects to confidence.

Answer: This is pathos because it appeals to emotion.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Speech line: "Students who rehearse three times are more likely to stay within the time limit."

Step 1: The speaker gives a measurable reason.

Step 2: The reason supports the point logically.

Answer: This is logos because it relies on evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: This is ethos because it sounds important.

The mistake weakens communication because it skips the thinking process behind the speaking choice.

Correct Example

Correct: This is ethos because the speaker uses experience to build audience trust.

The corrected response names the communication choice and explains its effect.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Rhetorical appeals, purpose, and figurative language

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.LST.03

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
