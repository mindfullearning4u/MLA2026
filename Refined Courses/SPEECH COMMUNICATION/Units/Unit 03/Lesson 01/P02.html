Notebook Title

Rhetorical Appeals and Purpose Notes

Vocabulary

  • Rhetoric: the choices a speaker makes to affect an audience.
  • Ethos: an appeal based on credibility or trust.
  • Pathos: an appeal based on emotion.
  • Logos: an appeal based on logic, facts, or evidence.
  • Figurative language: wording that creates meaning beyond the literal words, such as metaphor or imagery.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Find the speaker’s purpose. Ask, "What does the speaker want the audience to think, feel, or do?"

Step 2: Look for the appeal. If the speaker builds trust, it is ethos. If the speaker uses feeling, it is pathos. If the speaker uses facts or reasoning, it is logos.

Step 3: Notice figurative language. A phrase like "practice is the bridge to confidence" is not literal. It helps the audience picture the idea.

Step 4: Explain the effect. Do not stop at naming the appeal. Tell how the choice helps the message reach the audience.

Model Example

Speech example: "As a student who once feared public speaking, I know practice can turn nervous energy into confidence. Each rehearsal is a bridge from uncertainty to readiness."

Model thinking: The speaker uses ethos by sharing experience, pathos by naming fear and confidence, and figurative language with "bridge." The purpose is to encourage practice.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the vocabulary in your notebook.
  2. Copy the model example.
  3. Add one sentence explaining how the example helps a speaker or listener.
