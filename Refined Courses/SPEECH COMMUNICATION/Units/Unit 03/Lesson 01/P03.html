Deeper Communication Thinking

Rhetorical analysis means explaining how a choice works. A strong explanation has three parts: name the choice, point to the words that show it, and explain the effect on the audience.

Example: If a speaker says, "Research shows that students remember speeches better when ideas are organized," the appeal is logos because the speaker uses research. The effect is that the audience has a logical reason to value organization.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The speaker says something emotional, so it is always pathos.

Not every emotional word automatically proves pathos. You must connect the words to the speaker’s purpose and audience effect.

Correct Example

Correct: The speaker uses pathos only when the emotional wording is meant to affect how the audience feels.

This correction works because it uses the lesson skill deliberately instead of guessing.
