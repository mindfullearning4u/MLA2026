P04 Worked Example

Study how each example builds one part of a persuasive speech argument.

Example 1

Situation: A student wants to persuade classmates to rehearse presentations.

Step 1: Write a focused claim: Students should rehearse before presenting.

Step 2: Check that the claim can be supported with reasons and evidence.

Example 2

Claim: Students should rehearse before presenting.

Reasoning: Rehearsal helps speakers find unclear sections and improve timing.

Why it works: The reasoning explains how rehearsal improves the speech.

Example 3

Evidence: A speaker who times a practice run can see whether each section fits the time limit.

Connection: This evidence supports the claim because timing helps speakers revise before presenting.

Result: The argument is clearer and more credible.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: My claim is true because I think it is true.

The mistake skips the reasoning needed for strong communication.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: My claim is stronger when I add reasoning and credible evidence that the audience can understand.

The corrected answer names the communication move and explains why it works.
