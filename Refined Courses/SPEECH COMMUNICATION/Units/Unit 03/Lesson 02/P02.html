Notebook Title

Explaining Rhetorical Effects Notes

Vocabulary

  • Rhetorical choice: a deliberate decision about words, examples, structure, or appeals.
  • Audience impact: how the choice may affect what listeners think, feel, or understand.
  • Analysis: an explanation of how and why a communication choice works.
  • Evidence: the exact word, phrase, or detail that supports your explanation.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read the speech example and identify the speaker’s purpose.

Step 2: Choose one rhetorical choice to analyze. It may be an appeal, a repeated phrase, a question, a metaphor, or a contrast.

Step 3: Quote or describe the evidence. This proves that the choice is really there.

Step 4: Explain audience impact. A strong sentence sounds like this: "The speaker uses ___ to help the audience ___."

Model Example

Speech example: "If one rehearsal can prevent a confusing presentation, why would we skip it? Practice gives the speaker a map before the audience starts the journey."

Model thinking: The question pushes the audience to consider the value of rehearsal. The metaphor "map" shows that practice guides the speaker and helps the audience follow.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the vocabulary in your notebook.
  2. Copy the model example.
  3. Add one sentence explaining how the example helps a speaker or listener.
