P04 Worked Example

Each example shows how to move from counterclaim to rebuttal.

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Claim: Students should use note cards instead of full scripts.

Counterclaim: Full scripts help students remember every word.

Thinking: This concern is about confidence and memory.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Rebuttal: Note cards still support memory because they list the key points while allowing the speaker to sound natural.

Evidence: A card with focus, point, and support can guide delivery without requiring word-for-word reading.

Why it works: The rebuttal answers the memory concern directly.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Full response: Some people may prefer full scripts because they help speakers remember every word. However, note cards can still support memory because they list key points and allow more natural delivery.

Result: The response is respectful, direct, and supported.

Common Mistake - Incorrect

Incorrect: You are wrong because scripts are bad.

The mistake skips the reasoning needed for strong communication.

Common Mistake - Correct

Correct: I understand scripts can feel safer, but note cards can guide memory while helping the speaker sound more natural.

The corrected answer names the communication move and explains why it works.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Counterclaims and rebuttal

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.01

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Identify the claim first. A persuasive speech needs a clear position before evidence or rhetoric can be evaluated.
  2. Connect reasons and evidence directly to the claim. Evidence should prove, clarify, or justify the position.
  3. Address opposing views fairly. A strong rebuttal explains why the speaker's position remains stronger.
  4. Evaluate audience effect. Rhetorical choices should strengthen credibility, emotion, logic, or urgency without weakening fairness.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
