P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Identify the claim and audience.

  1. Claim: Peer tutoring should be offered during school support time.
  2. Audience: students and staff deciding how support time should be used.
  3. Reasoning: The audience needs practical benefits, not just opinions.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Evaluate evidence and explanation.

  1. Evidence: Students can ask questions in small groups.
  2. Explanation: Small groups can make it easier to ask for help.
  3. Evaluation: The evidence is relevant because it explains how tutoring supports learning.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Compare the opposing argument and rebuttal.

  1. Opposing argument: Students may not have extra time.
  2. Rebuttal: Short sessions during existing support time can reduce later confusion.
  3. Evaluation: This rebuttal is respectful because it answers the concern without dismissing it.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: People against tutoring do not care about learning.

Correct: Some people may worry about time, but short support sessions can make tutoring manageable and useful.

The incorrect example attacks motives. The correct example addresses the actual concern and keeps the speech credible.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
