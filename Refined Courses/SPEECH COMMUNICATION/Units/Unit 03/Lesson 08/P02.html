Step-by-Step Review

Unit 3 began with rhetorical choices and moved into argument planning. A persuasive speaker must know the purpose, understand the audience, choose words carefully, support claims with evidence, explain reasoning, and respond to opposing views. These skills work together. If one part is missing, the speech becomes harder to trust or follow.

  1. Identify the purpose and audience.
  2. Find the claim.
  3. Check evidence and reasoning.
  4. Notice rhetorical language and tone.
  5. Compare opposing arguments.
  6. Evaluate the rebuttal.

Model Scenario

A student wants to persuade classmates to support a peer tutoring program. The audience includes students who are busy, students who need support, and students who may not think tutoring is useful. A strong speaker would use respectful tone, relevant examples, clear evidence, and a rebuttal to concerns about time.
