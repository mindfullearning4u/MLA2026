Speech Communication | Unit 03

P06 Independent Work

Complete the following work independently in the same notebook used for your lesson notes.

Show your thinking clearly. Answer in complete sentences whenever an explanation is required.

Important Important:

The work completed below should be written in the same notebook as your lesson notes. Both your notes and your Independent Work will be included together when you submit your Notebook Evidence assignment.

Instructions

Create a persuasive speech plan about one school or community topic. Keep the topic specific so your plan can be clear and supported.

Part A

Write one clear claim. Then write one sentence explaining who your audience is and what they may already believe about the topic.

Part B

Write two pieces of evidence. For each piece of evidence, explain how it supports the claim.

Part C

Write one fair counterclaim and one respectful rebuttal. Make sure the rebuttal answers the opposing view without attacking people.

Independent Work Planning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated persuasive speech planning

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.01, MLA.SPEECH.LST.04

Use this checklist before completing Parts A, B, and C so your independent work stays aligned to the lesson standard.

  1. Identify the claim first. A persuasive speech needs a clear position before evidence or rhetoric can be evaluated.
  2. Connect reasons and evidence directly to the claim. Evidence should prove, clarify, or justify the position.
  3. Address opposing views fairly. A strong rebuttal explains why the speaker's position remains stronger.
  4. Evaluate audience effect. Rhetorical choices should strengthen credibility, emotion, logic, or urgency without weakening fairness.

P06 independent work quality check: Before you submit, make sure Part A shows the basic skill, Part B applies the skill to a communication situation, and Part C explains your reasoning. A strong response does not only complete the task; it shows why your choices fit the audience, purpose, evidence, and communication goal.

  • Use complete, specific responses instead of one-word answers.
  • Connect each answer to the lesson focus and mapped standard.
  • Reread your work as if an audience, listener, or collaborator will use it.
