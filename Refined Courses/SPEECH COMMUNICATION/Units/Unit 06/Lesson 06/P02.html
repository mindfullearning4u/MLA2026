P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Planning a Digital Publication for a Real Audience

Vocabulary

  • Digital publication: a communication product shared through a digital format, such as slides, a webpage, a video outline, or an online announcement.
  • Audience awareness: making choices based on what the audience needs, knows, and expects.
  • Platform: the digital place or format used to share a message.
  • Accessibility: making communication easy to see, hear, read, and understand.

Communication Scenario

Task: Create a digital announcement for students about a career skills workshop.

Audience: high school students who may not know why the workshop matters.

Purpose: explain what students will gain and how to participate.

Step-by-Step Teaching

  1. Identify the audience before choosing the format.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence.
  3. Choose the digital format that helps the audience receive the message.
  4. Organize information from most important to least important.
  5. Use design choices that support understanding, not decoration only.

A digital publication is still communication. The format should help the audience understand the message, take action, and know where to find key information.

Model Example

Publication choice: a one-page digital flyer.

Reason: students need quick information they can scan.

Audience-aware features: clear title, short benefit statement, date and time, sign-up link, and one simple visual related to career skills.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Audience-aware digital publication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.DIG.01

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
