Example 1

Task: Define the communication problem.

  1. Problem: event information reaches audiences unevenly.
  2. Audience: students, families, and staff.
  3. Need: a clearer communication plan with appropriate formats.

Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Select a format.

  1. Staff briefing: short slide presentation with evidence and recommendation.
  2. Family announcement: clear email with key details and next steps.
  3. Student announcement: digital flyer with concise benefits and action steps.

Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Explain the synthesis.

  1. Research supports the recommendation.
  2. Digital publication makes action steps accessible.
  3. Presentation planning helps the speaker explain the plan clearly.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will only make the slides look good.

Correct: I will make slides that organize evidence, support the speaker's explanation, and help the audience understand the recommendation.

Visual appeal matters, but presentation design must serve communication.
