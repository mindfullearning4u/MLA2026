P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Professional tone depends on the relationship and setting. A message to a close friend can be casual. A message to a supervisor, teacher, interviewer, or team leader should be more formal and specific. The speaker should still sound human, but the wording should show respect and purpose.

Deeper Communication Thinking

Audience awareness means asking what the listener needs to know. If the audience is busy, the message should be direct. If the topic is sensitive, the tone should be careful. If the speaker is making a request, the message should include enough detail for the audience to respond.

Embedded Example

Casual: "Can you fix my hours?"

Professional: "Could you help me review my schedule? I may have a conflict with the posted Friday shift."

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Professional communication means using long words and sounding distant.

Correct: Professional communication means being clear, respectful, specific, and appropriate for the audience.

The incorrect idea confuses professionalism with sounding complicated. The correct idea focuses on audience-ready communication.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Professional communication purpose and audience

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.09

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
