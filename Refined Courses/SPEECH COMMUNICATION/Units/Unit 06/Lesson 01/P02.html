P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title

Matching Purpose, Audience, Voice, and Tone

Vocabulary

  • Purpose: the reason for communicating.
  • Audience: the person or group receiving the message.
  • Voice: the overall style or personality of the message.
  • Tone: the attitude communicated through word choice and phrasing.
  • Professional communication: clear, respectful communication for school, work, or formal settings.

Professional Scenario

Situation: A student needs to ask a workplace supervisor to clarify a schedule change.

Weak message: "I do not get this. What am I supposed to do?"

Stronger message: "Could you please clarify which shift I should report to on Friday? I want to make sure I follow the updated schedule correctly."

Step-by-Step Teaching

Professional communication begins with purpose and audience. First, decide why you are communicating. Next, identify who will receive the message. Then choose words and tone that help the person respond. Clear professional communication is not cold or robotic; it is respectful, specific, and easy to understand.

  1. Identify the purpose.
  2. Identify the audience.
  3. Choose a respectful tone.
  4. State the request or message clearly.
  5. Check that the message fits the setting.

Model Example

Purpose: ask for clarification.

Audience: workplace supervisor.

Tone: respectful and responsible.

Message: "Could you please clarify which shift I should report to on Friday?"

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Professional communication purpose and audience

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.09

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
