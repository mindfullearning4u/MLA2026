P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Improve an interview answer.

Question: "What is one communication strength you have?"

  1. Weak: I am good at talking.
  2. Stronger: I explain ideas clearly by organizing my points before I speak.
  3. Reasoning: the stronger answer names a specific skill.

Worked Example 2

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Build on a discussion idea.

Comment: "We need a stronger conclusion."

  1. Response: I agree. We could restate the main idea and end with one action step.
  2. Why it works: it acknowledges and adds a useful suggestion.
  3. Communication skill: collaboration.

Worked Example 3

Step-by-step explanation: Follow each modeled step carefully and notice how the thinking moves from the task to evidence, reasoning, and a clear response.

Task: Ask a follow-up question.

Comment: "The audience may not understand this term."

  1. Follow-up: Which term do you think needs a clearer explanation?
  2. Why it works: it asks for specific clarification.
  3. Result: the group can revise more effectively.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I disagree, so your idea is wrong.

Correct: I see your point, but I think we may need more evidence before changing the claim.

The incorrect response shuts down discussion. The correct response disagrees respectfully and explains the reason.

Worked Example Reasoning Support

Lesson focus: Interview and discussion communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.08

As you study the worked examples, look for how each model makes a communication decision and explains why that decision works.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.

Teachable Explanation: The correct example works because it applies the lesson skill deliberately and explains the reasoning instead of only naming an answer.
