P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Professional discussions require both speaking and listening. A good response often begins by acknowledging what someone said. Then the speaker adds information, asks a clarifying question, or respectfully offers another view.

Deeper Communication Thinking

In an interview, short vague answers may make the speaker seem unprepared. In a group discussion, interrupting or ignoring ideas can weaken collaboration. Strong communicators show they heard the question or comment before responding.

Embedded Example

Discussion comment: "I think our slides have too much text."

Strong response: "I agree that the slides are crowded. Could we keep the key terms on the slides and move the full explanation into speaker notes?"

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will answer every question with the shortest possible response so I do not say too much.

Correct: I will answer directly and include a specific example when the question asks for explanation.

The incorrect approach may sound unprepared. The correct approach gives enough detail for the listener to understand the response.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Interview and discussion communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.08

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.
