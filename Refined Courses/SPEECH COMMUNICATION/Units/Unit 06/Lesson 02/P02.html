P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Responding in Interviews and Professional Discussions

Vocabulary

  • Interview: a structured conversation where questions and answers help someone learn about a person, role, or idea.
  • Discussion: a conversation where people exchange and build on ideas.
  • Active listening: listening carefully and showing understanding through response.
  • Follow-up question: a question that continues or deepens the conversation.

Interview Scenario

Question: "Tell me about a time you worked with a group."

Weak response: "I worked in groups before."

Stronger response: "In a class presentation group, I helped organize slide order and checked that our speaker notes matched the slides. That helped our group present more smoothly."

Step-by-Step Teaching

Strong interview and discussion responses are specific. They answer the question, give a relevant example, and connect the example to the purpose of the conversation. In discussions, strong participants also listen to others and build on ideas instead of only waiting to speak.

  1. Listen to the full question or comment.
  2. Answer directly.
  3. Add a specific example or detail.
  4. Connect the detail to the discussion purpose.
  5. Ask or answer a respectful follow-up when needed.

Model Example

Question: "How do you handle feedback?"

Response: "I first identify what the feedback asks me to improve. For example, if a peer says my presentation needs clearer transitions, I revise the order and add transition phrases between main points."

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Interview and discussion communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.COM.08

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Listen for the speaker's main point before preparing your response. Active listening starts with understanding, not reacting.
  2. Use collaborative moves such as asking a clarifying question, building on an idea, summarizing a point, or respectfully disagreeing.
  3. Respond to the actual idea that was shared. Avoid changing the topic before the discussion goal is met.
  4. Check that your communication helps the group move forward and shows respect for the speaker and audience.
