P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Professional briefings should not include every piece of research. Choose evidence that answers the question and supports the recommendation. Then explain the evidence so the audience does not have to guess why it matters.

Deeper Communication Thinking

A professional audience often wants information quickly. This means the speaker should be concise but not incomplete. A strong briefing says what should happen, why it should happen, and what evidence supports the recommendation.

Embedded Example

Weak evidence use: Clubs meet in many classrooms.

Stronger evidence use: Agenda reminders help members know what to expect before meetings.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I will include every source detail so the briefing sounds researched.

Correct: I will include the source details that directly support my recommendation and explain why they matter.

The incorrect version overloads the audience. The correct version uses research selectively and clearly.

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
