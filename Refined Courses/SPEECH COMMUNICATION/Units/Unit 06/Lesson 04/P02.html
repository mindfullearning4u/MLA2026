P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title

Planning a Research-Supported Briefing

Vocabulary

  • Briefing: a short, focused presentation that explains information or a recommendation.
  • Recommendation: a suggested action based on reasons and evidence.
  • Relevant evidence: information that directly supports the briefing purpose.
  • Research question: a question that guides what evidence is needed.

Briefing Scenario

Professional task: Recommend one way a school club can improve meeting attendance.

Research question: What meeting practices help members attend consistently?

Source excerpt: "Clubs with clear agendas and reminders sent before meetings often report steadier attendance because members know what to expect."

Step-by-Step Teaching

  1. Identify the professional problem.
  2. Write a focused research question.
  3. Select evidence that answers the question.
  4. Explain how the evidence supports a recommendation.
  5. Organize the briefing with a clear beginning, evidence, and recommendation.

A briefing should be direct. The audience needs the main point, the evidence, and the action you recommend.

Model Example

Recommendation: Send a short agenda and reminder before each club meeting.

Evidence: clubs with agendas and reminders report steadier attendance.

Explanation: members are more likely to attend when they know the purpose and plan for the meeting.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Research-supported professional briefing

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.RES.01

Use this sequence before completing the notebook task. It shows how to approach the communication skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the audience before choosing words, tone, or examples. A strong speaker adapts to what listeners need and expect.
  2. State the purpose in one clear sentence so every communication choice supports that goal.
  3. Choose voice and tone deliberately. Ask whether the language sounds respectful, appropriate, and useful for the situation.
  4. Check that the final message fits the audience, purpose, and setting without becoming too casual, too vague, or too formal.
