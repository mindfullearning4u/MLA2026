Speech Communication | Unit 06

P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continued Notebook Application

Now you will practice turning source information into communication reasoning. The goal is not to repeat a source word for word. The goal is to show that you understand what the source proves, what it suggests, and how it helps the audience understand the topic.

Embedded Example

Source note: In a practice presentation, listeners remembered the recommendation more accurately when the speaker explained the evidence before giving the final recommendation.

  1. Evidence: listeners remembered the recommendation more accurately after explanation.
  2. Inference: explanation helps listeners understand why the recommendation matters.
  3. Use in speech: explain the evidence first, then state the recommendation.

Step-by-Step Thinking

  1. Ask, "What does the source actually say?"
  2. Ask, "What conclusion can I make from that information?"
  3. Check that the conclusion does not go beyond the evidence.
  4. Write one sentence explaining why the source helps your audience understand the topic.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The source proves that every audience will agree with the recommendation.

Correct: The source suggests that explaining evidence before the recommendation can help listeners remember the recommendation more accurately.

The incorrect statement overclaims. A strong source explanation stays close to what the evidence supports and avoids promising more than the source proves.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Evidence, inference, and source explanation

Mapped standard focus: MLA.SPEECH.RES.02

Use this second-pass routine to check whether your thinking is specific, audience-aware, and connected to the lesson skill.

  1. Identify the claim first. A persuasive speech needs a clear position before evidence or rhetoric can be evaluated.
  2. Connect reasons and evidence directly to the claim. Evidence should prove, clarify, or justify the position.
  3. Address opposing views fairly. A strong rebuttal explains why the speaker's position remains stronger.
  4. Evaluate audience effect. Rhetorical choices should strengthen credibility, emotion, logic, or urgency without weakening fairness.
