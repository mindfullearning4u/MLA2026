Speech Communication | Unit 06

P04 Worked Example

Example 1

Task: Identify evidence.

Source note: A rehearsal log shows that speakers who practiced with a timer finished closer to the assigned presentation length.

  1. Evidence: speakers practiced with a timer and finished closer to the assigned length.
  2. Inference: timed rehearsal may help speakers manage presentation pacing.
  3. Explanation: this evidence supports using timed practice because timing affects whether a speech fits the audience's schedule.

Example 2

Task: Connect evidence to a claim.

Claim: Speakers should explain charts aloud instead of only showing them.

  1. Evidence: listeners often miss the main point of a chart when the speaker moves on too quickly.
  2. Inference: spoken explanation helps the audience process the visual.
  3. Explanation: the source supports the claim because it shows that visuals need speaker guidance to become meaningful.

Example 3

Task: Avoid overclaiming.

Source note: A small class survey found that most students preferred short bullet points on slides.

  1. Evidence: most students in one class preferred short bullet points.
  2. Careful inference: short bullet points may help this type of audience follow a slide presentation.
  3. Explanation: because the survey was small, the speaker should use it as support, not as proof about all audiences.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: This survey proves all presentations should use short bullet points.

Correct: This survey suggests short bullet points may help this class audience follow the presentation more easily.

The correct version respects the source. It uses the evidence, makes a reasonable inference, and avoids stretching a small source into an unsupported universal claim.
