# English I — Course Overview and Expectations

**Course:** English I  
**Credit:** 1.0 High School English Credit  
**Delivery:** Online, mastery-based independent learning  
**Moodle activity:** Course Overview and Expectations (four content pages)

This document preserves the approved student-facing orientation loaded into Moodle. The rendered Moodle pages use accessible colored information boxes; students should never see literal HTML source.

## Page 1 — Course Overview

### Course Description

English I develops the skills students need to understand complex literary and informational texts, communicate ideas clearly, use evidence responsibly, and write for different purposes and audiences. Lessons provide direct instruction, teacher modeling, guided practice, independent application, and opportunities to demonstrate mastery.

### Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Read and analyze literary and informational texts closely.
- Determine central ideas, themes, purpose, tone, and the effects of an author’s choices.
- Explain figurative language, rhetoric, structure, reasoning, and evidence.
- Write clear narrative, explanatory, and argumentative responses.
- Use relevant textual evidence and explain how it supports a claim.
- Apply grade-level grammar, usage, mechanics, and academic vocabulary.
- Conduct research, evaluate sources, and cite information responsibly.
- Revise work using feedback and communicate appropriately with the Teacher of Record.

### Course Organization

The course is organized into units. Each unit begins with a diagnostic pretest and contains eight carefully sequenced lessons. Lessons 1–7 teach and practice the unit skills. Lesson 8 brings the learning together through synthesis and the unit assessment.

Students complete activities in the order shown in Moodle. Required completion and mastery rules unlock the next activity.

### How Lessons Work

1. Read the overview, objectives, and evidence checklist.
2. Write the assigned vocabulary and definitions in the notebook.
3. Study the teacher-directed lesson and worked examples.
4. Complete guided practice and use detailed feedback to correct misunderstandings.
5. Complete the independent work and checkpoint.
6. Submit notebook evidence and checkpoint evidence as directed.
7. Complete the lesson quiz or unit assessment when it appears.

### Assessment and Grading

- **Unit pretests:** Diagnostic, 10 multiple-choice questions, one attempt, not weighted.
- **Guided practice:** 5 questions, repeat until at least 80% mastery, not weighted.
- **Notebook evidence:** 20% of the weighted grade.
- **Checkpoint submissions:** 20% of the weighted grade.
- **Lesson quizzes:** 30% of the weighted grade.
- **Unit assessments:** 30% of the weighted grade.

### Standards, Support, and Integrity

English I instruction is aligned to the applicable Grade 9 English Language Arts standards. Students submit original work, identify sources, and complete assessments independently unless collaboration is specifically allowed.

Students contact the Teacher of Record (TOR) when directions are unclear, feedback needs explanation, a technical problem blocks progress, or an accommodation or intervention is needed.

## Page 2 — How This Course Works

### Where You Begin

1. Complete all four orientation pages.
2. Read the expectations and acknowledgement carefully.
3. Open Unit 1 and complete the pretest independently.
4. Begin Lesson 1 only after Moodle shows it as available.

### Unit Workflow

1. **Pretest:** 10 multiple-choice questions, one attempt, diagnostic only.
2. **Lessons 1–7:** Learn, practice, submit evidence, and complete required quizzes.
3. **Lesson 8:** Synthesize the unit skills and prepare for the unit assessment.
4. **Unit assessment:** Complete the assigned random questions independently in one attempt.

### Lesson Page Sequence

1. **Overview:** Objectives, success criteria, materials, and evidence checklist.
2. **Vocabulary:** Terms and student-friendly definitions to write in the notebook.
3. **Direct Instruction:** A complete explanation of the skill, broken into manageable steps.
4. **Model and Worked Example:** How a strong reader or writer applies the skill.
5. **Guided Practice:** Five questions with detailed feedback; continue until reaching 80%.
6. **Independent Work:** Apply the skill without step-by-step prompting.
7. **Checkpoint:** Show understanding and prepare the required evidence.

### The English I Notebook

Students maintain one organized paper or digital notebook for the course. Every entry clearly identifies the student name, course, unit, lesson, and required section.

Notebook evidence may include:

- Vocabulary terms and definitions
- Assigned lesson notes and Page 3 questions
- Worked examples and corrections
- Independent work responses
- Checkpoint preparation when directed

### Mastery, Feedback, and Progress

Guided practice provides detailed, teachable feedback and may be repeated until 80% mastery. Graded quizzes and unit assessments allow one initial attempt. A student who does not demonstrate mastery contacts the TOR for review, intervention, and next-step directions.

Students do not skip locked activities. A lock normally means that an earlier completion or passing requirement remains unfinished.

## Page 3 — Expectations and Policies

### Student Responsibilities

Students will:

- Work through activities in the order assigned.
- Read every direction, model, example, and feedback message.
- Keep complete, organized notebook evidence.
- Submit readable work by the assigned deadline.
- Ask for help promptly when a skill or technical issue blocks progress.
- Check Moodle messages and feedback regularly.

### Grading and Feedback

- Pretests and guided practice are diagnostic and do not affect the final grade.
- Notebook evidence and checkpoint submissions each account for 20%.
- Lesson quizzes and unit assessments each account for 30%.
- A complete submission may still require revision if it does not meet the rubric or demonstrate the assigned skill.
- Students read TOR feedback before submitting a revision.

### Notebook Evidence

Notebook work is evaluated for required vocabulary and definitions, lesson notes or assigned questions, worked examples, independent work, completion, legibility, identification, and organization.

Every submission identifies the student name, course name, unit number, lesson number, and clear headings for each required section.

### Checkpoint Evidence

Checkpoint work must answer every prompt, demonstrate the assigned reading or writing skill, use relevant evidence when required, explain reasoning in complete sentences, and be readable and clearly labeled. Students submit only the work requested for that lesson.

### Academic Integrity and Responsible Use

- Students submit their own reading, thinking, and writing.
- Students do not copy answers, use unauthorized answer sites, or allow another person or tool to complete work for them.
- Students quote, paraphrase, and cite sources as directed.
- Students complete quizzes and assessments independently.
- Approved supports or accommodations may be used, but they must not replace the student’s own demonstration of the skill.

### Respectful Communication

Literature and informational texts may present unfamiliar cultures, beliefs, identities, conflicts, and perspectives. Students discuss ideas respectfully, support interpretations with evidence, and avoid demeaning language. A student contacts the TOR privately if a topic creates concern or an accommodation is needed.

### Technology, Accessibility, and Communication

Students use a reliable device, current browser, and stable internet connection and keep backup copies of written work. Broken links or upload problems should be reported with the activity name and a screenshot when possible.

Approved accommodations apply throughout the course. Students contact the TOR before a due date whenever possible so support can be arranged.
