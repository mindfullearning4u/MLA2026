# English I Course Structure Analysis

## Architecture decision

The proposed course contains **six units**:

1. Literary and Informational Foundations
2. Inside the Nightmare
3. Outsiders and Outcasts
4. Crazy, Stupid Love
5. Journeys of Transformation
6. World's End

This preserves the completed MLA prerequisite unit exactly as supplied by the user and then preserves the five-unit Savvas sequence. The Savvas units are renumbered as MLA Units 2-6. No work is proposed for Unit 1.

## Why Unit 1 remains foundational

Unit 1 establishes close reading, accurate paraphrase, vocabulary, evidence selection, short evidence-based response, conventions, and discussion habits. Its existing lessons, content, pretest, quizzes, and final synthesis/unit assessment are complete and authoritative.

Unit 1 is not a random review. Each lesson supports later work:

| Foundation | Later use |
|---|---|
| Close reading and paraphrase | Every literary and informational selection |
| Academic vocabulary | Savvas concept vocabulary and language/craft work |
| Selecting and explaining evidence | Comprehension, analysis, writing, and assessment tasks |
| Literary/informational distinction | Cross-genre and paired-text work across Units 2-6 |
| Short response and conventions | Checkpoints, quizzes, projects, and performance tasks |
| Discussion and notebook habits | Peer-group learning and TOR evidence review |

No Unit 1 architecture or production action is authorized or required.

## Savvas organization

Each Savvas thematic unit follows a recurring macrostructure:

1. Unit introduction and mentor text
2. Whole-Class Learning
3. Whole-class performance task and writing for assessment
4. Peer-Group Learning
5. Peer-group performance task
6. Independent Learning
7. Performance-Based Assessment
8. Reflection, projects, and two-part unit test

Savvas content items are not treated as one Moodle lesson each. Related selections and tasks are clustered into coherent MLA lessons. Teacher resources, accessible texts, language support, remediation, and enrichment remain resources attached to those lessons rather than separate units.

## Instructional progression

- Unit 1 teaches the transferable reading and response routines.
- Unit 2 applies them to Gothic literature, mood, theme, and literary/informational synthesis.
- Unit 3 increases interpretive and argumentative complexity through identity, exclusion, dystopia, comparison, and argument.
- Unit 4 provides sustained drama study, adaptation, literary criticism, and literary argument.
- Unit 5 moves to epic, archetype, research, transformation, and cross-media comparison.
- Unit 6 culminates in speculative literature, informational analysis, media credibility, narrative craft, and public presentation.

The progression is concrete-to-complex: short foundational practice precedes extended texts; supported whole-class selections precede peer/independent application; analysis precedes writing; and unit performance evidence follows instruction.

## Workload and unit size

| MLA unit | Proposed lessons | Workload finding |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 8 | Existing foundation; retain current lesson count |
| 2 | 9 | Gothic sequence, cross-genre work, poetry, and narrative/presentation synthesis |
| 3 | 10 | Extended novella plus paired dystopian/identity texts and argument |
| 4 | 12 | Five-act drama, classical source/adaptation, criticism, poetry, and media require protected time |
| 5 | 11 | Epic study, adaptation, procedural text, research, transformation texts, and publication |
| 6 | 9 | Speculative texts, media analysis, narrative writing, independent synthesis, and presentation |

The full course contains **59 lessons**: 8 existing Unit 1 lessons and 51 proposed lessons in Units 2-6. Units are intentionally unequal because the texts and performance demands differ.

## Required assessment sequence for Units 2-6

Every proposed unit begins with a pretest, continues through its instructional lessons, uses clustered quizzes at natural instructional breaks when appropriate, and ends with a synthesis lesson containing the unit assessment. The unit assessment is not a detached activity after the final lesson.

## Content classifications

- **Core:** unit introductions, selected whole-class texts, required paired texts, designated performance tasks, and unit assessment evidence.
- **Supporting:** mentor texts, concept vocabulary, selection summaries, close-read guides, language/craft work, and test practice.
- **Intervention:** accessible text, Language Lab, Foundational Skills Center, ELL Support, and Access Points Resources.
- **Enrichment:** Independent Learning choices, Digital Library, optional projects, and selected Appendix B readings.
- **Diagnostic:** MLA pretests, Savvas Benchmark Tests, and Preview/Practice resources.

## Architecture risks

- The current repository's skill-domain Units 2-6 do not correspond to Savvas themes.
- A thematic map can obscure standards coverage unless the crosswalk explicitly spirals standards across units.
- Extended works (*The Metamorphosis*, *Romeo and Juliet*, *The Odyssey*) require realistic pacing and cannot be compressed to match smaller units.
- Savvas licensing requires linking/assignment directions rather than copying proprietary texts.
