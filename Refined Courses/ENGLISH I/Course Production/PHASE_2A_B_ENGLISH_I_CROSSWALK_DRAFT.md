# Phase 2A-B: English I Crosswalk Draft

## Executive Summary

This document creates the English I Crosswalk Draft using the approved MLA English I Standard Inventory as the source of record. MLA standard numbers, standard titles, and Florida benchmark codes are preserved exactly as approved.

This draft assigns each MLA English I standard to an approved benchmark cluster, one primary unit, spiral reinforcement units, mastery/support status, and readiness-alignment tags. It does not validate the placements, create lesson-level mappings, create lessons, create assessments, create HTML files, create quizzes, or create GIFT files.

The crosswalk is intended to support future validation and mapping work after this draft has been reviewed.

## Potential Concerns

- Unit 2 and Unit 3 carry the highest benchmark density because literary analysis, informational text, rhetoric, and argument are concentrated there by the approved blueprint.
- Unit 5 must not become isolated grammar and vocabulary work; its standards need authentic reading and writing contexts.
- Unit 6 must not become the first meaningful experience with discussion, presentation, or digital communication.
- Support standards may be underestimated because they are not all primary mastery destinations, even though they require direct instruction and repeated evidence.

## Potential Overloads

| Unit | Potential Overload |
|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Foundational skills could expand too broadly if advanced literary and rhetorical work is introduced too early. |
| Unit 2 | Literary analysis has six reading standards plus narrative writing support. |
| Unit 3 | Informational text, rhetoric, argument evaluation, and argument writing create the densest readiness load. |
| Unit 4 | Research, expository writing, and collaborative publication can overload the unit if treated as separate unrelated products. |
| Unit 5 | Language, vocabulary, style, and revision may become too broad unless tied to authentic student writing. |
| Unit 6 | Presentation and digital communication can become too steep if earlier units do not provide smaller communication practice. |

## Potential Gaps

- Paired-text work needs to occur before Unit 6 so synthesis is not delayed until the final unit.
- Revision needs smaller practice cycles before Unit 5.
- Vocabulary-in-context must appear throughout the course, not only in Unit 5.
- Discussion and oral explanation should begin before Unit 6.
- Research readiness depends on earlier paraphrase, evidence, and informational-text work.

## Items Requiring Validation

- Confirm whether each standard has the correct single primary unit assignment.
- Confirm whether spiral units are sufficient without overextending the scope of each unit.
- Confirm whether `MLA.ENG1.L.01` should remain primary in Unit 5 while receiving early Unit 1 reinforcement.
- Confirm whether `MLA.ENG1.C.03` should remain primary in Unit 4 because it is tied to publication-ready source-based writing.
- Confirm whether Unit 2 and Unit 3 benchmark loads remain manageable within the approved 8-lesson structure.
- Confirm whether all support standards have enough recurring evidence opportunities in future mapping.
