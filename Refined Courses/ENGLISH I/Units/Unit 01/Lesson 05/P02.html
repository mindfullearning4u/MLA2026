P02 Notebook Task - Part 1

Notebook Title: Convention Check for Short Responses

Vocabulary

Complete sentence means a sentence has a subject, a verb, and a complete thought.

Fragment means an incomplete sentence.

Run-on means two complete thoughts are joined without correct punctuation.

Convention means a rule for grammar, punctuation, capitalization, or spelling.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Read the response aloud in your head.

Step 2: Ask whether the sentence has a subject and a verb.

Step 3: Check whether the sentence begins with a capital letter and ends with punctuation.

Step 4: Make sure the sentence still answers the question accurately after you revise it.

Read the Passage

The school added a water station near the track. Runners could refill bottles during practice instead of leaving the field.

Notebook Directions

  1. Copy this draft response: the water station helped runners refill bottles during practice
  2. Identify what convention is missing.
  3. Rewrite the response as a complete, correctly punctuated sentence.

Model Start

Problem: The sentence does not begin with a capital letter and does not end with punctuation.

Revision: The water station helped runners refill bottles during practice.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Standard English conventions in short responses

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.L.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It shows how to approach the lesson skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Read the sentence or paragraph as a reader would. Mark any place where the meaning, connection, or sentence control is unclear.
  2. Revise for one purpose at a time: clarity, organization, transitions, word choice, or conventions. Trying to fix everything at once can hide the real problem.
  3. Keep the writer's original meaning unless the task asks for a new idea. Revision improves communication; it should not replace the message with a different one.
  4. Reread the revised version to confirm that it is clearer, more precise, and appropriate for the audience.
