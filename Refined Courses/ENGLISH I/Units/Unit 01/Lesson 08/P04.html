P04 Worked Example: Complete Unit 1 Synthesis Model

This example shows the full thinking process. Watch how the response moves from reading to final answer without treating Unit 1 skills as separate tasks.

Model Passage

Passage: The cafeteria added signs that explained how to sort lunch waste into recycling, compost, and trash bins. The first signs used only pictures, but many students still placed items in the wrong bins. The updated signs included pictures, short labels, and one example for each bin. After the update, fewer items were misplaced because students could connect each item with a clear category.

Question: Why were the updated signs more effective than the first signs?

Example 1: Read and Analyze

Thinking: The passage compares two versions of cafeteria signs. The first version had pictures only. The updated version had pictures, labels, and examples. The result was that fewer items were placed in the wrong bins.

Why this matters: Before writing, the reader has to understand the passage's main idea: clear information helped students make better choices.

Example 2: Paraphrase, Evidence, and Vocabulary

Paraphrase: The updated signs worked better because they gave students clearer information about where each lunch item belonged.

Relevant evidence: The passage says the updated signs included pictures, short labels, and one example for each bin.

Academic vocabulary: The words effective, category, evidence, and precise fit this response because the task asks why one version worked better.

Why this matters: The paraphrase gives the main idea, the evidence proves it, and the vocabulary makes the explanation more exact.

Example 3: Construct, Check, and Finalize

Draft response: The updated signs were more effective because they gave students precise information about each waste category. The passage explains that the new signs included pictures, short labels, and one example for each bin, which helped students connect items with the correct category.

Convention check: The response uses complete sentences, capital letters at the beginning of sentences, correct punctuation, and clear word choice.

Final answer: The updated signs were more effective because they gave students precise information about each waste category. The passage explains that the new signs included pictures, short labels, and one example for each bin, which helped students connect items with the correct category.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The signs were better because they helped.

This answer is too vague. It does not paraphrase the main idea, does not include evidence, does not use academic vocabulary, and does not explain why the updated signs worked.

Correct: The updated signs were more effective because they used precise labels and examples to help students place items in the correct waste category.

This answer is stronger because it combines meaning, evidence, vocabulary, and reasoning in one clear sentence.
