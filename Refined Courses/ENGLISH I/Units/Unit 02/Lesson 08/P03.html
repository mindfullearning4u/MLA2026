Notebook Task Part 2: Connect Reading Analysis to Narrative Craft

In this task, you will use the same passage from Part 1 and think like both a reader and a writer.

Review Passage

Passage: "The Lantern Path"

Mara stood at the edge of the narrow path while the village lanterns trembled in the wind behind her. The forest ahead was dark, but not silent. Leaves scraped together like whispered warnings. In her pocket, the silver key felt heavier than it had that morning.

Her brother had said the old tower was only a story, something adults used to frighten children away from the cliffs. Mara had laughed then. Now, with the storm clouds folding over the moon, she wished she had listened more carefully.

She took one step forward. The lantern light slipped from her shoulders, and the path swallowed her shadow.

Step-by-Step Notebook Directions

  1. Write one sentence explaining the narrator's point of view.
  2. Identify one image or phrase that builds suspense.
  3. Explain how the author uses that image or phrase to affect the reader.
  4. Write two original sentences that could continue the scene. Use the same point of view and mood.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author makes it scary because the forest is scary.

This is too general. It names a feeling, but it does not explain which words create that feeling or how the author's craft affects the reader.

Corrected Thinking

Correct: The phrase "leaves scraped together like whispered warnings" creates suspense because the simile makes the forest seem alive and threatening.

This is stronger because it names the exact phrase, identifies the technique, and explains the effect on the reader.
