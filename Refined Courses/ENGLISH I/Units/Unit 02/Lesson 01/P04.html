P04 Worked Example

Study how each step moves from reading to analysis.

Worked Example 1: Identify the Element

Passage: Nia opened the old recipe book and found her grandmother's notes written in blue ink. The kitchen smelled like cinnamon, just as it had every winter when her grandmother was alive.

Question: Which literary element is most important?

Thinking: The setting is important because the kitchen and recipe book connect Nia to a memory. This is not just a place; it carries emotional meaning.

Worked Example 2: Add Evidence

Evidence: "her grandmother's notes written in blue ink" and "smelled like cinnamon."

Thinking: These details show that the setting brings back Nia's relationship with her grandmother. The evidence proves the kitchen matters emotionally.

Worked Example 3: Explain the Layer of Meaning

Complete response: The author uses setting to show Nia's connection to her grandmother. The recipe book, blue ink, and cinnamon smell make the kitchen feel like a place of memory, not just a room where Nia cooks.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: The story has a kitchen, a book, and cinnamon.

This lists details but does not explain what they show.

Correct: The kitchen setting helps reveal Nia's memory of her grandmother because the recipe book and cinnamon smell connect the present moment to the past.

This answer identifies the element, uses evidence, and explains meaning.
