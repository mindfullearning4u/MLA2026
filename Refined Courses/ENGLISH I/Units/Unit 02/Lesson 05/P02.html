Set up your notebook to track epic poetry traits.

Notebook Title

Epic Poetry: Character, Structure, and Theme

Vocabulary

Epic poetry: a long narrative poem about heroic actions, major conflicts, and important values.

Epic hero: a central figure whose courage, choices, or flaws affect many people.

Invocation: an opening request for help or inspiration, often found in traditional epics.

Elevated language: formal or serious language used to make events feel important.

In medias res: beginning in the middle of the action.

Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Paraphrase what happens in plain words.

Step 2: Identify the hero or central figure.

Step 3: Notice the structure: opening call, journey, challenge, choice, or return.

Step 4: Explain what value or theme the heroic action develops.

Notebook Task

Passage: Sing of Mira, keeper of the mountain gate, who stood while the winter armies crossed the ridge. Her shield was split, yet she lifted it again so the village fires could burn behind her.

Write three notebook lines: paraphrase, epic trait, and theme suggested by Mira's action.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It shows how to approach the lesson skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Start with what the text actually says before interpreting it. Identify the character, event, word choice, or structure the question is asking about.
  2. Ask what the author chose and why that choice matters. Literary analysis explains the effect of choices, not just what happened.
  3. Use evidence from the passage to support the interpretation. A strong answer connects a detail to theme, mood, perspective, or craft.
  4. Check that your explanation goes beyond identification. The goal is to explain how the detail shapes meaning for the reader.
