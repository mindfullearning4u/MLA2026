P04 Worked Example

Study how to analyze epic poetry without getting lost in formal language.

Worked Example 1: Paraphrase

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Passage: Tell of the runner who crossed the ash-covered plain, carrying the final message while thunder shook the hills.

Plain meaning: A runner travels through a dangerous place to deliver an important message.

Worked Example 2: Identify Epic Traits

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

The passage uses elevated language, a dangerous journey, and a central figure whose mission matters beyond personal comfort.

Worked Example 3: Explain Theme

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

The runner's journey develops a theme about duty because the character continues through danger to deliver a message others need.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incorrect: Epic poetry is confusing because the words are old.

This is a reaction, not analysis.

Correct: The elevated language makes the runner's mission feel serious and heroic.

This explains how style affects meaning.
