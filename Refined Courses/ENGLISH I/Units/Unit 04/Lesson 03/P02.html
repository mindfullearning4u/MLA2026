P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Research Notes and Accurate Paraphrase

Vocabulary

  • Note-taking: recording useful information from a source in a shortened form.
  • Paraphrase: restating source information in your own words without changing the meaning.
  • Source finding: an important piece of information learned from a source.
  • Synthesis: combining related ideas from more than one source.

Direct Teaching

Good research notes are short, accurate, and useful. Do not copy a whole paragraph just because it seems important. First, ask: What does this source help me understand about my research question?

Source sentence: Students with regular access to quiet study spaces reported fewer distractions while completing homework.

Useful note: quiet study access may reduce homework distractions.

The note keeps the meaning but shortens the wording.

Notebook Directions

Research question: How can quiet study spaces affect homework completion?

Source excerpt: A student survey found that students who had access to quiet spaces after school were more likely to report finishing homework before evening activities.

  1. Write one useful note from the source.
  2. Write one accurate paraphrase of the source excerpt.
  3. Explain how the information helps answer the research question.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Read the vocabulary and make sure you can say each term in your own words.
  2. Step 2: Study the direct teaching before you try the task.
  3. Step 3: Follow the model and notice how the thinking moves from evidence to explanation.
  4. Step 4: Complete the practice one part at a time instead of skipping ahead.
  5. Step 5: Check your answer against the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: Work with one sentence, term, or example at a time. Standard path: complete the full task and explain your reasoning. Accelerated path: add a second example, compare choices, or explain why a weaker answer does not meet the standard.
