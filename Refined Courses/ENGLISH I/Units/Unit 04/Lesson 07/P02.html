P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Publication-Ready Source-Based Communication

Vocabulary

  • Publication-ready: revised, accurate, organized, and prepared for an audience.
  • Source accuracy: source information is stated correctly and not exaggerated.
  • Audience fit: the content, tone, and format match the intended readers.
  • Checklist: a tool used to review required qualities before final submission.

Direct Teaching

A publication-ready product should pass four checks: accuracy, organization, clarity, and audience fit. If a research summary has strong evidence but confusing headings, it is not ready. If the headings are attractive but the evidence is inaccurate, it is not ready.

Source-based communication must protect both the research and the reader.

Notebook Directions

Draft product: A student-facing digital summary says, "Quiet rooms are perfect and prove students always finish homework." It has no source label and one heading: "Stuff."

  1. Identify two publication problems.
  2. Write one revision for source accuracy.
  3. Write one better heading for the student audience.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Publication-ready source-based communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.W.03, MLA.ENG1.C.03

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It shows how to approach the lesson skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the purpose of the task before choosing information. A research or publication task must answer a real question for a real audience.
  2. Separate information into useful categories: what the source says, why it matters, and how it will help the final product.
  3. Check whether each piece of information is reliable, relevant, and clear enough for the audience. If it does not help the audience understand the topic, revise or replace it.
  4. Use your own words when explaining source information, and keep the meaning accurate so the final communication remains trustworthy.
