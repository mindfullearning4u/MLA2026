P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Now use a publication-readiness checklist.

Checklist

  • The research question is clear.
  • Source findings are accurate.
  • Headings guide the reader.
  • Tone matches the audience.
  • Sentences follow standard English.

Notebook Directions

Use the checklist to evaluate this draft sentence: "The survey said online calendars helped some students miss fewer due dates, so families may want to check calendar access at home."

  1. Write two checklist items this sentence meets.
  2. Write one improvement that would make the product more publication-ready.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: It looks nice, so it is ready.

Appearance alone does not prove source accuracy, organization, or audience fit.

Correct Thinking

Correct: It is ready only if the research is accurate, the writing is clear, and the format helps the intended audience.

This checks both content and communication.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Publication-ready source-based communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.W.03, MLA.ENG1.C.03

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Identify the purpose of the task before choosing information. A research or publication task must answer a real question for a real audience.
  2. Separate information into useful categories: what the source says, why it matters, and how it will help the final product.
  3. Check whether each piece of information is reliable, relevant, and clear enough for the audience. If it does not help the audience understand the topic, revise or replace it.
  4. Use your own words when explaining source information, and keep the meaning accurate so the final communication remains trustworthy.
