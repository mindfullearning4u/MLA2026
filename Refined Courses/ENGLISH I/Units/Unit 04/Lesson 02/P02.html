P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Evaluating Source Quality

Vocabulary

  • Reliable: trustworthy enough to use because it is accurate, current, and supported.
  • Credible: created by a person or organization with knowledge or authority on the topic.
  • Valid: logically connected to the research question and supported by evidence.
  • Bias: a strong preference or one-sided viewpoint that may affect fairness.

Direct Teaching

When you evaluate a source, do not ask only, "Does this source agree with me?" Ask better questions:

  1. Who created the source?
  2. What evidence does it use?
  3. When was it published or updated?
  4. Does it answer my research question?
  5. Does the language sound fair or one-sided?

A source can sound confident and still be weak. Strong research depends on checking quality before using information.

Notebook Directions

Research question: How can school-issued laptops affect students' ability to complete assignments at home?

Copy the research question. Then make a four-row chart with these labels: reliable, credible, valid, biased.

For each label, write one question you could ask to evaluate a source about school-issued laptops.
