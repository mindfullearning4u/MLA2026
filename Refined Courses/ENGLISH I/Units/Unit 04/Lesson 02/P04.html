P04 Worked Example

Use source quality questions before deciding what to use.

Example 1: Research Question

Question: How does access to a quiet study space affect students' ability to complete homework?

This question needs sources about study spaces, student work habits, and homework completion.

Example 2: Evaluate a Source

Source: A recent education article summarizes a survey of 600 high school students about homework environments.

Evaluation: This source is useful because it is recent, has a large student sample, and connects directly to the question.

Example 3: Notice Bias

Source: "Only careless students need study spaces because responsible students can work anywhere."

This language is biased because it insults one group and makes a broad claim without evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: I should use the source that agrees with my opinion.

Agreement does not prove quality. A source must still be reliable, valid, credible, and fair.

Correct Approach

Correct: I should use the source that best answers my question with current, supported, and fair information.

This approach keeps the research focused on evidence.
