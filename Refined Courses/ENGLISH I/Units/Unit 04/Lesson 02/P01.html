P01 Lesson Overview: Lesson 2: Source Reliability, Validity, Credibility, and Bias

Lesson Title

Lesson 2: Source Reliability, Validity, Credibility, and Bias

Standards Covered in This Lesson

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG1.RS.01 - Conduct Reliable Research.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG1.R.06 - Evaluate Central Idea Support; MLA.ENG1.R.08 - Evaluate Opposing Arguments; MLA.ENG1.V.03 - Determine Word Meaning.

What You Will Learn

  • How to decide whether a source is reliable, valid, and credible.
  • How to notice bias or one-sided language in a source.
  • How source quality affects research answers.

Why This Matters

Research is only as strong as the sources behind it. A weak source can lead to a weak answer, even if the writing sounds confident. Strong researchers check who created the source, what evidence it uses, whether the information fits the question, and whether the source seems fair.

What You Will Do

  • Evaluate short source descriptions.
  • Identify signs of reliability, credibility, validity, and bias.
  • Choose sources that best fit a research question.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • You can explain why a source is or is not reliable.
  • You can choose a source that matches a research question.
  • You can identify biased or unsupported source language.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

You are learning how to judge whether a source deserves to be used in research and whether it actually helps answer your question.
