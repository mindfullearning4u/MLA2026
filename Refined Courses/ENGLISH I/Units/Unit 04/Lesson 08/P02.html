Notebook Title: Unit 04 Synthesis: Research and Expository Writing

Vocabulary: research question, reliable source, valid source, credibility, bias, paraphrase, synthesis, expository writing, transition, audience, publication-ready

This notebook task asks you to use several skills from Lessons 1-7 at the same time. Do not answer by naming one isolated skill. Read the passage or scenario, decide what the task requires, and connect evidence, vocabulary, and reasoning in one response.

Integrated Text

Passage / Scenario: Research Topic: How school start times affect student alertness. Source A is a district health office summary written by a nurse and based on attendance and sleep surveys from local high schools. Source B is a personal blog post by a student who says later start times helped them feel less tired. Source C is an article from a children's hospital explaining how sleep affects adolescent attention and memory. A strong research response would use the local survey data and medical explanation while treating the blog as a limited personal example.

Synthesis Focus: Evaluate the sources as a connected research set. Decide which sources are reliable, which are limited, and how the information could be synthesized into an expository explanation.

Notebook Directions

  1. Paraphrase the main situation or central idea in your own words.
  2. Identify at least two unit skills needed to respond well.
  3. Select one relevant detail as evidence or support.
  4. Use at least two unit vocabulary terms correctly.
  5. Explain how the evidence connects to your interpretation, claim, explanation, or communication purpose.

Model Start

A strong response begins by naming the task in your own words, then connecting the most important evidence to the unit skill being used. The response should sound like one complete explanation, not a list of unrelated answers.
