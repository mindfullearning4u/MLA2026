P04 Worked Example

This worked example models complete Unit 04 synthesis thinking. Notice how the response moves from reading and analysis to evidence, vocabulary, reasoning, conventions, and final response construction.

Example 1: Read and Identify the Task

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Modeled thinking: First, I identify what the task asks me to combine. I do not answer with one isolated skill. I look for the unit concepts that work together.

Reasoning: The task requires evidence, vocabulary, reasoning, and clear communication, so my response must connect them.

Example 2: Apply Unit Skills Together

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

A strong synthesis response begins with a focused research question: How do school start times affect student alertness? Source A is useful because it provides local survey data, while Source C is useful because it explains the science of sleep and attention. Source B may provide a student perspective, but it should not carry the explanation by itself. A clear expository paragraph would explain the issue, connect the local evidence to medical information, use transitions, and maintain a neutral tone for a school audience.

Reasoning: This model explains the thinking process instead of dropping evidence into a sentence without explanation.

Example 3: Check the Final Response

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Modeled thinking: Before submitting, I check that the response uses unit vocabulary accurately, includes relevant support, explains why the support matters, and follows standard English conventions.

Final check: The response should be clear enough for a reader to understand without hearing a teacher explain it aloud.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The answer gives a conclusion but does not show the evidence or reasoning behind it.

Correct: The answer states the conclusion, supports it with relevant evidence, explains the reasoning, and uses unit vocabulary accurately.

Feedback: If a reader cannot see how you moved from the text or scenario to your answer, the synthesis is not complete yet.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.
