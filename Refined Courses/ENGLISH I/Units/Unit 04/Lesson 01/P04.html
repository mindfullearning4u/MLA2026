P04 Worked Example

Watch how a broad topic becomes a focused inquiry path.

Example 1: Start with a Topic

Topic: school gardens.

This topic is useful, but it is too broad. A researcher would not know whether to study cost, health, science classes, food, or student behavior.

Example 2: Create a Question

Research question: How can a school garden support science learning for ninth-grade students?

This question is focused because it names the subject, the purpose, and the student group.

Example 3: Refine with Findings

Early finding: Many sources discuss school gardens as hands-on science tools.

Refined question: How can hands-on school garden activities help ninth-grade students understand biology concepts?

The refined question better matches what the sources are actually explaining.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Are school gardens good?

This is too vague. It does not explain good for whom, in what way, or for what purpose.

Correct Approach

Correct: How can a school garden support hands-on biology learning for ninth-grade students?

This question gives research a clear path.
