P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now you will refine questions so they match what research can actually answer.

Step-by-Step Explanation

A strong research question may need revision. If the question is too broad, you will find too much information. If it is too narrow, you may not find enough reliable information. If it is based only on opinion, sources may not be able to answer it clearly.

Too broad: Why is technology important?

Too narrow: What color is the computer cart in room 104?

Researchable: How can school-issued laptops affect students' ability to complete assignments at home?

Notebook Directions

Copy each weak research question. Then revise it into a stronger research question.

  1. Why are phones bad?
  2. Is homework annoying?
  3. What is the best club?

After each revision, write one sentence explaining why your revised question is more researchable.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Why is homework terrible?

This question is loaded with opinion. It pushes the researcher toward one answer before research begins.

Correct Thinking

Correct: How does the amount of homework affect ninth-grade students' time for sleep and extracurricular activities?

This question is focused, researchable, and open to evidence.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Research questions and inquiry focus

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.RS.01

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Identify the purpose of the task before choosing information. A research or publication task must answer a real question for a real audience.
  2. Separate information into useful categories: what the source says, why it matters, and how it will help the final product.
  3. Check whether each piece of information is reliable, relevant, and clear enough for the audience. If it does not help the audience understand the topic, revise or replace it.
  4. Use your own words when explaining source information, and keep the meaning accurate so the final communication remains trustworthy.
