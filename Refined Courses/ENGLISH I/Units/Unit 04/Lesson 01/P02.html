P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook Title: Research Questions and Inquiry Focus

Vocabulary

  • Topic: the general subject you want to learn about.
  • Research question: a focused question that guides what you search for and read.
  • Inquiry: the process of asking questions, finding information, and using that information to build understanding.
  • Scope: how broad or narrow your research question is.

Direct Teaching

A topic is not the same thing as a research question. A topic names the subject. A research question tells you exactly what you are trying to find out about that subject.

Broad topic: school lunches.

Focused research question: How would adding a grab-and-go breakfast option affect students who arrive early?

The focused question is stronger because it gives the researcher a direction. You know what to search for: breakfast access, early arrival, student hunger, and school meal options.

Notebook Directions

In your notebook, make a two-column chart. Label the left column Broad Topic and the right column Focused Research Question.

Write these three broad topics in the left column:

  1. School technology
  2. Homework
  3. Student clubs

For each topic, write one focused research question in the right column. Your question should be specific enough that a source could help answer it.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Research questions and inquiry focus

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.RS.01

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It shows how to approach the lesson skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Identify the purpose of the task before choosing information. A research or publication task must answer a real question for a real audience.
  2. Separate information into useful categories: what the source says, why it matters, and how it will help the final product.
  3. Check whether each piece of information is reliable, relevant, and clear enough for the audience. If it does not help the audience understand the topic, revise or replace it.
  4. Use your own words when explaining source information, and keep the meaning accurate so the final communication remains trustworthy.
