P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Feedback and Revision for Clarity

Vocabulary

  • Feedback: information that helps a writer improve a draft.
  • Revision: changing writing to make meaning clearer or stronger.
  • Clarity: how easy writing is to understand.
  • Actionable: specific enough to help a writer make a change.

Direct Teaching

Read the whole draft before revising. First decide what the reader may not understand. Then choose a revision that solves that exact problem.

A strong revision keeps the original meaning but makes the sentence or paragraph clearer.

Notebook Task

Draft: The evidence is good and it helps the claim. In your notebook, write one helpful feedback comment, one revision, and one sentence explaining why the revision is clearer.

Label your response: problem, revision, explanation.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Feedback and revision for clarity

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.W.04

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It shows how to approach the lesson skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Read the sentence or paragraph as a reader would. Mark any place where the meaning, connection, or sentence control is unclear.
  2. Revise for one purpose at a time: clarity, organization, transitions, word choice, or conventions. Trying to fix everything at once can hide the real problem.
  3. Keep the writer's original meaning unless the task asks for a new idea. Revision improves communication; it should not replace the message with a different one.
  4. Reread the revised version to confirm that it is clearer, more precise, and appropriate for the audience.
