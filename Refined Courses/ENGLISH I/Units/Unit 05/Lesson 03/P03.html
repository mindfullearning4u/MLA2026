P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Apply connotation to tone and mood.

Short Text

Text: The hallway was silent. A pale light slipped under the classroom door, and the empty lockers seemed to watch as Mara walked past.

The denotation tells what is there: hallway, light, lockers. The connotation creates a tense mood.

Notebook Directions

  1. Choose two words from the text that create mood.
  2. Write each word's denotation.
  3. Explain the connotation of each word.
  4. Write one sentence explaining the mood created by the word choices.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Saying a word creates mood just because it is in the sentence.

You need to explain the feeling the word creates and connect that feeling to the text.

Correct Approach

Correct: The word slipped suggests quiet, sneaky movement, which helps create a tense mood.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Context, connotation, denotation, and tone

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.V.03

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Read the whole sentence before deciding what a word means. Context often gives clues before and after the word.
  2. Separate literal meaning from feeling or tone. Denotation is the basic meaning; connotation is the feeling a word creates.
  3. Use word parts, roots, prefixes, suffixes, and related words when context alone is not enough.
  4. Choose the meaning that fits the passage, not just the first dictionary definition. Precise vocabulary must match the text and purpose.
