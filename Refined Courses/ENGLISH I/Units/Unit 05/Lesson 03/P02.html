P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Denotation, Connotation, and Context

Vocabulary

  • Denotation: the direct dictionary meaning of a word.
  • Connotation: the feeling or association a word carries.
  • Tone: the author's attitude toward the subject.
  • Mood: the feeling created for the reader.

Direct Teaching

Start with the denotation, then ask what feeling the word adds. For example, thin is mostly neutral, slender sounds positive, and scrawny sounds negative. All can describe size, but they do not create the same tone.

When reading, do not stop at "What does the word mean?" Also ask, "Why this word instead of another word?"

Notebook Task

Copy the word set: home, house, shack, residence.

  1. Write the shared denotation.
  2. Label each word as positive, neutral, formal, or negative in feeling.
  3. Write one sentence explaining which word would create the most negative tone and why.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Read the vocabulary and make sure you can say each term in your own words.
  2. Step 2: Study the direct teaching before you try the task.
  3. Step 3: Follow the model and notice how the thinking moves from evidence to explanation.
  4. Step 4: Complete the practice one part at a time instead of skipping ahead.
  5. Step 5: Check your answer against the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: Work with one sentence, term, or example at a time. Standard path: complete the full task and explain your reasoning. Accelerated path: add a second example, compare choices, or explain why a weaker answer does not meet the standard.
