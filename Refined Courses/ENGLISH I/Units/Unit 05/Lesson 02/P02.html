P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Word Families and Meaning Clues

Vocabulary

  • Etymology: the history or origin of a word.
  • Derivation: a word formed from another word or word part.
  • Root: the main meaning part of a word.
  • Prefix: a word part added to the beginning of a word.
  • Suffix: a word part added to the end of a word.

Direct Teaching

A word family is a group of words connected by a shared root or origin. The words may not mean exactly the same thing, but they usually keep a related idea.

Example: The words predict, prediction, and predictable are related. They all connect to saying or knowing something before it happens.

When you see an unfamiliar word, use this sequence: look for a known word part, think of related words, test the meaning in the sentence, and adjust if the sentence does not make sense.

Notebook Task

Copy this word family chart into your notebook and complete the meaning notes.

  • construct - to build
  • construction - the process of building
  • reconstruct - to build again
  • constructive - helpful in building or improving something

Write one sentence explaining what meaning connects all four words.

Step-by-Step Teaching Support

Lesson focus: Etymology, derivations, and word families

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.V.02

Use this sequence before you complete the notebook task. It shows how to approach the lesson skill without guessing or skipping steps.

  1. Read the whole sentence before deciding what a word means. Context often gives clues before and after the word.
  2. Separate literal meaning from feeling or tone. Denotation is the basic meaning; connotation is the feeling a word creates.
  3. Use word parts, roots, prefixes, suffixes, and related words when context alone is not enough.
  4. Choose the meaning that fits the passage, not just the first dictionary definition. Precise vocabulary must match the text and purpose.
