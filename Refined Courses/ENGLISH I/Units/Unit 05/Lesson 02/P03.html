P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Apply word-family thinking to academic vocabulary in context.

Sentence Set

Sentence 1: The speaker's conclusion was predictable because each reason led clearly to the final claim.

Sentence 2: The student made a prediction before reading the ending of the article.

Sentence 3: The data helped the class predict which study method might work best.

Notebook Directions

  1. Write the word family: predict, prediction, predictable.
  2. Write the shared meaning idea in your own words.
  3. Choose one sentence and paraphrase it without using the word from the word family.
  4. Explain how the related words helped you understand the sentence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Assuming every related word has the exact same part of speech.

Predict is a verb, prediction is a noun, and predictable is an adjective. They are related, but they work differently in sentences.

Correct Approach

Correct: Use the shared meaning idea, then check how the word functions in the sentence.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Etymology, derivations, and word families

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.V.02

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Read the whole sentence before deciding what a word means. Context often gives clues before and after the word.
  2. Separate literal meaning from feeling or tone. Denotation is the basic meaning; connotation is the feeling a word creates.
  3. Use word parts, roots, prefixes, suffixes, and related words when context alone is not enough.
  4. Choose the meaning that fits the passage, not just the first dictionary definition. Precise vocabulary must match the text and purpose.
