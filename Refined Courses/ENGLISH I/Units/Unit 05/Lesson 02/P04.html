P04 Worked Example

Use word parts and word families step by step.

Worked Example 1: Derivation

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Word: revision

Related words: revise, revised, revising

Step 1: Notice the shared base vis, connected to seeing or looking.

Step 2: Connect the word family to writing. To revise means to look again at writing and improve it.

Meaning: Revision is the process of looking again and improving a piece of writing.

Worked Example 2: Prefix

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Word: reconstruct

Known word: construct means build.

Prefix: re- means again.

Meaning: Reconstruct means build again.

Worked Example 3: Suffix

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Word: credible

Related word: credit can connect to belief or trust.

Suffix: -ible can mean able to be.

Meaning: Credible means able to be believed or trusted.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Because construct means build, constructive must mean a building.

This ignores the suffix and the sentence. Constructive feedback means feedback that helps improve or build a better draft.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct Approach

Correct: Use the root meaning, the suffix, and the sentence context together.

The word-family clue starts your thinking, but the sentence confirms the meaning.
