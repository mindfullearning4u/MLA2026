P04 Worked Example

Watch how conventions are corrected in context.

Example 1: Subject-Verb Agreement

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Draft: The report explain how tutoring helps students.

Step 1: Find the subject: The report.

Step 2: Match the verb to the singular subject.

Revision: The report explains how tutoring helps students.

Example 2: Sentence Boundary

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Draft: Students used the media center it was quiet before school.

Step 1: Notice that two complete ideas are run together.

Step 2: Separate or connect the ideas correctly.

Revision: Students used the media center because it was quiet before school.

Example 3: Pronoun Clarity

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Draft: The survey and article were useful because it explained student concerns.

Step 1: Ask what it means. The reader cannot tell.

Revision: The survey and article were useful because both sources explained student concerns.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The sources shows that students needs quiet places.

This sentence has two agreement errors. Sources is plural, so it needs show. Students is plural, so it needs need.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct Approach

Correct: The sources show that students need quiet places.

Both verbs now agree with their subjects, and the meaning is clear.
