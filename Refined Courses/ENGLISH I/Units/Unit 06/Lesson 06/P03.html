P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now revise an integrated communication plan for precision and polish.

Deeper Application

Weak slide note: Stuff about sources.

Weak speaker note: Sources are important and you should do stuff with them because that helps.

This pair is weak because the word choice is vague. The slide and speaker note do not guide the audience.

Improved slide note: Check source credibility before using evidence.

Improved speaker note: Before using a source, check who created it, when it was published, and why it was written. These checks help you avoid weak or misleading evidence.

Notebook Task

Weak slide note: Better writing stuff.

Weak speaker note: Feedback helps you fix things and make it better.

In your notebook, write:

  1. A revised slide note with precise wording.
  2. A revised speaker note with 2-3 clear sentences.
  3. One sentence explaining how your revision is more publication-ready.

Self-Check

Your slide note should be concise. Your speaker note should explain. Both should use clear, audience-friendly language.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving a general answer without naming the specific evidence, skill, or reader effect.

Correct: Name the exact word, detail, structure, or choice from the lesson and explain how it supports meaning, clarity, or the reader.

Why this matters: English I mastery requires more than a correct-sounding answer. You need to show the thinking that connects evidence to explanation.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Integrated oral and digital communication

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.C.01, MLA.ENG1.C.02

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Start by identifying the audience and purpose. What the audience needs determines what information should be included.
  2. Organize ideas so listeners or viewers can follow the message without rereading. Use a clear beginning, connected evidence, and a focused ending.
  3. Choose words, visuals, and examples that support the message instead of distracting from it.
  4. Check that the final communication is accurate, respectful, and easy to understand for the intended audience.
