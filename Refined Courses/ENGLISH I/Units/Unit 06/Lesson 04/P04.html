P04 Worked Example

Watch how a digital presentation becomes coherent through slide order.

Example 1: Choose a Structure

Purpose: Explain how students can improve assignment planning.

Step 1: Decide what kind of organization fits the purpose.

Best structure: Problem-solution, because the presentation can show the problem of missed deadlines and then present planning as a solution.

Example 2: Build a Slide Sequence

Slide 1: Perspective: Weekly planning helps students manage assignments before deadlines become stressful.

Slide 2: Problem: Assignments are easy to forget when students rely only on memory.

Slide 3: Solution: A weekly planning routine makes tasks visible.

Slide 4: How it works: List due dates, break large tasks into steps, and review the plan daily.

Slide 5: Final takeaway: Planning turns deadlines into manageable steps.

Example 3: Check Coherence

Question: Does each slide connect to the perspective?

Answer: Yes. Each slide explains either the problem, the solution, the steps, or the final takeaway. No slide is only decorative or unrelated.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Slide 1: title. Slide 2: funny picture. Slide 3: planner colors. Slide 4: homework is hard. Slide 5: thanks.

This sequence does not build coherent ideas. The slides may be related to school, but they do not develop one clear perspective.

Correct Approach

Correct: Slide 1: perspective. Slide 2: problem. Slide 3: solution. Slide 4: steps. Slide 5: final takeaway.

This sequence is coherent because each slide has a clear job and supports the presentation's purpose.
