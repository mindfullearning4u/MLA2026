P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now connect slide order to text structure.

Deeper Application

A digital presentation can use a structure you already know from reading and writing.

  • Problem-solution: explain a problem, then explain a solution.
  • Cause-effect: explain why something happens and what results from it.
  • Compare-contrast: show similarities and differences.
  • Sequence: show steps in order.

Choosing a structure helps you decide what each slide should do.

Notebook Task

Presentation purpose: Explain how students can avoid losing track of long-term assignments.

In your notebook, answer:

  1. Which structure fits best: problem-solution, cause-effect, compare-contrast, or sequence?
  2. Why does that structure fit the purpose?
  3. Write a five-slide outline using that structure.

Make sure the slide order helps the audience move from the problem or idea toward the final takeaway.

Self-Check

If the slides could be shuffled into any order without changing the meaning, the sequence probably needs stronger organization.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving a general answer without naming the specific evidence, skill, or reader effect.

Correct: Name the exact word, detail, structure, or choice from the lesson and explain how it supports meaning, clarity, or the reader.

Why this matters: English I mastery requires more than a correct-sounding answer. You need to show the thinking that connects evidence to explanation.
