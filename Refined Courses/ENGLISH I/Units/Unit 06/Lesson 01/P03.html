P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now apply the same planning steps to a more specific audience.

Deeper Application

A speaker's purpose may stay the same, but the audience changes how the message should be framed.

Topic: Students should have access to quiet study spaces after school.

Audience A: classmates

Audience B: school administrators

If you speak to classmates, you might use student experiences and a friendly tone. If you speak to administrators, you may need a more formal tone, clearer reasons, and specific benefits for the school.

Notebook Task

In your notebook, create a two-column chart.

  • Column 1: Classmate Audience
  • Column 2: Administrator Audience

For each audience, write:

  1. One purpose statement.
  2. One perspective statement.
  3. Two language choices that fit the audience.

Use complete sentences for your perspective statements. Use short phrases for the language choices.

Self-Check

Before you move on, check that your two perspective statements are not identical. The main idea can be similar, but the wording should show that you are thinking about the audience.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving a general answer without naming the specific evidence, skill, or reader effect.

Correct: Name the exact word, detail, structure, or choice from the lesson and explain how it supports meaning, clarity, or the reader.

Why this matters: English I mastery requires more than a correct-sounding answer. You need to show the thinking that connects evidence to explanation.
