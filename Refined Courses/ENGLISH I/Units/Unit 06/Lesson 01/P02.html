P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Use your notebook to build the planning habits speakers use before presenting.

Notebook Title

Purpose, Audience, and Perspective in Oral Communication

Vocabulary

  • Purpose: the reason a speaker communicates.
  • Audience: the people who will listen to the presentation.
  • Perspective: the speaker's clear point of view or position about the topic.
  • Tone: the speaker's attitude, shown through word choice and delivery.

Direct Teaching

Before you write or give an oral presentation, pause and answer three questions in order.

Step 1: What is my purpose? A speaker might inform, explain, persuade, reflect, or recommend. If the purpose is unclear, the presentation may become a list of disconnected details.

Step 2: Who is my audience? A presentation for classmates sounds different from a presentation for school leaders, family members, or community volunteers. The core idea may stay the same, but the examples, vocabulary, and tone should fit the listeners.

Step 3: What perspective should be clear? A perspective is more than a topic. The topic might be school clubs. A clear perspective would be school clubs help ninth-grade students build confidence and connection.

Notebook Directions

Scenario: A student is preparing a one-minute presentation for classmates about why reading before bed can help students relax.

In your notebook, write three labeled lines:

  1. Purpose: Write what the speaker is trying to do.
  2. Audience: Write who will listen.
  3. Perspective: Write one complete sentence that states the speaker's clear point of view.

Model: Perspective: Reading before bed can help students relax because it gives the brain a quiet routine before sleep.
