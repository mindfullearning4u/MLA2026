P04 Worked Example

Watch how a focused speaker keeps one perspective at the center.

Worked Example 1: Build a Focused Perspective

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Topic: classroom discussion.

Step 1: Avoid a broad topic sentence.

Step 2: State a clear perspective.

Focused perspective: Classroom discussion helps students understand complex texts because students hear different interpretations and must explain their own thinking.

Worked Example 2: Choose Details That Fit

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Detail A: Students can ask questions when a passage is confusing.

Detail B: Students can compare interpretations with classmates.

Detail C: The classroom has windows and posters.

Decision: Details A and B fit the perspective. Detail C may be true, but it does not support the point about discussion and text understanding.

Worked Example 3: Respond to an Opposing View

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Opposing view: Some students prefer quiet independent reading.

Focused response: Independent reading is valuable, but discussion gives students a chance to test their interpretations and hear ideas they may have missed.

The response is fair, but it returns to the speaker's main point.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Classroom discussion is useful. Also, the room should have more posters, students like fun activities, and some books are long.

This presentation loses coherent focus. The extra details may be related to school, but they do not all support the same perspective.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct Approach

Correct: Classroom discussion helps students understand complex texts because students can ask questions, compare interpretations, and explain their thinking aloud.

This version keeps every detail connected to the main perspective.
