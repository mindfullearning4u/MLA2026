P04 Worked Example

This worked example models complete Unit 06 synthesis thinking. Notice how the response moves from reading and analysis to evidence, vocabulary, reasoning, conventions, and final response construction.

Example 1: Read and Identify the Task

Modeled thinking: First, I identify what the task asks me to combine. I do not answer with one isolated skill. I look for the unit concepts that work together.

Reasoning: The task requires evidence, vocabulary, reasoning, and clear communication, so my response must connect them.

Example 2: Apply Unit Skills Together

A strong presentation plan begins with a clear perspective: specific repeated kindness messages can improve school climate. The presenter should use survey evidence to support the claim, organize the speech into problem, evidence, and proposed solution, and create slides with short phrases rather than full paragraphs. The digital slides should support the oral explanation, not replace it. Before presenting, the speaker should revise for clarity, precise vocabulary, and standard English conventions.

Reasoning: This model explains the thinking process instead of dropping evidence into a sentence without explanation.

Example 3: Check the Final Response

Modeled thinking: Before submitting, I check that the response uses unit vocabulary accurately, includes relevant support, explains why the support matters, and follows standard English conventions.

Final check: The response should be clear enough for a reader to understand without hearing a teacher explain it aloud.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The answer gives a conclusion but does not show the evidence or reasoning behind it.

Correct: The answer states the conclusion, supports it with relevant evidence, explains the reasoning, and uses unit vocabulary accurately.

Feedback: If a reader cannot see how you moved from the text or scenario to your answer, the synthesis is not complete yet.
