P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Use your notebook to connect spoken claims with credible evidence.

Notebook Title

Organizing Oral Information with Credible Evidence

Vocabulary

  • Claim: the main point the speaker wants the audience to accept or understand.
  • Reason: an explanation that supports the claim.
  • Evidence: facts, examples, details, or source information that support a reason.
  • Credible: trustworthy and reliable.
  • Organization: the order of ideas in a presentation.

Direct Teaching

A presentation needs more than a claim. It needs a path the listener can follow.

Step 1: State the claim or perspective.

Step 2: Give one reason that supports it.

Step 3: Add credible evidence that proves or explains the reason.

Step 4: Explain the evidence so the audience understands why it matters.

If you skip Step 4, the evidence may sound like a random fact instead of support for your point.

Notebook Directions

Presentation claim: Students benefit from having a quiet place to study after school.

Evidence: A school library sign-in sheet shows that the study area is used most often during the week before major tests.

In your notebook, write:

  1. One reason that connects to the claim.
  2. One sentence explaining why the evidence is credible.
  3. One sentence explaining how the evidence supports the claim.

Learner Support Path

Remedial support: reread the vocabulary, work with one sentence or detail at a time, and use the model before answering independently.

Standard path: complete the full task, cite or name the evidence, and explain how it proves your answer.

Accelerated path: add a second example, compare two possible answers, or explain why a weaker response does not meet mastery.
