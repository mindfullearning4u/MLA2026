Lesson 2: Organizing Oral Information with Credible Evidence

Standards Covered

Primary Standard: MLA.ENG1.C.01 - Present Ideas Orally.

Support Standards: MLA.ENG1.R.06 - Evaluate Central Idea Support; MLA.ENG1.RS.01 - Conduct Reliable Research; MLA.ENG1.W.02 - Write Arguments with Evidence.

What You Will Learn

  • How to organize oral information so listeners can follow it.
  • How credible evidence strengthens a spoken point.
  • How to connect a claim, reason, and evidence in a short presentation.
  • How to avoid listing facts without explaining why they matter.

Why This Matters

Listeners cannot reread a spoken presentation the way they can reread an essay. A speaker has to organize information clearly and explain evidence as it is presented. Credible evidence helps the audience trust the speaker, but the speaker still has to connect that evidence back to the main point.

What You Will Do

  • Study short oral presentation plans.
  • Identify claims, reasons, and evidence.
  • Decide whether evidence is credible and useful.
  • Organize evidence into a clear spoken sequence.

How You Will Show Mastery

  • You can choose evidence that supports a presentation's main point.
  • You can organize oral information in a logical order.
  • You can explain why a piece of evidence is credible and useful.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

You are learning how to present information orally by organizing your ideas and using evidence that listeners can trust.

Ask TOR for Help

Ask TOR for help if you cannot tell whether evidence is credible, whether it supports the point, or where it should go in the presentation.
