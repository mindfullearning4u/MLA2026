P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now organize evidence into a spoken sequence.

Deeper Application

Good evidence can still be confusing if it appears in the wrong order. Oral presentations usually work best when the speaker moves from general to specific.

Useful order: claim, reason, evidence, explanation, transition.

The transition matters because it tells the listener where the speaker is going next.

Notebook Task

Claim: Schools should encourage students to use planners or digital calendars.

Evidence options:

  • A student survey reports that many students forget due dates when assignments are only announced aloud.
  • A teacher explains that written reminders help students plan long-term assignments.
  • A student says planners are boring.

In your notebook, write:

  1. The two strongest evidence choices.
  2. One reason each choice is useful.
  3. A four-sentence oral sequence using claim, reason, evidence, and explanation.

Self-Check

Make sure your evidence does not just sound interesting. It must directly support the claim and help the audience understand your point.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Giving a general answer without naming the specific evidence, skill, or reader effect.

Correct: Name the exact word, detail, structure, or choice from the lesson and explain how it supports meaning, clarity, or the reader.

Why this matters: English I mastery requires more than a correct-sounding answer. You need to show the thinking that connects evidence to explanation.
