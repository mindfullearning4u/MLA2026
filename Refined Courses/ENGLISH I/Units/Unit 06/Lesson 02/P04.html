P04 Worked Example

Watch how evidence becomes part of a clear oral presentation plan.

Example 1: Start with a Claim

Topic: Quiet study spaces.

Step 1: Turn the topic into a claim.

Claim: Students should have access to quiet study spaces after school because focused work time can help them complete assignments more effectively.

Example 2: Choose Credible Evidence

Evidence option: The media center attendance log shows that the study area is busiest during project and testing weeks.

Step 1: Ask whether the evidence is connected to the claim.

Step 2: Ask whether the evidence comes from a reliable source.

Decision: This evidence is useful because it comes from a school record and shows that students already use quiet study space when academic demand increases.

Example 3: Organize for Speaking

Step 1: State the claim.

Step 2: Give the reason.

Step 3: Present the evidence.

Step 4: Explain the connection.

Oral sequence: Students should have access to quiet study spaces after school. A quiet space gives students a place to focus before they go home. The media center attendance log shows that the study area is busiest during project and testing weeks. This pattern suggests that students use quiet space when they need focused academic support.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Quiet study spaces are good. A lot of people like them. The media center is open sometimes.

This version is vague. It has no clear evidence, no source, and no explanation of how the details support the claim.

Correct Approach

Correct: Quiet study spaces support student work because they give students a focused place to complete assignments. A school media center attendance log shows that students use the space most during project and testing weeks, which suggests that the space helps when academic demands increase.

This version uses a claim, credible evidence, and explanation in a logical order.
