P04 Worked Example

Watch how a presentation plan becomes ready for an audience.

Worked Example 1: Check the Perspective

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

Weak: My topic is presentations.

Ready: A clear presentation uses concise slides and speaker notes that explain the message.

The ready version gives a perspective that can guide the full presentation.

Worked Example 2: Check Slide and Speaker Note

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

Slide note: Concise slides guide the audience.

Speaker note: A concise slide gives listeners a main idea to follow, while the speaker explains details and examples aloud.

The two parts work together instead of repeating each other.

Worked Example 3: Check Final Polish

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Ready check: The slide uses standard English, precise vocabulary, and consistent formatting. The speaker note explains the slide clearly for the audience.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: A presentation is ready because all slides have words on them.

Having words on slides is not enough. The slides must support a clear message, use credible evidence, and work with the speaker's explanation.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct Approach

Correct: A presentation is ready when the perspective, evidence, slide order, speaker notes, and polish all support the same audience-focused message.

This checks the whole communication product, not just one piece.
