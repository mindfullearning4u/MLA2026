P04 Worked Example

This example shows how to move from rhetorical choice to effect.

Example Passage

Imagine trying to study while the room beside you echoes with noise. Imagine reading the same paragraph five times because voices keep pulling your attention away. A quiet study room would give students a place to focus, think, and finish their work with less stress.

Example 1: Identify the Choice

Question: What rhetorical choice is repeated?

Thinking: The author begins two sentences with "Imagine."

Answer: The author uses repetition and direct address by asking readers to imagine the problem.

Example 2: Explain the Effect

Effect: The repeated "Imagine" makes readers picture the frustration of trying to study in a noisy place.

This effect matters because it makes the need for a quiet study room feel more personal and urgent.

Example 3: Connect to Purpose

The author's purpose is to persuade readers that a quiet study room is needed. Repetition and direct address support that purpose by helping readers experience the problem before the solution is proposed.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author repeats a word, so the text is persuasive.

This is incomplete. You must explain how the repetition affects the reader and supports the purpose.

Correct Approach

Correct: The repeated "Imagine" pulls readers into the problem, making the need for a quiet study room feel personal and reasonable.

This answer explains both the technique and its effect.
