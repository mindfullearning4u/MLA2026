P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Author Purpose and Appeals

Vocabulary

  • Author purpose: the reason an author writes a text.
  • Ethos: an appeal to credibility, trust, or expertise.
  • Pathos: an appeal to emotion.
  • Logos: an appeal to logic, facts, evidence, or reasoning.

Direct Teaching

To identify author purpose, first ask what the author wants the reader to understand, believe, or do. A text that explains how a system works may be written to inform. A text that asks readers to change behavior may be written to persuade or call readers to act.

Next, look for appeals. Facts and statistics often show logos. Expert statements and reliable experience often show ethos. Words that create concern, hope, pride, fear, or sympathy often show pathos.

A strong answer connects the appeal to the purpose: The author uses this appeal because it helps the reader...

Notebook Directions

Passage: A school nurse explains, "Drinking water throughout the day helps students stay alert. Even mild dehydration can cause headaches or tiredness, so students should bring a refillable bottle and use water fountains between classes."

  1. Write the author's purpose in one sentence.
  2. Identify one appeal used in the passage.
  3. Copy the words that show the appeal.
  4. Explain how the appeal supports the purpose.

Model

The author's purpose is to persuade students to drink water during the school day. The passage uses ethos because a school nurse gives the advice, and it uses logos because it explains that mild dehydration can cause headaches or tiredness.

Step-by-Step Learning Path

  1. Step 1: Read the vocabulary and make sure you can say each term in your own words.
  2. Step 2: Study the direct teaching before you try the task.
  3. Step 3: Follow the model and notice how the thinking moves from evidence to explanation.
  4. Step 4: Complete the practice one part at a time instead of skipping ahead.
  5. Step 5: Check your answer against the mastery criteria before submitting.

Remedial support: Work with one sentence, term, or example at a time. Standard path: complete the full task and explain your reasoning. Accelerated path: add a second example, compare choices, or explain why a weaker answer does not meet the standard.
