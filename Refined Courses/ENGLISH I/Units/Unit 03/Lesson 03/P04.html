P04 Worked Example

This example shows how to connect author purpose to rhetorical appeal.

Example Passage

Our school should add more shaded seating near the bus loop. On hot afternoons, students often wait outside for twenty minutes or more. The county health department warns that long exposure to high heat can cause dizziness or dehydration. Adding shade sails and benches would make dismissal safer and more comfortable.

Example 1: Identify Purpose

Question: Why did the author write this?

Thinking: The author says the school "should add" shaded seating. That means the author wants a change.

Answer: The author's purpose is to persuade the school to add shaded seating near the bus loop.

Example 2: Identify Appeal

Evidence: "The county health department warns that long exposure to high heat can cause dizziness or dehydration."

Appeal: This is ethos because the health department is a credible source. It also supports logos because it gives a reason based on safety.

Example 3: Explain Effect

The author uses a credible health warning to make the request seem reasonable and urgent. The appeal supports the purpose because readers may be more likely to agree when the concern is connected to student safety.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The author uses pathos because the passage is about students.

This is weak because it names an appeal without explaining which words create emotion or why that appeal supports the purpose.

Correct Approach

Correct: The author uses ethos by citing the county health department and logos by explaining the safety risk of heat exposure.

This answer connects the appeal to specific evidence.
