P04 Worked Example

This example shows how to build a complete argument paragraph.

Prompt and Evidence

Prompt: Should the school extend library hours?

Evidence: The library counted 180 students using the space during the final half hour before closing last month.

Counterclaim: Some may argue that later hours require extra staffing.

Example 1: Claim and Evidence

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify the specific lesson skill being modeled. Step 2: examine the words, evidence, sentence, or passage detail that matters most. Step 3: explain why the final response or revision demonstrates the skill accurately.

The school should extend library hours because many students are still using the library near closing time.

Example 2: Reasoning

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: identify what changed from the first example. Step 2: connect the example to the lesson focus. Step 3: explain how the response uses clearer reasoning, stronger evidence, or more precise language.

The count of 180 students during the final half hour shows demand near closing, so extended hours could give students more time to complete work.

Example 3: Counterclaim and Rebuttal

Step-by-step explanation: Step 1: combine the lesson skill with evidence or revision choices. Step 2: check that the explanation stays focused on the assigned standard. Step 3: confirm that the final response is clear, accurate, and written in standard English.

Some may argue that later hours require extra staffing. However, the school could begin with one extended day each week and use sign-in data to decide whether the schedule is worth continuing.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Anyone against later hours just hates libraries.

This attacks people instead of addressing the counterclaim. It also damages academic tone.

Teachable Explanation: A strong answer does more than choose the correct wording. It names the specific issue, corrects it, and explains why the correction fits the lesson skill, audience, evidence, or meaning.

Correct Approach

Correct: Some may worry about staffing, but a one-day trial would let the school test the idea before making a larger commitment.

This is respectful, practical, and connected to the issue.
