P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now place argument parts in a logical order.

Direct Teaching

A short argument paragraph usually works best in this order: claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, rebuttal. The order helps readers understand your position before they see the opposing view.

Tone matters in every part. Avoid phrases like "Only foolish people think..." Use fair language instead.

Notebook Directions

Argument parts: Claim: The school should add more outdoor seating. Evidence: A survey found that 64 percent of students would use more outdoor tables. Reasoning: This shows that additional seating would meet a real student need. Counterclaim: Some may argue that tables cost too much. Rebuttal: The school could start with a small number of tables and evaluate use before adding more.

  1. Number the parts in the strongest order.
  2. Underline the counterclaim.
  3. Circle the rebuttal.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: People who disagree are wrong and do not care about students.

This is disrespectful and unsupported. It attacks people instead of answering the opposing idea.

Correct Thinking

Correct: Some may worry about cost, but the school could begin with a small number of tables and review student use before buying more.

This response is respectful and practical.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Argument writing: counterclaims, organization, and tone

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.W.02

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Find the exact claim before judging the argument. A claim is the position the writer wants the reader to accept.
  2. Connect each reason or piece of evidence back to the claim. Evidence is useful only when it actually proves or explains the claim.
  3. Look for a counterclaim or opposing point of view. Strong argument work does not ignore disagreement; it responds to it with reasoning.
  4. Check tone and organization. A strong argument sounds fair, uses clear transitions, and explains how the evidence supports the position.
