P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now connect text structure to text features.

Direct Teaching

A structure tells how the ideas are arranged. A feature helps the reader use or understand those ideas. For example, a problem-solution article might use a heading called "The Problem" followed by another heading called "Possible Solutions." Those headings do not add new facts, but they guide the reader through the organization.

To explain a feature, do not only name it. Explain what it helps the reader do.

Notebook Directions

Passage: Heading: Why Sleep Matters Teens who get enough sleep often have better focus during the school day. Lack of sleep can make it harder to remember information, manage emotions, and complete tasks. Sidebar: Quick Tip Keeping the same bedtime on school nights can help the body settle into a routine.

  1. Identify one text feature in the passage.
  2. Explain what that feature helps the reader notice.
  3. Identify the structure used in the main paragraph.
  4. Write one sentence explaining how the feature and structure work together.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The sidebar is there because the author put it there.

This is not enough. It names the feature but does not explain how the feature helps the reader.

Correct Thinking

Correct: The sidebar gives a quick practical tip, so it helps the reader apply the information about sleep to daily life.

This is stronger because it explains the purpose of the feature.

Deeper Learning Support

Lesson focus: Informational text structure and features

Mapped standard focus: MLA.ENG1.R.05

Use this second-pass routine to check the quality of your thinking before you submit your notebook work.

  1. Identify the main point or purpose before analyzing details. Informational texts are organized to help the reader understand an idea.
  2. Notice how the structure works. Headings, order, examples, comparisons, and cause-effect relationships all guide meaning.
  3. Connect evidence to the author's purpose. A detail matters when it helps explain, persuade, clarify, or emphasize the central idea.
  4. Evaluate the effect of the author's choices. Explain how the structure, evidence, or rhetoric changes the reader's understanding.
