P04 Worked Example

This example shows how to identify structure and explain a text feature step by step.

Example Passage

Heading: Reducing Plastic Waste

Plastic bags are lightweight and convenient, but they can blow into waterways and harm wildlife. Some communities respond by encouraging reusable bags, adding recycling bins near stores, and teaching shoppers how to reduce single-use plastics. These solutions do not remove every plastic item, but they can reduce the amount that reaches streets and rivers.

Caption under photo: Volunteers collect plastic waste near a river after a weekend market.

Example 1: Identify the Structure

Question: How is the paragraph organized?

Thinking: The first sentence names a problem: plastic bags can harm waterways and wildlife. The next sentences describe responses such as reusable bags, recycling bins, and teaching shoppers.

Answer: The structure is problem and solution.

Example 2: Explain the Feature

Feature: Caption under photo.

Thinking: The caption connects the topic to a real example of plastic waste near a river.

Answer: The caption helps readers see why the problem matters by connecting plastic waste to a real cleanup location.

Example 3: Put It Together

The passage uses a problem-solution structure because it first explains that plastic bags can harm waterways and wildlife, then lists ways communities respond. The caption supports the structure by showing a real example of the problem near a river.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The structure is about plastic, and the caption is a picture.

This is too vague. It names the topic and feature but does not explain organization or purpose.

Correct Approach

Correct: The structure is problem and solution because the passage explains the plastic waste problem and then gives community responses. The caption adds a real-world example of the problem.

This answer explains both the organization and the feature's purpose.
