P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Informational Text Structure and Features

Vocabulary

  • Informational text: writing that explains facts, ideas, events, or processes.
  • Text structure: the way information is organized.
  • Text feature: a visual or print tool such as a heading, chart, caption, sidebar, or bold word.
  • Purpose: the reason a structure or feature is used.

Direct Teaching

Informational texts are not organized randomly. Authors choose a structure because that structure helps readers understand the topic. A passage about steps in a process may use sequence. A passage explaining why something happened may use cause and effect. A passage naming a challenge and explaining how people respond may use problem and solution.

Text features work like signposts. A heading tells you the section topic. A caption explains an image. A chart organizes details so you can compare them quickly. Bold words signal terms that matter.

When you read informational text, ask two questions: How is this organized? and What feature helps me understand it?

Notebook Directions

Passage: Community gardens can turn empty lots into useful spaces. First, neighbors choose a location with sunlight and safe soil. Next, they plan beds, gather tools, and assign regular care. Over time, the garden can provide fresh food, outdoor learning, and a place for neighbors to work together.

  1. Write the text structure you notice.
  2. Copy two words or phrases that helped you identify the structure.
  3. Write one sentence explaining how the structure helps the reader understand the information.

Model

The structure is sequence because the passage uses time-order words such as "First," "Next," and "Over time." This structure helps readers understand the steps for creating a community garden.
