P04 Worked Example

This example shows how to move from central idea to evidence quality.

Example Passage

After-school tutoring can help students build confidence as well as skills. In a small tutoring group, students can ask questions they might avoid in a crowded classroom. Tutors can also notice patterns in mistakes and give targeted practice. Over time, students may feel more prepared because they have repeated support instead of one-time correction.

Example 1: Identify the Central Idea

Question: What is the central idea?

Thinking: The passage is not just about tutoring. It explains that tutoring can build both confidence and skills.

Answer: After-school tutoring can help students improve skills and feel more confident.

Example 2: Find Strong Support

Detail: "Tutors can also notice patterns in mistakes and give targeted practice."

Why it is strong: This detail is specific and directly explains how tutoring improves skills.

Example 3: Explain Evidence Quality

The detail about targeted practice is strong evidence because it does more than repeat the central idea. It explains a specific way tutoring helps students improve: tutors notice patterns and give practice that fits the student's needs.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: The best evidence is that tutoring happens after school because that is in the topic.

This detail is related, but it does not explain how tutoring builds confidence or skills.

Correct Approach

Correct: The best evidence explains that tutors notice mistake patterns and give targeted practice because it directly supports skill improvement.

This answer judges evidence by how well it supports the central idea.
