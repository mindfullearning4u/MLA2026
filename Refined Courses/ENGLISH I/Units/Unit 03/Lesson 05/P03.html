P03 Notebook Task Part 2

Now compare two opposing claims.

Direct Teaching

Opposing arguments take different positions on the same issue. To compare them fairly, do not choose the one you like first. Check which argument has clearer reasons and stronger evidence.

An argument is stronger when its evidence is relevant, specific, and connected to the claim.

Notebook Directions

Argument A: The cafeteria should add more vegetarian options because students have different dietary needs. A student survey found that 42 percent of respondents wanted at least one vegetarian entree each day.

Argument B: The cafeteria should not change anything because the current menu is fine and people should just eat what is available.

  1. Write the claim in Argument A.
  2. Write the claim in Argument B.
  3. Identify which argument has stronger evidence.
  4. Explain why that evidence is stronger.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: Argument B is stronger because it sounds confident.

Confidence is not the same as evidence. A strong argument must support its claim with relevant facts or examples.

Correct Thinking

Correct: Argument A is stronger because it gives a survey result showing that many students want vegetarian options.

This answer judges the argument by evidence, not by tone or personal preference.
