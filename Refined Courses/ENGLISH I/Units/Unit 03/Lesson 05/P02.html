P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Claims, Reasons, Evidence, and Validity

Vocabulary

  • Claim: the position or point an argument tries to prove.
  • Reason: an explanation for why the claim should be accepted.
  • Evidence: facts, examples, data, or expert information that support a reason.
  • Valid reasoning: reasoning that makes sense and is supported by relevant evidence.

Direct Teaching

An argument has parts. The claim tells what the author believes. The reason explains why. The evidence proves or supports the reason. If a claim has no evidence, it may be only an opinion. If evidence does not connect to the claim, the argument is weak.

To evaluate an argument, ask: What is the claim? What reason is given? What evidence supports it? Does the evidence actually prove the point?

Notebook Directions

Passage: The school should add a second bike rack near the gym. Many students arrive for morning practice by bicycle, and the current rack often fills before first period. A second rack would reduce crowding and keep bikes from blocking the walkway.

  1. Write the claim.
  2. Write one reason.
  3. Write one piece of evidence.
  4. Explain whether the evidence supports the claim.

Model

The claim is that the school should add a second bike rack near the gym. The evidence that the current rack often fills before first period supports the claim because it shows a real need for more bike space.
