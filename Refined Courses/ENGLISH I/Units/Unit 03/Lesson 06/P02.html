P02 Notebook Task Part 1

Notebook title: Argument Writing: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning

Vocabulary

  • Argument: writing that supports a position with reasons and evidence.
  • Claim: the position the writer argues for.
  • Evidence: information that supports the claim.
  • Reasoning: the explanation of how evidence proves the claim.

Direct Teaching

A claim should be clear and arguable. "School lunches exist" is not a strong claim because it is only a fact. "The school should add more fresh fruit choices at lunch" is stronger because a writer can support it with evidence and reasoning.

Evidence should fit the claim. Reasoning explains the connection. Without reasoning, the reader may not understand why the evidence matters.

Notebook Directions

Prompt: Should the school add more fresh fruit choices at lunch?

Evidence: A student survey found that 58 percent of students would choose fruit more often if more fresh options were available.

  1. Write a clear claim that answers the prompt.
  2. Copy the evidence.
  3. Write one reasoning sentence explaining how the evidence supports the claim.

Model

The school should add more fresh fruit choices at lunch. The survey result supports this claim because it shows that many students would choose fruit more often if the cafeteria offered more fresh options.
