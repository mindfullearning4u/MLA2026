# Phase 3B: English I Unit-Level Mapping Validation

## Executive Summary

This validation reviews the English I Unit-Level Mapping against the approved English I Crosswalk Draft, Mapping Framework, Blueprint, and MLA English Instructional Design Standards.

Final decision: **APPROVED WITH REVISIONS**

The unit-level mapping is complete, coherent, and aligned to the approved blueprint. All 24 MLA English I standards appear exactly once in the unit-level mapping table. Primary unit assignments are generally logical and reflect the approved crosswalk. Support and spiral placements are mostly sufficient.

However, the mapping should not move into lesson-level mapping without targeted revisions or controls in Phase 3A-B-2. The major concerns are workload density in Unit 2 and Unit 3, multi-step instructional load in Unit 4, and the need to make earlier communication reinforcement visible before Unit 6.

This phase validates unit-level mapping only. It does not create lesson mappings, lessons, assessments, HTML files, quizzes, or GIFT files.

### Unit 1 Validation

Primary standards:

- `MLA.ENG1.R.10` Paraphrase Grade-Level Texts
- `MLA.ENG1.V.01` Integrate Academic Vocabulary

Validation finding: Unit 1 placement is logical.

These standards establish the course foundation for reading comprehension, evidence-based response, vocabulary access, and later research/writing tasks. Placement is consistent with the approved blueprint and provides prerequisite readiness for Units 2-6.

Concern: Unit 1 must also support evidence and conventions early even though those are not primary standards here.

### Unit 2 Validation

Primary standards:

- `MLA.ENG1.R.01` Analyze Literary Elements
- `MLA.ENG1.R.02` Analyze Theme Development
- `MLA.ENG1.R.03` Analyze Narrator Perspective
- `MLA.ENG1.R.04` Analyze Epic Poetry
- `MLA.ENG1.R.09` Explain Figurative Language and Mood
- `MLA.ENG1.R.11` Compare Literary Adaptations
- `MLA.ENG1.W.01` Write Narrative Texts

Validation finding: Primary placement is logical but workload-sensitive.

The standards belong in Unit 2 because they are literary analysis and narrative craft standards. Prerequisite skills from Unit 1 are available if paraphrase, academic vocabulary, and evidence-based response are taught well.

Concern: Seven primary mastery standards in one unit create a significant risk for self-paced learners unless future lesson mapping combines natural pairs and protects instructional depth.

### Unit 3 Validation

Primary standards:

- `MLA.ENG1.R.05` Analyze Text Structure
- `MLA.ENG1.R.06` Evaluate Central Idea Support
- `MLA.ENG1.R.07` Analyze Author Purpose and Rhetoric
- `MLA.ENG1.R.08` Evaluate Opposing Arguments
- `MLA.ENG1.R.12` Explain Rhetorical Choices
- `MLA.ENG1.W.02` Write Arguments with Evidence

Validation finding: Primary placement is logical but workload-sensitive.

These standards belong in Unit 3 because they form the approved informational text, rhetoric, and argument cluster. Unit 1 provides reading/evidence foundations, and Unit 2 supports transfer of author craft and perspective into nonfiction.

Concern: Argument writing must not be mapped before students have received sufficient instruction in argument reading, claims, evidence, reasoning, central idea support, and rhetorical analysis.

### Unit 4 Validation

Primary standards:

- `MLA.ENG1.W.03` Write Expository Texts
- `MLA.ENG1.RS.01` Conduct Reliable Research
- `MLA.ENG1.C.03` Publish Collaborative Writing

Validation finding: Primary placement is logical.

Research, expository writing, and collaborative publication naturally belong together as source-based communication. Unit 4 follows informational and argument work, so students should have prerequisite experience with evidence, source ideas, and organization.

Concern: The count is only three standards, but each is multi-step. Future lesson mapping must treat Unit 4 as a connected research-to-expository-writing process, not three isolated products.

### Unit 6 Validation

Primary standards:

- `MLA.ENG1.C.01` Present Ideas Orally
- `MLA.ENG1.C.02` Create Digital Presentations

Validation finding: Primary placement is logical.

Unit 6 is correctly positioned as the culminating synthesis, presentation, and digital communication unit. The two primary standards are manageable if earlier units provide discussion, evidence explanation, audience awareness, and source-based communication exposure.

## Workload Validation

### Unit 2 Workload

Validation finding: **Concern identified**

Unit 2 is logically mapped but heavy. Seven primary standards, all mastery-oriented, must fit within the 8-lesson structure. This is realistic only if future lesson mapping uses natural pairings and shared texts.

Standards that naturally group:

- Literary elements and theme development.
- Narrator perspective with figurative language and mood.
- Epic/classical material with adaptation analysis where text selection supports it.
- Narrative writing with point of view and literary craft.

Workload risk:

- Too many literary modes or genres could fragment the unit.
- Epic poetry and adaptation may require more background support than expected.
- Narrative writing could crowd out analysis if treated as a separate extended writing arc.

### Unit 3 Workload

Validation finding: **Concern identified**

Unit 3 is logically mapped but heavy. Six primary mastery standards must support informational reading, rhetorical analysis, argument evaluation, and argument writing.

Standards that naturally group:

- Text structure and central idea support.
- Author purpose and rhetorical choices.
- Opposing argument evaluation and argument writing preparation.

Workload risk:

- Students may be asked to write arguments before they can evaluate arguments.
- Rhetorical analysis may become vocabulary-only if not modeled deeply.
- Central idea support, claim support, evidence credibility, and reasoning may blur without explicit instruction.

### Unit 4 Workload

Validation finding: **Concern identified**

Unit 4 appears lighter by standard count but is instructionally demanding. Research, expository writing, and collaborative publication require multi-step self-paced instruction.

Standards naturally group as one source-based communication process:

- Research question and source evaluation.
- Source notes and synthesis.
- Expository organization and explanation.
- Collaborative publication and audience awareness.

Workload risk:

- Treating research, expository writing, and publication as separate major products would overload the unit.
- Research cannot be assigned without explicit instruction in question refinement, source reliability, validity, credibility, bias, note-taking, and synthesis.

### Workload Validation Result

The unit-level mapping is workable but should be approved only with revisions/controls for Phase 3A-B-2. Unit 2, Unit 3, and Unit 4 require careful lesson-level distribution before curriculum development can begin.

## Self-Paced Learner Validation

### Remedial Learners

Remedial learners can succeed under this mapping only if Phase 3A-B-2 preserves instructional depth.

Required protections:

- Explicit instruction before application.
- Multiple models and worked examples.
- Common mistake examples.
- Guided practice before independent practice.
- Scaffolded text reading.
- Sentence frames and response models for writing.
- Clear mastery criteria.

Concern: Unit 2 and Unit 3 are especially risky for remedial learners because both require abstract reasoning and evidence-based explanation.

### Standard Learners

Standard learners can succeed if the mapping maintains clear sequencing.

Required protections:

- Prerequisite skills must appear before advanced application.
- Reading analysis must precede extended writing.
- Research must be broken into procedural steps.
- Revision must be modeled, not merely assigned.

Concern: Unit 3 may become too compressed if argument writing is mapped before sufficient argument analysis.

### Accelerated Learners

Accelerated learners can succeed if the mapping allows efficient movement without skipping required mastery evidence.

Required protections:

- Clear targets and mastery criteria.
- Avoidance of unnecessary repetition after mastery is demonstrated.
- Opportunities for deeper synthesis where appropriate.
- No removal of required instruction or evidence.

Concern: Accelerated pacing should not be used to justify overloaded lessons.

### Self-Paced Validation Result

The mapping can support remedial, standard, and accelerated learners if future lesson-level mapping follows MLA instructional design standards. Without careful Phase 3A-B-2 controls, Unit 2 and Unit 3 could become too dense for independent mastery.

## Final Decision

**APPROVED WITH REVISIONS**

The English I Unit-Level Mapping is approved to proceed to Phase 3A-B-2, but only with the following required revisions/controls:

1. Phase 3A-B-2 must explicitly protect Unit 2 from literary-analysis overload.
2. Phase 3A-B-2 must explicitly protect Unit 3 from rhetoric/argument overload.
3. Phase 3A-B-2 must treat Unit 4 as a connected research-to-expository-writing process.
4. Phase 3A-B-2 must make communication reinforcement visible before Unit 6.
5. Phase 3A-B-2 must keep Lesson 8 from becoming a catch-all for untaught standards.
6. Phase 3A-B-2 must preserve MLA self-paced design requirements for remedial, standard, and accelerated learners.
