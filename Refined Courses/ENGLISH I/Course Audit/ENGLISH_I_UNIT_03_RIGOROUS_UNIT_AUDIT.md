## Unit Identity

Unit 3: Informational Text, Rhetoric, and Argument

Unit 3 correctly follows the approved sequence:

1. Informational text structure and features
2. Central idea support and evidence quality
3. Author purpose and rhetorical appeals
4. Rhetorical choices and effect
5. Opposing arguments, claims, and validity
6. Argument writing: claims, evidence, and reasoning
7. Argument writing: counterclaims, organization, and tone
8. Putting It All Together: Unit 3 synthesis

The unit preserves the approved progression from informational reading into rhetoric, then argument evaluation, then argument writing.

## Content and Rigor Findings

Strengths:

- The unit sequence is pedagogically sound: informational structure precedes central idea, purpose, rhetoric, validity, and argument writing.
- Argument writing is appropriately split across Lessons 6 and 7 to avoid overload.
- Lessons remain self-paced and provide explicit modeling for independent learners.
- Assessments are aligned to the exact lesson or unit standard set.
- Passages and prompts are embedded directly in questions, assignments, and assessment items.
- Feedback is detailed enough to support remedial, standard, and accelerated learners.

Risks:

- Unit 3 is naturally high-rigor because it combines informational reading, rhetoric, evaluation, and argument writing.
- This risk is controlled by the approved lesson sequence and by dividing argument writing into two lessons.
