# ENGLISH I Course Overview

---

## Course Overview

English I develops students' ability to read complex literary and informational texts, analyze how authors create meaning, write clearly for different purposes, communicate effectively, and use academic vocabulary and standard English conventions. Students examine literary elements, universal themes, narrator perspective, foundational and literary texts including epic poetry, informational text structures, central ideas, rhetoric, argument, research, revision, oral presentation, digital communication, and vocabulary in context.

The course is organized around competency-based progression. Students move through the course by demonstrating mastery of required instructional components, with emphasis on close reading of grade-level complex texts, text-based analysis, evidence-based writing, academic discussion, language development, research, revision, and presentation.

Instruction and assessment are aligned to Florida B.E.S.T. ELA Grade 9 Standards with a strong emphasis on analysis, interpretation, justification, and communication. Students engage in multi-step reading tasks, literary and informational analysis, argument writing, expository writing, narrative writing, research, vocabulary application, revision, and synthesis. Students are expected to demonstrate both writing fluency and deep comprehension of texts through the consistent use of textual evidence.

## Course Structure

| Unit | Unit Title | Primary Focus |
|------|------------|---------------|
| Unit 01 | Foundations of Reading and Writing | Close reading, paraphrasing, academic vocabulary, conventions, evidence, and written response foundations |
| Unit 02 | Literary Analysis and Theme | Literary elements, universal themes, narrator perspective, foundational and literary texts including epic works, figurative language, adaptation, and narrative writing |
| Unit 03 | Informational Text and Argument | Text structures, central ideas, rhetoric, opposing arguments, claims, evidence, and argumentative writing |
| Unit 04 | Writing Development and Research | Expository writing, research questions, credible sources, organization, source integration, formal writing structure, and collaborative publication |
| Unit 05 | Language, Style, and Revision | Vocabulary, etymology, derivations, context, connotation, conventions, clarity, cohesion, and revision |
| Unit 06 | Synthesis, Presentation, and Application | Synthesis across texts, oral presentation, digital communication, audience awareness, perspective, and capstone application |

## Standards Progression

ELA standards are skill-based and develop across the full course through a spiral progression. Each benchmark has a primary unit for focused instruction and assessment, but reading, writing, communication, and vocabulary skills reappear as supporting skills in later units to deepen understanding and complexity.

The course progression is based on increasing complexity rather than isolated coverage. Students begin with foundational comprehension, paraphrasing, academic vocabulary, and conventions; apply those skills to literary and informational analysis; extend them through research, argument, expository writing, and revision; and synthesize them in oral and digital communication.

---

## Mastery Model

Students are expected to demonstrate a minimum of 80% mastery across all required instructional components, including Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, Quizzes, and the Unit Assessment.

Students are not advanced to the next unit until mastery is achieved. Mastery is determined through consistent performance across multiple measures and is not based on a single assessment.

The Unit Assessment is required for all students and contributes to the overall mastery determination.

Students participating in the Accelerated Path must meet additional expectations. Please refer to the official Accelerated Path Overview document for full eligibility, requirements, and continuation criteria.
