Worked Example 1: Identify reactants

Problem: Which substances enter photosynthesis?

  1. Step 1: Read the input side of the model. Why: inputs are reactants.
  2. Step 2: Find carbon dioxide and water. Why: they are matter used in the process.
  3. Step 3: Name light as energy input. Why: light powers the process but is not matter.

Interpretation: Reactants are the matter used by photosynthesis.

Worked Example 2: Identify products

Problem: Which products are made?

  1. Step 1: Read the output side. Why: outputs are products.
  2. Step 2: Find glucose and oxygen. Why: they are made by the process.
  3. Step 3: Connect glucose to stored energy. Why: glucose stores chemical energy.

Interpretation: Products are made during photosynthesis.

Worked Example 3: Use data

Problem: A graph shows oxygen production rising as light increases up to a point. What does this suggest?

  1. Step 1: Identify measured output: oxygen. Why: oxygen is a photosynthesis product.
  2. Step 2: Compare oxygen to light. Why: light supports photosynthesis.
  3. Step 3: State that increased light can increase photosynthesis until another factor limits it. Why: data show a pattern, not unlimited increase.

Interpretation: Photosynthesis data must be interpreted with limits.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Photosynthesis uses oxygen and glucose to make carbon dioxide."

Correct: "Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide and water with light energy to make glucose and oxygen."

Teachable explanation: Reactants and products must be read in the correct direction.
