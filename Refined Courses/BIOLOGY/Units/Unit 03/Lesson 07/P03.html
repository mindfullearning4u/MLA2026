Continue the Teaching Sequence

Cycle diagrams must be read by following arrows. The arrow direction shows movement. The box or location shows a reservoir. A strong explanation names the reservoir, the process, and the matter being moved.

How to use NASA Earth Observatory: The Carbon Cycle

  1. Click the link and wait for the NASA Earth Observatory page or visual feature to load.
  2. Use the main image, map, timeline, caption, or labeled visual on the linked page.
  3. Identify what is changing over time or what cycle/process the visual shows.
  4. Record one evidence statement from the image, caption, map, or timeline.
  5. Connect that evidence to the lesson concept, such as cycling of matter, photosynthesis, respiration, ecosystems, or environmental change.

How to use USGS Water Science School: The Water Cycle

  1. Click the link and use the page section, diagram, map, or data table that opens on the linked USGS page.
  2. Read the labels and captions before reading the full page.
  3. Record the process, pattern, or data trend shown by the diagram, map, or table.
  4. Explain how that evidence supports the lesson concept.
  5. Do not search the USGS site for another activity unless your Teacher of Record specifically directs you to do so.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the process, molecule, condition, or cycle in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the graph, table, diagram, or model. Why: biology answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence. Why: mastery requires reasoning, not just naming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Energy and matter both cycle the exact same way."

Correct: "Matter cycles through reservoirs, while energy flows through systems and changes form."

Teachable explanation: Unit 3 requires separating matter cycling from energy flow.

Virtual Lab Variables and Model Boundaries

  • Water model independent variable: air temperature.
  • Water model dependent variable: water evaporated in 60 minutes.
  • Carbon model independent variable: ecosystem scenario.
  • Carbon model dependent variable: net atmospheric carbon change.
  • Controls: equal starting water volume, equal time, equal surface area, and the same carbon accounting interval.

The numbers are simplified simulated units. They show relationships but are not a forecast for a particular place or year.

Procedure - Complete Every Step

  1. Copy the laboratory question and both data tables.
  2. Predict how evaporation will change as temperature increases. Give a molecular explanation.
  3. Graph Data Table A as a line graph: temperature on the x-axis and water evaporated on the y-axis.
  4. For each carbon scenario, add respiration, decomposition, and combustion to find total carbon entering the atmosphere.
  5. Calculate net atmospheric carbon change: total entering the atmosphere minus carbon removed by photosynthesis.
  6. Graph the three net changes as a bar graph. Begin the y-axis below zero so negative and positive values are visible.
  7. Use negative values to show net removal from the atmosphere and positive values to show net addition.
  8. Answer P06 and submit the P07 CER conclusion.
