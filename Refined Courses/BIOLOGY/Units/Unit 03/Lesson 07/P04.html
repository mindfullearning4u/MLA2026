Worked Example 1: Trace carbon

Problem: A carbon-cycle arrow goes from atmosphere to plant sugar. What process is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify starting reservoir: atmosphere. Why: carbon begins as CO2.
  2. Step 2: Identify ending form: plant sugar. Why: carbon enters organic molecules.
  3. Step 3: Name photosynthesis. Why: photosynthesis moves CO2 into glucose.

Interpretation: Cycle arrows connect reservoirs through processes.

Worked Example 2: Trace water

Problem: Water moves from plant leaves to the atmosphere. What process is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify source: plant leaves. Why: source shows the reservoir.
  2. Step 2: Identify destination: atmosphere. Why: arrow direction matters.
  3. Step 3: Name transpiration. Why: plants release water vapor.

Interpretation: Water-cycle processes can involve organisms.

Worked Example 3: Interpret data

Problem: A graph shows atmospheric CO2 rising while combustion increases. What can be inferred?

  1. Step 1: Read the trend. Why: data show change over time.
  2. Step 2: Connect combustion to carbon release. Why: combustion adds CO2.
  3. Step 3: State a supported inference without overclaiming. Why: graphs support patterns, not every cause alone.

Interpretation: Data must be interpreted carefully and with evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Energy and matter both cycle the exact same way."

Correct: "Matter cycles through reservoirs, while energy flows through systems and changes form."

Teachable explanation: Unit 3 requires separating matter cycling from energy flow.
