Worked Example 1: Read the ATP cycle

Problem: ATP becomes ADP and a phosphate. What happens to energy?

  1. Step 1: Identify the model change. Why: phosphate removal is the key event.
  2. Step 2: Connect removal to release. Why: ATP breakdown releases usable energy.
  3. Step 3: State energy is available for cell work. Why: ATP powers processes.

Interpretation: ATP breakdown transfers energy to cell work.

Worked Example 2: Recharge ADP

Problem: What must happen for ADP to become ATP?

  1. Step 1: Start with ADP. Why: ADP has fewer phosphates.
  2. Step 2: Add a phosphate using energy. Why: recharging requires energy input.
  3. Step 3: Form ATP. Why: ATP stores transferable energy.

Interpretation: ATP cycling is reversible with energy input.

Worked Example 3: Avoid a misconception

Problem: Why is ATP not a long-term energy storage molecule?

  1. Step 1: Compare ATP to lipids/carbohydrates. Why: ATP is used quickly.
  2. Step 2: Identify ATP's role as transfer. Why: it moves energy to work sites.
  3. Step 3: Explain long-term storage uses other molecules. Why: cells need both storage and transfer.

Interpretation: ATP is immediate energy transfer, not the main long-term storage.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "ATP makes unlimited energy from nothing."

Correct: "ATP transfers usable energy by cycling between ATP and ADP."

Teachable explanation: Energy is transformed and transferred; it is not created from nothing by ATP.
