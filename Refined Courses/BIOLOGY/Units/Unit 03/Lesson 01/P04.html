Worked Example 1: Classify by function

Problem: A molecule speeds up a reaction in a cell. Which macromolecule group is most likely involved?

  1. Step 1: Identify the job: speeding a reaction. Why: function is the strongest clue.
  2. Step 2: Connect the job to enzymes. Why: enzymes speed chemical reactions.
  3. Step 3: Classify it as a protein. Why: many enzymes are proteins.

Interpretation: Function evidence supports macromolecule classification.

Worked Example 2: Interpret water polarity

Problem: A diagram shows oxygen slightly negative and hydrogens slightly positive. What property is shown?

  1. Step 1: Read the charge labels. Why: the model gives evidence.
  2. Step 2: Name uneven charge distribution as polarity. Why: polarity means unequal charge.
  3. Step 3: Connect polarity to dissolving materials. Why: this property helps water support cells.

Interpretation: A water model helps explain water's biological role.

Worked Example 3: Use a molecule chart

Problem: A molecule is made of nucleotides and stores instructions. Identify it.

  1. Step 1: Find the building block: nucleotide. Why: building blocks help identify molecule type.
  2. Step 2: Match nucleotides to nucleic acids. Why: nucleic acids are made of nucleotides.
  3. Step 3: Connect to function: genetic information. Why: structure and function agree.

Interpretation: Both structure and function support the classification.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "All molecules do the same job because cells need them."

Correct: "Different molecules have different jobs: carbohydrates often provide quick energy, proteins perform cell work, lipids form membranes, and nucleic acids store information."

Teachable explanation: Biology requires matching each molecule to evidence about its structure and function.
