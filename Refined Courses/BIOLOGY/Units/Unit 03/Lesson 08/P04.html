Worked Example 1: Connect enzymes and data

Problem: An enzyme graph peaks at pH 7. How does that support cell processes?

  1. Step 1: Find the peak. Why: peak shows optimum.
  2. Step 2: Connect optimum to enzyme function. Why: enzymes work best under certain conditions.
  3. Step 3: Connect function to cells. Why: cells depend on efficient reactions.

Interpretation: Data explain how conditions affect life processes.

Worked Example 2: Connect photosynthesis and respiration

Problem: How do glucose and oxygen connect the two processes?

  1. Step 1: Identify them as photosynthesis products. Why: photosynthesis makes them.
  2. Step 2: Identify them as respiration reactants. Why: respiration uses them.
  3. Step 3: Explain energy transfer. Why: glucose stores energy that respiration releases.

Interpretation: The processes are linked through matter and energy roles.

Worked Example 3: Connect cycles

Problem: How does carbon move from air to organisms and back?

  1. Step 1: Name photosynthesis moving CO2 into sugars. Why: producers capture carbon.
  2. Step 2: Name feeding or cellular use moving carbon through organisms. Why: matter moves in living systems.
  3. Step 3: Name respiration/decomposition returning CO2. Why: carbon cycles back.

Interpretation: Carbon cycling links cell processes to ecosystems.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "ATP, enzymes, photosynthesis, and cycles are connected because they are all science words."

Correct: "Enzymes help reactions, ATP transfers usable energy, photosynthesis stores energy in glucose, respiration releases usable energy, and cycles move matter through systems."

Teachable explanation: Synthesis requires specific relationships between concepts, not a list of terms.
