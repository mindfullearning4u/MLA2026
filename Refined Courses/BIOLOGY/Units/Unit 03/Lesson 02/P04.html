Worked Example 1: Find optimum pH

Problem: Use the table to identify the optimum pH.

  1. Step 1: Find the highest activity value. Why: optimum means maximum activity.
  2. Step 2: Locate its pH. Activity is 100% at pH 7. Why: the x-value at the maximum is the condition.
  3. Step 3: State pH 7 as optimum. Why: it gives the best activity in this data.

Interpretation: Optimum conditions come from the data peak.

Worked Example 2: Explain lower activity

Problem: Why is activity lower at pH 11?

  1. Step 1: Compare pH 11 to optimum. Why: distance from optimum matters.
  2. Step 2: Notice activity drops to 5%. Why: the table shows reduced function.
  3. Step 3: Explain shape disruption. Why: extreme pH can change enzyme shape.

Interpretation: Enzyme shape and function are connected.

Worked Example 3: Use activation energy

Problem: Why do enzymes speed reactions?

  1. Step 1: Identify activation energy as the starting energy needed. Why: reactions need energy to begin.
  2. Step 2: Explain enzymes lower that energy. Why: a lower barrier speeds reaction rate.
  3. Step 3: Connect to cells. Why: cells need reactions to happen efficiently.

Interpretation: Enzymes help reactions proceed under cell conditions.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The enzyme works best at the highest pH because bigger numbers are better."

Correct: "The enzyme works best at the pH with the highest activity value."

Teachable explanation: Graphs and tables must be read by the measured result, not by assuming bigger x-axis values are better.
