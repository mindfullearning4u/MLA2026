P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Identify reactants

Problem: What enters cellular respiration?

  1. Step 1: Read the input side. Why: inputs are reactants.
  2. Step 2: Identify glucose and oxygen. Why: they are used in aerobic respiration.
  3. Step 3: Connect glucose to stored energy. Why: respiration releases energy from glucose.

Interpretation: Reactants are used to release energy.

Worked Example 2: Identify products

Problem: What products are made besides usable energy transfer?

  1. Step 1: Read the output side. Why: outputs are products.
  2. Step 2: Identify carbon dioxide and water. Why: these are matter products.
  3. Step 3: Connect ATP to energy transfer. Why: ATP carries usable energy.

Interpretation: Respiration products include carbon dioxide and water.

Worked Example 3: Use an energy table

Problem: A table shows more ATP with oxygen than without oxygen. What does this suggest?

  1. Step 1: Identify the compared condition: oxygen present or absent. Why: conditions affect energy yield.
  2. Step 2: Read ATP yield. Why: ATP indicates usable energy transfer.
  3. Step 3: Conclude oxygen supports higher ATP yield in aerobic respiration. Why: the data show more ATP.

Interpretation: Data tables show how conditions affect energy release.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Cellular respiration is the same thing as breathing."

Correct: "Cellular respiration is a cell process that releases usable energy from glucose, often using oxygen."

Teachable explanation: Breathing supports gas exchange; cellular respiration is the energy-releasing cell process.
