Notebook Title: Environmental Monitoring and Policy Decisions

Mapped Standards for This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.BIO.ECO.03; MLA.BIO.SCI.02

Support Standards: SC.912.L.17.13; ELA.K12.EE.1.1

Vocabulary

  • Environmental monitoring: repeated measurement of environmental conditions.
  • Indicator: measurable sign of ecosystem condition.
  • Policy decision: rule or action chosen to address a problem.
  • Baseline: starting data used for comparison.
  • Evidence-based argument: claim supported by data and reasoning.

Detailed Teaching Sequence

Environmental policy decisions should be based on reliable monitoring data. Monitoring may track water quality, air quality, land use, species abundance, pollution, temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen, or biodiversity. A policy argument should identify the problem, use baseline and trend data, compare options, and explain likely impacts.

Monitoring DataWhat It Can ShowPolicy Use
Water qualitypH, oxygen, nitrate, turbidityProtect aquatic life
Air qualityParticulate or ozone levelsReduce health/ecosystem risk
Land dataHabitat loss or fragmentationGuide land-use decisions
Species dataPopulation or biodiversity trendsProtect threatened ecosystems
