Continue the Teaching Sequence

Good policy reasoning does not start with an opinion. It starts with a measurable problem, reads the data trend, evaluates costs and benefits, and then recommends an action. If the data are limited, the answer should say what additional monitoring is needed.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the ecology concept, relationship, trend, or decision in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the map, graph, data table, food web, energy pyramid, timeline, or monitoring display. Why: ecology answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence and include any limit. Why: mastery requires careful reasoning, not overclaiming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Environmental policy should be chosen before looking at data."

Correct: "Environmental policy should be supported by monitoring data, trend evidence, costs, benefits, and limits."

Teachable explanation: Policy decisions are stronger when the evidence explains the problem and supports the response.

  4. Explain how that evidence supports the lesson concept.
  5. Do not search the USGS site for another activity unless your Teacher of Record specifically directs you to do so.

Important: The map shows spatial order and nearby land context. It does not prove that one land use caused every measured difference.

Procedure - Follow Every Step

  1. Copy the laboratory question, map order, and monitoring table.
  2. For each variable, identify its unit and whether a higher value is usually favorable, unfavorable, or context-dependent.
  3. Create a two-line graph for nitrate and dissolved oxygen because both use mg/L. Put sites S1-S4 on the x-axis.
  4. Create a bar graph for macroinvertebrate richness.
  5. Calculate the change from S1 to each other site for nitrate, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, and richness.
  6. Compare the policy options with the site evidence. Do not select an option only because it is most expensive.
  7. List one alternative explanation and one additional measurement needed.
  8. Complete P06 and the P07 policy CER.
