P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

What the Mapped Standards Require

  • MLA.BIO.ECO.03: Evaluate biodiversity loss, environmental monitoring, resource use, sustainability, and human impacts on environmental systems.
  • SC.912.L.17.11: Costs and benefits of renewable and nonrenewable resources.
  • SC.912.L.17.20: Individual impacts, lifestyle, and sustainability.

Continue the Teaching Sequence

Evaluate both costs and benefits. Do not label a choice perfect or useless. A stronger response states the benefit, the environmental cost, and one evidence-based way to reduce the impact.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the ecology concept, relationship, trend, or decision in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the map, graph, data table, food web, energy pyramid, timeline, or monitoring display. Why: ecology answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence and include any limit. Why: mastery requires careful reasoning, not overclaiming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Renewable resources have no environmental costs."

Correct: "Renewable resources may reduce some impacts but still require evidence-based cost-benefit analysis."

Teachable explanation: Sustainability reasoning weighs benefits, costs, and possible mitigation.

How to use NASA Earth Observatory: World of Change

  1. Click the link and wait for the NASA Earth Observatory page or visual feature to load.
  2. Use the main image, map, timeline, caption, or labeled visual on the linked page.
  3. Identify what is changing over time or what cycle/process the visual shows.
  4. Record one evidence statement from the image, caption, map, or timeline.
  5. Connect that evidence to the lesson concept, such as cycling of matter, photosynthesis, respiration, ecosystems, or environmental change.
