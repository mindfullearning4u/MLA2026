Support Standards: SC.912.L.17.8

Continue the Teaching Sequence

When reading biodiversity data, compare before and after values, native species counts, invasive species abundance, and habitat condition. A good answer identifies the cause, the data pattern, and the likely effect on biodiversity.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the ecology concept, relationship, trend, or decision in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the map, graph, data table, food web, energy pyramid, timeline, or monitoring display. Why: ecology answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence and include any limit. Why: mastery requires careful reasoning, not overclaiming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A new species always increases biodiversity and helps the ecosystem."

Correct: "A nonnative species may reduce biodiversity if it harms native species or disrupts ecosystem relationships."

Teachable explanation: The effect depends on evidence, not just the number of species present.
