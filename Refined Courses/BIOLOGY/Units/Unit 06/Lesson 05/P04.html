Mapped Standards for This Lesson

Primary Standards: MLA.BIO.ECO.03; MLA.BIO.SCI.02

Support Standards: SC.912.L.17.8

Worked Example 1: Use invasive species data

Problem: A graph shows invasive plant cover rising while native plant richness falls. What is supported?

  1. Step 1: Identify both trends. Why: two data patterns are shown.
  2. Step 2: Connect invasive increase to native decrease. Why: competition may be occurring.
  3. Step 3: State biodiversity loss cautiously. Why: data supports a relationship.

Interpretation: The invasive plant may be contributing to biodiversity loss.

Worked Example 2: Use catastrophic event evidence

Problem: A hurricane destroys nesting habitat and bird counts fall. What cause is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify the disturbance. Why: hurricane is a catastrophic event.
  2. Step 2: Connect habitat loss to population decline. Why: nesting habitat supports reproduction.
  3. Step 3: Explain biodiversity impact. Why: fewer species or individuals reduces biodiversity.

Interpretation: Catastrophic disturbance can reduce biodiversity.

Worked Example 3: Avoid overclaiming

Problem: A data table shows two years of decline. What should the conclusion include?

  1. Step 1: State the observed decline. Why: use direct evidence.
  2. Step 2: Name possible cause only if data supports it. Why: correlation is not always causation.
  3. Step 3: Identify what extra evidence would help. Why: scientific claims need support.

Interpretation: Careful claims include evidence and limits.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A new species always increases biodiversity and helps the ecosystem."

Correct: "A nonnative species may reduce biodiversity if it harms native species or disrupts ecosystem relationships."

Teachable explanation: The effect depends on evidence, not just the number of species present.
