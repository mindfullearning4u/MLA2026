Worked Example 1: Calculate population change

Problem: A population has 20 births, 8 deaths, 5 immigrants, and 7 emigrants. What is the net change?

  1. Step 1: Add births and immigrants. Why: they increase population size.
  2. Step 2: Add deaths and emigrants. Why: they decrease population size.
  3. Step 3: Subtract removals from additions. Why: net change shows direction.

Interpretation: Net change is (20 + 5) - (8 + 7) = 10 individuals added.

Worked Example 2: Identify carrying capacity

Problem: A graph rises quickly and then levels near 500 individuals. What does 500 likely represent?

  1. Step 1: Read the leveling point. Why: carrying capacity appears where growth stabilizes.
  2. Step 2: Connect to resources. Why: environment limits growth.
  3. Step 3: Use likely wording. Why: data supports an interpretation.

Interpretation: The graph likely levels near carrying capacity.

Worked Example 3: Find a limiting factor

Problem: A deer population drops after drought reduces plant growth. What factor limited the population?

  1. Step 1: Identify environmental change. Why: drought is abiotic.
  2. Step 2: Connect drought to food. Why: reduced plants affect deer resources.
  3. Step 3: Name food/water availability as limiting. Why: it restricts survival and reproduction.

Interpretation: Resource availability can limit population size.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A population can grow forever if it has a few resources."

Correct: "Population growth is limited by resources and conditions, so populations often level near carrying capacity."

Teachable explanation: Carrying capacity reasoning connects graph shape to limiting factors.
