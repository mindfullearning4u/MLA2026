P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Connect population and food web

Problem: A limiting factor reduces rabbits in a food web. What could happen to predators?

  1. Step 1: Identify limiting factor effect. Why: rabbit population decreases.
  2. Step 2: Trace food web arrows. Why: predators receive energy from prey.
  3. Step 3: Predict predator decrease if rabbits are major food. Why: less food can reduce predator survival/reproduction.

Interpretation: Population data and food webs can be connected.

Worked Example 2: Connect biodiversity and policy

Problem: Monitoring data show invasive species increasing and native species declining. What decision is supported?

  1. Step 1: Read both trends. Why: evidence shows ecosystem change.
  2. Step 2: Connect invasive increase to biodiversity risk. Why: native decline matters.
  3. Step 3: Recommend monitoring/control with limits. Why: policy needs evidence and follow-up data.

Interpretation: Biodiversity data can support management decisions.

Worked Example 3: Connect resource tradeoffs

Problem: A community considers a lower-emission resource with higher land-use impact. What should the analysis include?

  1. Step 1: Identify benefit. Why: lower emissions are positive evidence.
  2. Step 2: Identify cost. Why: land-use impact affects habitat.
  3. Step 3: Weigh tradeoff and mitigation. Why: sustainability decisions require balanced evidence.

Interpretation: Resource decisions require cost-benefit evidence.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Ecology questions can be answered with opinions because they involve human decisions."

Correct: "Ecology decisions should be supported by ecosystem evidence, data trends, costs, benefits, and clear limits."

Teachable explanation: Human-impact questions still require scientific evidence and reasoning.
