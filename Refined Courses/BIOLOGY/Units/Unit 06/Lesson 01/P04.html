Worked Example 1: Use light and depth

Problem: A data table shows high algae growth in shallow clear water and low algae growth in deep dark water. What explains the pattern?

  1. Step 1: Identify the producer. Why: algae need photosynthesis.
  2. Step 2: Read light and depth. Why: light decreases with depth.
  3. Step 3: Connect light to growth. Why: producers grow where photosynthesis can occur.

Interpretation: Light availability helps explain aquatic producer distribution.

Worked Example 2: Use salinity

Problem: A fish is found in an estuary where salinity changes daily. What trait matters?

  1. Step 1: Identify the location. Why: estuaries mix fresh and salt water.
  2. Step 2: Read salinity variation. Why: changing salt levels stress organisms.
  3. Step 3: Connect to tolerance. Why: organisms must tolerate the abiotic range.

Interpretation: Salinity tolerance affects distribution.

Worked Example 3: Use map evidence

Problem: A map shows low oxygen near a river outflow and fewer fish there. What conclusion is supported?

  1. Step 1: Read the map pattern. Why: location and oxygen are linked.
  2. Step 2: Compare fish abundance. Why: distribution data shows where fish are found.
  3. Step 3: Connect oxygen to survival. Why: many fish need dissolved oxygen.

Interpretation: Low oxygen can limit aquatic consumers.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Aquatic organisms live anywhere in the water as long as water is present."

Correct: "Aquatic organisms are distributed according to abiotic conditions such as light, depth, temperature, salinity, oxygen, nutrients, and geography."

Teachable explanation: Water alone is not enough evidence; students must connect the specific condition to organism survival.
