Worked Example 1: Trace energy

Problem: In a web, algae -> small fish -> large fish. Which organism receives energy from small fish?

  1. Step 1: Read arrow direction. Why: arrow shows energy flow.
  2. Step 2: Locate small fish as food source for large fish. Why: arrow points to receiver.
  3. Step 3: Name large fish. Why: it receives energy from small fish.

Interpretation: The large fish receives energy from the small fish.

Worked Example 2: Predict impact

Problem: If grass decreases, what may happen to grasshoppers that eat grass?

  1. Step 1: Identify grass as producer. Why: it supports primary consumers.
  2. Step 2: Find grasshopper feeding relationship. Why: it eats grass.
  3. Step 3: Predict decrease. Why: less food can reduce population size.

Interpretation: Grasshopper populations may decrease if food decreases.

Worked Example 3: Use energy pyramid

Problem: Why is there usually less biomass at higher trophic levels?

  1. Step 1: Recall energy transfer. Why: not all energy moves to the next level.
  2. Step 2: Connect to biomass. Why: less available energy supports fewer organisms.
  3. Step 3: State higher levels have less available energy. Why: energy is reduced at each transfer.

Interpretation: Energy decreases at higher trophic levels.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Food web arrows point to what an organism wants to eat."

Correct: "Food web arrows point from the food source to the organism receiving energy."

Teachable explanation: Arrow direction is essential for accurate energy-transfer reasoning.
