Worked Example 1: Connect DNA and protein

Problem: How can a DNA change affect a protein?

  1. Step 1: Identify the DNA sequence change. Why: mutation starts at DNA.
  2. Step 2: Connect DNA to mRNA codons. Why: expression reads sequence information.
  3. Step 3: Connect codons to amino acids. Why: protein sequence can change.

Interpretation: DNA changes may affect protein structure and function.

Worked Example 2: Connect meiosis and inheritance

Problem: Why does meiosis matter for Punnett square predictions?

  1. Step 1: Identify gametes as meiosis products. Why: parents pass alleles through gametes.
  2. Step 2: Connect allele combinations to offspring. Why: Punnett squares model gamete combinations.
  3. Step 3: Explain variation. Why: meiosis can produce different gametes.

Interpretation: Inheritance predictions depend on gamete formation.

Worked Example 3: Connect biotechnology and health

Problem: How should a genetics case be evaluated?

  1. Step 1: Identify the biological mechanism. Why: the case must be scientifically grounded.
  2. Step 2: Use evidence for benefits. Why: benefits must be supported.
  3. Step 3: Check risks and limits. Why: responsible evaluation avoids overclaiming.

Interpretation: Biotechnology decisions require evidence-based reasoning.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "DNA, proteins, traits, and biotechnology are connected because they are all about genetics."

Correct: "DNA stores information, expression can produce proteins, mutations may change traits, inheritance passes alleles, and biotechnology applies genetics evidence to solve problems."

Teachable explanation: Synthesis must name specific relationships between the genetics concepts.
