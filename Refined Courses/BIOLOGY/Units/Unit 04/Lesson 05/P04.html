Worked Example 1: Identify codominance

Problem: A heterozygous fictional flower shows both red and white spots. What pattern is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify heterozygote phenotype. Why: the pattern is visible there.
  2. Step 2: Notice both traits appear. Why: neither allele is hidden.
  3. Step 3: Name codominance. Why: both alleles are expressed.

Interpretation: Both traits showing together supports codominance.

Worked Example 2: Identify incomplete dominance

Problem: Red x white produces all pink offspring. What pattern is suggested?

  1. Step 1: Compare parent phenotypes. Why: parents show extremes.
  2. Step 2: Observe offspring intermediate phenotype. Why: pink is between red and white.
  3. Step 3: Name incomplete dominance. Why: heterozygote is intermediate.

Interpretation: Intermediate heterozygote supports incomplete dominance.

Worked Example 3: Identify polygenic evidence

Problem: A trait shows many small differences across a population. What pattern is likely?

  1. Step 1: Look for continuous variation. Why: many phenotypes suggest multiple influences.
  2. Step 2: Connect multiple genes to range. Why: polygenic traits often show a spectrum.
  3. Step 3: Avoid simple two-box thinking. Why: one-gene models do not explain continuous range well.

Interpretation: Continuous variation supports polygenic inheritance.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Every inheritance problem can be solved as simple dominant and recessive."

Correct: "Some traits follow non-Mendelian patterns, so the evidence must be checked before choosing a model."

Teachable explanation: Using the wrong inheritance model leads to wrong predictions.
