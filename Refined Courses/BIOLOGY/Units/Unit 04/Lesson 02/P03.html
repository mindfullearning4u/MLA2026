Continue the Teaching Sequence

To use a codon table, first make sure the sequence is mRNA, not DNA. RNA uses U instead of T. Read bases in groups of three from the correct starting point. Then match each codon to the amino acid listed in the table.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the genetics process, pattern, model, or case claim in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the sequence, codon table, Punnett square, diagram, or case table. Why: genetics answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence. Why: mastery requires reasoning, not just naming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A codon can be read as any three letters in any order."

Correct: "A codon is read as a specific three-base mRNA sequence in the correct order."

Teachable explanation: Changing the order changes the codon and can change the amino acid.
