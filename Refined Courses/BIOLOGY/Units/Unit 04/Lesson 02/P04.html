Worked Example 1: Transcribe DNA to mRNA

Problem: DNA template: A C T. What mRNA codon forms?

  1. Step 1: Pair A in DNA with U in RNA. Why: RNA uses U instead of T.
  2. Step 2: Pair C with G. Why: C-G pairing still applies.
  3. Step 3: Pair T with A. Why: T in DNA pairs with A in RNA.

Interpretation: The mRNA codon is U G A.

Worked Example 2: Use a codon table

Problem: mRNA codon AUG codes for methionine/start in the provided table. What does AUG signal?

  1. Step 1: Confirm the sequence is mRNA. Why: codon tables use mRNA.
  2. Step 2: Read AUG as one codon. Why: codons have three bases.
  3. Step 3: Match AUG to the table entry. Why: the table provides the amino acid/signal.

Interpretation: AUG signals methionine/start in the table.

Worked Example 3: Connect DNA to trait

Problem: Why can a gene affect a trait?

  1. Step 1: A gene provides DNA instructions. Why: genes are DNA segments.
  2. Step 2: Expression can produce a protein. Why: transcription and translation use the information.
  3. Step 3: Protein function can affect phenotype. Why: traits often depend on protein activity.

Interpretation: Gene expression links DNA information to phenotype.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A codon can be read as any three letters in any order."

Correct: "A codon is read as a specific three-base mRNA sequence in the correct order."

Teachable explanation: Changing the order changes the codon and can change the amino acid.
