Worked Example 1: Identify the Evidence

Problem: A student needs to explain gene expression transcription and using a model or data table. What should the student identify first?

  1. Step 1: Identify the claim or question. Why: Evidence only matters when it matches the question being answered.
  2. Step 2: Locate a specific data point, model label, or relationship. Why: Specific evidence is stronger than a general statement.
  3. Step 3: Connect the evidence to the biology idea. Why: The reasoning explains why the evidence supports the answer.

Answer: Identify the claim or question first, then select evidence that directly addresses it.

Interpretation: This prevents unsupported guessing.

Worked Example 2: Use a Model

Problem: A model shows two parts of a biological system connected by an arrow. What does the arrow usually represent?

  1. Step 1: Read the labels on both parts. Why: The labels define what is being connected.
  2. Step 2: Determine whether the arrow shows movement, cause/effect, sequence, or energy/matter transfer. Why: Arrows have meaning in science models.
  3. Step 3: Explain the relationship in words. Why: Written reasoning proves you understand the model.

Answer: The arrow represents a relationship or process between the labeled parts.

Interpretation: Models must be read carefully; the arrow is evidence, not decoration.

Worked Example 3: Build a CER Response

Problem: Write a CER response for a data table connected to gene expression transcription and.

  1. Step 1: Claim. Write the answer to the question. Why: The claim tells the reader your conclusion.
  2. Step 2: Evidence. Cite exact data or model details. Why: Evidence makes the claim checkable.
  3. Step 3: Reasoning. Explain the science connection. Why: Reasoning shows why the evidence matters.

Interpretation: A complete answer includes all three parts: claim, evidence, and reasoning.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The answer is obvious from the diagram."

Correct: "The diagram shows a labeled relationship; that relationship supports the answer because it connects the structure or process to its function."

Teachable Explanation: Never assume the reader can see your thinking. State the evidence and explain it.
