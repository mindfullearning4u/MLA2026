Worked Example 1: Build a complementary strand

Problem: Template strand: A T C G. What new strand forms?

  1. Step 1: Pair A with T. Why: A-T is a DNA base pair.
  2. Step 2: Pair T with A. Why: pairing is complementary.
  3. Step 3: Pair C with G and G with C. Why: C-G is the other base pair.

Interpretation: The complementary strand is T A G C.

Worked Example 2: Explain information conservation

Problem: How does replication conserve genetic information?

  1. Step 1: Start with each original strand as a template. Why: the template controls the new strand.
  2. Step 2: Apply base-pairing rules. Why: rules make copying accurate.
  3. Step 3: Produce two matching DNA molecules. Why: each has one original and one new strand.

Interpretation: Conserved information comes from template-based copying.

Worked Example 3: Find an error

Problem: A student pairs A with C. What is wrong?

  1. Step 1: Identify the claimed pair. Why: the mistake is in the pair.
  2. Step 2: Recall the rule A pairs with T. Why: DNA pairing is specific.
  3. Step 3: Correct A-C to A-T. Why: correct pairing preserves information.

Interpretation: Accurate base pairing is essential for replication.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Replication means the new strand has the exact same letters as the template strand."

Correct: "Replication builds a complementary strand using base-pairing rules."

Teachable explanation: The template and new strand are related by pairing rules, not by copying the same letter into every position.
