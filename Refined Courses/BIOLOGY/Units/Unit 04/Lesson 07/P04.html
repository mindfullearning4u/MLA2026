Worked Example 1: Evaluate a case

Problem: A bacteria strain is engineered to produce human insulin. What benefit is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify the biotechnology process. Why: the organism is engineered.
  2. Step 2: Identify the product: insulin. Why: product connects to purpose.
  3. Step 3: State the benefit: medicine production. Why: insulin can support health needs.

Interpretation: The case shows a medical biotechnology benefit.

Worked Example 2: Connect cell cycle and cancer

Problem: Why can failed cell-cycle control be harmful?

  1. Step 1: Identify normal control. Why: checkpoints regulate division.
  2. Step 2: Describe failure: cells divide too much. Why: uncontrolled division disrupts tissues.
  3. Step 3: Connect to cancer. Why: cancer involves uncontrolled cell growth.

Interpretation: Cell-cycle regulation protects organism health.

Worked Example 3: Use evidence carefully

Problem: A case table shows a benefit but no long-term risk data. What should be concluded?

  1. Step 1: Use the benefit evidence. Why: available data can support part of a claim.
  2. Step 2: Identify missing risk data. Why: limits matter.
  3. Step 3: Avoid overclaiming. Why: a full decision needs benefits and risks.

Interpretation: Evidence-based evaluation includes limitations.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "All biotechnology is automatically safe or automatically unsafe."

Correct: "Biotechnology must be evaluated with evidence about purpose, benefits, risks, limits, and biological mechanism."

Teachable explanation: A scientific evaluation weighs evidence instead of using one-sided assumptions.
