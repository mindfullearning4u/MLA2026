Worked Example 1: Identify xylem function

Problem: A diagram shows water moving from roots to leaves. Which tissue is responsible?

  1. Step 1: Identify the material: water. Why: the transported material is the clue.
  2. Step 2: Identify the direction: upward from roots. Why: xylem moves water and minerals upward.
  3. Step 3: Choose xylem. Why: its function matches the diagram.

Interpretation: Xylem is the water-transport tissue.

Worked Example 2: Connect leaf structure to function

Problem: Why does a broad leaf surface support photosynthesis?

  1. Step 1: Name the function: photosynthesis captures light. Why: function tells what the structure must support.
  2. Step 2: Name the structure: broad surface. Why: a broad surface can capture more light.
  3. Step 3: Connect them. Why: structure supports the process.

Interpretation: A structure-function answer must connect the physical feature to the process.

Worked Example 3: Analyze plant data

Problem: A plant with damaged roots wilts even when light is available. Why?

  1. Step 1: Identify damaged structure: roots. Why: roots absorb water.
  2. Step 2: Connect to process: water uptake decreases. Why: less water reaches cells and leaves.
  3. Step 3: Explain wilting. Why: cells lose water pressure and cannot maintain support.

Interpretation: Plant processes depend on connected organs.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Leaves absorb all water because they are green."

Correct: "Roots absorb most water, xylem transports water upward, and leaves use water in photosynthesis and gas exchange."

Teachable explanation: Color is not the function clue. Use the movement of materials and the structure's job.
