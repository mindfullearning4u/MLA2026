Visual Model to Use Before Solving

For every transport example, picture a membrane model with two sides, a concentration gradient, and arrows showing particle movement. Read the direction of the arrows first, then decide whether the movement goes from high to low concentration or against the gradient.

Worked Example 1: Identify diffusion

Problem: Oxygen is higher outside a cell than inside and moves into the cell. What transport is this?

  1. Step 1: Compare concentrations. Outside is higher. Why: gradient direction controls movement.
  2. Step 2: Track movement. Oxygen moves from high to low. Why: high-to-low movement follows the gradient.
  3. Step 3: Classify it as diffusion. Why: diffusion is passive movement down a gradient.

Interpretation: No energy is needed when movement follows the gradient.

Worked Example 2: Identify osmosis

Problem: Water moves across a membrane toward the side with more dissolved solute. What process is occurring?

  1. Step 1: Identify the moving material: water. Why: water movement across a membrane is the key clue.
  2. Step 2: Check that it crosses a membrane. It does. Why: osmosis specifically involves a membrane.
  3. Step 3: Classify it as osmosis. Why: osmosis is diffusion of water.

Interpretation: Osmosis is a specific type of diffusion.

Worked Example 3: Identify active transport

Problem: A cell uses energy to move ions from low concentration to high concentration. What process is this?

  1. Step 1: Compare direction. Low to high is against the gradient. Why: this direction is not passive.
  2. Step 2: Look for energy use. Energy is used. Why: energy is required to move against the gradient.
  3. Step 3: Classify it as active transport. Why: active transport uses energy.

Interpretation: Energy use plus movement against the gradient identifies active transport.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "All movement across a membrane requires energy."

Correct: "Movement down a concentration gradient is passive; movement against the gradient requires energy."

Teachable explanation: The direction of movement compared with the gradient determines whether energy is needed.
