Comparing Cell Types

Read the Evidence Before Explaining It

A scientific explanation begins with a visible detail, not a memorized slogan. In the table, the middle column states what is directly given. The final column models the biological meaning. Read across each row.

Evidence and Meaning

Part, Condition, or SourceWhat the Evidence ShowsWhat It Means Biologically
Bacterial cellDNA lies in a nucleoid; a membrane, ribosomes, and cell wall are present; no nucleus is present.The missing nucleus identifies a prokaryotic cell.
Plant cellA nucleus, cell wall, chloroplasts, and large central vacuole are visible.These structures identify a plant eukaryotic cell.
Animal cellA nucleus and flexible membrane are visible; there is no cell wall or chloroplast.These features identify an animal eukaryotic cell.
Shared structureAll three cell types have a plasma membrane and ribosomes.All cells regulate a boundary and build proteins.
ScaleA typical bacterium is much smaller than a typical plant or animal cell.Cell drawings are often not drawn to the same scale.
Evidence ruleShape alone is weaker evidence than labeled internal structures.A rectangular-looking cell without a visible wall is not automatically a plant cell.

Guided Notes Check — Completed Model

Question: What does the first evidence row show, and why does it matter?

Completed answer: The evidence shows DNA lies in a nucleoid; a membrane, ribosomes, and cell wall are present; no nucleus is present. This matters because the missing nucleus identifies a prokaryotic cell.

Where the answer came from: The exact observation is in row 1, column 2. The biological meaning is in row 1, column 3. A complete answer uses both.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incomplete: “The picture proves the standard.”

Improved: Name the visible structure, value, trend, or relationship; then explain the exact biological process it supports. A model shows selected features and never proves more than it displays.
