Worked Example 1: Identify the stimulus

Problem: A cell is placed in a salty solution and water leaves the cell. What is the stimulus?

  1. Step 1: Find what changed first. The outside solution became salty. Why: the stimulus is the change that starts the response.
  2. Step 2: Separate stimulus from response. Water leaving is a result. Why: do not confuse the trigger with what happens next.
  3. Step 3: Identify the salty outside solution as the stimulus. Why: it caused the water movement.

Interpretation: The stimulus begins the feedback sequence.

Worked Example 2: Connect transport to homeostasis

Problem: Why does membrane transport help cells maintain homeostasis?

  1. Step 1: Name what cells must control. Cells control water, ions, nutrients, and wastes. Why: these affect internal conditions.
  2. Step 2: Name the membrane role. The membrane controls movement. Why: selective movement keeps balance.
  3. Step 3: Connect the ideas. Why: transport helps stabilize conditions.

Interpretation: Homeostasis depends on controlled movement across boundaries.

Worked Example 3: Evaluate a response

Problem: A person gets hot and sweats. How does sweating support homeostasis?

  1. Step 1: Identify the regulated condition: body temperature. Why: homeostasis is always about a condition.
  2. Step 2: Identify the response: sweating. Why: the response is the action taken.
  3. Step 3: Explain the effect: sweating helps cool the body. Why: cooling moves temperature back toward a stable range.

Interpretation: A homeostasis response reduces the original imbalance.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Homeostasis means nothing in the body ever changes."

Correct: "Homeostasis means the body or cell responds to changes to keep internal conditions within a stable range."

Teachable explanation: Living systems constantly change. Regulation is the process of responding to those changes.
