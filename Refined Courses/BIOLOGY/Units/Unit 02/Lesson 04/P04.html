Evidence Source: Changes Across a Selectively Permeable Membrane

Scenario: Identical cells begin with 1.0% dissolved salt inside. They are placed in three different outside solutions for 15 minutes. A negative mass change means the cell lost water; a positive mass change means the cell gained water.

Outside solutionCell mass changeObserved water movement
0.2% salt+8%Net movement into cell
1.0% salt0%No net movement
5.0% salt-12%Net movement out of cell

Process model: outside change (stimulus) → membrane transport (response) → change in internal water balance (effect).

How to use it: First name what changed outside the cell. Next identify the measured response. Finally explain whether the response changes or stabilizes the cell's internal condition.

Worked Example 1: Identify the stimulus

Problem: Use the 5.0% salt row. What is the stimulus, and what evidence shows the cell's response?

  1. Step 1: Find what was changed by the investigation: the outside solution was 5.0% salt. Why: the stimulus is the external change that begins the sequence.
  2. Step 2: Find the measured response in that same row: cell mass decreased 12%, and the table identifies net water movement out of the cell. Why: these observations occurred after exposure to the stimulus.
  3. Step 3: State both parts without confusing cause and result: The 5.0% outside salt solution is the stimulus; the 12% mass loss is evidence that water moved out through the selectively permeable membrane.

Interpretation: A sound explanation distinguishes the condition deliberately changed from the response that was measured.

Worked Example 2: Connect transport to homeostasis

Problem: Why does membrane transport help cells maintain homeostasis?

  1. Step 1: Name what cells must control. Cells control water, ions, nutrients, and wastes. Why: these affect internal conditions.
  2. Step 2: Name the membrane role. The membrane controls movement. Why: selective movement keeps balance.
  3. Step 3: Connect the ideas. Why: transport helps stabilize conditions.

Interpretation: Homeostasis depends on controlled movement across boundaries.

Worked Example 3: Evaluate a response

Problem: A person gets hot and sweats. How does sweating support homeostasis?

  1. Step 1: Identify the regulated condition: body temperature. Why: homeostasis is always about a condition.
  2. Step 2: Identify the response: sweating. Why: the response is the action taken.
  3. Step 3: Explain the effect: sweating helps cool the body. Why: cooling moves temperature back toward a stable range.

Interpretation: A homeostasis response reduces the original imbalance.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Homeostasis means nothing in the body ever changes."

Correct: "Homeostasis means the body or cell responds to changes to keep internal conditions within a stable range."

Teachable explanation: Living systems constantly change. Regulation is the process of responding to those changes.
