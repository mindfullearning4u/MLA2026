P04 Worked Example

Worked Example 1: Connect cell type and function

Problem: A diagram shows a plant cell with chloroplasts and a large vacuole. How does this support plant function?

  1. Step 1: Classify the cell as plant. Why: chloroplasts and large vacuole are plant evidence.
  2. Step 2: Name the functions. Chloroplasts support photosynthesis; vacuoles store water/materials. Why: functions explain why structures matter.
  3. Step 3: Connect to plant processes. Why: cell structures support organism-level plant function.

Interpretation: Cell structures support larger plant processes.

Worked Example 2: Connect transport and homeostasis

Problem: A membrane model shows water leaving a cell in a salty solution. How does this relate to homeostasis?

  1. Step 1: Identify transport: osmosis. Why: water crosses a membrane.
  2. Step 2: Identify imbalance: outside solution is salty. Why: this changes water movement.
  3. Step 3: Explain regulation need. Why: cells must regulate water balance to maintain homeostasis.

Interpretation: Transport is one mechanism that affects homeostasis.

Worked Example 3: Evaluate health evidence

Problem: A health claim includes a transmission diagram and a risk-factor table. How should it be evaluated?

  1. Step 1: Identify the claim. Why: evidence must match the claim.
  2. Step 2: Check the diagram and data. Why: visuals and tables can support or limit a claim.
  3. Step 3: Write a CER conclusion. Why: health claims need evidence and reasoning.

Interpretation: Health conclusions should be evidence-based.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Cells, plants, and health are connected because they are all in Biology."

Correct: "Cells, plants, and health connect when cell structures and transport processes support organism functions and evidence-based health explanations."

Teachable explanation: Synthesis requires a specific evidence-based connection, not a broad topic list.
