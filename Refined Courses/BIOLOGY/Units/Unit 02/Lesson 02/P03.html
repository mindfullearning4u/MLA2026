P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue the Teaching Sequence

When a question asks about an organelle, do not memorize a random list. Ask: what job must the cell perform? Then choose the organelle built for that job. If the task is protein building, look for ribosomes. If the task is energy release, look for mitochondria. If the task is storing instructions, look for the nucleus.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the structure, process, or claim in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the table, diagram, model, or scenario. Why: biology answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the standard vocabulary. Why: the correct answer must match the mapped lesson skill.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence. Why: mastery requires reasoning, not just naming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Mitochondria store DNA because they are large organelles."

Correct: "The nucleus stores most cell DNA; mitochondria release usable energy for cell work."

Teachable explanation: Size is not the best clue. Match each organelle to the job it is specialized to perform.
