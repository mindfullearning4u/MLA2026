Worked Example 1: Identify a Macromolecule Function

Problem: A cell uses an enzyme to speed up a chemical reaction. Which macromolecule group does the enzyme most likely belong to?

  1. Step 1: Identify the function. The molecule performs chemical work. Why: Function is the clue that tells you what the molecule does in the cell.
  2. Step 2: Match function to chart. Proteins often act as enzymes. Why: The chart connects each macromolecule group to its common biological role.
  3. Step 3: Choose the group. The enzyme is a protein. Why: The evidence matches the protein function, so protein is the best classification.

Interpretation: The function gives evidence for classification.

Worked Example 2: Interpret a Water Model

Problem: In a water model, oxygen is labeled slightly negative and hydrogen is labeled slightly positive. What property does this show?

  1. Step 1: Read the charge labels. The molecule has uneven charges. Why: The labels show the evidence in the model.
  2. Step 2: Name the property. Uneven charge distribution is polarity. Why: Polarity means a molecule has uneven electrical charge across it.
  3. Step 3: Connect to life. Polarity helps water dissolve many charged or polar substances. Why: Dissolving and moving materials is one reason water is essential for cells.

Interpretation: The model explains why water can transport dissolved materials.

Worked Example 3: Connect Structure and Function

Problem: A molecule stores genetic instructions using a sequence of nucleotide building blocks. Identify the macromolecule and explain why.

  1. Step 1: Identify the building block. The building blocks are nucleotides. Why: Building blocks help identify the macromolecule group.
  2. Step 2: Match to the chart. Nucleic acids are made of nucleotides. Why: The chart links structure to classification.
  3. Step 3: Check the function. Genetic information is a nucleic acid function. Why: A correct classification should match both structure and function when both clues are available.

Interpretation: The molecule is a nucleic acid because both its building blocks and function match that group.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "All large molecules are used for energy."

Correct: "Different macromolecules have different main functions: carbohydrates often provide quick energy, lipids store energy and form membranes, proteins do cell work, and nucleic acids store genetic information."

Teachable explanation: Use structure and function evidence instead of one broad statement.
