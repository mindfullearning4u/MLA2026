Independent Laboratory Analysis

Use only the P03 simulation tables, your calculated values, your graph, and the macromolecule chart. Complete Parts A–C in your notebook. Do not perform household chemical or food tests.

Part A: Analyze Particle Dispersion

  1. Which solvent dispersed all 20 charged particles?
  2. How many more charged particles dispersed in water than in oil?
  3. Which solvent dispersed more nonpolar particles, and what exact values support your answer?
  4. Write one conclusion connecting the pattern to water's polarity. Limit the conclusion to the simulated conditions.

Part B: Analyze Temperature Stability

  1. Show both final-minus-initial calculations with units.
  2. State which material had the smaller temperature change.
  3. Describe what your graph shows using both bar values.
  4. Explain how water's smaller change can help cells or organisms maintain stable conditions.

Part C: Connect the Lab to Macromolecules

Classify each clue and state its biological function: a phospholipid with mostly nonpolar tails; an amino-acid chain acting as an enzyme; a nucleotide sequence storing instructions. Then write four to six sentences explaining why water properties and different macromolecule functions are necessary for cells. Use at least two lab values.

Independent Work Self-Check

Before you submit this independent work, check that each answer connects directly to MLA.BIO.ENE.01. A complete response should name the science idea, point to the data, model, diagram, source detail, or observation used as evidence, and explain why that evidence supports the answer.

  • Step 1: Reread the task and underline the science word or relationship being tested.
  • Step 2: Add the specific evidence you used. Do not write only a general opinion or memory statement.
  • Step 3: Explain the reasoning in one clear sentence so another student could follow your thinking without a live teacher.
  • Step 4: Check units, labels, arrows, categories, or vocabulary before moving to the checkpoint.
