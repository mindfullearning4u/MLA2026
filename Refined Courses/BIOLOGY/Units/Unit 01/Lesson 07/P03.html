Water Polarity and Hydrogen Bonding

A water molecule has one oxygen atom and two hydrogen atoms. Oxygen pulls shared electrons more strongly, so the oxygen side is slightly negative and the hydrogen sides are slightly positive. This uneven charge is called polarity.

Water Polarity Diagram

H(delta+) - O(delta-) - H(delta+)

The slightly positive hydrogen side of one water molecule can attract the slightly negative oxygen side of another water molecule. That attraction is a hydrogen bond.

Virtual Lab Setup: What the Simulation Represents

The following tables are the recorded output of a controlled computer model. Treat each row as one simulated trial. Do not perform the activity with household substances.

  • Investigation A independent variable: solvent type—water or nonpolar oil.
  • Investigation A dependent variable: number of solute particles dispersed after mixing.
  • Investigation B independent variable: material—water or dry sand.
  • Investigation B dependent variable: temperature change after equal energy input.
  • Controlled conditions: equal sample volume, equal number of solute particles, equal mixing time, equal starting temperature, equal energy input, and the same observation time.

Procedure—Follow Every Step

  1. In your notebook, write the laboratory question from P01.
  2. Draw Data Table A and Data Table B exactly as shown below.
  3. Before reading the results, predict which solvent will disperse more charged particles. Write one reason using polarity.
  4. Read Data Table A one row at a time. Record all four trials.
  5. For each row, compare “particles added” with “particles dispersed.”
  6. Read Data Table B. Calculate temperature change for each material using final temperature − initial temperature.
  7. Create a two-bar graph titled “Temperature Change After Equal Energy Input.” Put material on the x-axis and temperature change in °C on the y-axis.
  8. Use the results to complete P06 and the P07 laboratory conclusion.

Graph check: Include a title, two labeled axes, a consistent scale beginning at 0°C, and bars at the calculated values.

Why Water Properties Matter

Water PropertyCauseLife Connection
CohesionWater molecules attract each other.Helps water move as a connected column in plants.
Solvent abilityPolarity attracts charged or polar substances.Helps transport dissolved materials in cells and blood.
Temperature stabilityHydrogen bonds absorb and release heat.Helps organisms maintain stable conditions.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Water is important only because organisms drink it."

Correct: "Water is important because its polarity and hydrogen bonding help dissolve substances, move materials, and stabilize temperature."

Teachable Explanation: Biology explanations should connect water's structure to its function in living systems.
