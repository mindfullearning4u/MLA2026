P01 Lesson Overview

What Counts as Biology Evidence?

Lesson Title: What Counts as Biology Evidence?

Standards Covered in This Lesson

  • MLA.BIO.SCI.01: Use scientific inquiry to ask testable biology questions, collect evidence, analyze data, and communicate explanations.
  • MLA.BIO.SCI.03: Distinguish observations, inferences, models, theories, and science-based decisions in biology contexts.

What You Will Learn

You will learn how biology uses evidence. Biology is not a collection of guesses. It is a science that studies living things by asking testable questions, making careful observations, collecting data, and using that evidence to support claims.

By the end of the lesson, you should be able to tell the difference between an observation, an inference, a claim, and evidence.

What You Will Do

  • Classify statements as observations, inferences, claims, or evidence.
  • Use a claim-evidence-reasoning organizer.
  • Decide whether a question can be tested scientifically.
  • Explain why evidence must match the claim it supports.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will show mastery by completing notebook evidence, guided practice, a checkpoint submission, and the lesson quiz. Your work must show that you can use biological evidence instead of opinion or unsupported guesses.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

This lesson helps you think like a biologist. When you study cells, genetics, evolution, ecology, or human body systems later in the course, you will need to ask, "What evidence supports this explanation?" This lesson teaches that habit first.

Science Safety and Resource Note

This lesson uses safe data analysis, model interpretation, virtual investigation planning, observation evidence, or public-data reasoning. Do not perform any hands-on experiment, field collection, chemical use, specimen handling, heat/flame activity, sharp-tool activity, or outdoor investigation unless your Teacher of Record or school workflow gives specific safety clearance.

Required direct resource links are included when a free, student-safe resource supports the lesson. Use the exact lesson link provided so your evidence matches the lesson.
