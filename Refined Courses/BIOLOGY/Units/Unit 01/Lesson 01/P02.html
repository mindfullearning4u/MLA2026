Notebook Title

Lesson 1: What Counts as Biology Evidence?

Vocabulary

  • Observation: Information gathered directly with senses or tools.
  • Inference: A logical explanation based on observations and prior knowledge.
  • Claim: A statement that answers a question or explains something.
  • Evidence: Data, observations, or reliable source information that supports a claim.
  • Reasoning: The science explanation that connects evidence to a claim.
  • Testable question: A question that can be answered with evidence from an investigation or reliable data.

Biology Starts With Evidence

Biology is the scientific study of living things. A biologist may study cells, organisms, ecosystems, DNA, disease, or how living things change over time. In every case, the biologist must use evidence.

Evidence is what keeps biology scientific. A statement may sound convincing, but it is not a scientific explanation unless it is supported by observations, measurements, data, or reliable scientific sources.

Observation, Inference, Claim, and Evidence

Term What It Means Biology Example
Observation What you directly notice or measure. The plant in Cup A is 12 cm tall.
Inference A logical explanation based on evidence. Cup A may have received more light.
Claim The answer or explanation you are trying to support. Light affects plant growth.
Evidence The data or observations that support the claim. Plants under light grew taller than plants kept in darkness.

Step-by-Step: How to Decide What Counts as Evidence

  1. Read the claim. Identify exactly what the statement says.
  2. Look for data or observations. Evidence must be something measured, observed, or documented by a reliable source.
  3. Check whether the evidence matches the claim. Evidence about plant height supports a claim about plant growth, but it would not support a claim about animal behavior.
  4. Use reasoning. Explain why the evidence supports the claim using biology vocabulary.

Claim-Evidence-Reasoning Organizer

Claim Evidence Reasoning
What answer or explanation is being supported? What data, observation, or source information supports it? What biology idea explains why the evidence supports the claim?
