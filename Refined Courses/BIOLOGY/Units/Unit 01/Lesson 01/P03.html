A Scientific Question Must Be Testable

A biology question is scientific when it can be answered with evidence. That evidence might come from an experiment, a field study, a microscope image, a data table, or a reliable scientific source.

A question is not scientific if it can only be answered by personal opinion, preference, or belief.

Testable and Not Testable

Question Scientific? Why?
Do bean plants grow taller with 8 hours of light or 2 hours of light each day? Yes Plant height and light exposure can be measured.
Are butterflies more beautiful than beetles? No Beauty is a personal opinion, not biological evidence.
Which surface has more bacterial colonies after being swabbed? Yes The number of colonies can be counted using safe approved procedures or provided data.

How to Classify a Statement

  1. Ask whether it is directly noticed or measured. If yes, it is an observation.
  2. Ask whether it explains what might be happening. If yes, it is probably an inference.
  3. Ask whether it answers the main question. If yes, it is a claim.
  4. Ask whether it supports the claim with data or source information. If yes, it is evidence.

Guided Classification Example

Scenario: A student compares two cups of seedlings. Cup A receives 8 hours of light each day. Cup B receives 2 hours of light each day. After 10 days, the seedlings in Cup A average 14 cm tall. The seedlings in Cup B average 6 cm tall.

Statement Classification Reason
Cup A seedlings averaged 14 cm tall. Observation/Data It is measured information.
More daily light increased seedling growth. Claim It explains the result and needs evidence.
Cup A may have grown taller because light supports photosynthesis. Reasoning It connects the evidence to a biology idea.

Common Mistake

Incorrect idea: A claim is evidence because it sounds scientific.

Why this is incorrect: A claim is the statement being supported. Evidence is what supports the claim. Scientific-sounding words do not make a statement evidence.

Correct Thinking

Correct idea: A claim needs evidence. Evidence must be data, observations, or reliable source information that matches the claim.

Teachable explanation: If the claim is "light affects seedling growth," then plant height data from different light conditions is relevant evidence. A statement such as "plants need light" may be true, but by itself it is not enough evidence for the specific investigation unless it is connected to data.
