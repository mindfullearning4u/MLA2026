Worked Example 1: Calculate Magnification

Problem: A microscope has a 10x eyepiece and a 40x objective. What is the total magnification?

  1. Step 1: Identify both lens values. Why: Both lenses enlarge the image.
  2. Step 2: Multiply 10 x 40. Why: Total magnification is eyepiece times objective.
  3. Step 3: Write 400x. Why: The x shows how many times larger the image appears.

Interpretation: The object appears 400 times larger than its actual size.

Worked Example 2: Choose the Right Tool

Problem: A student needs to compare onion cell shapes. Should the student use a hand lens, microscope image, or food web model?

  1. Step 1: Identify the object: cells. Why: Cells are usually too small for unaided observation.
  2. Step 2: Choose the tool that shows cells: microscope image. Why: A microscope provides the needed magnification and detail.
  3. Step 3: Reject unrelated models. Why: A food web model explains feeding relationships, not cell shape.

Interpretation: The tool must match the evidence needed.

Worked Example 3: Interpret a Micrograph

Problem: A micrograph-style image shows repeated box-like shapes with small darker circles inside. What observation and interpretation can be written?

  1. Step 1: Write what is visible: repeated box-like shapes and small circles. Why: Observations should stay close to the image.
  2. Step 2: Use biology vocabulary carefully: the boxes may be plant cell boundaries and the circles may be nuclei. Why: Interpretation connects the image to a concept.
  3. Step 3: Note a limit: the image alone does not show all cell parts. Why: Evidence has boundaries.

Interpretation: The image supports the idea that cells have visible structures, but a labeled model may be needed to learn all parts.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "10x plus 40x equals 50x total magnification."

Correct: "10x times 40x equals 400x total magnification."

Teachable explanation: Each lens magnifies the image produced by the other lens, so microscope magnification is multiplication, not addition.
