Worked Example 1: Match Evidence to Cell Theory

Problem: A microscope image shows that a thin slice of leaf is made of many small compartments. Which part of cell theory does this support?

  1. Step 1: Identify the object. A leaf is part of a plant, and plants are living things. Why: Cell theory applies to living things, so first confirm the object is biological.
  2. Step 2: Identify the evidence. The image shows many cell-like compartments. Why: The visible observation is the evidence, not the conclusion.
  3. Step 3: Connect evidence to theory. The evidence supports that living things are made of cells. Why: The compartments shown by the microscope match the cell theory statement.

Interpretation: One image is not the whole theory, but it is one piece of evidence supporting it.

Worked Example 2: Model or Theory?

Problem: Is a labeled drawing of a plant cell a model or a theory?

  1. Step 1: Ask what it does. The drawing represents cell structures. Why: A representation that helps you study something is usually a model.
  2. Step 2: Ask whether it broadly explains living things. It does not; it shows one representation. Why: A scientific theory explains a broad pattern using many lines of evidence.
  3. Step 3: Classify it. It is a model. Why: The drawing helps communicate the idea, but it is not the evidence-supported explanation itself.

Interpretation: Models help communicate evidence, but a theory explains a broad pattern supported by evidence.

Worked Example 3: Use a Timeline

Problem: Which timeline evidence best supports the statement "all cells come from existing cells"?

EvidenceObservation
Microscope improvedCells became visible.
Cell division observedOne cell split into two cells.
  1. Step 1: Focus on the wording "come from existing cells." Why: The wording tells you which part of cell theory needs support.
  2. Step 2: Choose evidence that shows cells producing cells. Why: Evidence must match the specific statement, not just the general topic of cells.
  3. Step 3: Select cell division observed. Why: Seeing one cell divide into two cells directly supports the idea that cells come from cells.

Interpretation: Cell division directly supports the origin statement in cell theory.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A drawing proves cell theory."

Correct: "A drawing is a model that can show cell structures; repeated observations and data support cell theory."

Teachable explanation: Models communicate ideas, but evidence supports scientific explanations.
