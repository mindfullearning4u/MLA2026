Notebook Title: Evaluating Biology Sources

What the Mapped Standards Require

  • MLA.BIO.SCI.02: Evaluate scientific claims using evidence, reasoning, alternative explanations, reliable sources, and scientific argumentation.
  • SC.912.N.1.3: Scientific claims and argumentation.
  • SC.912.N.1.4: Source reliability under scientific investigation standards.
  • ELA.K12.EE.1.1: Cite evidence to explain and justify reasoning.

Vocabulary

  • Claim: a statement that can be supported or challenged with evidence.
  • Source: the person, organization, article, image, or data set that provides information.
  • Reliability: how trustworthy a source is for a specific scientific question.
  • Competing explanation: a different claim that could explain the same evidence.
  • CER: claim, evidence, and reasoning.

How to Evaluate a Source

Do not accept a biology claim just because it sounds scientific. First decide whether the source is appropriate for the claim. A source can be reliable for one purpose and weak for another.

Checklist QuestionWhy It Matters
Who created the source?Expertise and responsibility affect trustworthiness.
What evidence is provided?Scientific claims need data, observations, or tested results.
Is the source current enough?Biology knowledge can change when new evidence appears.
Could bias affect the claim?A source may select only evidence that supports one view.

Notebook Task

Copy the checklist into your notebook. Under it, write this claim: "The pond algae increased because fertilizer runoff entered the water." Then list two kinds of evidence that would support the claim, such as nutrient measurements and algae counts over time.
