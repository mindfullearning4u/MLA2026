From Evidence to Argument

A scientific argument is not an argument based on volume or opinion. It is a logical explanation built from evidence. Use the CER organizer below whenever you answer a biology claim question.

CER PartWhat to WriteQuestion to Ask
ClaimYour answer or explanation.What do I think is true?
EvidenceSpecific data, observations, or source details.What proves or challenges it?
ReasoningThe science idea connecting evidence to claim.Why does the evidence matter?

Competing Explanations

Sometimes two explanations fit part of the evidence. Compare them by asking which explanation accounts for more of the evidence with fewer unsupported assumptions.

Evidence: Pond algae rose after a week of heavy rain. Nitrate levels rose from 2 mg/L to 12 mg/L. Water temperature stayed nearly the same.

  • Explanation A: Fertilizer runoff increased nutrients and algae growth.
  • Explanation B: Warmer water caused algae growth.

Explanation A is stronger because it uses both the rain event and the nitrate increase. Explanation B is weaker because the temperature evidence does not show warming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The website says fertilizer causes algae, so it must be true."

This uses the source as authority but does not evaluate the source or cite evidence.

Correct: "The fertilizer explanation is better supported because nitrate increased from 2 mg/L to 12 mg/L after heavy rain, while temperature stayed nearly the same."

Teachable Explanation: The correct answer uses specific evidence and rejects a competing explanation that does not match the data.
