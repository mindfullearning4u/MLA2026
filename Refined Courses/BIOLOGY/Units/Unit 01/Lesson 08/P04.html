Worked Example 1: Identify the Investigation Pieces

Problem: A provided data set compares pond nitrate and algae coverage before and after heavy rain. Identify the investigation question, independent variable, dependent variable, and one control.

Investigation PartAnswerWhy It Fits
QuestionDid nutrient runoff affect algae coverage?Why: It can be answered with measured evidence.
Independent variableBefore rain vs. after rain conditionWhy: This is the condition being compared.
Dependent variableAlgae coverageWhy: This is the measured outcome.
ControlSame pond sampling locationWhy: Keeping location consistent makes the comparison fairer.

Interpretation: A synthesis task still starts with the basics: question, variables, measurements, and controls.

Worked Example 2: Choose the Strongest Evidence

Problem: Which evidence best supports the claim that runoff affected the pond sample?

  1. Step 1: Read the claim carefully. It is about runoff affecting the pond sample. Why: The best evidence must match the claim, not just mention biology.
  2. Step 2: Compare the measured data. Nitrate increased from 2 mg/L to 12 mg/L, and algae coverage increased from 10% to 45%. Why: The numbers show the pattern that supports the explanation.
  3. Step 3: Avoid weaker evidence. An opinion post saying the pond "looks greener" is less useful. Why: Measured data are stronger than unsupported descriptions.

Interpretation: The strongest evidence is the data table because it gives measurable changes before and after rain.

Worked Example 3: Write a Unit 1 Synthesis CER

Problem: Write a CER response that uses inquiry, data quality, source reliability, cell theory, and molecule foundations.

  1. Step 1, Claim: Nutrient runoff likely affected the pond sample. Why: The claim directly answers the investigation question.
  2. Step 2, Evidence: Nitrate rose from 2 mg/L to 12 mg/L, algae coverage rose from 10% to 45%, and microscope evidence showed living cells in the sample. Why: These details combine data, tool evidence, and cell theory evidence.
  3. Step 3, Reasoning: Nutrients dissolved in water can support growth of living cells, and reliable measured data are stronger than opinion claims. Why: This reasoning connects water/molecules, cells, source reliability, and data quality.

Interpretation: A strong Unit 1 answer connects several taught skills without adding new biology content.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "The pond changed because all science lessons connect together."

Correct: "The pond data support the runoff explanation because nitrate and algae both increased after rain, and algae are living cells that can use materials dissolved in water."

Teachable explanation: Synthesis does not mean writing a broad sentence. It means using specific evidence from more than one Unit 1 skill.
