Questions, Variables, and Investigation Design

Read the Evidence Before Explaining It

A scientific explanation begins with a visible detail, not a memorized slogan. In the table, the middle column states what is directly given. The final column models the biological meaning. Read across each row.

Evidence and Meaning

Part, Condition, or SourceWhat the Evidence ShowsWhat It Means Biologically
Radish light studyLight duration is 4, 8, or 12 h/day; mean height after 14 days is measured.Light duration is the independent variable and mean height is the dependent variable.
Fair comparisonAll trays use the same seed type, soil mass, water volume, pot size, and temperature.Keeping these conditions constant reduces alternative explanations.
Repeated trialsTen seedlings are grown in each light condition.Replication reveals variation and makes one unusual seedling less influential.
Confounded designThe 12-hour tray also receives twice as much water.Light and water both change, so the cause of a height difference cannot be isolated.
Safe procedureUse a virtual or teacher-approved investigation and record data without tasting or handling unknown materials.Safety requirements are part of valid procedure design.
Data tableColumns are light (h/day), trial number, height (cm), and mean height (cm).Headings identify the variable, measurement, unit, and summary.

Common Mistake to Avoid

Incomplete: “The picture proves the standard.”

Improved: Name the visible structure, value, trend, or relationship; then explain the exact biological process it supports. A model shows selected features and never proves more than it displays.
