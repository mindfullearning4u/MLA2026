A Fair Test Changes One Main Factor

A fair test compares groups in a way that lets you connect the outcome to the independent variable. If too many factors change at the same time, the evidence becomes unclear.

Example: If Cup A gets more light and more water than Cup B, you cannot tell whether height changed because of light, water, or both. To test light, water must stay the same.

Mini Example: Match the Data Table to the Question

Question: How does water amount affect seed germination after 7 days?

The data table should include water amount and number or percent of seeds germinated. It should not focus on leaf color if the question is about germination.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "I will test fertilizer by giving one plant fertilizer, more water, and a bigger pot."

This changes three factors, so the result would not clearly show the effect of fertilizer.

Correct: "I will test fertilizer by changing only the fertilizer amount while keeping plant type, water amount, light, soil, pot size, and time the same."

Teachable Explanation: Controls protect the investigation from confusing evidence. They make it more reasonable to connect the measured outcome to the independent variable.
