Worked Example 1: Choose a Safe Procedure

Problem: A student wants to compare bacteria growth on surfaces at home. What is the safest course format?

  1. Step 1: Identify the risk. Why: Growing unknown microbes can be unsafe.
  2. Step 2: Replace the physical lab with provided images or data. Why: The standard can be met by safe data analysis.
  3. Step 3: Analyze the provided table and write a CER response. Why: Evidence reasoning remains the learning goal.

Interpretation: Safe science uses the safest method that still produces usable evidence.

Worked Example 2: Read Measurement Precision

Problem: Two students measure the same seedling as 6 cm and 6.2 cm. Which measurement is more precise?

  1. Step 1: Compare the place value. Why: More decimal places usually show a more specific measurement.
  2. Step 2: Check the unit. Why: A number without a unit is incomplete.
  3. Step 3: State the answer. Why: Precision describes how detailed the measurement is.

Answer: 6.2 cm is more precise than 6 cm because it measures to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.

Interpretation: Precision helps readers understand how carefully data were recorded.

Worked Example 3: Interpret an Outlier

Problem: Trial values are 8.1, 8.2, 8.0, and 18.4 cm. What should the student do before making a conclusion?

  1. Step 1: Identify the pattern. Why: Three values cluster around 8 cm.
  2. Step 2: Flag 18.4 cm as a possible outlier. Why: It is far from the pattern.
  3. Step 3: Look for a recording or procedure explanation. Why: A value should be explained before it is removed or emphasized.

Interpretation: The conclusion should mention data quality because one value may not represent the condition accurately.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Safety rules are separate from data quality."

Correct: "Safety rules affect data quality because rushed, unsafe, or unclear procedures can cause unreliable measurements."

Teachable explanation: Safe procedure and reliable evidence are connected. Careful procedure reduces risk and makes the data easier to trust.
