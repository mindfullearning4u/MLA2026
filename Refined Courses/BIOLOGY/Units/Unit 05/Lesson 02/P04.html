Worked Example 1: Identify selection pressure

Problem: A drought makes large seeds more common. Birds with larger beaks survive better. What is the selection pressure?

  1. Step 1: Identify the environmental change. Why: selection pressure comes from environment.
  2. Step 2: Name drought/seed size change. Why: it affects food access.
  3. Step 3: Connect to survival. Why: birds with helpful beaks survive/reproduce more.

Interpretation: The drought-related food change is the selection pressure.

Worked Example 2: Use frequency data

Problem: A dark moth trait rises from 20% to 75% after pollution darkens trees. What changed?

  1. Step 1: Read starting frequency. Why: baseline matters.
  2. Step 2: Read ending frequency. Why: change over generations is evidence.
  3. Step 3: Connect to selection if inherited and survival differs. Why: helpful camouflage may increase reproduction.

Interpretation: Trait frequency changed in the population.

Worked Example 3: Avoid purpose language

Problem: Why is 'the rabbit grew thicker fur because it needed it' inaccurate?

  1. Step 1: Identify need/want wording. Why: that implies purpose.
  2. Step 2: Recall variation already exists. Why: selection acts on inherited differences.
  3. Step 3: Correct with population change. Why: thick-fur rabbits may leave more offspring in cold conditions.

Interpretation: Natural selection is not individual wish-based change.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Animals evolve because they need or want a better trait."

Correct: "Natural selection changes trait frequencies when inherited variation affects survival and reproduction."

Teachable explanation: Selection acts on existing inherited variation; it does not create traits because organisms want them.
