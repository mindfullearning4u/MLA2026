Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the evolution concept, mechanism, relationship, or trend in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the graph, evidence chart, cladogram, classification table, or timeline. Why: evolution answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence and include any limit. Why: mastery requires careful reasoning, not overclaiming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Every older hominid fossil is a direct ancestor of every newer hominid."

Correct: "Fossils can show trends and relationships, but direct ancestry requires careful evidence and is not assumed from age alone."

Teachable explanation: Trend data supports patterns; it does not automatically prove direct ancestor-descendant relationships.

How to Read the Fossil Model

  • Age: millions of years before present (Ma). A larger number is older.
  • Cranial capacity: internal skull volume in cubic centimeters (cm3); it is not a direct measure of intelligence.
  • Bipedal score: a simplified 1-5 index from skeletal indicators, where 5 means the strongest modeled evidence of habitual upright walking.
  • Jaw projection score: a simplified 1-5 index, where 5 means more projection.
  • Behavior evidence: artifacts associated with a site, not proof that one individual made them.

Procedure - Follow Every Step

  1. Copy the laboratory question and data table.
  2. Arrange Specimens A-E from oldest to most recent using age.
  3. Create a line graph with age before present on the x-axis and cranial capacity on the y-axis. Place the oldest age at the left and the most recent at the right.
  4. Create a second two-line graph for bipedal score and jaw-projection score. Include a key.
  5. Identify increases, decreases, exceptions, and measurements that remain unchanged.
  6. Use the trait matrix to identify the two specimens with the most similar measured traits.
  7. Write conclusions as trends in this sample. Do not call the specimens a direct sequence of ancestors.
  8. Complete P06 and the P07 CER.
