P03 Notebook Task - Part 2

Continue the Teaching Sequence

Evaluate origin-of-life models by separating supported evidence from speculation. A model may show that certain molecules can form under certain conditions, but it may not prove every step from chemistry to cells. Careful scientific reasoning includes both support and limits.

Step-by-Step Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the evolution concept, mechanism, relationship, or trend in the question. Why: this prevents guessing from topic words only.
  2. Find the evidence in the graph, evidence chart, cladogram, classification table, or timeline. Why: evolution answers must be supported by evidence.
  3. Connect the evidence to the mapped standard vocabulary. Why: the answer must stay inside the lesson scope.
  4. Explain the conclusion in one complete sentence and include any limit. Why: mastery requires careful reasoning, not overclaiming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "A scientific model must answer every origin question completely or it is useless."

Correct: "A scientific model can support part of an explanation while still having limits."

Teachable explanation: Science often builds explanations from partial, testable evidence and revises them as evidence improves.
