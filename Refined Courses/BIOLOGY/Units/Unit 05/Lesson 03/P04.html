Worked Example 1: Identify gene flow

Problem: Several individuals move into a population and reproduce, adding allele B. What mechanism is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify movement between populations. Why: migration is the clue.
  2. Step 2: Check reproduction. Why: alleles enter only if migrants reproduce.
  3. Step 3: Name gene flow. Why: alleles moved between populations.

Interpretation: Migration plus reproduction supports gene flow.

Worked Example 2: Identify drift

Problem: A storm randomly leaves only 8 beetles alive, changing allele frequencies. What mechanism is shown?

  1. Step 1: Identify random event. Why: drift is chance-based.
  2. Step 2: Notice small surviving group. Why: drift is stronger in small populations.
  3. Step 3: Name genetic drift. Why: allele frequencies changed by chance.

Interpretation: Random survival supports genetic drift.

Worked Example 3: Identify mutation

Problem: A DNA change creates a new allele in a gamete. What mechanism creates the allele?

  1. Step 1: Identify DNA change. Why: mutation is sequence change.
  2. Step 2: Note new allele. Why: mutation can create new variation.
  3. Step 3: Connect to inheritance if in gamete. Why: it can enter the population.

Interpretation: Mutation can introduce new alleles.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "All allele-frequency changes are natural selection."

Correct: "Allele frequencies can change through mutation, recombination, drift, gene flow, or natural selection depending on the evidence."

Teachable explanation: The mechanism must match the scenario evidence.
