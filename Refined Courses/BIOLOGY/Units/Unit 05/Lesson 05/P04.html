Worked Example 1: Use cell type

Problem: An organism has cells with nuclei. Which domain is supported?

  1. Step 1: Identify nucleus evidence. Why: nucleus is a cell-type clue.
  2. Step 2: Connect nucleus to eukaryotic cells. Why: Eukarya has nuclei.
  3. Step 3: Name Eukarya. Why: the domain is defined by that evidence.

Interpretation: A nucleus supports Eukarya.

Worked Example 2: Separate bacteria and archaea

Problem: Two organisms are prokaryotic, but one has molecular traits distinct from bacteria. What should you check?

  1. Step 1: Recognize both lack nuclei. Why: both are prokaryotic.
  2. Step 2: Use molecular/cell membrane evidence. Why: Bacteria and Archaea differ there.
  3. Step 3: Avoid classifying by size alone. Why: both may be microscopic.

Interpretation: Prokaryotes can belong to different domains.

Worked Example 3: Use kingdom traits

Problem: A multicellular eukaryote absorbs nutrients and has cell walls. Which kingdom is suggested?

  1. Step 1: Identify eukaryote. Why: kingdom options here are in Eukarya.
  2. Step 2: Find nutrient mode: absorption. Why: fungi absorb nutrients.
  3. Step 3: Use cell wall evidence. Why: fungi also have cell walls.

Interpretation: The evidence supports fungi.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "All microscopic organisms belong to the same domain."

Correct: "Microscopic organisms can belong to different domains; classification depends on cellular and molecular traits."

Teachable explanation: Size alone is weak classification evidence.
