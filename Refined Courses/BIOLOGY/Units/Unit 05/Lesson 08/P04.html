  • MA.K12.MTR.5.1: Use patterns and structure to connect concepts.

Worked Example 1: Connect evidence and mechanism

Problem: A fossil record shows change and a selection graph shows trait-frequency shift. How do they work together?

  1. Step 1: Identify fossil evidence. Why: it supports change over time.
  2. Step 2: Identify selection graph. Why: it shows a possible mechanism.
  3. Step 3: Connect evidence and mechanism. Why: one supports pattern, the other explains process.

Interpretation: Evidence and mechanism answer different parts of the explanation.

Worked Example 2: Connect classification

Problem: A cladogram and DNA table group two species closely. What does that support?

  1. Step 1: Read the cladogram relationship. Why: branch points show relatedness.
  2. Step 2: Use DNA similarity. Why: molecular data is independent evidence.
  3. Step 3: Support close relationship. Why: both evidence sources agree.

Interpretation: Classification can be evidence-based.

Worked Example 3: Use cautious trend reasoning

Problem: A hominid chart shows a broad cranial capacity trend. What should the answer avoid?

  1. Step 1: State the trend. Why: data supports a pattern.
  2. Step 2: Avoid direct ancestry claims from age alone. Why: more evidence is needed.
  3. Step 3: Avoid value ranking. Why: evolution is not a ladder.

Interpretation: Careful trend reasoning avoids overclaiming.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: "Evolution, classification, and hominid trends are all the same thing."

Correct: "Evolution evidence supports change and relatedness, mechanisms explain how populations change, and classification organizes relationships using evidence."

Teachable explanation: Synthesis requires specific relationships, not a list of topic words.
