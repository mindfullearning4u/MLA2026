# BIO - Biology
**Credit:** 1.0 High School Science Credit  
**Course Type:** Mastery-Based High School Laboratory Science Course  
**Delivery Format:** Self-Directed Online Instruction with Teacher of Record Support

---

## Course Description

Biology is a laboratory science course focused on living systems, scientific inquiry, data analysis, cells, homeostasis, cellular energy, DNA, genetics, evolution, classification, ecology, interdependence, and human impact.

The course is written for students completing lessons independently in Moodle. A live teacher is not assumed to be present during instruction. Therefore, each lesson must function like a teacher guiding a class: unfamiliar vocabulary is defined before use; processes and relationships are explained in small steps; evidence sources are displayed where students need them; worked examples show how and why an answer is derived; practice directions identify exactly what to observe, record, explain, and submit; and checkpoints state the finished response requirements.

The instructional supports needed for foundational review, grade-level mastery, and deeper application are embedded in the same lesson without labeling students by level. Students who already understand prerequisite ideas may progress more quickly, while students who need more support can use definitions, models, completed examples, reasoning frames, common-mistake corrections, self-checks, and optional resources.

Teacher of Record support remains available for clarification, checkpoint feedback, remediation, revision, and retake workflow. Teacher support supplements the lesson; it does not replace complete student-facing instruction.

This is a laboratory science course. Lessons 1-6 in each unit teach the core content through the standard seven-page instructional workflow. Lesson 7 in each unit is the dedicated laboratory, simulation, model, graph, or data-investigation lesson. Lesson 8 is the unit synthesis and assessment lesson.

The approved course architecture contains 48 lessons, 336 Moodle-fragment HTML lesson pages, 102 Moodle XML assessment banks, and 1,590 Moodle XML questions.

## Learning Objectives and Outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Ask testable biological questions and distinguish observations, inferences, scientific explanations, and unsupported claims.
- Identify variables, controls, measurements, limitations, and safety requirements in biological investigations.
- Read and interpret biological models, diagrams, microscope representations, tables, graphs, sequences, maps, and data displays.
- Compare cell types and relate biological structures to their functions.
- Explain membrane transport, homeostasis, and relationships among plant and human body systems.
- Explain how water, macromolecules, enzymes, ATP, photosynthesis, and cellular respiration support living systems.
- Explain DNA replication, gene expression, mutation, inheritance, mitosis, meiosis, and sources of genetic variation.
- Evaluate evidence for evolution, natural selection, classification, diversity, and scientific origin-of-life explanations without overclaiming beyond the evidence.
- Analyze ecosystem change, populations, food webs, energy transfer, biodiversity, invasive species, resource use, sustainability, and human impact.
- Construct clear claim-evidence-reasoning explanations that cite exact biological evidence and state appropriate limitations.

## Prerequisite Knowledge and Embedded Support

The course does not assume that students already know specialized biology vocabulary, laboratory routines, graph-reading conventions, or scientific reasoning formats. When prior knowledge is useful, the lesson introduces or reviews it before students are expected to apply it.

Each lesson may include:

- Student-friendly definitions with biological examples
- Labeled models, data tables, graphs, diagrams, sequences, maps, or scenarios
- A step-by-step reasoning routine explaining what to do, why to do it, and how to check the result
- Fully solved worked examples using the displayed evidence
- Common incorrect answers followed by correction and explanation
- Sentence frames and completed guided-note checks
- Independent-work completion criteria and checkpoint submission checklists
- Optional extension or review resources for further understanding

These supports are available to every student. They are not identified as remedial, standard, or accelerated assignments.

## Course Structure

Approved unit sequence:

- **Unit 1: Scientific Thinking and Biology Foundations**
- **Unit 2: Cells, Homeostasis, and Transport**
- **Unit 3: Cellular Energy and Life Processes**
- **Unit 4: DNA, Genetics, and Heredity**
- **Unit 5: Evolution, Classification, and Diversity of Life**
- **Unit 6: Ecology, Interdependence, and Human Impact**

Each unit begins with a Unit Pretest. Lessons 1-6 follow the standard ALG1-style MLA instructional shell adapted for Biology. Lesson 7 is the dedicated science investigation. Lesson 8 is Putting It All Together and contains Guided Practice plus the Unit Assessment instead of a Lesson Quiz.

Required evidence is self-contained in the lesson page and repeated in the relevant Moodle XML question stimulus when students need it for an assessment. Students are not expected to locate an unnamed model, graph, table, simulation, passage, or website.

## Standard Lesson Workflow: Lessons 1-6

### P01 - Lesson Overview

Introduces the lesson title, mapped standards and their meaning, learning target, required activities, mastery evidence, safety expectations, and Teacher of Record support.

### P02 - Notebook Task Part 1

Defines vocabulary and teaches the core structure, process, or relationship. The page includes the model, table, graph, diagram, sequence, or scenario students must use and explains how to read it. A teacher model demonstrates how to identify the concept, locate evidence, describe what the evidence shows, and connect it to the biological function or process.

### P03 - Notebook Task Part 2

Deepens the concept with a second explanation, a guided reasoning routine, a common-mistake correction, and a completed guided-notes check. When an outside resource is offered, it is clearly labeled optional unless the approved mapping makes it part of Lesson 7.

### P04 - Worked Examples

Provides three distinct, fully solved examples. Each example displays or reproduces the evidence needed, works through the answer step by step, explains why each step is taken, gives the complete answer, and states the limits of the evidence. Examples use different biological questions so students practice transferring the reasoning process.

### P05 - Guided Practice

Directs students to the Moodle Guided Practice. Questions use lesson-aligned stimuli and provide immediate, answer-specific feedback that explains why the selected answer is correct or incorrect.

### P06 - Independent Work

Reproduces the required evidence source and states exactly what to complete in Parts A, B, and C. Students label or classify evidence, interpret or compare biological information, and complete a claim-evidence-reasoning application. The page defines what the finished notebook work must contain.

### P07 - Checkpoint

Reproduces the required evidence, states the exact graded question, identifies notebook evidence to submit, defines the response format, gives mastery criteria, and explains the revision or resubmission workflow. A checkpoint must be answerable without a live teacher or an undisclosed source.

### Lesson Quiz

Measures lesson-level mastery using self-contained questions, necessary visuals or data, one correct answer, and answer-specific feedback.

## Lesson 7 Laboratory and Data-Investigation Workflow

Lesson 7 is the dedicated investigation lesson in every unit. It may use a course-contained virtual laboratory, simulation, model, graph, mapped data set, or public-data investigation. It is not a short reading followed by generic questions.

Lesson 7 follows this science workflow:

1. **Investigation Overview:** purpose, mapped standards, biological context, safety, required evidence, and final submission.
2. **Question and Setup:** testable question, hypothesis or prediction when appropriate, independent and dependent variables, controlled conditions, comparison groups, and a notebook data organizer.
3. **Procedure and Evidence Path:** numbered directions stating what to open or examine, what to click or choose when a simulation is used, what variable to change, what to keep constant, what to observe, how many trials or comparisons to complete, and exactly what to record.
4. **Data Collection and Analysis:** displayed data or an accessible recording table, graphing directions, calculations when required, pattern identification, limitations, and a worked interpretation.
5. **Lab-Based Guided Practice:** questions based on the investigation setup, procedure, variables, safety, data, graph, limitations, and evidence.
6. **Independent Investigation Work:** completed data organizer, analysis questions, graph or model interpretation, and CER response.
7. **Lab/Data Checkpoint:** exact submission list and a polished evidence-based explanation using the investigation results.
8. **Lesson Quiz:** lab-aligned questions with the necessary stimulus reproduced in the assessment.

If a required simulation or website is used, the lesson must provide a direct link to the exact activity and step-by-step directions for opening, navigating, changing variables, observing, recording, and returning to Moodle. Required external resources must be free and must not require a subscription or student login.

Lesson Overview -> Notebook Task Part 1 -> Notebook Task Part 2 -> Worked Example and Synthesis Modeling -> Guided Practice -> Independent Synthesis Work -> Checkpoint -> Unit Assessment

Lesson 8 contains no separate Lesson Quiz bank. The Unit Assessment replaces it.

## External Resource Policy

Required instruction, evidence, and assessment stimuli are included within the course. Additional websites may be offered for review, extension, or further understanding, but they must:

- Be clearly labeled optional
- Be free to access
- Open directly to the relevant material
- Require no subscription, payment, or student login
- Include brief directions explaining what the student may examine
- Be excluded from required notebook and checkpoint submission evidence

An optional resource must never be the only place a student can find information required to answer a graded question.

## Assessment Structure

- **Unit Pretest:** 10 Moodle XML questions per unit; diagnostic and not a substitute for required mastery evidence.
- **Guided Practice:** 5 Moodle XML questions in every lesson, including Lessons 7 and 8.
- **Lesson Quiz:** 25 Moodle XML questions in Lessons 1-7. Lesson 7 quizzes are based on the laboratory or data investigation.
- **Unit Assessment:** 40 Moodle XML questions in Lesson 8.
- **Notebook Evidence:** vocabulary, teaching notes, models or data organizers, guided-note corrections, and completed independent work identified on the checkpoint page.
- **Checkpoint Response:** the exact standards-aligned response requested on P07, normally including a claim, at least two precise pieces of displayed evidence, biological reasoning, and an evidence boundary or limitation when appropriate.

Audit-confirmed production totals:

- Unit Pretests: 6 banks, 60 questions
- Guided Practice: 48 banks, 240 questions
- Lesson Quizzes: 42 banks, 1,050 questions
- Unit Assessments: 6 banks, 240 questions
- Total Moodle XML: 102 banks, 1,590 questions

Assessment items must include the visual, table, graph, model, sequence, map, or scenario needed to answer the question. Every multiple-choice item must contain four meaningful choices, exactly one correct answer, answer-specific teachable feedback, and a trace to the mapped lesson standard.

## Submission, Mastery, and Progression

Students must follow the submission instructions displayed on each checkpoint page. A complete submission includes the listed notebook evidence and the final checkpoint response in the required Moodle location. Students should not submit only copied definitions, an unlabeled table, or an unsupported answer.

Mastery requires at least 80% and accurate use of standards-aligned biological evidence and reasoning. When mastery is not demonstrated, students use feedback to correct misunderstandings, complete assigned intervention or additional practice, and revise or resubmit according to the Teacher of Record workflow.

Students with strong prior knowledge may move through explicit instruction and examples more quickly, but all required mastery evidence and assessments remain standards aligned. Progression is based on demonstrated mastery rather than time spent in the course.
