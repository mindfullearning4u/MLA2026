# BIO - Biology

**Credit:** 1.0 High School Science Credit  
**Lab Designation:** High School Laboratory Science  
**Course Type:** Mastery-Based High School Biology Course  
**Florida Course Reference:** Biology 1, course number 2000310

---

## Course Description

Biology is a mastery-based high-school laboratory science course focused on living systems, scientific inquiry, data analysis, cells, homeostasis, cellular energy, DNA, genetics, evolution, classification, ecology, interdependence, and human impact.

Students develop scientific thinking through evidence-based reasoning, experimental design, variables, lab safety, measurement, biological models, data displays, source evaluation, scientific argumentation, and technical communication. The course emphasizes Biology 1 expectations while maintaining clear boundaries from Chemistry, Physics, Environmental Science, Earth/Space Science, Marine Science, and Anatomy and Physiology.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of Biology, students will be able to:

- Ask testable biological questions and design evidence-based investigations.
- Apply lab safety, measurement, data collection, and data interpretation expectations.
- Evaluate scientific claims, sources, explanations, theories, laws, observations, and inferences.
- Explain cell theory, cell structure, transport, and homeostasis.
- Relate selected plant and human structures to biological functions required by Biology 1.
- Explain biological molecules, water, enzymes, ATP, photosynthesis, cellular respiration, and matter/energy cycling.
- Analyze DNA replication, gene expression, mutation, inheritance, mitosis, meiosis, biotechnology, reproduction, and genetic health connections.
- Explain evidence for evolution, natural selection, evolutionary mechanisms, classification, diversity, origin-of-life explanations, and hominid evolution trends.
- Analyze aquatic systems, ecosystem change, populations, food webs, biodiversity, resource use, sustainability, environmental monitoring, and human impact.
- Communicate scientific findings using evidence, data, models, technical vocabulary, appropriate format, and appropriate tone.

## Prerequisite Knowledge

Students should enter Biology with general middle school science readiness, including:

- reading grade-level informational text
- using basic scientific vocabulary
- identifying observations and evidence
- reading tables, graphs, and diagrams
- performing basic measurement
- following written procedures
- using arithmetic and proportional reasoning in science contexts
- writing short evidence-based explanations

Students are not expected to begin with mastery of high-school Biology 1 benchmarks. Inquiry, data analysis, biological vocabulary, lab expectations, and standards-based reasoning are explicitly built across the course.

## Six-Unit Sequence

| Unit | Unit Title | Course Role |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | Scientific Thinking and Biology Foundations | Establishes inquiry, lab safety, tools, evidence, cell theory, molecules, and water. |
| Unit 2 | Cells, Homeostasis, and Transport | Develops cells, structures, transport, homeostasis, selected systems, and health connections. |
| Unit 3 | Cellular Energy and Life Processes | Develops enzymes, ATP, photosynthesis, respiration, matter cycling, and energy flow. |
| Unit 4 | DNA, Genetics, and Heredity | Develops DNA, gene expression, mutation, inheritance, cell division, biotechnology, and reproduction. |
| Unit 5 | Evolution, Classification, and Diversity of Life | Develops evolution evidence, natural selection, variation, classification, diversity, and origins topics. |
| Unit 6 | Ecology, Interdependence, and Human Impact | Develops ecosystems, populations, food webs, biodiversity, sustainability, monitoring, and human impact. |

## Assessment Structure

Biology uses the approved MLA assessment model:

### Unit Pretests

Each unit begins with a diagnostic Unit Pretest aligned to the unit's MLA standards and Florida benchmark expectations.

### Notebook Evidence

Notebook evidence may include observations, models, data tables, graph interpretations, vocabulary reasoning, source notes, investigation planning, safety reasoning, and evidence-based explanations.

### Guided Practice

Lessons 1-7 include guided practice aligned to the lesson's primary standards.

### Checkpoints

Checkpoints require students to apply the lesson's standards independently through biological evidence, data, models, explanations, or argumentation.

### Lesson Quizzes

Lessons 1-7 include standards-aligned lesson quizzes.

### Unit Assessments

Lesson 8 in every unit is titled Putting It All Together and contains the Unit Assessment. Lesson 8 synthesizes standards already taught in Lessons 1-7 and introduces no new primary standard.

## Lab Expectations

Biology is a laboratory science course. Lab architecture must include:

- scientific inquiry
- safe procedures
- variables
- measurement
- biological tools or models
- data tables and graphs
- evidence-based interpretation
- technical communication
- reflection on uncertainty, error, and reliability where appropriate

---

## Virtual Lab Expectations

Virtual labs may use simulations, models, microscopy images, authentic data sets, field-observation data, case data, or graph-based investigations. Virtual labs must preserve the lab purpose by requiring students to observe, collect or interpret data, identify variables, analyze evidence, and communicate conclusions.

## Mastery-Based Credit Structure

Students earn credit through demonstrated mastery of Biology standards across unit pretests, lesson evidence, checkpoints, lesson quizzes, and unit assessments. Mastery evidence must align to the MLA Biology standards and the Florida Biology 1 benchmark expectations in the architecture package.
