## Alignment Decision

World History is governed first by the Florida CPALMS Social Studies standards assigned to course #2109310.

The course also includes college-readiness and literacy support alignment through:

- Florida B.E.S.T. / ELA evidence expectations included in the CPALMS course listing
- ELD Social Studies communication support
- Common Core literacy-in-history/social-studies style skills where appropriate as support skills
- SAT reading, evidence, vocabulary-in-context, informational-text, chart/table, and argument-readiness skills
- ACT reading, social science passage, data interpretation, main idea, inference, and evidence-based reasoning skills

These support alignments do not replace the CPALMS Social Studies standards. They strengthen how students read, write, reason, and show mastery inside the World History standards.

## Crosswalk Enforcement Rule

Every World History crosswalk row must preserve this logic:

1. The primary row standard must be a CPALMS/FDOE World History Social Studies benchmark or required CPALMS-listed support standard.
2. Florida B.E.S.T./ELA, ELD, Common Core, SAT, and ACT are documented as support alignments attached to the historical thinking, source reading, writing, map/chart, data, and evidence demands of the lesson.
3. A support alignment cannot create a new lesson topic, add unmapped historical content, or move content outside the approved unit/lesson sequence.
4. Assessments must test the assigned Social Studies standard and may also require evidence reading, passage interpretation, map/chart analysis, or written reasoning that supports B.E.S.T./Common Core/SAT/ACT readiness.
5. If a lesson has a source, passage, map, timeline, chart, table, political cartoon, image, or data display requirement, that stimulus must be placed directly in the lesson or XML assessment question so students do not have to search elsewhere.

## Common Core Literacy Support Alignment

Common Core is treated as a support alignment only. It is used to reinforce literacy skills commonly expected in history/social studies coursework:

| Common Core Literacy Skill Area | World History Course Application |
|---|---|
| Cite textual evidence | Students support claims with primary/secondary source evidence. |
| Determine central ideas | Students identify central claims in historical sources, speeches, charts, and textbook-style passages. |
| Analyze sequence and cause/effect | Students use timelines and historical narratives to explain relationships among events. |
| Compare perspectives | Students compare interpretations, regions, civilizations, and historical actors. |
| Integrate visual information | Students use maps, tables, charts, timelines, and historical visuals as evidence. |
| Write explanatory/argument responses | Students write structured historical explanations using claims, evidence, and reasoning. |

## SAT Readiness Alignment

SAT readiness is embedded as reading and evidence practice, not as a separate course standard.

| SAT Readiness Skill | World History Course Application |
|---|---|
| Command of evidence | Students choose and explain evidence that supports a historical claim. |
| Words in context | Students learn historical vocabulary in context rather than as isolated definitions. |
| Central idea and purpose | Students identify the purpose and main idea of historical passages and sources. |
| Inference | Students infer causes, motives, consequences, and limitations from evidence. |
| Data and graphics | Students interpret maps, timelines, charts, demographic tables, and comparison matrices. |
| Cross-text connection | Students compare sources, perspectives, and historical interpretations. |

## ACT Readiness Alignment

ACT readiness is embedded as social science reading, data interpretation, and reasoning practice.

| ACT Readiness Skill | World History Course Application |
|---|---|
| Main idea and details | Students identify the main claim and supporting evidence in historical text. |
| Sequential and comparative relationships | Students use chronology, comparison, and continuity/change reasoning. |
| Inference and interpretation | Students interpret source meaning, author purpose, and historical significance. |
| Data representation | Students read tables, maps, timelines, and charts embedded in lessons and assessments. |
| Evaluation of evidence | Students distinguish strong evidence from related but incomplete facts. |
| Writing and revision readiness | Students build claims, organize evidence, and revise checkpoint responses from feedback. |

## Course Production Requirement

Every World History lesson and assessment must preserve this hierarchy:

1. CPALMS World History Social Studies standards define required content.
2. Unit mapping and lesson mapping define where each standard is taught and assessed.
3. Florida B.E.S.T. / ELA, ELD, Common Core literacy, SAT, and ACT readiness skills support the reading, writing, evidence, and reasoning demands of the lesson.
4. Support alignments must not introduce off-standard or future-unit content.

## Audit Requirement

During unit and course audits, agents must verify:

- CPALMS Social Studies standards are taught and assessed.
- Florida B.E.S.T., CPALMS, Common Core, ACT, and SAT were checked as part of crosswalk and mapping validation.
- Evidence, reading, writing, and source-analysis tasks are present.
- Maps, timelines, tables, charts, passages, and primary/secondary source stimuli appear when needed.
- SAT/ACT readiness is embedded through evidence-based reasoning and visual/data interpretation.
- Common Core literacy support does not replace or distort Florida course standards.

Final Decision: PASS
