Unit 1, Lesson 1
Lesson Identification
Field	Specification
Lesson Title	Historical Inquiry, Timelines, and Cause and Effect
Lesson Type	Instructional Lesson
MLA Standards	MLA.WH.HI.01, MLA.WH.HI.02, MLA.WH.HI.03
Investigation Question	How do historians use evidence to determine what most likely happened in the past?
Primary Historical-Thinking Skill	Historical Inquiry
Supporting Skills	Chronology, Corroboration, Cause and Effect, Claim-Evidence-Reasoning
Lesson Focus

Students learn how historians ask questions, organize chronology, examine primary and secondary sources, compare evidence, recognize limitations, and construct defensible historical conclusions.

Required Historical Content
Purpose and process of historical inquiry
Difference between evidence, interpretation, opinion, and unsupported claim
Primary and secondary sources
Chronology and sequencing
Historical context
Long-term cause, immediate cause, event, and effect
Corroboration across sources
Reliability, usefulness, and limitations of evidence
How historians revise conclusions when new evidence appears
Required Vocabulary
Term	Required Student-Friendly Meaning
Historical inquiry	The process of asking questions and using evidence to investigate the past
Evidence	Information that supports or challenges a historical claim
Primary source	Evidence created during the period being studied by a person or group connected to it
Secondary source	An explanation or interpretation created later using historical evidence
Chronology	The order in which events occurred
Context	The time, place, conditions, beliefs, and circumstances surrounding an event or source
Corroboration	Comparing multiple sources to identify agreement, disagreement, and stronger support
Cause	A condition, action, or event that contributed to another event
Effect	A result or consequence
Historical claim	A conclusion about the past that must be supported with evidence
Interpretation	An evidence-based explanation of what the past means
Limitation	What a source cannot establish by itself
Required Evidence and Visuals
Original MLA historical-inquiry process SVG: Question → Context → Evidence → Comparison → Conclusion
Chronology timeline showing background condition, immediate cause, event, and effect
Primary-versus-secondary-source comparison table
Corroboration table containing two sources that partly agree and partly differ
Claim-evidence-reasoning flowchart
No political map is required
Required Historical Sources

Use a short, age-appropriate evidence case that does not require substantial prior historical knowledge.

Evidence A: Brief primary-source account
Evidence B: Brief secondary-source interpretation
Evidence C: Timeline, artifact record, population entry, or other non-narrative evidence
Sources must support more than one possible initial explanation, but one conclusion must be better corroborated
Do not fabricate quotations or sources
