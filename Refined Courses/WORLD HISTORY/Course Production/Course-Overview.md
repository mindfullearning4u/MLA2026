<section class="mla-course-header">
  <h1>WH – World History</h1>

  <div class="mla-course-meta">
    <p><strong>Credit:</strong> 1.0 High School Social Studies Credit</p>
    <p><strong>Course Type:</strong> Mastery-Based High School World History</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Course Description</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>
      World History develops students’ ability to explain global change over time by investigating
      historical evidence, geography, chronology, primary and secondary sources, maps, timelines,
      historical data, comparisons, and cause-and-effect relationships.
    </p>

    <p>
      Students learn to think like historians by asking historical questions, examining evidence,
      evaluating sources, analyzing different perspectives, identifying patterns, and constructing
      evidence-based explanations. Throughout the course, students investigate how societies,
      civilizations, religions, governments, economies, technologies, conflicts, and global
      connections developed and changed over time.
    </p>

    <p>
      Instruction is designed for independent student mastery in an online learning environment.
      Each lesson moves students from historical understanding to evidence analysis, guided
      investigation, independent investigation, and mastery demonstration.
    </p>

    <p>
      Teacher of Record support is available for clarification, feedback, remediation, revision,
      reassessment, and mastery progression when needed.
    </p>

    <div class="mla-highlight">
      <strong>Central Course Approach:</strong> Students do more than learn what happened. They
      investigate why historical developments occurred, what evidence supports historical
      conclusions, how societies changed, and why those developments continue to matter.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Standards Alignment</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>
      As a registered private school in the State of Florida, Mindful Learning Academy uses Florida
      academic standards and official course expectations as the primary academic framework for
      this course.
    </p>

    <p>
      To support students across the United States and prepare them for future academic success,
      course competencies are also cross-referenced to applicable national and college-readiness
      frameworks, including:
    </p>

    <ul>
      <li>Florida World History standards and CPALMS course expectations</li>
      <li>Florida social studies and literacy expectations</li>
      <li>Common Core Literacy in History/Social Studies standards</li>
      <li>SAT evidence, reading, interpretation, and data-analysis expectations</li>
      <li>ACT reading, research, source-analysis, and communication expectations</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Mindful Learning Academy uses the MLA Standards Framework, a competency-based system that
      organizes learning outcomes, assessments, and mastery progression. Each MLA competency is
      mapped to one or more external academic frameworks to support comprehensive standards
      coverage and academic rigor.
    </p>

    <p>
      Students residing in states that do not use Florida or Common Core standards should notify
      the Academy during enrollment. Upon request, MLA can provide information regarding how
      course competencies align with applicable state-specific academic standards.
    </p>

    <p>
      Instruction emphasizes historical inquiry, conceptual understanding, evidence-based
      reasoning, source analysis, academic communication, geographic reasoning, real-world
      connection, and mastery of the approved unit and lesson mapping.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Course Learning Outcomes</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>By the end of this course, students will be able to:</p>

    <ul>
      <li>
        Use historical thinking, chronology, geography, sourcing, contextualization, and evidence
        to explain global developments.
      </li>
      <li>
        Investigate historical questions and develop defensible conclusions supported by relevant
        evidence.
      </li>
      <li>
        Analyze civilizations, societies, religions, empires, trade systems, conflicts,
        revolutions, industrialization, imperialism, global warfare, globalization, and
        contemporary world issues.
      </li>
      <li>
        Interpret maps, timelines, charts, source excerpts, political visuals, artifacts,
        photographs, and historical data displays.
      </li>
      <li>
        Compare historical causes, consequences, continuities, changes, turning points, and
        perspectives.
      </li>
      <li>
        Evaluate primary and secondary sources for author, audience, purpose, context,
        perspective, reliability, limitations, and usefulness.
      </li>
      <li>
        Corroborate information by comparing evidence from multiple historical sources.
      </li>
      <li>
        Write evidence-based historical explanations using accurate academic vocabulary and
        relevant source support.
      </li>
      <li>
        Explain connections among historical, civic, geographic, economic, political, social, and
        cultural developments.
      </li>
      <li>
        Demonstrate mastery through historical investigations, Mastery Checkpoints, lesson
        quizzes, source-based tasks, and unit assessments.
      </li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>
      Students entering this course should have prior experience with foundational middle school
      social studies skills, including:
    </p>

    <ul>
      <li>Reading basic maps and timelines</li>
      <li>Identifying chronological sequence</li>
      <li>Recognizing basic cause-and-effect relationships</li>
      <li>Reading informational and historical texts</li>
      <li>Identifying evidence in a source</li>
      <li>Writing organized paragraphs</li>
      <li>Using academic vocabulary in context</li>
      <li>Supporting explanations with evidence</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Students are not expected to begin the course with mastery of all course standards.
      Foundational skills are reviewed, reinforced, and extended throughout the course. Students
      receive structured instruction, investigation opportunities, feedback, revision support,
      and mastery checks throughout the learning sequence.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Course Structure</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>
      World History is organized into six units. The sequence develops historical-thinking skills
      first and then applies those skills to increasingly complex historical developments,
      evidence, and global issues.
    </p>

    <ul class="mla-unit-list">
      <li>
        <strong>Unit 1:</strong> Historical Thinking, Geography, and Medieval Foundations
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Unit 2:</strong> Islamic Civilizations, African Kingdoms, and American
        Civilizations
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Unit 3:</strong> Japan, Renaissance, Reformation, Scientific Revolution, and
        Exploration
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Unit 4:</strong> Absolutism, Enlightenment, Revolutions, Industrialization, and
        Imperialism
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Unit 5:</strong> World War I, Interwar Crisis, World War II, and the Holocaust
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Unit 6:</strong> Cold War, Decolonization, Globalization, Genocide, and
        Contemporary World Issues
      </li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Each unit begins with a Unit Pretest. Lessons 1–7 follow the MLA Social Studies
      instructional framework and conclude with a Lesson Quiz. Lesson 8 serves as the unit
      synthesis experience and contains the Unit Assessment instead of a Lesson Quiz.
    </p>

    <p>
      This structure provides repeated opportunities to learn, investigate, analyze, apply,
      revise, and demonstrate understanding before advancing.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">

      <h3>Lesson Learning Sequence</h3>
      <p>
        <strong>Orientation → Historical Understanding → Evidence Analysis → Historical Reasoning

      </p>

    <p>
      After completing the seven-page lesson sequence, students complete the Lesson Quiz.
      Lesson 8 follows a synthesis structure and culminates in the Unit Assessment.
    </p>
  </div>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Historian’s Notebook</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>
      The Historian’s Notebook is the student’s working learning record. Students use it to
      collect, organize, and apply information throughout each lesson.
    </p>

    <p>Notebook requirements may include:</p>

    <ul>
      <li>Copying assigned vocabulary terms and the provided definitions</li>
      <li>Recording lesson notes and essential historical information</li>
      <li>Creating or completing timeline entries</li>
      <li>Recording map observations or geographic evidence</li>
      <li>Analyzing a primary or secondary source</li>
      <li>Completing a cause-and-effect organizer</li>
      <li>Completing a comparison, continuity-and-change, or perspective organizer</li>
      <li>Recording evidence gathered during an investigation</li>
      <li>Completing the Independent Investigation</li>
    </ul>

    <div class="mla-note">
      <strong>Important:</strong> The Historian’s Notebook is a learning and reference tool. It is
      not the Mastery Checkpoint. Students use the completed notebook to prepare and support the
      separate Mastery Checkpoint submission.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Assessment Structure</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">

    <div class="mla-assessment">
      <h3>Unit Pretests</h3>
      <p>
        Each unit begins with a diagnostic pretest aligned to the unit competencies and applicable
        academic standards. The pretest identifies readiness, prior mastery, and learning gaps and
        may be used to determine eligibility for accelerated unit progression.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="mla-assessment">
      <h3>Guided Investigations</h3>
      <p>
        Guided Investigations provide structured, lesson-level practice with instructional
        support and immediate feedback before students begin the Independent Investigation.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="mla-assessment">
      <h3>Independent Investigations</h3>
      <p>
        Independent Investigations require students to apply lesson content and historical
        reasoning without guided support. Students complete this work in the Historian’s Notebook
        and use it to prepare for the Mastery Checkpoint.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="mla-assessment">
      <h3>Mastery Checkpoints — Teacher of Record Graded</h3>
      <p>
        Mastery Checkpoints are application-based tasks requiring students to independently
        demonstrate historical understanding, evidence use, reasoning, organization, and academic
        communication.
      </p>

      <p>
        Students use their completed Historian’s Notebook as a reference but submit the Mastery
        Checkpoint separately through the designated Moodle assignment.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="mla-assessment">
      <h3>Lesson Quizzes</h3>
      <p>
        Standards-aligned Lesson Quizzes appear in Lessons 1–7. They verify understanding of the
        historical content, evidence, vocabulary, visuals, and historical-thinking skills taught
        during the lesson.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="mla-assessment">
      <h3>Unit Assessments</h3>
      <p>
        Each Unit Assessment measures cumulative mastery of the standards, content, source
        analysis, historical reasoning, and evidence-based skills developed throughout the unit.
        The Unit Assessment appears in Lesson 8.
      </p>
    </div>

    <p>
      Course grades are based on the approved MLA World History gradebook categories and weights
      established in Moodle. Diagnostic pretests are used to guide instruction and acceleration
      decisions rather than replace required mastery evidence.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Mastery and Progression Criteria</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>
      Mindful Learning Academy follows a mastery-based learning model. Advancement is based on
      demonstrated learning rather than time spent in the course.
    </p>

    <p>To progress successfully, students must:</p>

    <ul>
      <li>Complete all required lesson pages and activities</li>
      <li>Complete assigned Historian’s Notebook work</li>
      <li>Complete each Guided Investigation</li>
      <li>Complete each Independent Investigation</li>
      <li>Submit each required Mastery Checkpoint</li>
      <li>Complete Lessons 1–7 quizzes</li>
      <li>Complete each Unit Assessment</li>
      <li>Revise and resubmit work when required</li>
      <li>Address identified learning gaps before advancement</li>
    </ul>

    <p>Mastery is demonstrated through consistent performance across:</p>

    <ul>
      <li>Mastery Checkpoint submissions</li>
      <li>Lesson Quizzes</li>
      <li>Unit Assessments</li>
      <li>Other required standards-aligned source-based or performance tasks</li>
    </ul>

    <div class="mla-highlight">
      A minimum mastery level of <strong>80%</strong> is required before advancement.
    </div>

    <p>
      Students may be required to revise and resubmit work until mastery is demonstrated.
      Revision opportunities, additional investigation practice, remediation, Teacher of Record
      support, and progress monitoring may be provided when students need assistance.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mla-section">
  <h2 class="mla-section-title">Teacher of Record Support</h2>

  <div class="mla-section-content">
    <p>The Teacher of Record supports students by:</p>

    <ul>
      <li>Clarifying lesson concepts and directions</li>
      <li>Reviewing Mastery Checkpoint submissions</li>
      <li>Providing rubric-based feedback</li>
      <li>Identifying incomplete or inaccurate evidence</li>
      <li>Assigning remediation when needed</li>
      <li>Managing revision and reassessment workflows</li>
      <li>Monitoring mastery and course progression</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Students should review the lesson content, evidence, Historical Thinking page, Guided
      Investigation, and completed Historian’s Notebook before requesting clarification or
      submitting a Mastery Checkpoint.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
