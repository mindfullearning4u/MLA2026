Lesson Title	Italian City-States and Renaissance Humanism
Lesson Type	Instructional Lesson
MLA Standards	MLA.WH.REN.01, MLA.WH.REN.02
Investigation Question	Why did Renaissance culture develop first in Italian city-states?
Primary Historical-Thinking Skill	Cause and Effect
Supporting Historical-Thinking Skills	Contextualization, Geographic Reasoning, Claim-Evidence-Reasoning
Lesson Focus

Students investigate why Renaissance culture developed first in Italian city-states.

They examine the interaction of:

Mediterranean trade
Urban development
Merchant wealth
Banking
Political competition
Patronage
Classical Greek and Roman heritage
Humanism

Students must understand that the Renaissance was not caused by one factor and did not appear suddenly. It developed through interacting economic, political, geographic, intellectual, and cultural conditions.

The lesson must also establish continuity between medieval Europe and the Renaissance rather than teaching the Renaissance as a complete break with the medieval period.

Required Historical Content

Codex must teach all of the following.

1. Historical Context of the Renaissance

Teach:

Meaning of the term Renaissance
Approximate historical period
Development first in Italian city-states
Continued importance of Christianity
Continued medieval institutions and traditions
Renewed interest in classical Greek and Roman learning
Increasing attention to human experience, achievement, and civic life

Students must understand:

The Renaissance involved important cultural and intellectual changes, but it also developed out of medieval European society.

Do not frame the Renaissance as an instant transformation from a "dark" medieval world into a modern one.

2. Geography of the Italian Peninsula

Teach:

Location of the Italian Peninsula
Mediterranean position
Access to maritime trade
Connections with Byzantine, Islamic, and other Mediterranean commercial networks
Importance of ports
Regional political fragmentation
Development of independent or semi-independent city-states

Required city-states include:

Florence
Venice
Milan

Rome may be included where appropriate.

Students must connect geography to economic opportunity.

3. Mediterranean Trade

Teach:

Trade routes connecting Italian commercial centers with other regions
Movement of goods
Movement of wealth
Movement of ideas
Merchant networks
Commercial competition
Growth of urban centers

Examples of traded goods may include:

Spices
Silk
Textiles
Luxury goods
Metals
Other appropriate commodities

The purpose is not memorization of trade goods. Students must understand the relationship:

Trade → Wealth → Urban Growth → Patronage → Cultural Production

4. Banking and Merchant Wealth

Teach:

Expansion of banking
Credit
Financial networks
Merchant families
Investment
Economic power of wealthy urban families

Use the Medici family as a major example.

Students must understand that wealthy merchants and bankers could gain:

Political influence
Social status
Cultural influence
5. Patronage

Teach patronage as a major Renaissance institution.

Patrons included:

Wealthy families
Political leaders
Religious institutions
Civic governments

Patrons supported:

Artists
Architects
Scholars
Writers
Public buildings
Religious works

Students must analyze patronage as both:

Financial support for cultural production
A means of displaying wealth, prestige, political influence, or religious devotion
6. Classical Heritage

Teach:

Renewed study of Greek and Roman texts
Classical literature
Philosophy
History
Political thought
Architecture
Artistic traditions

Students must understand that Renaissance scholars did not simply rediscover a completely forgotten classical world.

Classical knowledge had been preserved, transmitted, studied, and developed through:

Medieval European institutions
Byzantine scholarship
Islamic scholarship
Libraries and manuscript traditions

This continuity must be explicitly stated.

7. Humanism

Teach:

Humanism as an intellectual movement
Study of classical texts
Grammar
Rhetoric
History
Poetry
Moral philosophy
Interest in human experience and achievement

Introduce Petrarch as an important early humanist figure.

Students should understand that humanism was not synonymous with atheism or rejection of religion.

Many humanists remained deeply religious.

8. Civic Humanism

Where developmentally appropriate, teach:

Application of classical learning to civic life
Public service
Political participation
Responsibilities of citizens
Education for public leadership

This can be connected especially to Florence.

9. Individual Achievement

Teach the growing cultural emphasis on:

Individual talent
Reputation
Accomplishment
Education
Public recognition

Codex must avoid suggesting that medieval Europeans had no concept of individuality.

The lesson should instead explain a shift in emphasis.

10. Political Competition

Teach how competition among city-states and elites could encourage:

Architecture
Public works
Art
Scholarship
Civic display
Patronage

Students should recognize political competition as one of several interacting causes.

Required Vocabulary

Students must copy all provided terms and definitions into the Historian's Notebook.

Term	Required Student-Friendly Definition
Renaissance	A period of cultural, intellectual, artistic, and social development associated with renewed interest in classical Greek and Roman learning
Humanism	An intellectual movement emphasizing classical learning, human experience, education, and achievement
City-state	An independent or largely self-governing political unit centered on a city and surrounding territory
Patron	A person, family, government, or institution that financially supports artists, scholars, or other creators
Patronage	Financial or political support given to artistic, intellectual, or cultural work
Merchant	A person involved in buying, selling, or trading goods
Banking	Financial activities involving money, credit, loans, deposits, and investment
Classical	Related to ancient Greek or Roman culture
Secular	Related to worldly or nonreligious aspects of life
Medici	A powerful Florentine banking and political family associated with Renaissance patronage
Civic humanism	The application of humanist learning to citizenship and public life
Individualism	An emphasis on individual ability, achievement, identity, or reputation
Contextualization	Placing a historical development within the larger circumstances of its time
Cause	A condition, event, decision, or development that contributes to an historical outcome

P04 Historical Thinking Specification
Primary Skill — Cause and Effect

P04 must explicitly teach historical causation.

Students must learn that historians distinguish among:

Long-term causes
Short-term causes
Contributing conditions
Triggers where applicable
Interacting causes
Effects

For this lesson, the focus is on interacting causes.

Required Cause-and-Effect Process
Step 1 — Identify the outcome

Outcome:

Development of Renaissance culture in Italian city-states.

Step 2 — Identify possible causes

Examples:

Geography
Trade
Wealth
Urbanization
Banking
Political competition
Patronage
Classical heritage
Humanism
Step 3 — Find evidence for each cause

Students must connect claims to maps, sources, artwork, and historical information.

Step 4 — Determine relationships among causes

Ask:

Did one cause make another possible?
Did causes reinforce each other?
Which were long-term conditions?
Which had more direct influence?
Step 5 — Rank significance

Students must decide which causes mattered most and justify the ranking.

Required P04 Modeled Example

Model reasoning such as:

Florence's commercial wealth alone does not explain Renaissance development. Banking and trade created resources that wealthy families could use for patronage. At the same time, renewed interest in classical learning shaped what scholars and artists studied. Economic wealth therefore interacted with intellectual developments rather than acting as a single independent cause.

The purpose is to model reasoning, not provide the student's checkpoint response.

Required P04 Common Misconception
Common mistake

The Renaissance happened because wealthy Italians suddenly became interested in art.

Correction

Renaissance development resulted from several interacting conditions, including commerce, urban wealth, political competition, patronage, classical traditions, and humanism. Art was one expression of broader intellectual and cultural changes.

Independent Investigation — P06
Investigation Task

Students answer:

Which two factors were most important in explaining why Renaissance culture developed first in Italian city-states?

Students must evaluate all five:

Mediterranean trade and wealth
Urbanization
Classical heritage
Patronage
Political competition
Required Product
Part A — Evidence Ranking

Rank all five from most to least influential.

Part B — Top Two Causes

For each of the top two:

Identify specific historical evidence
Explain how it contributed
Explain how it interacted with another cause
Part C — Challenge the Ranking

Students must identify:

One reason another historian might choose a different cause
One piece of evidence supporting that alternative
Part D — Final Conclusion

Write a short conclusion explaining why a single-cause explanation is insufficient.
