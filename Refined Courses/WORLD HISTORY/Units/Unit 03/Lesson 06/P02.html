Learn the Historical Content

1. Why European States Explored

  • Teach multiple motivations.

Economic motivations

Access to Asian trade

Desire for spices and luxury goods

Search for direct maritime routes

Commercial competition

Desire for wealth and resources

Political motivations

Competition among European monarchies

Expansion of state power

Prestige

Territorial claims

Religious motivations

Spread of Christianity

Missionary goals

Religious rivalry

You must understand:

  • Exploration had multiple interacting motivations. No single explanation accounts for all voyages.

2. Geographic Knowledge and Maritime Expansion

  • Teach:

Mediterranean commercial experience

Atlantic navigation

Coastal knowledge

Mapmaking

Portolan charts where appropriate

Improved geographic information

Knowledge transferred across cultural networks

You should understand that European maritime expansion drew upon knowledge from many regions.

3. Navigation Technologies

  • Teach representative technologies such as:

Compass

Astrolabe

Lateen sail

Caravel

Improved ship design

Mapping techniques

Navigation by stars

Dead reckoning where appropriate

Accuracy Requirement

  • Do not imply Europeans invented all navigation technologies independently.
  • Explain that technologies and knowledge had earlier origins and circulated through intercultural exchange.

4. Portuguese Exploration

  • Teach:

Portuguese Atlantic and African coastal exploration

Prince Henry as a patron of exploration

Bartolomeu Dias

Vasco da Gama

Cape of Good Hope

Maritime route to the Indian Ocean

Portuguese trading-post strategy where appropriate

You must understand how geography shaped Portuguese strategy.

5. Christopher Columbus

  • Teach:

Genoese mariner sailing for Spain

  • 1492 voyage

Goal of reaching Asian markets by sailing west

Arrival in the Caribbean

Misidentification of encountered lands

Subsequent voyages

  • Beginning of sustained European colonization in the Americas

Critical Accuracy Requirement

  • Do not teach:
  • Columbus discovered America.
  • Instead explain that Indigenous peoples had lived in the Americas for thousands of years and that Norse voyages preceded Columbus.
  • The historical significance of 1492 lies in sustained transatlantic contact and colonization.

  • Detailed Atlantic slavery systems belong primarily in Lesson 7.

14. Perspective

You must compare at least:

European explorer or colonizer perspective

Indigenous perspective

  • Optional additional perspectives:

African

  • missionary
  • merchant
  • political ruler

You must understand that the meaning of "exploration" depends partly on historical perspective.

  • For Europeans, a voyage might represent expansion and discovery.
  • For Indigenous societies, the same event might represent encounter, disruption, invasion, opportunity, alliance, or danger.
