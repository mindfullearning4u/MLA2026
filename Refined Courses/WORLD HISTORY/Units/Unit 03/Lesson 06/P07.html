Age of Exploration and the Columbian Exchange

Mastery Checkpoint Directions

Mastery Checkpoint

Investigation Question: How did oceanic exploration create lasting global connections and consequences?

Investigation Question

  • How did oceanic exploration create lasting global connections and consequences?

Required Task

You construct a historical explanation showing:

Why European oceanic exploration expanded

  • How exploration connected previously separate regional systems more directly
  • How the Columbian Exchange changed societies and environments

Why consequences differed among populations

Response Format

  • 250–300 words

Two or three developed paragraphs

Recommended organization:

Paragraph 1

  • Historical context + exploration causes

Paragraph 2

  • Global connections + Columbian Exchange evidence

Paragraph 3

  • Demographic/environmental consequences + perspective + conclusion
  • Alternative structures are acceptable if all requirements are met.

Required Checkpoint Evidence

You must include:

At least two motivations for exploration

At least one navigation or geographic factor

At least two specific route/map details

At least two Columbian Exchange examples

At least one disease or demographic consequence

At least one environmental or agricultural consequence

Evidence from one European source

Evidence from one Indigenous or non-European perspective

At least one explanation of unequal consequences

Required Historical Reasoning

You must demonstrate:

Cause and Effect

Geographic Reasoning

Perspective

Claim-Evidence-Reasoning

Mastery Success Criteria

  • A mastery response must:

Directly answer the Investigation Question

Establish historical context

Explain multiple motivations for exploration

Distinguish causes from enabling technologies

Use map evidence accurately

  • Explain the Columbian Exchange as more than food transfer

Identify both demographic and environmental consequences

  • Accurately explain the historical significance of 1492 without using "discovered America"

Include European and Indigenous perspectives fairly

Explain that consequences differed across populations

Connect evidence to historical reasoning

Use at least five assigned vocabulary terms accurately

Meet the required response length

Represent the student's own understanding

Checkpoint Organization

  1. Introduction: establish context and state a direct claim.
  2. Body: present the exact required evidence and explain how it supports the claim.
  3. Conclusion: answer the question, show the required historical-thinking skill, and explain significance or limits.

Use this introduction–body–conclusion approach whenever a World History assignment requires developed writing.
