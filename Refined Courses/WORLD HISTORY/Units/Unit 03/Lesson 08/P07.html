Unit 3 Synthesis: Innovation, Reform, and Global Exchange

Mastery Checkpoint Directions

Mastery Checkpoint

Investigation Question: Which developments in Unit 3 most transformed societies, ideas, and global connections?

Investigation Question

  • Which developments in Unit 3 most transformed societies, ideas, and global connections?

Required Task

You construct a comparative historical argument identifying the three Unit 3 developments they judge most transformative.

  • They must explain:

What changed

Why each development mattered

  • How the developments connected to other Unit 3 events or processes

Which development was most significant overall

Response Format

  • 325–375 words

Three or four developed paragraphs

  • Suggested structure:

Paragraph 1

  • Historical context + thesis naming three developments

Paragraph 2

  • Development 1 + evidence + significance

Paragraph 3

  • Developments 2 and 3 + comparison/evidence

Paragraph 4

  • Cross-unit connections + final ranking/conclusion
  • Alternative organization is acceptable if all requirements are met.

Required Checkpoint Evidence

You must include:

Evidence from at least four different Unit 3 lessons

At least one map detail

At least two written-source details

  • At least one artwork, artifact, scientific diagram, or other visual detail

At least one timeline or quantitative/data detail

At least one example of continuity and change

At least one explicit comparison

At least one cause-and-effect relationship

At least two historical-significance criteria

At least one cross-unit connection

Required Historical Reasoning

You must demonstrate:

Comparison

Cause and Effect

Continuity and Change

Historical Significance

Claim-Evidence-Reasoning

  • Geographic Reasoning, Perspective, and Contextualization may support the response.

Mastery Success Criteria

  • A mastery response must:

Directly answer the Investigation Question

Identify exactly three major developments

Establish a clear ranking

Use evidence from at least four lessons

  • Compare developments rather than summarize them separately

Explain at least one cause-and-effect relationship

Explain at least one continuity-and-change relationship

Use at least two historical-significance criteria

Include at least one geographic or map-based conclusion

Make at least one meaningful cross-lesson connection

Address a plausible alternative interpretation

Use at least six Unit 3 vocabulary terms accurately

Meet the required response length

Represent the student's own understanding

Checkpoint Organization

  1. Introduction: establish context and state a direct claim.
  2. Body: present the exact required evidence and explain how it supports the claim.
  3. Conclusion: answer the question, show the required historical-thinking skill, and explain significance or limits.

Use this introduction–body–conclusion approach whenever a World History assignment requires developed writing.
