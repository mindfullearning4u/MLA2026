Required P04 Modeled Synthesis

Codex must model a cross-lesson argument such as:

Enlightenment ideas about rights and political authority shaped revolutionary arguments, but political revolutions alone did not produce the greatest transformation of everyday life. Industrialization changed how people worked, where they lived, how goods were produced, and how states competed economically. Industrial growth also contributed to new labor ideologies and to imperial expansion. A historian might therefore judge industrialization as especially significant because its economic effects interacted with political and global changes across several Unit 4 developments.

This models synthesis and historical significance without becoming the student's final answer.

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

The Enlightenment directly caused every revolution in Unit 4.

Correction

Enlightenment ideas influenced political arguments, but revolutions also resulted from social, economic, political, and imperial conditions.

Misconception 2

Revolution always produces equality.

Correction

Many revolutions expanded rights claims while leaving major inequalities intact.

Misconception 3

Industrialization was only an economic change.

Correction

It transformed labor, urban life, class relations, politics, the environment, and global power.

Misconception 4

Nationalism and imperialism were opposites.

Correction

Nationalism could support self-determination, but powerful nation-states also used national competition to justify imperial expansion.
