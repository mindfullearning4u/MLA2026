Required P04 Modeled Example

Both classical liberal capitalism and socialism responded to the changing industrial economy, but they diagnosed its problems differently. Classical economic thinkers generally emphasized private property, markets, and competition, while socialist thinkers placed greater emphasis on inequality and collective responsibility. Marx went further by arguing that class conflict would lead to revolutionary transformation. Comparing the systems requires examining their assumptions about ownership, labor, inequality, and change rather than simply labeling one as "free" and another as "controlled."

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

Capitalism means government never participates in the economy.

Correction: Historical capitalist societies included laws, public institutions, taxation, and varying levels of regulation.

Misconception 2

Socialism and communism mean exactly the same thing.

Correction: Socialism is a broad family of economic and political ideas; Marxist communism represents a specific revolutionary theoretical tradition.

Misconception 3

Marx described twentieth-century communist governments.

Correction: Marx wrote in the nineteenth century about industrial capitalism and revolutionary theory; later governments developed in different historical circumstances.

Misconception 4

Workers gained protections immediately after industrial problems became visible.

Correction: Labor reform developed gradually through activism, organization, political pressure, and legislation.

Nationalism
Imperialism

Required P04 Modeled Example

Nationalism helped support German unification by encouraging the idea that German-speaking territories should share a political state, but nationalism alone did not cause unification. Prussian military power, Bismarck's diplomacy, economic integration, and wars also contributed. Once industrial nation-states became more powerful, some governments used national competition and prestige to justify overseas imperial expansion. Nationalism could therefore support political unity at home while also encouraging rivalry and expansion abroad.

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

Nationalism always creates independence and freedom.

Correction: Nationalism could promote self-determination, but it could also contribute to exclusion, conflict, imperial competition, or domination.

Misconception 2

Italy and Germany unified simply because their people wanted one country.

Correction: National sentiment interacted with diplomacy, warfare, political leadership, and existing state power.

Misconception 3

Imperialism happened because Europeans had better technology.

Correction: Technology helped enable conquest; economic, political, strategic, and ideological motives drove expansion.

Misconception 4

China became a European colony after the Opium Wars.

Correction: Qing China remained formally sovereign but lost significant economic and legal autonomy through unequal treaties and spheres of influence.

Misconception 5

The Opium Wars were simply wars for free trade.

Correction: The conflicts involved British commercial interests, the opium trade, Chinese sovereignty, and imperial military power.
