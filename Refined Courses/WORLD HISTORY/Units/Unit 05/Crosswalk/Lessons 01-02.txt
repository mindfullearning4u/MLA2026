Required P04 Modeled Example

The assassination of Franz Ferdinand triggered the July Crisis, but the assassination alone does not explain why several major powers entered the war. Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia, Russian mobilization, German military planning, alliance expectations, and the invasion of Belgium widened the conflict. The war therefore resulted from interaction between long-term tensions and political decisions made during the crisis.

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

The assassination of Franz Ferdinand caused World War I by itself.

Correction: It triggered a crisis within an already tense international system.

Misconception 2

All alliances automatically required every member to enter the war.

Correction: Alliance relationships differed, and governments made political decisions about entering the conflict.

Misconception 3

Trench warfare occurred everywhere in exactly the same way.

Correction: Trench stalemate was especially associated with the Western Front; other fronts differed considerably.

Misconception 4

New technology made warfare quicker and easier.

Correction: Industrial weapons often strengthened defensive positions and contributed to prolonged, high-casualty warfare.

Required P04 Modeled Example

The Treaty of Versailles restricted German military power partly to improve French security. This was an intended effect. However, treaty restrictions and reparations also became important symbols of resentment within Germany. That resentment later contributed to political instability, but it did not make the rise of dictatorship or another world war inevitable. Later economic crises, political movements, and government decisions also mattered.

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

The armistice and Treaty of Versailles were the same event.

Correction: The 1918 armistice stopped major fighting; the Versailles peace treaty was signed in 1919.

Misconception 2

Wilson's Fourteen Points became the Treaty of Versailles.

Correction: Some principles influenced the settlement, but many proposals were modified, rejected, or unevenly applied.

Misconception 3

Self-determination was applied equally to every population.

Correction: Its application was selective and particularly limited for colonized peoples.

Misconception 4

The Treaty of Versailles directly caused World War II.

Correction: The settlement contributed to later tensions, but multiple later economic, political, ideological, and diplomatic developments were also necessary.
