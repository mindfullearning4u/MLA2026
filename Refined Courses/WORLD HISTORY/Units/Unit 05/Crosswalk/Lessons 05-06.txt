Required P04 Modeled Example

The Treaty of Versailles and Great Depression contributed to instability, but neither automatically caused World War II. Nazi expansion, the failure of collective security, appeasement, and specific political decisions escalated tensions. Germany's invasion of Poland triggered war with Britain and France. Later, geography and logistics helped shape the conflict: Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union stretched supply lines across enormous distances, while Allied industrial capacity supported sustained operations across multiple theaters.

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

The Treaty of Versailles alone caused WWII.

Correction: It contributed to tensions, but later ideology, Depression, expansionism, diplomacy, and political decisions were also essential.

Misconception 2

Appeasement simply means leaders were weak.

Correction: Appeasement reflected fear of another war, military conditions, political constraints, and misjudgment of Hitler's goals.

Misconception 3

Pearl Harbor started World War II.

Correction: War was already underway in Europe and Asia before U.S. entry.

Misconception 4

D-Day alone defeated Germany.

Correction: Nazi Germany was defeated through combined Allied pressure, with the Eastern Front playing a central role.

Required P04 Modeled Example

Nazi antisemitism provided an ideological foundation, but ideology alone did not murder millions of people. The Nazi state translated ideology into laws, propaganda, arrests, confiscation, forced confinement, deportation, and organized killing. War expanded Nazi control over millions more Jews and enabled further radicalization. Police, administrators, railway systems, military units, collaborators, and camp personnel carried out policies. The Holocaust therefore resulted from ideology combined with state power, war, bureaucracy, and repeated human choices.

Required P04 Common Misconceptions
Misconception 1

The Holocaust began suddenly with concentration camps.

Correction: Nazi persecution escalated over years from exclusion and discrimination to mass murder.

Misconception 2

All concentration camps were killing centers.

Correction: Camps had different functions; killing centers were established specifically for systematic mass murder.

Misconception 3

Jewish victims did not resist.

Correction: Resistance included armed revolt, escape, documentation, cultural survival, education, and many other actions.

Misconception 4

Everyone living under Nazi rule was equally responsible.

Correction: Historical responsibility differed dramatically among perpetrators, collaborators, bystanders, rescuers, resisters, and victims.

Misconception 5

The Holocaust was inevitable once Hitler came to power.

Correction: Escalation occurred through repeated political, institutional, and individual decisions.
