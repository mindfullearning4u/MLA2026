Visual A: Europe’s military alliances in 1914

Evidence A: Austria-Hungary’s ultimatum to Serbia, July 23, 1914

“The Royal Serbian Government shall condemn the propaganda directed against Austria-Hungary…”

Context: Austria-Hungary issued a list of demands after the assassination. The wording presents Serbian nationalism as a threat and demands state action. Limitation: It represents Austria-Hungary’s official position, not Serbia’s response or the private motives of every decision-maker.

Source: Austro-Hungarian Government, Ultimatum to Serbia, July 23, 1914, reproduced by FirstWorldWar.com. Public-domain historical document. Excerpt preserves original translated wording; ellipsis marks omitted text. https://www.firstworldwar.com/source/austrianultimatum.htm

Evidence B: trench photograph

What it shows: Soldiers devote major labor to defensive earthworks. What it cannot show alone: the entire trench system, combat conditions, soldiers’ thoughts, or whether every front operated in the same way.

Write in your Historian’s Notebook

Write this evidence summary in your own words as six numbered notes:

  1. The map places Germany between France and Russia and places Serbia beside Austria-Hungary in the Balkans.
  2. The assassination was the trigger; militarism, alliances, nationalism, imperialism, and Balkan tensions were underlying conditions.
  3. The July Crisis widened because governments chose ultimatums, mobilization, and declarations of war.
  4. Evidence A shows Austria-Hungary’s official accusation and demands, but it does not include Serbia’s full perspective.
  5. Evidence B shows the labor and defensive organization of trench warfare, but one photograph cannot represent every battlefront.
  6. Industrial firepower helped defense overpower attack, contributing to trenches, casualties, and stalemate.

These notes have merit because they preserve the evidence and its limitations for later use; you are not copying the entire step-by-step analysis.
