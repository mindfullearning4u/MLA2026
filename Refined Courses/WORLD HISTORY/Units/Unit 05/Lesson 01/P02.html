Europe before 1914: pressures beneath the crisis

World War I grew from several conditions that made Europe tense before the Sarajevo assassination. European states expanded armies and military plans, formed competing alliances, competed for colonies, and used nationalism to strengthen loyalty or demand independence. The Balkans were especially unstable because the Ottoman Empire was weakening, Austria-Hungary and Russia competed for influence, and nationalist movements challenged multinational empires.

From assassination to wider war

On June 28, 1914, Gavrilo Princip assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo. Austria-Hungary issued Serbia an ultimatum. During the July Crisis, leaders chose mobilization, rejected or limited negotiations, and declared war. Alliance commitments and military schedules widened the conflict. These choices made escalation more likely, but war was not automatic.

Industrialized warfare

Industrial production supplied artillery, machine guns, railroads, submarines, poison gas, aircraft, and enormous quantities of ammunition. Defensive firepower often overwhelmed attacking soldiers. On the Western Front, armies constructed trench systems separated by no man’s land, producing a deadly stalemate. The Eastern Front was more mobile but covered a much larger region. European empires also drew soldiers, labor, food, and resources from Africa, Asia, the Pacific, and the Americas, making the conflict global.

TermStudent-friendly meaning
MilitarismStrong emphasis on military power, preparedness, and influence in national affairs.
AllianceAn agreement or relationship among states involving cooperation or mutual support.
NationalismStrong identification with a nation and its political interests.
ImperialismExtension of political, economic, or military power over other territories or peoples.
MobilizationOrganization and movement of military forces for war.
UltimatumA final demand accompanied by threatened consequences if it is rejected.
Western FrontThe major World War I battlefront in France and Belgium.
Eastern FrontThe major battlefront involving Germany, Austria-Hungary, Russia, and nearby regions.
Trench warfareWarfare in which opposing armies fight from fortified trench systems.
StalemateA situation in which neither side can gain a decisive advantage.
No man’s landThe exposed area between opposing trench systems.
ArtilleryLarge weapons designed to fire shells over considerable distances.
U-boatA German submarine used during World War I.
Total warConflict in which governments mobilize extensive military, economic, and civilian resources.
EscalationThe process by which a conflict increases in scope or intensity.
