Unit 5 Lesson 1: Causes and Changing Warfare of World War I

Investigation Question: Why did a regional crisis in 1914 develop into a major world war, and how did industrial technology change the nature of warfare?

What you will learn

  • Distinguish long-term causes, immediate causes, and the triggering event.
  • Trace the July Crisis in chronological order and explain how government decisions escalated the conflict.
  • Use maps to explain why the Balkans and alliance geography mattered.
  • Explain how industrial weapons, trenches, and global empires changed warfare.

Your learning sequence

  1. P02: Learn the historical content and record vocabulary with meanings.
  2. P03: Examine maps, a timeline, and historical sources; record meaningful evidence notes.
  3. P04: Study a complete cause-and-effect model. No notebook writing is required.
  4. P05: Follow a guided investigation and record the assigned summary in your notebook.
  5. P06: Complete the independent investigation in the exact organizer shown.
  6. P07: Use the directions and checklist to prepare the separate Mastery Checkpoint submission.

Important historical caution

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand was the trigger, but it did not make a world war automatic. Historians must examine militarism, alliances, nationalism, imperial rivalry, Balkan tensions, mobilization plans, and decisions made during the July Crisis.
