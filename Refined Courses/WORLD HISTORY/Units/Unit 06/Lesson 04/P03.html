Decolonization, Nationalist Leaders, and Democratic Reform

Notebook evidence

Complete one concise row for each source. Preserve the goal, strategy, context, usefulness, and limitation.

Evidence A — Gandhi

“Civil disobedience becomes a sacred duty when the State becomes lawless or corrupt.”

M. K. Gandhi, Young India, 1930. Short historical excerpt. Limitation: a leader’s statement does not represent every participant or all causes of independence.

Evidence B — Jinnah

Jinnah argued that Muslims required effective political safeguards and ultimately supported a separate Pakistan.

Contextual summary of Muslim League positions; summary, not quotation. Use alongside Congress and partition evidence.

Evidence C — Nkrumah

“Seek ye first the political kingdom and all things shall be added unto you.”

Kwame Nkrumah, widely documented independence-era political slogan. It expresses priority for sovereignty but cannot predict later governance.

Evidence D — Mandela

“I have cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society in which all persons live together in harmony and with equal opportunities.”

Nelson Mandela, Rivonia Trial statement, April 20, 1964. Nelson Mandela Foundation transcript; excerpt preserved.
