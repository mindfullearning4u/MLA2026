P04 Historical Thinking

Decolonization, Nationalist Leaders, and Democratic Reform

Comparison: six-step process

  1. Establish the colonial system.
  2. Identify political goals: independence, reform, equality, or partition.
  3. Identify strategies: negotiation, nonviolence, protest, armed struggle, or international pressure.
  4. Identify external conditions: World War II, Cold War, UN, and imperial weakness.
  5. Compare outcomes: independence, partition, democracy, continued conflict, or authoritarian rule.
  6. Explain differences: institutions, settlement patterns, social divisions, leadership, mass participation, and imperial response.

Modeled comparison

India and Algeria both sought independence, but their paths differed. Gandhi and Congress made nonviolent mass resistance important, although repression and partition violence also occurred. Algeria’s FLN fought sustained armed conflict against France. Leadership alone does not explain the difference: colonial settlement patterns, institutions, social divisions, and imperial responses also shaped each outcome.

Example only—not required notebook evidence.

Misconception check

  • Gandhi did not win independence alone.
  • European powers did not simply leave voluntarily.
  • All postcolonial states did not become democracies.
  • Nonalignment did not mean neutrality on every issue.
  • Mandela did not end apartheid alone.
