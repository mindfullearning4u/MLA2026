Genocide, Ethnic Cleansing, and Global Responses

Notebook evidence

Complete the table with concise evidence notes. Distinguish legal classification from the broader human harm.

Evidence A — Genocide Convention, Article II (1948)

“intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group”

Authentic short excerpt; United Nations treaty text, 1948.

Evidence B — Cambodia

Survivor oral histories held by recognized archives describe forced evacuation, family separation, hunger, labor, fear, and the loss of relatives. These accounts reveal experience and perspective but do not alone establish the legal classification of every death.

Editorial summary, not a quotation; based on Documentation Center of Cambodia and USC Shoah Foundation testimony collections.

Evidence C — Bosnia/Srebrenica

Tribunal records document the July 1995 killing of more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys after the failure to protect a declared UN safe area; international courts ruled the crime genocide.

Editorial summary, not a quotation; ICTY/IRMCT case records.

Evidence D — Rwanda and international response

UN records show that peacekeepers operated with limited authority and resources while member states debated reduction and response. Survivor and tribunal records document organized targeting, local choices, resistance, rescue, and coercion.

Editorial summary, not a quotation; United Nations and ICTR records.
