P04 Historical Thinking

Unit 6 Synthesis: The Modern World and Historical Evidence

Integrated process

  1. Historical Inquiry: ask a defensible question.
  2. Chronology and Contextualization: sequence and conditions.
  3. Sourcing and Perspective: creator, audience, purpose.
  4. Corroboration and Comparison: test agreements/differences.
  5. Cause and Effect; Continuity and Change; Geographic Reasoning.
  6. Historical Significance: scale, reach, duration, degree of change, later influence.
  7. CER: claim supported by accurate evidence and reasoning.

Modeled synthesis

Decolonization and globalization both transformed relationships after 1945, but differently. Decolonization expanded formal sovereignty as empires ended; globalization increased economic interdependence among sovereign states. Nationalism could support self-determination and conflict. The world became more politically independent in some respects and more economically/technologically connected.

Example only; no notebook writing required.

The Cold War’s end did not end conflict; decolonization did not end outside influence; globalization did not erase borders; sources require context and corroboration.
