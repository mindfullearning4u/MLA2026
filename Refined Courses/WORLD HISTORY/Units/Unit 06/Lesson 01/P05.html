P05 Guided Investigation

Cold War Europe, Ideology, and Early Conflict

Claim to investigate

The early Cold War developed through an action-response cycle in which Soviet security policies and U.S. containment policies increased mutual fear and divided Europe.

Follow the reasoning

  1. Begin with context: Germany was defeated, Europe was weakened, and Soviet forces remained across much of Eastern Europe.
  2. Identify Soviet action and purpose: Soviet leaders supported friendly communist governments as a security buffer and sphere of influence.
  3. Identify the Western interpretation: Western leaders saw suppression of opposition and feared expansion.
  4. Identify the U.S. response: The Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan combined political, military, economic, and humanitarian purposes.
  5. Use geography: West Berlin’s location made access vulnerable; the blockade produced the airlift.
  6. Trace institutional effects: NATO formed in 1949; the Warsaw Pact followed in 1955.
  7. State the limitation: This cycle explains escalation but does not make every action equivalent or erase coercion and repression.

Notebook summary

Write this idea in your own words in 4–6 sentences: Postwar conditions created tension; Soviet and Western leaders interpreted security differently; political and economic actions produced responses; Berlin shows how geography intensified conflict; and alliances made division more permanent. Include at least four vocabulary terms. Do not copy the entire guided process.
