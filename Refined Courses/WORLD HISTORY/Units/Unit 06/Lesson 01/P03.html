Evidence A — Western perception

“From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, an iron curtain has descended across the Continent.”

Creator/context: Winston Churchill, “Sinews of Peace,” March 5, 1946, speaking in Fulton, Missouri. Limitation: It presents a prominent Western perspective, not a neutral description or the views of all Europeans.

Source: UK National Archives, FO 371/51624. Public historical government record; excerpt preserved.

Evidence B — Truman Doctrine

“It must be the policy of the United States to support free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation...”

Creator/context: President Harry S. Truman addressing Congress, March 12, 1947, while requesting aid for Greece and Turkey. Limitation: The public speech frames a complicated conflict as a choice between political systems.

Source: U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian; public-domain U.S. government text. Excerpt preserved; omission marked by ellipsis.

Evidence C — Marshall recovery proposal

“Our policy is directed not against any country or doctrine but against hunger, poverty, desperation and chaos.”

Creator/context: Secretary of State George C. Marshall, Harvard University, June 5, 1947. Use: Compare the public humanitarian claim with the program’s economic and strategic effects.

Source: U.S. Department of State, FRUS 1947, vol. III, document 140; public-domain U.S. government text.

Evidence D — Soviet-bloc criticism

Soviet representatives argued that U.S.-led recovery planning could interfere in national affairs and increase American economic influence.

Editorial note: This is a clearly labeled summary, not a quotation. Use it to identify the Soviet interpretation, then compare it with the Western sources. Limitation: Official diplomatic positions served political purposes and did not represent every citizen.

Context verified against U.S. Department of State FRUS 1947 diplomatic records concerning the Marshall Plan and Soviet responses.
