WORLD HISTORY • UNIT 6 • LESSON 5

P04 Historical Thinking

Science, Technology, Demography, Migration, and Pandemics

Continuity and Change: six-step process

  1. Establish the earlier pattern: higher mortality, slower travel, localized communication, more rural populations.
  2. Identify change: vaccination, aviation, urbanization, internet, or agricultural science.
  3. Measure degree and pace: gradual or rapid? global or uneven?
  4. Identify continuity: inequality, disease, migration, and uneven access continued.
  5. Explain causes: research, institutions, investment, policy, and networks.
  6. Explain consequences: who benefited, who faced new costs, and where?

Modeled reasoning

Modern medicine reduced deaths from many infectious diseases and increased life expectancy in much of the world—a substantial change. Unequal access to health care remained an important continuity. Scientific innovation transformed global health while economic and geographic inequalities continued to shape who benefited most.

Example only—not required notebook evidence.

  • Technology does not improve everyone’s life equally.
  • Urbanization does not mean all rural populations disappear.
  • Migration can be voluntary, pressured, or forced.
  • Modern transportation accelerates some transmission; it did not create pandemics.
