P04 Historical Thinking

Modern Israel, Middle East Conflict, and Religious Fundamentalism

Cause and Effect: seven-step process

  1. Establish context: Ottoman collapse, British Mandate, Zionism, and Arab/Palestinian nationalism.
  2. Identify competing claims: what did each national movement seek, and why?
  3. Identify external intervention: Britain, the UN, neighboring states, the United States, and the Soviet Union.
  4. Trace territorial change: distinguish the 1947 proposal, 1949 armistice reference, and post-1967 occupation.
  5. Examine consequences: state creation, war, security, refugees, displacement, and occupation.
  6. Identify attempts at resolution: Camp David and Oslo changed some relationships but did not settle every issue.
  7. Reject single causes: religion, nationalism, or foreign intervention alone is insufficient.

Modeled reasoning

Jewish and Palestinian nationalism both claimed self-determination in the same territory, within the added context of Ottoman collapse, British administration, Jewish migration, European antisemitism, the Holocaust, Arab political opposition, and international intervention. The 1948 war produced Israeli statehood and large-scale Palestinian displacement; later wars and occupation added security and territorial disputes. The conflict therefore developed through interacting modern processes, not ancient religious hostility alone.

This model is an example only; it is not required notebook evidence.

Misconception check

  • The UN proposal is not today’s border map.
  • Religion is one factor, not the only cause.
  • Israeli and Palestinian societies each contain diverse views.
  • Do not assign collective guilt to an ethnicity, nationality, or religion.
