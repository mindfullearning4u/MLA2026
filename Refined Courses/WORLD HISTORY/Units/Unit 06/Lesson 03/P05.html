P05 Guided Investigation

Modern Israel, Middle East Conflict, and Religious Fundamentalism

Claim to investigate

The conflict persisted because competing national claims became tied to changing territory, security fears, displacement, sovereignty, religion, and repeated outside intervention.

Follow the reasoning

  1. Begin with Ottoman collapse and British mandate rule—not ancient hostility.
  2. Explain Zionist and Palestinian/Arab goals distinctly.
  3. Use the Balfour wording to show British ambiguity.
  4. Use the 1947, 1949, and 1967 maps to separate proposals, armistice lines, and occupied territories.
  5. Explain both Israeli statehood/security and Palestinian displacement/sovereignty concerns.
  6. Show how diplomacy changed some issues while leaving others unresolved.
  7. Explain religion as one factor alongside territory, nationalism, state power, and history.

Notebook summary

Write 5–7 sentences that preserve the most useful reasoning: one long-term condition, two competing goals, one territorial change, two different consequences, and one diplomatic effort. Include both Israeli and Palestinian perspectives and at least five vocabulary terms. Do not copy the full guided list.
