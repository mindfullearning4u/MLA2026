P04 Historical Thinking

China, Arms Race, Proxy Wars, and the Fall of Communism

Comparison: five-step process

  1. Establish context: locate each case in time and place; identify its domestic history.
  2. Identify outside involvement: specify U.S., Soviet, and Chinese roles.
  3. Identify local goals: ask what Korean, Vietnamese, Chinese, or Eastern European actors wanted.
  4. Compare outcomes: Korea stayed divided; Vietnam reunified; Soviet and Eastern European regimes fell; Chinese party rule continued.
  5. Explain differences: use nationalism, domestic politics, economics, geography, military conditions, leadership, reform, and international context.

Modeled example

Korea and Vietnam were both Cold War proxy conflicts, but their outcomes differed. Korea ended with an armistice and continued division, while Vietnam ended with reunification under communist rule. Local nationalism and political goals mattered alongside outside support, so neither war can be reduced to a U.S.–Soviet contest.

This is an example only; it is not required notebook evidence.

Misconception check

  • Moscow did not completely control all communist states; the Sino-Soviet split demonstrates independent interests.
  • Proxy-war actors were not puppets.
  • The Korean armistice was not a peace treaty.
  • Not all communist governments collapsed in 1991.
  • No single leader, speech, or U.S. policy caused the Berlin Wall or Soviet collapse.
