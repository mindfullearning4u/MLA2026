Notebook evidence

Copy and complete the evidence table. Record each source’s claim, context, use, and limitation—not the entire passage.

Evidence A — Mao, 1949

“The Chinese people have stood up.”

Mao Zedong, opening address to the Chinese People’s Political Consultative Conference, September 1949. The claim connects revolution with national independence. Limitation: a victorious leader’s public claim does not reveal every Chinese experience. Source: Marxists Internet Archive transcription of a historical speech; short quotation used for instruction.

Evidence B — Korean local perspective

The 1953 Armistice Agreement required commanders to stop hostilities and created military arrangements; it did not establish a final political peace.

Editorial summary based on the Korean Armistice Agreement, National Archives holdings. This is a summary, not a quotation.

Evidence C — Cuban Missile Crisis

Kennedy publicly demanded removal of Soviet missiles from Cuba; Khrushchev’s messages emphasized invasion fears and negotiated withdrawal.

Paired contextual summary based on the U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian. A confidential U.S. commitment concerning Jupiter missiles in Turkey also formed part of the settlement. Summary, not quotation.

Evidence D — Gorbachev reform

Gorbachev presented openness and restructuring as necessary to address stagnation and renew Soviet society.

Contextual summary based on public speeches and the Gorbachev Foundation archive. Summary, not quotation; official reform language may understate resistance and unintended consequences.
