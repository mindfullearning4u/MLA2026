Content summary

China’s 1949 revolution grew from Chinese civil war, foreign invasion, social conflict, and competing Nationalist and Communist movements—not simply Soviet direction. Mao-era campaigns transformed society but produced coercion, persecution, disruption, and catastrophic famine. The Sino-Soviet split later proved shared ideology did not erase national interests.

Nuclear arsenals created a paradox: retaliation could deter direct superpower attack while making any failure catastrophic. Korea ended with a 1953 armistice and continued division. Vietnam combined colonial history, nationalism, civil conflict, and Cold War intervention and ended in reunification in 1975.

In the 1980s, stagnation, political rigidity, national movements, reform, popular protest, and leadership choices interacted. Eastern European communist governments fell in varied ways and the USSR dissolved in 1991. China instead expanded market mechanisms while Communist Party rule continued.
