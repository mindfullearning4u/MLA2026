Byzantine Figures, Achievements, Decline, and Ottoman Transition

Historical Evidence Lab

Investigation Question

Why did the Byzantine Empire endure for centuries and eventually fall?

Evidence Set A: Decline and Conquest Timeline

DateDevelopmentCausal importance
527–565Justinian and Theodora rule; Belisarius reconquers territory; law and Hagia Sophia strengthen imperial prestige.Achievement and endurance, but wars and construction were costly.
1054Great Schism formalizes division between eastern and western churches.Long-term religious and political division.
1204Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople and establishes Latin rule.Severe loss of wealth, trust, territory, and stability.
1261Byzantine rule returns to Constantinople.Political recovery without full restoration of earlier power.
1300s–1400sOttoman territory expands around the shrinking Byzantine state.Increasing long-term military and territorial pressure.
April–May 1453Mehmed II besieges Constantinople; Ottoman artillery attacks the walls.Immediate military cause of the conquest.
29 May 1453Ottoman forces capture Constantinople.End of the Byzantine Empire and Ottoman imperial transition.

Evidence Set B: Ottoman Expansion Schematic

What to notice: By 1453, Constantinople was not the center of a broad territorial empire. Ottoman-controlled lands and routes surrounded the city, limiting outside support and resources. The diagram is instructional and schematic, not a boundary-precise political map.

Evidence Set C: Historical Source Perspectives

Source A — Byzantine perspective: Michael Kritoboulos, a fifteenth-century Greek historian who entered Ottoman service, wrote a history of Mehmed II’s reign. His account describes Mehmed’s preparations, the siege, and the conquest. Instructional editorial summary—not a quotation.

Source B — Ottoman transition: Ottoman chronicles and administrative traditions presented Mehmed II as a conqueror and imperial ruler who rebuilt Constantinople as a capital. Instructional editorial summary—not a quotation.

Source C — Modern historical interpretation: The Metropolitan Museum of Art explains that the Ottoman capture in 1453 ended Byzantine political rule, while Byzantine artistic traditions continued to influence the Islamic world and Latin West.

Evidence Set D: Hagia Sophia as Legacy Evidence

Before 1453After Ottoman conquestWhat continued and changed
Eastern Orthodox patriarchal church; imperial ceremonies; dome and mosaics represented Byzantine sacred authority.Converted to an imperial mosque; the building was preserved, adapted, and later influenced Ottoman architecture.The structure endured, while its religious function and imperial meaning changed.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art notes that after the conquest Hagia Sophia became an imperial mosque and influenced Ottoman architects. The building therefore demonstrates both transition and enduring legacy.
