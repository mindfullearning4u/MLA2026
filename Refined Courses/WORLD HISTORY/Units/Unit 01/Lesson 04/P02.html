Byzantine Figures, Achievements, Decline, and Ottoman Transition

Learn the Historical Content

Investigation Question

Why did the Byzantine Empire endure for centuries and eventually fall?

How Byzantium Endured

Leadership and government: Justinian and Theodora reinforced imperial authority. Belisarius led reconquests. Officials collected taxes and administered law. The theme system later connected provincial military defense with local organization.

Achievement and legacy: Byzantine scholars copied and preserved classical texts. The Justinian Code influenced later legal traditions. Mosaics and domed architecture shaped Christian and Islamic artistic worlds. Diplomacy—alliances, marriages, gifts, conversion, and negotiation—sometimes achieved what armies could not.

Why power weakened: Repeated wars, succession struggles, shrinking tax revenue, territorial losses, and religious division reduced resilience. The Fourth Crusade’s sack of 1204 fractured the empire. Byzantine rule returned in 1261, but the restored state was smaller and poorer. By the fifteenth century, Ottoman expansion surrounded Constantinople. Mehmed II’s organized siege, large forces, artillery, and control of nearby territory produced the immediate military crisis of 1453.

Why 1453 mattered: The conquest ended the Byzantine state and made Constantinople an Ottoman capital. Byzantine institutions and cultural traditions did not simply disappear: texts, art, Orthodox Christianity, law, and architectural knowledge continued across regions.

Vocabulary and Meanings

AchievementAn important accomplishment produced by a person or society.
LegacyA lasting influence inherited from the past.
DiplomacyManaging relations among states through negotiation, alliances, gifts, or agreements.
Great SchismThe formal break in 1054 between the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches.
CrusadeA religiously authorized military expedition; the Fourth Crusade captured Constantinople in 1204.
DeclineA long-term reduction in a state’s power, territory, resources, or stability.
SiegeA military operation that surrounds and attacks a fortified place.
Ottoman EmpireA state founded in Anatolia that expanded into southeastern Europe and captured Constantinople.
Mehmed IIThe Ottoman sultan who directed the conquest of Constantinople in 1453.
Turning pointAn event that produces important and lasting historical change.
Long-term causeA condition that develops over an extended period and makes an event more likely.
Immediate causeAn action or condition that directly triggers or produces an event.
