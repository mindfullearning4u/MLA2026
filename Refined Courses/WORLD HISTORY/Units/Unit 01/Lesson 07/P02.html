Medieval Crisis and Economic Change

Learn the Historical Content

Investigation Question

How did crisis weaken medieval systems and accelerate economic and social change?

Learn the Historical Content

Trade and travel connected medieval societies but also helped disease move. The Black Death reached Europe in the late 1340s and caused enormous mortality, though estimates vary by region. Population decline produced immediate grief, abandoned property, and disrupted production. A smaller labor force could increase workers’ bargaining power and wage demands, while rulers and landlords sometimes tried to restrict movement or pay. Serfdom changed unevenly rather than ending instantly everywhere. Revolts, warfare, growing towns, merchants, and guilds compounded long-term social and economic change.

Vocabulary and Meanings

Black DeathThe devastating plague pandemic that struck Europe, North Africa, and Asia in the fourteenth century.
PandemicAn epidemic spreading across countries or continents.
Population declineA reduction in the number of people living in a place.
Labor shortageA condition in which fewer workers are available than employers need.
WagePayment for labor.
SerfdomA system binding peasants to a lord’s land and obligations.
Peasant revoltOrganized resistance by rural workers or tenants.
GuildAn association regulating a craft or trade.
MerchantA person engaged in buying and selling goods.
UrbanizationGrowth in the population and importance of towns and cities.
Economic changeA shift in production, labor, trade, wealth, or institutions.
Long-term effectA consequence that develops or continues over an extended period.
