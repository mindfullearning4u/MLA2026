Medieval Crisis and Economic Change

Lesson Overview

Investigation Question

How did crisis weaken medieval systems and accelerate economic and social change?

Lesson Overview

Trade and travel connected medieval societies but also helped disease move. The Black Death reached Europe in the late 1340s and caused enormous mortality, though estimates vary by region. Population decline produced immediate grief, abandoned property, and disrupted production. A smaller labor force could increase workers’ bargaining power and wage demands, while rulers and landlords sometimes tried to restrict movement or pay. Serfdom changed unevenly rather than ending instantly everywhere. Revolts, warfare, growing towns, merchants, and guilds compounded long-term social and economic change.
