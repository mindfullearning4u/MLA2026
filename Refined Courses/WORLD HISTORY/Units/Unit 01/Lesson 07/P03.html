Medieval Crisis and Economic Change

Historical Evidence Lab

Investigation Question

How did crisis weaken medieval systems and accelerate economic and social change?

Evidence Set A: Timeline

DateDevelopment
1347Plague reaches major Mediterranean ports.
1348–1351Black Death spreads across much of Europe.
Mid-1300sSevere population decline creates labor shortages.
1351English Statute of Labourers attempts to restrain wages and mobility.
1358Jacquerie revolt occurs in France.
1381Peasants’ Revolt occurs in England.
Later 1300s–1400sLabor, urban, trade, and manorial relationships continue changing unevenly.

Evidence Set B: Instructional Map

What to notice: Identify location, movement, connections, and geographic limits. This schematic teaches relationships and is not a boundary-precise political map.

Evidence Set C: Written Sources

A contemporary plague account describes widespread death and social disruption; a labor law attempts to hold wages near pre-plague levels; population estimates show a sharp but regionally unequal decline. Exact totals are estimates, so historians compare multiple records.

Source method: Identify creator and context, extract a useful detail, and state one limitation. Never treat a single source as the whole story.

Required Historian’s Notebook Evidence

  1. Copy every timeline entry.
  2. Record three direct map observations.
  3. Create a source table: Source | Context | Useful evidence | Limitation.
  4. Write a 3–4 sentence summary explaining the most important historical relationship shown by the evidence.
