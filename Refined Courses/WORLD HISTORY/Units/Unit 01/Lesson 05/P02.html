Step-by-Step Teaching

Step 1: Anchor the lesson. The topic is Western Europe After Rome. Before reading details, name the time period, place, people, and problem being studied. This prevents guessing from a word that sounds familiar.
Step 2: Read the standard like a historian. The mapped standards are SS.912.W.2.9, SS.912.W.2.10, SS.912.W.2.12, SS.912.W.2.13. Circle the action words in the standard, such as explain, analyze, compare, locate, trace, or evaluate. Those action words tell you what kind of thinking the lesson requires.
Step 3: Use the visual before answering. The required visual is medieval social hierarchy diagram. First identify what the visual shows. Then ask what changed, what stayed the same, what caused the change, and what evidence supports that answer.
Step 4: Build the explanation in order. Start with context, add one precise piece of evidence, explain the relationship, and finish with why it mattered. A strong World History answer never gives only a name, date, or place.
Step 5: Check for confusion before moving on. If two answer choices both sound possible, choose the one that stays inside Western Europe After Rome and directly answers the standard. If you cannot explain why the visual supports your answer, reread the visual and ask the Teacher of Record for help.
