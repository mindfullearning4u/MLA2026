Western Europe After Rome

Learn the Historical Content

Investigation Question

How did Western European societies reorganize after the collapse of Roman authority?

Learn the Historical Content

After western Roman imperial authority ended, Western Europe did not become empty or entirely lawless. Germanic kingdoms, local rulers, bishops, monasteries, towns, and rural communities reorganized authority. Charlemagne temporarily united a large realm, but later division and Viking, Magyar, and Muslim attacks increased demands for local defense. Feudal relationships connected lords, vassals, and knights through negotiated obligations. Manorialism organized agriculture and labor locally. The Christian Church preserved learning, provided services, owned land, and shaped culture. These arrangements varied by region; medieval Europe never operated as one identical system.

Vocabulary and Meanings

MedievalRelating to the Middle Ages in Europe.
FeudalismA broad term for political and military relationships based on land, service, and personal obligations.
ManorialismA locally organized agricultural and economic system centered on a manor.
LordA landholding noble with authority and obligations.
VassalA person who pledged service and loyalty to a lord in return for protection or a fief.
KnightAn armed mounted warrior who often served a lord.
SerfA peasant legally bound to a lord’s land but not owned as personal property.
ManorAn agricultural estate and local community under a lord’s authority.
FiefLand or rights granted in return for service.
ClergyPeople formally authorized to perform religious duties.
MonasteryA religious community where monks or nuns lived, worshiped, studied, and worked.
Political fragmentationThe division of authority among many local rulers rather than one strong central government.
