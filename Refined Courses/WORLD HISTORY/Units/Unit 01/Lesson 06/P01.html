Medieval Monarchies, Law, and National Identity

Lesson Overview

Investigation Question

How did medieval rulers strengthen monarchies while law began to limit royal power?

Lesson Overview

Medieval monarchs expanded royal authority through conquest, taxation, courts, officials, and war. William the Conqueror strengthened Norman rule after 1066. Henry II expanded royal courts and common law. Conflict over taxation and royal decisions led English barons to compel King John to accept Magna Carta in 1215. The charter did not create modern democracy, but it expressed the principle that the king was subject to law. Parliament developed gradually from councils called to approve taxation and advise the king. French Capetian rulers expanded their territory and administration by different paths. The Hundred Years’ War and figures such as Joan of Arc contributed to stronger political identities in England and France.
