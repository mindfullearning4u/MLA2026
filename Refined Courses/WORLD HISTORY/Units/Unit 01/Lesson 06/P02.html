Medieval Monarchies, Law, and National Identity

Learn the Historical Content

Investigation Question

How did medieval rulers strengthen monarchies while law began to limit royal power?

Learn the Historical Content

Medieval monarchs expanded royal authority through conquest, taxation, courts, officials, and war. William the Conqueror strengthened Norman rule after 1066. Henry II expanded royal courts and common law. Conflict over taxation and royal decisions led English barons to compel King John to accept Magna Carta in 1215. The charter did not create modern democracy, but it expressed the principle that the king was subject to law. Parliament developed gradually from councils called to approve taxation and advise the king. French Capetian rulers expanded their territory and administration by different paths. The Hundred Years’ War and figures such as Joan of Arc contributed to stronger political identities in England and France.

Vocabulary and Meanings

MonarchyA government led by a hereditary king or queen.
Common lawLaw developed from court decisions and customary practice.
Magna CartaThe 1215 charter that required the English king to recognize specified rights and legal limits.
ParliamentAn English representative institution that developed from royal councils.
Representative institutionA body in which selected people speak or act for larger groups.
Nation-stateA state whose political identity is associated with a shared national community.
National identityA sense of belonging based on shared history, language, institutions, culture, or political experience.
Royal authorityThe power exercised by a monarch.
Limited governmentGovernment whose power is restricted by law or recognized rights.
Due processThe principle that government must follow established legal procedures.
Hundred Years’ WarA series of conflicts between England and France from 1337 to 1453.
