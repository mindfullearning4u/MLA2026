Context and Evidence Available to You

Medieval Icelandic stories described Norse voyages west of Greenland around 1000 CE. Beginning in 1960, investigators excavated structures at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland.

  • Evidence A—Primary-source recollection: Archaeologist Birgitta Wallace recalled recognizing building forms like Norse ruins she had seen in Iceland. Her participation makes the recollection useful, but later memory cannot date or identify the site alone.
  • Evidence B—Secondary-source interpretation: Parks Canada reports eight eleventh-century Norse-style buildings and related artifacts. The synthesis combines excavation findings but does not display every original excavation record.
  • Evidence C—Archaeological evidence: Excavators found a Norse-style cloak pin, forge remains, iron boat nails, and metal-cut wood dated to 1021 CE. These findings establish material activity and a date but cannot name every occupant.

Chronology: Saga traditions describe voyages around 1000; wood was cut in 1021; investigators located the site in 1960; major excavations followed from 1961–1968.

Choose One Claim

Claim A: Norse people established and used a base camp in North America around the early eleventh century.

Claim B: The evidence shows only that later people told stories about Norse voyages; it does not establish a physical Norse presence in North America.

Select the claim that is better supported. Historical inquiry does not require treating unequal claims as equally strong.

Required Notebook Product

  1. State the claim you selected.
  2. Identify three specific pieces of evidence from the lesson evidence set.
  3. Use evidence from at least two different source types.
  4. Explain one important chronological relationship.
  5. Explain how the evidence agrees, differs, or has limitations.
  6. State one conclusion and one remaining uncertainty.

Required historical-thinking skills: Historical Inquiry, Chronology, Corroboration, Cause and Effect, and Claim-Evidence-Reasoning.

Required vocabulary: Accurately use at least six of these terms: historical inquiry, evidence, primary source, secondary source, chronology, context, corroboration, cause, effect, historical claim, interpretation, limitation.

Self-Check Before Continuing

  • My claim answers the question and is not only an opinion.
  • I named three precise details rather than saying “the sources show.”
  • I explained why the evidence supports the claim.
  • I compared evidence from at least two source types.
  • I identified a limitation or remaining uncertainty.
  • The response reflects my own reasoning.
