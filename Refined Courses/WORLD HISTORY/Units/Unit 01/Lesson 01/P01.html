Historical Inquiry, Timelines, and Cause and Effect

Lesson Overview

Investigation Question

How do historians use evidence to determine what most likely happened in the past?

Quick Lesson Overview

History is more than a list of dates. Historians ask focused questions, place events in chronological context, examine different kinds of evidence, and compare what sources can and cannot establish.

In this lesson, you will follow that inquiry process. You will organize events, compare a primary source with later interpretations and non-narrative evidence, examine cause-and-effect relationships, and decide which historical explanation is best supported.

Why This Lesson Matters

Every later lesson in World History asks you to make decisions about evidence. Learning how to distinguish evidence from opinion, recognize a source's limits, and corroborate information will help you evaluate maps, timelines, laws, artifacts, accounts, and historical arguments throughout the course.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish evidence, interpretation, opinion, and unsupported claims.
  • Classify primary and secondary sources and explain their usefulness and limitations.
  • Organize background conditions, immediate causes, events, and effects chronologically.
  • Corroborate information from multiple source types.
  • Construct a defensible historical claim supported by evidence and reasoning.

MLA Standards Connection

MLA Standards: MLA.WH.HI.01, MLA.WH.HI.02, MLA.WH.HI.03

These standards require you to investigate historical questions, analyze and compare evidence, organize chronology and causation, and support a historical conclusion with defensible reasoning.
