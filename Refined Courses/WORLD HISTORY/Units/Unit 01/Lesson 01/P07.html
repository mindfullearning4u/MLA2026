Mastery Checkpoint Directions and Writing Guide

Checkpoint Question

How do historians use evidence to determine what most likely happened in the past?

What You Must Submit

Write a 200–250-word response in three organized paragraphs: an introduction, a body paragraph, and a conclusion.

  • Use at least two specific pieces of evidence from at least two different source types.
  • Include at least one accurate chronological relationship.
  • Explain at least one source's usefulness or limitation.
  • Apply Historical Inquiry, Corroboration, and Claim-Evidence-Reasoning.
  • Use at least four assigned vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Write in your own words and show your own reasoning.

The Coursewide Historical Writing Method

Use the introduction–body–conclusion approach below for this checkpoint and for future World History assignments that require extended writing. Learning this structure now will help you organize increasingly complex historical arguments throughout the course.

Paragraph 1: Introduction

Your introduction tells the reader what you will prove.

  1. Directly answer the checkpoint question.
  2. State a clear historical claim.
  3. Briefly identify the kinds of evidence you will use.

Sentence starters:

  • Historians determine what most likely happened by...
  • The strongest historical conclusion is based on...
  • To investigate the past, historians compare...

Do not list every detail in the introduction. Save specific evidence and explanation for the body paragraph.

Paragraph 2: Body

Your body paragraph proves the claim with evidence and reasoning.

  1. Introduce the first source and identify its source type.
  2. State one specific piece of evidence from that source.
  3. Explain how the evidence supports your claim.
  4. Introduce a second source of a different type and state specific evidence from it.
  5. Explain how the two sources agree, differ, or corroborate one another.
  6. Include an accurate chronological relationship.
  7. Explain at least one source's usefulness or limitation.

Sentence starters:

  • Evidence from the primary source shows...
  • The secondary interpretation supports this because...
  • This evidence is useful because..., but its limitation is...
  • Chronologically, ___ occurred before/after ___, which matters because...
  • Taken together, these sources...

Paragraph 3: Conclusion

Your conclusion explains what the combined evidence establishes.

  1. Restate your claim using different words.
  2. Explain what the evidence most strongly supports.
  3. Identify one remaining limitation, uncertainty, or unanswered question.
  4. End by explaining why comparing multiple sources creates a stronger historical conclusion.

Sentence starters:

  • Overall, the combined evidence most strongly supports...
  • Although the evidence establishes..., it cannot determine...
  • Therefore, corroborating multiple sources allows historians to...
