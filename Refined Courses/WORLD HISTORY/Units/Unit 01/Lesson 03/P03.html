Byzantine Empire: Continuity and Change

Historical Evidence Lab

Investigation Question

In what ways did the Byzantine Empire preserve Roman traditions while creating a distinct civilization?

Evidence Set A: Timeline

DateEvidence
330Constantine dedicates Constantinople as an imperial capital.
395Permanent administrative division of the Roman Empire.
476Western imperial government ends; the eastern empire continues.
527–565Reign of Justinian; legal compilation, expansion, and Hagia Sophia.
532–537Hagia Sophia is constructed.

Evidence Set B: Strategic Location

Direct observations: Constantinople lies between Europe and Asia, beside a narrow waterway, and along routes connecting the Black Sea with Mediterranean networks.

Evidence Set C: Justinian Code

“Justice is the constant and perpetual wish to render every one his due.”

Source: Justinian, Institutes, Book I, translated by J. B. Moyle (1913), public-domain translation. Excerpt preserved; spelling unchanged.

Evidence Set D: Hagia Sophia

The building preserves Roman engineering knowledge while adapting it to Eastern Christian worship and distinctive Byzantine mosaics.

Required Historian’s Notebook Evidence

  1. Copy the five timeline entries.
  2. Record three direct map observations.
  3. Copy the Justinian Code excerpt and write one sentence explaining how it reflects Roman legal continuity.
  4. Write this evidence summary: Byzantium preserved Roman imperial government, law, and engineering while adapting them through Greek language, Eastern Orthodox Christianity, strategic trade, and distinctive sacred art.
