Byzantine Empire: Continuity and Change

Historical Thinking

Investigation Question

In what ways did the Byzantine Empire preserve Roman traditions while creating a distinct civilization?

How Historians Classify Evidence

CategoryMeaningExampleReasoning
ContinuityRemains substantially presentEmperor and Roman lawThe state retained Roman political and legal foundations.
AdaptationPreserved but modifiedJustinian CodeRoman laws were reorganized for sixth-century government.
ChangeBecomes meaningfully differentGreek language and Eastern OrthodoxyThe empire developed a distinct eastern cultural identity.

Worked Example: Hagia Sophia

Step 1—Identify the feature: A vast domed Christian church built in Constantinople.

Step 2—Look backward: Roman builders had developed domes, arches, concrete construction, and imperial monumentality.

Step 3—Identify modification: Byzantine architects used those traditions for Eastern Christian worship, abundant interior light, and mosaic decoration.

Classification: Hagia Sophia is best classified as an adaptation because it preserves Roman engineering while transforming its purpose and visual meaning.

Model CER: Hagia Sophia shows adaptation. Its dome and structural engineering continue Roman building knowledge, while its Christian worship space and mosaics express a distinctive Byzantine religious culture. Therefore, Byzantine builders transformed a Roman architectural inheritance rather than merely copying it.
