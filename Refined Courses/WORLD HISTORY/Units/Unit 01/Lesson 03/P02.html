Byzantine Empire: Continuity and Change

Learn the Historical Content

Investigation Question

In what ways did the Byzantine Empire preserve Roman traditions while creating a distinct civilization?

From Rome to Byzantium

In 395 CE, the Roman Empire was permanently divided into western and eastern administrations. The western imperial government ended in 476, but the eastern empire continued for nearly one thousand more years. Its residents called themselves Romans. Modern historians usually call their state the Byzantine Empire.

Constantinople occupied a defensible peninsula beside the Bosphorus, the waterway linking the Black Sea and Mediterranean trade systems. The capital connected Europe and Asia and collected wealth through trade and taxation.

Emperor Justinian and Empress Theodora strengthened imperial rule during the sixth century. Justinian ordered jurists to organize centuries of Roman law into the Justinian Code. The empire remained Christian, but Greek language and Eastern Orthodox traditions became increasingly distinctive. Hagia Sophia joined Roman engineering traditions with an enormous dome, Christian worship, light, and mosaics.

Vocabulary and Meanings

Byzantine EmpireThe continuation of the Eastern Roman Empire, centered at Constantinople.
ConstantinopleThe imperial capital founded by Constantine at the Bosphorus.
JustinianByzantine emperor from 527–565 who sponsored legal reform and imperial expansion.
TheodoraEmpress and influential political partner of Justinian.
Justinian CodeA compilation and organization of Roman law ordered by Justinian.
Eastern Orthodox ChristianityThe Christian tradition that developed in the Byzantine East.
PatriarchA leading bishop in Eastern Orthodox Christianity.
MosaicA picture or design made from small pieces of colored glass, stone, or tile.
ContinuityA feature that remains substantially present over time.
ChangeA feature that becomes meaningfully different over time.
AdaptationA preserved idea or institution modified for new conditions.
Cultural diffusionThe spread of beliefs, customs, technologies, or ideas among societies.
