P04 Civic Reasoning

Articles of Confederation and the Constitutional Convention

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.3: Compare the Articles of Confederation with the Constitution and explain why the Constitutional Convention created a stronger federal system.

Student-friendly meaning: I can compare the first national government with the Constitution and explain how specific weaknesses led delegates to design stronger—but still limited—federal authority.

Reasoning Focus: Reform, Compromise, and Limits

The Convention met in Philadelphia in 1787 with instructions to revise the Articles. Delegates soon decided to draft a new framework. The change was not simply “weak government to strong government.” The central design problem was how to make national authority effective while dividing and limiting that authority.

Major Convention Decisions

DisputeDecisionConstitutional effect
Large-state vs. small-state representationGreat (Connecticut) CompromiseHouse based on population; Senate gives equal state representation
How enslaved people would be counted for representation and direct taxationThree-Fifths CompromiseIncreased slaveholding states’ representation while denying enslaved people political rights; a profound injustice, later superseded by constitutional change
Commerce and the transatlantic slave tradeCongress received commerce power, while the Constitution barred prohibition of the importation of enslaved persons before 1808Expanded national economic authority while protecting slavery through a morally indefensible concession
Fear of concentrated powerSeparated branches, checks, elections, federalism, and enumerated powersStronger authority was dispersed and constrained

Claim–Evidence–Reasoning Model

Claim: The Constitution responded to the Articles’ enforcement problem without giving one institution unlimited power.

Evidence: Congress gained taxing and commerce powers, but legislation requires two chambers and is subject to presidential veto and judicial review within the constitutional system.

Reasoning: The national government can act directly, yet separated institutions and constitutional limits make action accountable.

Misconception Check

  • The Articles were not “no government”; they created a Congress and national responsibilities.
  • Shays’ Rebellion did not single-handedly cause the Constitution; it intensified broader concerns.
  • “Stronger federal government” does not mean unlimited federal government.
  • Convention compromises should be evaluated for both institutional effect and human consequences.
