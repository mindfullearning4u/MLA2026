P06 Independent Civic Investigation

Articles of Confederation and the Constitutional Convention

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.3: Compare the Articles of Confederation with the Constitution and explain why the Constitutional Convention created a stronger federal system.

Student-friendly meaning: I can compare the first national government with the Constitution and explain how specific weaknesses led delegates to design stronger—but still limited—federal authority.

Independent Directions

  1. Complete every part without AI-written reasoning.
  2. Use the comparison table and official-source evidence.
  3. Label each answer and write complete sentences. Submit this page with Notebook Evidence.

Part A: Compare Structures

  1. Compare the source of authority under the Articles and Constitution.
  2. Compare national revenue powers.
  3. Compare enforcement and judicial structures.
  4. Compare amendment requirements and explain the tradeoff between stability and adaptability.

Part B: Diagnose Problems

  1. A national treaty requires compliance, but a state ignores it. Explain why the Articles system struggled and identify a constitutional response.
  2. Three states tax goods from neighboring states. Explain the collective-action problem and the relevant federal power.
  3. A proposal would place all legislative, executive, and judicial authority in one national council. Explain why this is inconsistent with the Convention’s limited-government design.
  4. A speaker says the Three-Fifths Compromise “gave enslaved people representation.” Correct the claim accurately and explain the political effect.

Part C: Evidence-Based Argument

Prompt: Which change most directly transformed the United States from a confederation into an effective federal system: national taxing power, national commerce power, separate executive/judiciary, or federal supremacy?

Choose one, acknowledge at least one competing answer, use three specific pieces of evidence, and explain why your choice is most fundamental.

Independent Work Checklist

  • Eight numbered responses are complete.
  • The argument uses three accurate pieces of evidence and a counterclaim.
  • Historical compromises are described accurately and without minimizing slavery.
  • Any assistance is disclosed; the reasoning is your own.
