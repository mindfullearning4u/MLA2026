P05 Guided Civic Investigation

Articles of Confederation and the Constitutional Convention

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.3: Compare the Articles of Confederation with the Constitution and explain why the Constitutional Convention created a stronger federal system.

Student-friendly meaning: I can compare the first national government with the Constitution and explain how specific weaknesses led delegates to design stronger—but still limited—federal authority.

How Guided Practice Works

Study each model: notice the decisive evidence, identify the relevant structural problem, connect it to a constitutional response, and verify the conclusion.

Guided Example 1: Revenue

Scenario: Congress owes soldiers and foreign creditors but can only ask states for money. Several states send less than requested.

  1. Notice: The obligation is national, but revenue depends on voluntary state compliance.
  2. Concept: A requisition is not an enforceable taxing power.
  3. Response: The Constitution authorizes Congress to lay and collect taxes.
  4. Limit: Tax laws still require bicameral passage, presentment, and constitutional compliance.

Conclusion: The Constitution made national commitments fundable while keeping fiscal authority inside a limited lawmaking process.

Guided Example 2: Interstate Commerce

Scenario: Neighboring states impose conflicting trade rules and retaliatory fees.

  1. The evidence shows a multi-state coordination problem.
  2. Independent state policies can create barriers that no single state can resolve.
  3. The Commerce Clause gives Congress authority over interstate commerce.

Conclusion: National commerce authority addresses collective-action problems; it does not erase all state regulatory authority.

Guided Example 3: Representation

Question: Why did the Great Compromise create two chambers instead of choosing only population or state equality?

Model reasoning: Population-based representation reflected individuals in larger states, while equal Senate representation protected states as political units. Requiring both chambers to agree created a dual constituency and an internal check.

Guided Example 4: Evaluate a Claim

Claim: “Because the Constitution strengthened the national government, the states ceased to possess constitutional authority.”

Answer: Incorrect. The Constitution created federalism: national powers are enumerated, states retain substantial governing authority, and the Supremacy Clause makes valid federal law supreme only within the constitutional system.
