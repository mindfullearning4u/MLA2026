Scenario: Several states impose conflicting trade rules, Congress cannot reliably raise revenue to pay debts, and an amendment fails even though twelve of thirteen states support it.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The facts reveal structural weaknesses under the Articles: weak commerce authority, dependence on state requisitions, and an unanimity rule that made change nearly impossible. The Constitution responded with enumerated taxing and commerce powers, a new federal structure, and a less-than-unanimous amendment process while retaining limits.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: The Constitution did not simply create unlimited national power. It strengthened specified national capacities while dividing power among branches and between the nation and states.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
