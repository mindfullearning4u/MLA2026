Detailed Teaching Sequence

  1. Name the actor. Write who is acting and at what level or branch. This prevents using a rule that belongs to someone else.
  2. Name the action. Describe exactly what the actor proposes, requires, prohibits, funds, or decides.
  3. Select the rule. Use the visual below to find the rule that directly addresses that action.
  4. Test the facts. Identify one fact supporting the action and one fact showing a possible limit.
  5. Write the reasoning link. Complete: “This evidence matters because the rule requires ___, and the facts show ___.”

Evidence Set: Articles-versus-Constitution comparison chart

Scenario: Several states impose conflicting trade rules, Congress cannot reliably raise revenue to pay debts, and an amendment fails even though twelve of thirteen states support it.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Articles: structureA one-house Congress; no separate national executive or federal judiciary.
Articles: revenueCongress requested funds from states and lacked a direct general taxing power.
Articles: commerceCongress could not effectively regulate interstate and foreign commerce.
Articles: amendmentAmendments required unanimous state approval.
Constitutional responseSeparate branches, enumerated national powers, federal courts, and enforceable federal law.
Continuing limitsFederal powers remained listed and divided; states retained important authority.
