Evidence Set: Power-and-checks system map

Scenario: Congress passes a law, the President vetoes it, Congress overrides the veto, a state challenges the law, and a federal court reviews the constitutional dispute.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The sequence shows distinct principles working together: separation of powers assigns different roles, the veto and override are checks, federalism creates the national-state dispute, and judicial review allows a court to decide a concrete constitutional claim. No single principle fully explains the pathway.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: Separation of powers identifies primary roles; checks and balances identify specific interactions. The terms are related but not interchangeable.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
