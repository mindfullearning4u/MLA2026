Federalism, Separation of Powers, and Checks and Balances

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.4: Explain federalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, popular sovereignty, and individual rights in the U.S. constitutional design.

Student-friendly meaning: I can trace who has constitutional authority, identify the safeguard that controls it, and explain how the safeguard protects self-government and rights.

Notebook Directions

Define each term, add one constitutional example, and answer Questions 1–5.

Vocabulary

  • popular sovereignty: government authority originates with the people.
  • federalism: constitutional division of authority between national and state governments.
  • delegated/enumerated power: authority granted to the national government.
  • reserved power: authority retained by states or the people.
  • concurrent power: authority exercised by both national and state governments, such as taxation.
  • separation of powers: legislative, executive, and judicial functions are assigned to different branches.
  • check and balance: a constitutional tool one institution uses to limit another.
  • individual right: a protected freedom or legal guarantee held by a person.
