P01 Lesson Overview

Unit 1 · Lesson 4: Constitutional Principles in Action

Lesson Title

Constitutional Principles in Action

Standards Covered in This Lesson

MLA.USG.FND.4: Analyze federalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, popular sovereignty, and protection of rights in the constitutional system.

What You Will Learn

You will learn to identify the correct government actor, apply the mapped rule, use relevant evidence, and explain a limit or competing consideration.

What You Will Do

  1. Learn six precise terms.
  2. Study the power-and-checks system map.
  3. Watch three complete reasoning models.
  4. Practice with feedback.
  5. Complete self-contained independent work.
  6. Submit a self-contained mastery checkpoint.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will earn at least 80% by making an accurate claim, citing two relevant details supplied with the task, explaining why each detail matters, and addressing a limit or counterclaim.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can answer “How do constitutional principles distribute power while protecting liberty?” with accurate civic vocabulary, evidence, and reasoning.

Evidence Set: Power-and-checks system map

Scenario: Congress passes a law, the President vetoes it, Congress overrides the veto, a state challenges the law, and a federal court reviews the constitutional dispute.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Popular sovereigntyThe people authorize the Constitution and choose representatives.
FederalismNational and state governments possess different, sometimes overlapping powers.
Separation of powersCongress legislates, the President executes, and courts decide cases.
Checks and balancesExamples include veto, override, confirmation, and judicial review.
Rights protectionConstitutional guarantees restrict what majorities and officials may do.
AccountabilityElections, oversight, courts, and lawful participation provide different checks.
