P06 Independent Civic Investigation

Federalism, Separation of Powers, and Checks and Balances

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.4: Explain federalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, popular sovereignty, and individual rights in the U.S. constitutional design.

Student-friendly meaning: I can trace who has constitutional authority, identify the safeguard that controls it, and explain how the safeguard protects self-government and rights.

Independent Directions

Complete all responses independently and submit with Notebook Evidence.

Part A: Classify

  1. Classify coinage, public schooling, taxation, declaring war, and establishing courts as national, state, or concurrent; explain each.
  2. Distinguish separation of powers and checks and balances using an original example.
  3. Explain why popular sovereignty and individual rights can create tension but are not opposites.

Part B: Analyze

  1. A governor refuses to follow a valid federal court order. Identify the federalism and rule-of-law issues.
  2. Congress orders a criminal conviction without trial. Identify the branch and rights problems.
  3. The President signs a treaty and claims Senate approval is unnecessary. Correct the claim.
  4. A state regulates an entirely local matter not prohibited by federal law. Explain why federalism does not automatically invalidate the state action.

Part C: Argument

Which safeguard most effectively prevents concentrated power: federalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, elections, or individual rights? Use three constitutional examples and answer a counterclaim.

Checklist

  • Every conclusion identifies constitutional authority and a limit.
  • Examples distinguish level, branch, and safeguard.
  • The argument uses three accurate examples and a counterclaim.
  • Reasoning is original and assistance is disclosed.
