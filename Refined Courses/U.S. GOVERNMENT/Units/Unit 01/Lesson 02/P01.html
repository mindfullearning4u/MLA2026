Founding Documents and Enlightenment Influences

Essential Question

How did earlier documents, political experiences, and Enlightenment thinkers shape American ideas about rights, consent, and limited government?

What You Will Learn and Do

  • Distinguish the contribution of each source instead of treating all sources as identical.
  • Trace natural rights and consent to Locke and the Declaration.
  • Trace limits on power to English constitutional traditions and Montesquieu.
  • Analyze primary-source language, a historical document image, and an AI-generated claim.
  • Construct an evidence-based influence explanation.

Lesson Thinking Tool: Influence Chain

Source or thinker Key contribution Later American connection
Magna Carta (1215) Government power is subject to law; lawful procedure protects liberty. Limited government and due process.
English Bill of Rights (1689) The monarch cannot suspend laws or tax without Parliament; subjects possess stated protections. Legislative authority, petition, limits on executive power, and a bill of rights.
Mayflower Compact (1620) Settlers consented to form a civil body politic and make laws for the common good. Self-government, consent, and written political agreement.
John Locke People possess natural rights; government exists by consent to protect them. Declaration language and popular sovereignty.
Montesquieu Dividing governmental power can prevent tyranny. Separation of powers and checks and balances.
Common Sense (1776) Thomas Paine argued publicly for independence and republican government. Broader support for breaking from monarchy.
Declaration of Independence (1776) Natural rights, equality, consent, and a right to alter destructive government. A statement of American political ideals and justification for independence.
