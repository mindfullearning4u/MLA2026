Unit 1 · Lesson 2: Ideas and Documents That Shaped American Government

Lesson Title

Ideas and Documents That Shaped American Government

Standards Covered in This Lesson

MLA.USG.FND.2: Analyze how classical, English, colonial, and Enlightenment ideas and documents influenced American constitutional government.

What You Will Learn

You will learn to identify the correct government actor, apply the mapped rule, use relevant evidence, and explain a limit or competing consideration.

What You Will Do

  1. Learn six precise terms.
  2. Study the influence-and-evidence comparison table.
  3. Watch three complete reasoning models.
  4. Practice with feedback.
  5. Complete self-contained independent work.
  6. Submit a self-contained mastery checkpoint.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will earn at least 80% by making an accurate claim, citing two relevant details supplied with the task, explaining why each detail matters, and addressing a limit or counterclaim.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can answer “How did earlier ideas and documents shape American constitutional principles?” with accurate civic vocabulary, evidence, and reasoning.

Evidence Set: Influence-and-evidence comparison table

Scenario: Delegates propose a constitution with an elected assembly, separate executive, independent courts, protected jury trial, and a declaration that authority comes from the people.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Magna CartaHelped establish that rulers are subject to law and lawful procedures.
English Bill of RightsStrengthened legislative limits on executive power and listed protected rights.
Locke and social contractConnected legitimate authority to consent and protection of natural rights.
MontesquieuInfluenced the division of legislative, executive, and judicial functions.
Colonial self-governmentGave colonists experience with assemblies, compacts, and local participation.
Classical republicanismEmphasized civic responsibility and resistance to corruption.
