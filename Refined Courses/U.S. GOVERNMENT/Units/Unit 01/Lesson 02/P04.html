P04 Civic Reasoning: Worked Examples

Founding Documents and Enlightenment Influences

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.2: Analyze how Magna Carta, the English Bill of Rights, the Mayflower Compact, Common Sense, the Declaration of Independence, and Enlightenment political thought influenced the American constitutional system.

Student-friendly meaning: I can trace a constitutional idea to an earlier source, explain what the source contributed, and support the connection with evidence.

Reasoning Routine

  1. Name the later American principle.
  2. Locate precise earlier evidence.
  3. Explain the logical connection.
  4. State a limit: influence does not mean an exact copy.

Worked Example 1: Magna Carta to Limited Government

Question: How did Magna Carta influence American constitutional government?

  1. Later principle: Government is limited by higher law.
  2. Earlier evidence: The charter subjected royal action to legal constraint and lawful judgment.
  3. Connection: If even a monarch is not above law, constitutional rules may bind later officials.
  4. Limit: Magna Carta did not establish modern American equality or democracy.

Answer: Magna Carta influenced the principle of limited government by establishing that a ruler's power could be constrained by law and lawful procedure.

Worked Example 2: Locke to the Declaration

Evidence: Locke argued that people possess rights and create government by consent; the Declaration identifies inherent rights and government power derived from consent.

Reasoning: The shared structure—rights first, government second—shows intellectual influence.

Answer: Locke influenced the Declaration's explanation that government is legitimate only when it protects inherent rights with the people's consent.

Worked Example 3: Montesquieu to Constitutional Structure

Evidence: Montesquieu warned against concentrating legislative, executive, and judicial power.

Reasoning: Dividing power creates institutional barriers to arbitrary rule.

Answer: Montesquieu influenced separation of powers because his reasoning connected divided authority to the prevention of tyranny.
