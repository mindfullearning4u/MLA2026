P05 Guided Civic Investigation

Founding Documents and Enlightenment Influences

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.2: Analyze how Magna Carta, the English Bill of Rights, the Mayflower Compact, Common Sense, the Declaration of Independence, and Enlightenment political thought influenced the American constitutional system.

Student-friendly meaning: I can trace a constitutional idea to an earlier source, explain what the source contributed, and support the connection with evidence.

How Guided Practice Works

Each model below shows the question, what to notice, the evidence, how to derive the answer, the answer, and the reasoning. Study the process before attempting independent work.

Model 1: Source-to-Principle Match

Question: Which source most directly supports the claim that political authority begins with the people agreeing to form a government?

What to notice: “agreeing to form” points to consent and a written political compact.

Evidence: The Mayflower signers formed a civil political body and agreed to create laws for the common good.

Derivation: Eliminate Magna Carta because its strongest contribution is a legal limit on rulers. Eliminate Montesquieu because his focus is divided power. The Mayflower Compact directly records agreement to create a government.

Answer: The Mayflower Compact.

Reasoning: Its signers used consent and a written agreement to establish self-government.

Model 2: Compare Two Sources

Question: How do Locke and the Declaration connect?

What to notice: Look for the same logical order, not identical wording.

Evidence: Locke places natural rights before government and makes government dependent on consent. The Declaration identifies inherent rights and just powers derived from consent.

Derivation: Both begin with people and rights, then explain government's purpose and authority.

Answer: Locke influenced the Declaration's natural-rights and consent reasoning.

Reasoning: Both make rights inherent and government legitimate only through the people.

Model 3: Evaluate an AI Claim

Claim: “The English Bill of Rights and Declaration of Independence are the same because both list rights.”

What to notice: Sharing a feature does not make two documents identical.

Evidence: The English Bill of Rights restricts monarchical power within the English system. The Declaration justifies independence using natural rights, consent, and grievances.

Derivation: Identify the similarity, then compare purpose, audience, and political result.

Answer: The claim is misleading.

Reasoning: Both protect liberty, but one limits monarchy within an existing system while the other justifies creating a separate government.
