Scenario: Delegates propose a constitution with an elected assembly, separate executive, independent courts, protected jury trial, and a declaration that authority comes from the people.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The proposal combines several influences rather than copying one source. Popular authority and rights reflect social-contract thinking; separate institutions reflect Montesquieu; jury protection and limits on rulers reflect English legal traditions; the elected assembly also reflects colonial practice.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: One constitutional feature may have multiple influences. Accurate analysis identifies a specific similarity and explains the connection instead of claiming that one thinker or document created the entire Constitution.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
