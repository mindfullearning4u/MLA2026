Founding Documents and Enlightenment Influences

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.2: Analyze how Magna Carta, the English Bill of Rights, the Mayflower Compact, Common Sense, the Declaration of Independence, and Enlightenment political thought influenced the American constitutional system.

Student-friendly meaning: I can trace a constitutional idea to an earlier source, explain what the source contributed, and support the connection with evidence.

What to Do

  1. Study the influence chain and source excerpts.
  2. Examine the document image for visual evidence.
  3. Answer Questions 1-7 in your notebook with source names and specific details.
  4. Keep these responses as required Notebook Evidence.

Evidence Set

Source A: Magna Carta

The 1215 charter established an enduring principle: the ruler's power is constrained by law and lawful judgment.

Source: UK Parliament, Magna Carta.

Source B: Mayflower Compact

The signers agreed to form a civil political community and create laws for the general good, illustrating consent and written self-government.

Source: U.S. National Archives, Mayflower Compact.

Source C: Declaration of Independence

“deriving their just powers from the consent of the governed”

The phrase connects legitimate government to popular consent.

Source: U.S. National Archives, Declaration transcript.

Notebook Questions

  1. What limit on rulers is associated with Magna Carta?
  2. What evidence makes the Mayflower Compact an example of consent and self-government?
  3. Which Declaration words most directly connect to Locke?
  4. How did Montesquieu address the danger of concentrated power?
  5. Why did Common Sense matter even though it did not create a constitution?
  6. Identify one similarity and one difference between the English Bill of Rights and the Declaration.
  7. What does the Declaration image reveal that a short quotation does not?
