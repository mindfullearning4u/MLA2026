Vocabulary

TermDefinitionExample or connection
influenceAn idea or practice that helps shape a later idea or institution.Magna Carta influenced later thinking about lawful limits on rulers.
due processFair legal procedures government must follow before depriving a person of life, liberty, or property.An accusation alone is not a lawful conviction.
natural rightsRights people possess by being human rather than by government permission.Locke identified life, liberty, and property.
social contractThe idea that people create government and accept obligations in exchange for protection of rights and order.Government loses legitimacy when it destroys the purposes for which it was created.
popular sovereigntyThe people are the ultimate source of governmental authority.Legitimate power begins with the people.
republicA government in which public power is exercised through representatives and law rather than a hereditary monarch.Paine promoted republican government.
separation of powersGovernmental authority is divided among institutions with different functions.Legislative, executive, and judicial powers are not concentrated in one person.
primary sourceEvidence created during the period being studied or by a direct participant.The Declaration's text is a primary source.

Influence Chain

Source or thinkerKey contributionLater American connection
Magna Carta (1215)Government power is subject to law; lawful procedure protects liberty.Limited government and due process.
English Bill of Rights (1689)The monarch cannot suspend laws or tax without Parliament; subjects possess stated protections.Legislative authority, petition, limits on executive power, and a bill of rights.
Mayflower Compact (1620)Settlers consented to form a civil body politic and make laws for the common good.Self-government, consent, and written political agreement.
John LockePeople possess natural rights; government exists by consent to protect them.Declaration language and popular sovereignty.
MontesquieuDividing governmental power can prevent tyranny.Separation of powers and checks and balances.
Common Sense (1776)Thomas Paine argued publicly for independence and republican government.Broader support for breaking from monarchy.
Declaration of Independence (1776)Natural rights, equality, consent, and a right to alter destructive government.A statement of American political ideals and justification for independence.
