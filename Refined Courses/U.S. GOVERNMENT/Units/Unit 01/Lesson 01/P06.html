Evidence Set: Foundational-principles evidence matrix

Scenario: A governor orders the detention of peaceful critics without a hearing, arguing that public unity is more important than ordinary legal protections.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Natural rightsGovernment exists to secure rights rather than create every human right.
ConsentPublic authority must be traceable to the people and constitutional processes.
Rule of lawOfficials must follow valid law; office does not place a person above it.
Limited governmentA desirable goal does not erase constitutional limits or procedures.
RepublicanismElections and representation connect public power to the governed.
Civic virtueInformed, lawful participation helps institutions remain accountable.

Instructions

  1. Read the complete evidence set.
  2. Complete Parts A–C in order.
  3. Use only the printed vocabulary, scenario, and visual.
  4. Check each response against the completion criteria.

Part A — Precision

Choose four terms from this page’s visual or scenario. For each, write a definition, accurate example, tempting non-example, and the evidence that distinguishes them.

Part B — Evidence Analysis

Analyze the detention order under natural rights, consent, rule of law, and limited government. For each principle, cite one printed fact and explain the connection.

Part C — Competing Claims

Claim A: the proposed government action is adequately supported. Claim B: the action requires revision or more evidence. Choose the stronger claim, cite two details printed on this page, explain each link, and identify one fact that could reverse your conclusion.
