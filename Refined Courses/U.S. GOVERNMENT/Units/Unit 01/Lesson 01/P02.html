Natural Rights, Rule of Law, and Limited Government

Standard for This Page

MLA.USG.FND.1: Explain natural rights, consent of the governed, republicanism, limited government, rule of law, and civic virtue as foundations of American constitutional government.

Student-friendly meaning: I can explain where government authority comes from, which rights government must protect, and why leaders and citizens must follow constitutional rules.

Vocabulary and Meaning

TermStudent-friendly meaningExample
natural rightsBasic rights people possess because they are human, not because a government grants them.A person does not need a ruler's permission to possess liberty.
consent of the governedThe principle that legitimate government receives authority from the people.Citizens authorize representatives through lawful elections.
republicanismA system in which citizens exercise political power by choosing representatives who govern under law.Voters choose lawmakers who must follow the Constitution.
limited governmentGovernment may exercise only the powers allowed by law and the Constitution.An official cannot ignore due process simply because it is convenient.
rule of lawKnown laws govern public decisions and apply to officials as well as citizens.A governor can be investigated for violating the same law that applies to others.
civic virtueResponsible habits that support self-government and the common good.A citizen studies reliable information before voting.
legitimateLawful and accepted as having proper authority.A government created through constitutional procedures has legitimate authority.
arbitrary powerPower exercised according to personal will rather than known law or fair rules.A leader punishes a critic even though no law was broken.

How the Ideas Connect

People possess rights → they create government by consent → they select representatives in a republic → a constitution limits power → the rule of law holds everyone accountable → civic virtue helps the system endure.

PrincipleAsk yourselfEvidence to look forNon-example
Natural rightsWhat rights belong to every person?Life, liberty, equality, safety, property, or pursuit of happinessA privilege that a leader may remove for any reason
Consent of the governedWho gives government legitimate authority?Elections, representation, public approval, or the people creating governmentA ruler claiming power without public authorization
RepublicanismHow do citizens govern in a large nation?Citizens choose representatives who act under lawOfficials inherit permanent power
Limited governmentWhat prevents government from using unlimited power?A constitution, enumerated powers, rights, procedures, and enforceable limitsOfficials may do anything they choose
Rule of lawWho must obey the law?Citizens and officials are accountable to known, fairly applied lawsLeaders are above the law
Civic virtueWhat habits help self-government work?Informed participation, responsibility, honesty, respect, and concern for the common goodIgnoring lawful duties while demanding benefits
