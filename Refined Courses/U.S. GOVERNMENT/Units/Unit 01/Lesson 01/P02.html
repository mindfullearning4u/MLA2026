Natural Rights, Rule of Law, and Limited Government

Standard for This Page

Vocabulary and Meaning

Term Student-friendly meaning Example
natural rights Basic rights people possess because they are human, not because a government grants them. A person does not need a ruler's permission to possess liberty.
consent of the governed The principle that legitimate government receives authority from the people. Citizens authorize representatives through lawful elections.
republicanism A system in which citizens exercise political power by choosing representatives who govern under law. Voters choose lawmakers who must follow the Constitution.
limited government Government may exercise only the powers allowed by law and the Constitution. An official cannot ignore due process simply because it is convenient.
rule of law Known laws govern public decisions and apply to officials as well as citizens. A governor can be investigated for violating the same law that applies to others.
civic virtue Responsible habits that support self-government and the common good. A citizen studies reliable information before voting.
legitimate Lawful and accepted as having proper authority. A government created through constitutional procedures has legitimate authority.
arbitrary power Power exercised according to personal will rather than known law or fair rules. A leader punishes a critic even though no law was broken.

How the Ideas Connect

People possess rights → they create government by consent → they select representatives in a republic → a constitution limits power → the rule of law holds everyone accountable → civic virtue helps the system endure.

Principle Ask yourself Evidence to look for Non-example
Natural rights What rights belong to every person? Life, liberty, equality, safety, property, or pursuit of happiness A privilege that a leader may remove for any reason
Consent of the governed Who gives government legitimate authority? Elections, representation, public approval, or the people creating government A ruler claiming power without public authorization
Republicanism How do citizens govern in a large nation? Citizens choose representatives who act under law Officials inherit permanent power
Limited government What prevents government from using unlimited power? A constitution, enumerated powers, rights, procedures, and enforceable limits Officials may do anything they choose
Rule of law Who must obey the law? Citizens and officials are accountable to known, fairly applied laws Leaders are above the law
Civic virtue What habits help self-government work? Informed participation, responsibility, honesty, respect, and concern for the common good Ignoring lawful duties while demanding benefits
