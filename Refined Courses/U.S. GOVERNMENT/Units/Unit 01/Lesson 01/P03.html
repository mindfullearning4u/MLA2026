Notebook Task — Part 2

Evidence Set: Foundational-principles evidence matrix

Scenario: A governor orders the detention of peaceful critics without a hearing, arguing that public unity is more important than ordinary legal protections.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Natural rightsGovernment exists to secure rights rather than create every human right.

Rule of lawOfficials must follow valid law; office does not place a person above it.
Limited governmentA desirable goal does not erase constitutional limits or procedures.
RepublicanismElections and representation connect public power to the governed.
Civic virtueInformed, lawful participation helps institutions remain accountable.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The order conflicts with rule of law and limited government because an official cannot replace established legal process with personal preference. Natural-rights reasoning also requires government to justify restrictions on liberty. Public unity alone does not prove lawful authority.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: Consent of the governed does not mean a temporary majority may ignore individual rights or constitutional procedure; legitimate authority remains limited by higher law.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
