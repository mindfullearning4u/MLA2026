1. Rights Come Before Government

Natural-rights theory begins with the individual. Government does not create a person's humanity or basic liberty. People establish government to protect rights and provide lawful order. This principle does not mean every personal preference is a right; a claim must be connected to liberty, equality, safety, or another defensible constitutional principle.

2. Legitimate Power Comes from Consent

Consent does not require every person to agree with every law. It means public authority originates with the people and is exercised through constitutional procedures such as elections, representation, lawmaking, and judicial review.

3. Power Must Be Limited and Lawful

Limited government answers how much power government may exercise. Rule of law answers how power must be exercised and who is accountable. The concepts overlap, but they are not identical.

Primary Source: Declaration of Independence

“deriving their just powers from the consent of the governed”

This short excerpt directly supports the idea that legitimate government authority comes from the people.

Source: U.S. National Archives, Declaration of Independence: A Transcription.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “Consent means every citizen personally agrees with every government decision.”

Correct: Consent means public authority originates with the people and operates through lawful constitutional processes.
