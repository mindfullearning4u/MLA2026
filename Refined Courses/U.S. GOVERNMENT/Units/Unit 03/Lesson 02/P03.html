Unit 3 · Lesson 2: Due Process, Equal Protection, and Incorporation

Notebook Task, Part 2: Analyze a constitutional problem

Scenario: A public school suspends a student for ten days based on an anonymous accusation, gives no description of the evidence, and provides no opportunity for the student to respond.

Four-pass analysis

  1. Actors: Identify every branch, level of government, official, or party.
  2. Authority: Name the constitutional or statutory source each actor relies on.
  3. Procedure and limit: Identify the required step, check, jurisdictional rule, or prohibition.
  4. Conclusion: State what follows and qualify the answer if a missing fact matters.

Model analysis

Because a public school is a state actor and suspension affects recognized educational and reputational interests, due process requires an appropriately timed explanation and opportunity to respond. The Constitution does not demand a full criminal trial, but no meaningful notice or response procedure is constitutionally suspect.

Common misconception

Due process and equal protection are related but distinct. Due process asks about lawful deprivation and fairness; equal protection asks whether government has treated similarly situated people differently without adequate constitutional justification.

Your turn

Write a CER response: one constitutional claim, two pieces of relevant evidence, and reasoning that explains both the rule and its limit. Then identify the strongest counterclaim and answer it.
