Rights evidence map

Rights principleWhat the evidence establishes
Fourteenth Amendment citizenshipDefines national and state citizenship and rejects rules that deny citizenship to people born or naturalized in the United States, subject to its jurisdiction.
Due Process ClauseRestrains states from depriving any person of life, liberty, or property without due process of law.
Equal Protection ClauseRequires state laws and enforcement to provide equal protection; courts examine classifications under different levels of scrutiny.
Selective incorporationThe Supreme Court has applied most, but not every, Bill of Rights guarantee to states through Fourteenth Amendment due process.
Procedural analysisAsk whether a protected interest exists and what notice, hearing, neutrality, and timing the situation requires.
Equality analysisIdentify the classification, government objective, fit between means and objective, applicable judicial scrutiny, and evidence of discriminatory purpose or effect.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Everything needed for this task is provided on this page. Use the rights principle chart, scenario facts, definitions, and worked reasoning supplied in the lesson.

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Evidence-write

  1. Select two rows from the evidence map.
  2. For each, write a claim beginning “The constitutional rights system…”
  3. Cite the precise article, clause, institutional step, or case fact.
  4. Explain the link with “This matters because…”
  5. Write one limitation: what does the evidence not prove?
Accuracy checkpoint: Do not treat a branch’s general purpose as proof of a specific power. Constitutional reasoning requires the correct actor, source of authority, procedure, and limit.
