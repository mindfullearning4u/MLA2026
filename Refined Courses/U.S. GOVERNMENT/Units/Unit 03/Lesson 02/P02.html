Notebook Title

Due Process, Equal Protection, and Incorporation: Vocabulary and Evidence

Vocabulary

TermPrecise meaningUse it accurately
due processThe constitutional requirement that government use fair procedures and respect protected life, liberty, and property.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
procedural due processRequired notice, a meaningful opportunity to be heard, and a fair decision process before certain government deprivations.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
substantive due processDoctrine asking whether government may infringe certain fundamental liberties regardless of the procedure used.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
equal protectionThe Fourteenth Amendment command that states not deny any person equal protection of the laws.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
incorporationApplication of most Bill of Rights protections to state and local governments through the Fourteenth Amendment.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
state actionGovernment conduct, rather than purely private behavior, necessary for most constitutional-rights claims.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.

Detailed Teaching Sequence

  1. Name the actor. Write who is acting and at what level or branch. This prevents using a rule that belongs to someone else.
  2. Name the action. Describe exactly what the actor proposes, requires, prohibits, funds, or decides.
  3. Select the rule. Use the visual below to find the rule that directly addresses that action.
  4. Test the facts. Identify one fact supporting the action and one fact showing a possible limit.
  5. Write the reasoning link. Complete: “This evidence matters because the rule requires ___, and the facts show ___.”

Evidence Set: Rights pathway diagram

Scenario: A public school suspends a student for ten days based on an anonymous accusation, gives no description of the evidence, and provides no opportunity for the student to respond.

Rights principleWhat the evidence establishes
Fourteenth Amendment citizenshipDefines national and state citizenship and rejects rules that deny citizenship to people born or naturalized in the United States, subject to its jurisdiction.
Due Process ClauseRestrains states from depriving any person of life, liberty, or property without due process of law.
Equal Protection ClauseRequires state laws and enforcement to provide equal protection; courts examine classifications under different levels of scrutiny.
Selective incorporationThe Supreme Court has applied most, but not every, Bill of Rights guarantee to states through Fourteenth Amendment due process.
Procedural analysisAsk whether a protected interest exists and what notice, hearing, neutrality, and timing the situation requires.
Equality analysisIdentify the classification, government objective, fit between means and objective, applicable judicial scrutiny, and evidence of discriminatory purpose or effect.
