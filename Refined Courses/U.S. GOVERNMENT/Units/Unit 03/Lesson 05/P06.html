Evidence Set: Rights balancing matrix

Scenario: A city bans all demonstrations near government buildings for one year after several events caused traffic delays. The ban applies to every viewpoint, but it eliminates the principal public forum for petitioning officials and leaves only remote locations.

Rights principleWhat the evidence establishes
Identify the claimant and actorDetermine who asserts a right and whether the challenged conduct is attributable to federal, state, or local government.
Define the protected interestName the specific liberty, equality guarantee, procedure, or participation right rather than invoking freedom generally.
Characterize the burdenAsk whether government targets content or identity, imposes a neutral incidental burden, denies process, or applies a rule unequally.
Test justification and fitIdentify the government interest, the applicable constitutional standard, and whether the means are appropriately connected and no broader than permitted.
Select institutional responseCourts, legislatures, executives, agencies, states, communities, and citizens possess different lawful powers and remedies.
Evaluate implementationA formal ruling or statute matters only if institutions communicate, fund, administer, monitor, and enforce the protection.

Instructions

  1. Read the complete evidence set.
  2. Complete Parts A–C in order.
  3. Use only the printed vocabulary, scenario, and visual.
  4. Check each response against the completion criteria.

Part A — Precision

Choose four terms from this page’s visual or scenario. For each, write a definition, accurate example, tempting non-example, and the evidence that distinguishes them.

Part B — Evidence Analysis

Complete a rights-balancing matrix for the demonstration ban, the school suspension, the voting test, and the mayor’s refusal. For each, connect a Unit 3 standard to the right, government justification, decisive facts, institutional response, and civic responsibility.

Part C — Competing Claims

Claim A: the proposed government action is adequately supported. Claim B: the action requires revision or more evidence. Choose the stronger claim, cite two details printed on this page, explain each link, and identify one fact that could reverse your conclusion.
