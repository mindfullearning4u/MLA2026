Notebook Task — Part 2

Evidence Set: Rights balancing matrix

Scenario: A city bans all demonstrations near government buildings for one year after several events caused traffic delays. The ban applies to every viewpoint, but it eliminates the principal public forum for petitioning officials and leaves only remote locations.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

Viewpoint neutrality answers one concern but not the entire First Amendment inquiry. The city has legitimate safety and access interests, yet a yearlong blanket ban may burden substantially more expression than necessary and leave inadequate alternatives. Analysis must test scope, duration, forum, alternatives, evidence, and less restrictive options.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: Balancing is not choosing whichever outcome feels fair. Constitutional analysis identifies the right, actor, burden, justification, standard, evidence, institutional authority, and remedy before drawing a proportionate conclusion.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
