Evidence Set: Responsibility evidence chart

Scenario: A mayor orders city employees to ignore a court judgment, claiming that a recent election gives the administration authority to disregard any legal limit opposed by a majority.

Rights principleWhat the evidence establishes
Sources of citizenshipThe Constitution recognizes birth and naturalization as paths to citizenship; legal status is distinct from the quality or frequency of civic participation.
Rights and responsibilitiesConstitutional rights protect participation and dissent, while duties and voluntary responsibilities help institutions function and remain accountable.
Informed participationResponsible citizens compare evidence, distinguish fact from assertion, understand institutional authority, and revise beliefs when reliable evidence changes.
Voting and representationVoting is a protected political right and a mechanism of accountability, but other lawful forms of participation also shape agendas and oversight.
Jury service and legal complianceJuries place citizens inside the justice system; compliance with valid law supports order while lawful challenge and reform remain possible.
Rule-of-law safeguardsGenerality, publicity, stable procedures, independent adjudication, equal application, and lawful remedies reduce arbitrary power.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

Checkpoint Task

Evaluate the mayor’s claim. Distinguish electoral authority from unlimited power, identify two rule-of-law safeguards, and explain two lawful actions citizens or institutions could take in response.
