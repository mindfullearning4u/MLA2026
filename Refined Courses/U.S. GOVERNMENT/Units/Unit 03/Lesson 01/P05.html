Unit 3 · Lesson 1: Bill of Rights and Civil Liberties

Guided Practice Readiness

Standard: MLA.USG.RGT.1: Analyze the protections, applications, and limits of the Bill of Rights and civil liberties.

Evidence Set: Rights scenario table

Scenario: A city requires a permit for every public demonstration, gives officials no written standards, and denies a permit after criticizing the group’s message.

Rights principleWhat the evidence establishes
First AmendmentProtects religion, speech, press, peaceful assembly, and petition; protection is strong but does not make every act or expression immune from neutral law.
Second AmendmentProtects an individual right to keep and bear arms, while constitutional doctrine also permits some regulation consistent with the amendment.
Fourth AmendmentGuards against unreasonable searches and seizures and requires warrants to be supported by probable cause and particularity.
Fifth AmendmentProtects due process, bars compelled self-incrimination and double jeopardy, and governs federal takings of private property.
Sixth and Seventh AmendmentsProtect fair criminal procedures and preserve civil jury trial in covered federal cases.
Eighth through Tenth AmendmentsAddress bail, fines, punishment, unenumerated rights, and powers reserved to states or the people.
