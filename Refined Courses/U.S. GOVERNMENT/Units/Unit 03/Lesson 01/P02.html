Notebook Title

Bill of Rights and Civil Liberties: Vocabulary and Evidence

Vocabulary

TermPrecise meaningUse it accurately
civil libertyA constitutional protection against improper government interference with individual freedom.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
establishmentGovernment action that impermissibly sponsors or favors religion under the First Amendment.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
free exerciseThe First Amendment protection for religious belief and, subject to lawful limits, religious practice.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
prior restraintGovernment suppression of speech or publication before expression occurs; it faces an especially demanding constitutional test.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
probable causeReasonable grounds, based on facts, to believe that a crime occurred or evidence will be found in a particular place.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.
exclusionary ruleA judicial remedy that generally prevents evidence obtained through an unconstitutional search from being used in a criminal trial.Use this term only when the cited constitutional text, institutional action, or case fact supports it.

Detailed Teaching Sequence

  1. Name the actor. Write who is acting and at what level or branch. This prevents using a rule that belongs to someone else.
  2. Name the action. Describe exactly what the actor proposes, requires, prohibits, funds, or decides.
  3. Select the rule. Use the visual below to find the rule that directly addresses that action.
  4. Test the facts. Identify one fact supporting the action and one fact showing a possible limit.
  5. Write the reasoning link. Complete: “This evidence matters because the rule requires ___, and the facts show ___.”

Evidence Set: Rights scenario table

Scenario: A city requires a permit for every public demonstration, gives officials no written standards, and denies a permit after criticizing the group’s message.

Rights principleWhat the evidence establishes
First AmendmentProtects religion, speech, press, peaceful assembly, and petition; protection is strong but does not make every act or expression immune from neutral law.
Second AmendmentProtects an individual right to keep and bear arms, while constitutional doctrine also permits some regulation consistent with the amendment.
Fourth AmendmentGuards against unreasonable searches and seizures and requires warrants to be supported by probable cause and particularity.
Fifth AmendmentProtects due process, bars compelled self-incrimination and double jeopardy, and governs federal takings of private property.
Sixth and Seventh AmendmentsProtect fair criminal procedures and preserve civil jury trial in covered federal cases.
Eighth through Tenth AmendmentsAddress bail, fines, punishment, unenumerated rights, and powers reserved to states or the people.
