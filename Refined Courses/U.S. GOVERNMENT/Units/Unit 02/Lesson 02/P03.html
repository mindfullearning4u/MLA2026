Notebook Task, Part 2: Analyze a constitutional problem

Scenario: The House passes a transportation bill, but the Senate approves a version with different funding rules. The President supports the policy goal.

Four-pass analysis

  1. Actors: Identify every branch, level of government, official, or party.
  2. Authority: Name the constitutional or statutory source each actor relies on.
  3. Procedure and limit: Identify the required step, check, jurisdictional rule, or prohibition.
  4. Conclusion: State what follows and qualify the answer if a missing fact matters.

Model analysis

The bill is not ready for presidential action because the chambers have not passed identical text. Congress must reconcile the versions and each chamber must approve the same final language before presentment. Presidential support cannot substitute for bicameral passage.

Common misconception

A majority vote in one chamber does not create federal law. Ordinary legislation must survive bicameralism and presentment, subject to the Constitution’s veto and override rules.

Your turn

Write a CER response: one constitutional claim, two pieces of relevant evidence, and reasoning that explains both the rule and its limit. Then identify the strongest counterclaim and answer it.

Lesson Evidence Anchor
