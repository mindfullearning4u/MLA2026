Unit 2 · Lesson 2: Congress and the Lawmaking Process

Notebook Task — Part 2

Evidence Set: Bill-to-law flowchart

Scenario: The House passes a transportation bill, but the Senate approves a version with different funding rules. The President supports the policy goal.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
RepresentationHouse seats are apportioned by population and members serve two-year terms; each state has two senators serving staggered six-year terms.
Enumerated powersArticle I, Section 8 lists powers including taxation, borrowing, regulating interstate and foreign commerce, coining money, and raising and supporting armed forces.
Necessary and Proper ClauseCongress may make laws necessary and proper for carrying its enumerated powers into execution; it is not an unlimited independent grant.
Committee stageCommittees and subcommittees gather information, hold hearings, revise bills, and decide whether measures advance. Many bills stop here.
Bicameral passageThe House and Senate must approve identical text. Disagreement may be resolved by amendment exchanges or a conference process.
PresentmentThe President may sign or veto a bill. Congress can override a veto with two-thirds in each chamber; constitutional timing rules also govern unsigned bills.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The bill is not ready for presidential action because the chambers have not passed identical text. Congress must reconcile the versions and each chamber must approve the same final language before presentment. Presidential support cannot substitute for bicameral passage.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: A majority vote in one chamber does not create federal law. Ordinary legislation must survive bicameralism and presentment, subject to the Constitution’s veto and override rules.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
