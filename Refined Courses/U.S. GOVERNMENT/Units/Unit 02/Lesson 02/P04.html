Worked Example 1

Prompt: A bill moves from introduction to committee, floor votes, the other chamber, and the President.

Step 1: Start the bill. This step matters because it prevents guessing.

Step 2: Identify where a proposal enters Congress. This step ties the answer to evidence.

Step 3: A bill can begin in either chamber, except revenue bills begin in the House. This is the teachable conclusion for MLA.USG.CON.2.

Worked Example 2

Prompt: A bill moves from introduction to committee, floor votes, the other chamber, and the President.

Step 1: Track committee. This step matters because it prevents guessing.

Step 2: Notice if the bill is researched or revised. This step ties the answer to evidence.

Step 3: Committee work is part of lawmaking, not enforcement. This is the teachable conclusion for MLA.USG.CON.2.

Worked Example 3

Prompt: A bill moves from introduction to committee, floor votes, the other chamber, and the President.

Step 1: Finish lawmaking. This step matters because it prevents guessing.

Step 2: Check both chambers and the President. This step ties the answer to evidence.

Step 3: One vote alone is not usually enough. This is the teachable conclusion for MLA.USG.CON.2.
