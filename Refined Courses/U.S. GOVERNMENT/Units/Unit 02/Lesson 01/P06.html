Evidence Set: Constitution map

Scenario: A state law conflicts with a federal statute that Congress enacted within an enumerated power. A court must decide which rule governs.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
PreambleStates six purposes, including establishing justice, providing for common defense, and securing liberty; it announces goals but does not itself grant a specific branch a power.
Articles I–IIICreate three branches and assign different functions: legislating, executing, and judging. This allocation supports separation of powers.
Articles IV–VIIAddress interstate relations, amendment, federal supremacy, prior obligations, ratification, and related structural rules.
Article VRequires proposal by two-thirds of both houses of Congress or a convention called by two-thirds of state legislatures, followed by ratification by three-fourths of the states.
Article VIMakes the Constitution, federal laws made pursuant to it, and treaties the supreme law, while binding judges and prohibiting religious tests for federal office.
AmendmentsAlter or add to the constitutional system without replacing the entire document; the Bill of Rights consists of the first ten amendments.

Instructions

  1. Read the complete evidence set.
  2. Complete Parts A–C in order.
  3. Use only the printed vocabulary, scenario, and visual.
  4. Check each response against the completion criteria.

Part A — Precision

Choose four terms from this page’s visual or scenario. For each, write a definition, accurate example, tempting non-example, and the evidence that distinguishes them.

Part B — Evidence Analysis

Use the Constitution map to trace one problem—such as a proposed national tax, a presidential enforcement action, or a dispute between states—to the relevant article. Then explain one structural limit and one method of constitutional change.

Part C — Competing Claims

Claim A: the proposed government action is adequately supported. Claim B: the action requires revision or more evidence. Choose the stronger claim, cite two details printed on this page, explain each link, and identify one fact that could reverse your conclusion.
