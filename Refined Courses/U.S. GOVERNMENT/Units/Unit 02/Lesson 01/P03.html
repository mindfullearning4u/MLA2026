Notebook Task, Part 2: Analyze a constitutional problem

Scenario: A state law conflicts with a federal statute that Congress enacted within an enumerated power. A court must decide which rule governs.

Four-pass analysis

  1. Actors: Identify every branch, level of government, official, or party.
  2. Authority: Name the constitutional or statutory source each actor relies on.
  3. Procedure and limit: Identify the required step, check, jurisdictional rule, or prohibition.
  4. Conclusion: State what follows and qualify the answer if a missing fact matters.

Model analysis

First ask whether the federal statute is constitutionally authorized. If it is, Article VI gives that valid federal law priority over the conflicting state rule. “Federal always wins” is overbroad because an act outside federal constitutional authority is not made in pursuance of the Constitution.

Common misconception

The Preamble lists important purposes, but the operative grants and limits of power appear in the articles and amendments. Do not cite a Preamble goal as if it were an enumerated power.

Your turn

Write a CER response: one constitutional claim, two pieces of relevant evidence, and reasoning that explains both the rule and its limit. Then identify the strongest counterclaim and answer it.

Primary-source anchor

Authoritative source: National Archives transcript of the Constitution and Congress.gov Constitution Annotated. https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the operative constitutional words, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the text proves. A citation without reasoning is not analysis.
