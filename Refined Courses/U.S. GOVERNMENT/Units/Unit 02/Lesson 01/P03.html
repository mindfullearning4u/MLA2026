Notebook Task — Part 2

Evidence Set: Constitution map

Scenario: A state law conflicts with a federal statute that Congress enacted within an enumerated power. A court must decide which rule governs.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
PreambleStates six purposes, including establishing justice, providing for common defense, and securing liberty; it announces goals but does not itself grant a specific branch a power.
Articles I–IIICreate three branches and assign different functions: legislating, executing, and judging. This allocation supports separation of powers.
Articles IV–VIIAddress interstate relations, amendment, federal supremacy, prior obligations, ratification, and related structural rules.
Article VRequires proposal by two-thirds of both houses of Congress or a convention called by two-thirds of state legislatures, followed by ratification by three-fourths of the states.
Article VIMakes the Constitution, federal laws made pursuant to it, and treaties the supreme law, while binding judges and prohibiting religious tests for federal office.
AmendmentsAlter or add to the constitutional system without replacing the entire document; the Bill of Rights consists of the first ten amendments.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

First ask whether the federal statute is constitutionally authorized. If it is, Article VI gives that valid federal law priority over the conflicting state rule. “Federal always wins” is overbroad because an act outside federal constitutional authority is not made in pursuance of the Constitution.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: The Preamble lists important purposes, but the operative grants and limits of power appear in the articles and amendments. Do not cite a Preamble goal as if it were an enumerated power.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
