Evidence Set: Federal court hierarchy diagram

Scenario: A federal district court finds a statute unconstitutional. The losing party argues that the trial court misunderstood Supreme Court precedent and appeals.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Article IIIVests federal judicial power in one Supreme Court and inferior courts Congress establishes, while defining categories of federal cases and controversies.
District courtsFederal trial courts receive evidence, determine facts through judges or juries, and apply law in the first instance.
Courts of appealsRegional appellate courts review district-court decisions for legal error; they generally do not hear new witnesses or retry facts.
Supreme CourtExercises limited original jurisdiction and mostly discretionary appellate review; denial of review is not a ruling endorsing the lower court’s reasoning.
Judicial reviewMarbury v. Madison articulated the judiciary’s duty to apply the Constitution when a statute conflicts with superior constitutional law.
LimitsCourts need jurisdiction and an actual justiciable dispute; judges do not initiate legislation or issue general advisory policy opinions.

Part A — Precision

Part B — Evidence Analysis

Map a federal constitutional claim from district court through a court of appeals to possible Supreme Court review. For each level, state its main function, the record it uses, and the kind of question it decides.

Part C — Competing Claims

Claim A: the proposed government action is adequately supported. Claim B: the action requires revision or more evidence. Choose the stronger claim, cite two details printed on this page, explain each link, and identify one fact that could reverse your conclusion.
