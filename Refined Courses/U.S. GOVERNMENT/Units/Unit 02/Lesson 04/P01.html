Lesson Overview

Unit 2 · Lesson 4: Federal Courts and Judicial Review

Standard: MLA.USG.CON.4: Explain the federal court system, judicial review, precedent, constitutional interpretation, and the role of the Supreme Court.

Essential question: How do federal courts resolve concrete disputes while interpreting and limiting government power?

Learning target: I can make a defensible constitutional claim, cite exact institutional or textual evidence, and explain why the evidence supports the claim.

Mastery: At least 80%, including accurate vocabulary, relevant evidence, and reasoning that addresses the constitutional rule rather than merely naming a branch.

Lesson roadmap

  1. Read the constitutional framework.
  2. Build precise vocabulary in your notebook.
  3. Analyze a realistic institutional scenario.
  4. Study worked examples and correct misconceptions.
  5. Practice with feedback, then complete independent evidence work.
  6. Submit the mastery checkpoint.

Federal court hierarchy diagram

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Article IIIVests federal judicial power in one Supreme Court and inferior courts Congress establishes, while defining categories of federal cases and controversies.
District courtsFederal trial courts receive evidence, determine facts through judges or juries, and apply law in the first instance.
Courts of appealsRegional appellate courts review district-court decisions for legal error; they generally do not hear new witnesses or retry facts.
Supreme CourtExercises limited original jurisdiction and mostly discretionary appellate review; denial of review is not a ruling endorsing the lower court’s reasoning.
Judicial reviewMarbury v. Madison articulated the judiciary’s duty to apply the Constitution when a statute conflicts with superior constitutional law.
LimitsCourts need jurisdiction and an actual justiciable dispute; judges do not initiate legislation or issue general advisory policy opinions.
