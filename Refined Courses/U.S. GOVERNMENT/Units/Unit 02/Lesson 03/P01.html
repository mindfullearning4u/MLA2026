Lesson Overview

Unit 2 · Lesson 3: President and Executive Branch

Standard: MLA.USG.CON.3: Explain the constitutional powers, roles, limits, and responsibilities of the President and executive branch.

Essential question: How can the President act decisively while remaining constrained by law and the other branches?

Learning target: I can make a defensible constitutional claim, cite exact institutional or textual evidence, and explain why the evidence supports the claim.

Mastery: At least 80%, including accurate vocabulary, relevant evidence, and reasoning that addresses the constitutional rule rather than merely naming a branch.

Lesson roadmap

  1. Read the constitutional framework.
  2. Build precise vocabulary in your notebook.
  3. Analyze a realistic institutional scenario.
  4. Study worked examples and correct misconceptions.
  5. Practice with feedback, then complete independent evidence work.
  6. Submit the mastery checkpoint.

Executive power organizer

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Faithful executionArticle II requires the President to take care that the laws be faithfully executed; enforcement discretion does not include rewriting statutes at will.
AppointmentsThe President nominates ambassadors, Supreme Court justices, and other officers, while Senate confirmation supplies an interbranch check for many positions.
Foreign affairsThe President receives ambassadors and negotiates treaties, but treaties require a two-thirds Senate vote and funding often depends on Congress.
Military roleThe President commands the armed forces; Congress declares war, regulates the military, raises forces, and controls appropriations.
Legislative interactionThe President recommends measures, signs or vetoes bills, and reports on the state of the union but does not vote in Congress.
AccountabilityCourts can review executive legality, Congress conducts oversight and controls funding, and impeachment provides a constitutional removal mechanism.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Everything needed for this task is provided on this page. Use the constitutional feature chart, scenario facts, definitions, and worked reasoning supplied in the lesson.

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Opening analysis: Before continuing, write a two-sentence hypothesis answering the essential question. Revisit it on Page 6 and revise it using constitutional evidence.
Teacher of Record support: If the constitutional text, vocabulary, case facts, or feedback remain unclear, contact your Teacher of Record. Bring the exact sentence or step causing difficulty so support can target the misconception before a new attempt.
