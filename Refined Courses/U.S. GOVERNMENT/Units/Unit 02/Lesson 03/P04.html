Worked Examples

Unit 2 · Lesson 3: President and Executive Branch

Worked Example 1: Classify the power

Prompt: Congress enacts a statute directing an agency to protect a defined class of records. A President orders the agency to ignore that category because compliance is inconvenient.

  1. Underline the government action—not merely the topic.
  2. Identify the actor and its claimed authority.
  3. Locate the rule in the executive power organizer.
  4. Test the action against every required procedure or limit.
  5. Write a conclusion narrow enough to follow from the evidence.
Reasoned result: The Take Care Clause points toward faithful implementation of the statute. An executive directive cannot lawfully cancel a valid act of Congress merely because the President disagrees with it. The correct analysis identifies the source of claimed authority and the statutory limit.

Worked Example 2: Diagnose weak reasoning

“This action is constitutional because the government has power.”

This response is inadequate. It does not identify which government actor, the source and scope of power, the required procedure, or a competing limit. Repair it by citing the relevant constitutional feature and explaining how the facts satisfy—or fail to satisfy—its requirements.

Worked Example 3: Handle an ambiguous fact

If a scenario omits a decisive fact, state a conditional conclusion: “If ___, then ___ because ___; however, if ___, then ___.” Constitutional analysis is stronger when it acknowledges what the record cannot establish.

Notebook repair: Rewrite the weak response as a four-sentence CER analysis. Sentence 4 must address a limit, counterexample, or missing fact.
Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Faithful executionArticle II requires the President to take care that the laws be faithfully executed; enforcement discretion does not include rewriting statutes at will.
AppointmentsThe President nominates ambassadors, Supreme Court justices, and other officers, while Senate confirmation supplies an interbranch check for many positions.
Foreign affairsThe President receives ambassadors and negotiates treaties, but treaties require a two-thirds Senate vote and funding often depends on Congress.
Military roleThe President commands the armed forces; Congress declares war, regulates the military, raises forces, and controls appropriations.
Legislative interactionThe President recommends measures, signs or vetoes bills, and reports on the state of the union but does not vote in Congress.
AccountabilityCourts can review executive legality, Congress conducts oversight and controls funding, and impeachment provides a constitutional removal mechanism.
