Unit 2 · Lesson 3: President and Executive Branch

Worked Example 1: Apply the Rule

Evidence Set: Executive power organizer

Scenario: Congress enacts a statute directing an agency to protect a defined class of records. A President orders the agency to ignore that category because compliance is inconvenient.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Faithful executionArticle II requires the President to take care that the laws be faithfully executed; enforcement discretion does not include rewriting statutes at will.
AppointmentsThe President nominates ambassadors, Supreme Court justices, and other officers, while Senate confirmation supplies an interbranch check for many positions.
Foreign affairsThe President receives ambassadors and negotiates treaties, but treaties require a two-thirds Senate vote and funding often depends on Congress.
Military roleThe President commands the armed forces; Congress declares war, regulates the military, raises forces, and controls appropriations.
Legislative interactionThe President recommends measures, signs or vetoes bills, and reports on the state of the union but does not vote in Congress.
AccountabilityCourts can review executive legality, Congress conducts oversight and controls funding, and impeachment provides a constitutional removal mechanism.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

  1. Step 1 — Identify the action: Underline what the government actor did. Why: rights and powers attach to actions, not broad topics.
  2. Step 2 — Select evidence: Match the action to the most specific row in the visual. Why: a nearby concept is not automatically controlling.
  3. Step 3 — Test the facts: Find one supporting fact and one limiting fact. Why: civic conclusions require both authority and constraint.
  4. Step 4 — Conclude narrowly: State what follows and identify any missing decisive fact. Why: evidence cannot support an absolute claim unless the rule is absolute.
Interpreted answer: The Take Care Clause points toward faithful implementation of the statute. An executive directive cannot lawfully cancel a valid act of Congress merely because the President disagrees with it. The correct analysis identifies the source of claimed authority and the statutory limit.

Worked Example 2: Repair Weak Reasoning

Incorrect: “This policy is valid because government must solve problems.”
  1. Replace “government” with the named actor.
  2. Replace “solve problems” with the exact authority or protection.
  3. Add the decisive printed fact.
  4. Add the procedure, limit, or counterclaim.

Worked Example 3: Handle Uncertainty

Problem: Suppose the scenario does not tell whether a required procedure occurred.

  1. Name the missing fact.
  2. Write: “If the procedure occurred, then ___ because ___.”
  3. Write: “If it did not occur, then ___ because ___.”
  4. Identify the record, document, or testimony that would resolve the uncertainty.

Interpretation: Conditional reasoning is more accurate than guessing. It shows exactly how new evidence would change the conclusion.
