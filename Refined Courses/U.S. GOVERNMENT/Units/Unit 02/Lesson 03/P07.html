Evidence Set: Executive power organizer

Scenario: Congress enacts a statute directing an agency to protect a defined class of records. A President orders the agency to ignore that category because compliance is inconvenient.

Constitutional featureWhat the evidence establishes
Faithful executionArticle II requires the President to take care that the laws be faithfully executed; enforcement discretion does not include rewriting statutes at will.
AppointmentsThe President nominates ambassadors, Supreme Court justices, and other officers, while Senate confirmation supplies an interbranch check for many positions.
Foreign affairsThe President receives ambassadors and negotiates treaties, but treaties require a two-thirds Senate vote and funding often depends on Congress.
Military roleThe President commands the armed forces; Congress declares war, regulates the military, raises forces, and controls appropriations.
Legislative interactionThe President recommends measures, signs or vetoes bills, and reports on the state of the union but does not vote in Congress.
AccountabilityCourts can review executive legality, Congress conducts oversight and controls funding, and impeachment provides a constitutional removal mechanism.
