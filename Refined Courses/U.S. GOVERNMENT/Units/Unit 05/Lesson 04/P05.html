Budget tradeoff table

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Revenue choicesIncome, payroll, sales, property, excise, user-fee, and other systems differ in base, rate, administration, stability, incidence, and incentives.
Budget authorityLegislatures authorize taxes and appropriations; executives propose and administer budgets within law; agencies obligate and spend funds.
Baseline and time horizonA budget comparison depends on current-law assumptions, inflation, population, economic conditions, and the years included.
Distribution and incidenceLegal liability may differ from who ultimately bears a tax or receives a benefit; effects vary across income, place, age, and industry.
TradeoffsEvery allocation has opportunity costs involving services, taxes, borrowing, reserves, timing, quality, equity, and risk.
AccountabilityPublic budgets require transparent categories, legal authorization, controls, audits, performance measures, and comparison of planned and actual results.

For every item

  1. Restate the question as a policy claim you must test.
  2. Eliminate choices that use the wrong actor or branch.
  3. Eliminate choices that skip a required procedure or invent an unlimited power.
  4. Select the answer best supported by the provided evidence.
  5. Explain why the correct choice is stronger than the most plausible distractor.
