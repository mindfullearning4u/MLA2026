Lesson Overview

Unit 5 · Lesson 5: Synthesis: Policy, Courts, and Federalism

Standard: MLA.USG.PCY.1: Explain how public policy problems are identified, debated, written into law, implemented, funded, and evaluated. MLA.USG.PCY.2: Compare federal, state, local, and tribal government roles and explain how federalism works in practical policy decisions. MLA.USG.PCY.3: Analyze landmark Supreme Court cases and explain how constitutional interpretation affects rights, powers, and public policy. MLA.USG.PCY.4: Explain taxation, budgeting, spending, opportunity cost, and public-policy tradeoffs in representative government.

Essential question: How can governments make, fund, administer, and review policy across institutions and levels of government?

Learning target: I can make a defensible policy claim, cite exact institutional or textual evidence, and explain why the evidence supports the claim.

Mastery: At least 80%, including accurate vocabulary, relevant evidence, and reasoning that addresses the constitutional rule rather than merely naming a branch.

Lesson roadmap

  1. Read the constitutional framework.
  2. Build precise vocabulary in your notebook.
  3. Analyze a realistic institutional scenario.
  4. Study worked examples and correct misconceptions.
  5. Practice with feedback, then complete independent evidence work.
  6. Submit the mastery checkpoint.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Everything needed for this task is provided on this page. Use the policy principle chart, scenario facts, definitions, and worked reasoning supplied in the lesson.

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Opening analysis: Before continuing, write a two-sentence hypothesis answering the essential question. Revisit it on Page 6 and revise it using policy evidence.
Teacher of Record support: If the constitutional text, vocabulary, case facts, or feedback remain unclear, contact your Teacher of Record. Bring the exact sentence or step causing difficulty so support can target the misconception before a new attempt.
