Unit 5 · Lesson 5: Synthesis: Policy, Courts, and Federalism

Notebook Task — Part 2

Evidence Set: Policy decision record

Scenario: Congress offers matching grants for states to reduce wildfire risk. A state accepts funds, delegates projects to counties, coordinates with a Tribal Nation, and faces a lawsuit alleging that one land-clearing rule exceeds statutory authority and burdens protected rights.

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Authority recordIdentify each actor, source of power, jurisdiction, procedural duty, legal limit, and review mechanism.
Policy recordDefine the problem, baseline, alternatives, causal theory, stakeholders, distribution, and adoption decision.
Fiscal recordState revenue, appropriation, grant conditions, cost horizon, opportunity cost, reserves, and audit requirements.
Implementation recordAssign administrators, milestones, rules, procurement, data, coordination, communication, enforcement, and contingency plans.
Court recordAnticipate standing, jurisdiction, legal question, precedent, standard, facts, holding possibilities, and remedy.
Evaluation recordPredefine outputs, outcomes, comparison method, equity measures, unintended effects, reporting dates, and revision triggers.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The policy record must trace federal spending authority and grant terms, state acceptance and administration, county delegation, Tribal jurisdiction and consultation, budget choices, implementation evidence, the plaintiff’s legal claim, precedent, and remedy. Effectiveness evidence cannot cure unlawful authority, and a legal victory alone does not prove policy success.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: Policy analysis, federalism, budgeting, and constitutional review are not separate boxes. One decision can be effective but unlawful, lawful but underfunded, well funded but poorly implemented, or properly administered yet inequitable.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
