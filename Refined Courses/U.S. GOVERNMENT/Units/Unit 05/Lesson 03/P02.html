Policy evidence map

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Marbury v. MadisonEstablished judicial review in a concrete dispute by treating the Constitution as superior law when a statute conflicts with it.
McCulloch v. MarylandRead the Necessary and Proper Clause to permit appropriate means for enumerated powers and rejected a state tax interfering with a federal instrument.
Brown v. Board of EducationHeld state-mandated racial segregation in public schools inconsistent with equal protection, rejecting separate educational facilities as inherently unequal.
Gideon v. WainwrightApplied the Sixth Amendment right to appointed counsel in felony cases to states through the Fourteenth Amendment.
Tinker v. Des MoinesProtected student expression that did not materially and substantially disrupt school operations while recognizing the school context.
Case-reading methodSeparate facts, procedural posture, question, constitutional text, rule, reasoning, holding, remedy, concurrence or dissent, and later application.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Everything needed for this task is provided on this page. Use the policy principle chart, scenario facts, definitions, and worked reasoning supplied in the lesson.

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Evidence-write

  1. Select two rows from the evidence map.
  2. For each, write a claim beginning “The public-policy system…”
  3. Cite the precise article, clause, institutional step, or case fact.
  4. Explain the link with “This matters because…”
  5. Write one limitation: what does the evidence not prove?
Accuracy checkpoint: Do not treat a branch’s general purpose as proof of a specific power. Constitutional reasoning requires the correct actor, source of authority, procedure, and limit.
