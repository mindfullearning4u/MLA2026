Unit 5 · Lesson 1: How Public Policy Is Made

Worked Example 1: Apply the Rule

Evidence Set: Policy cycle

Scenario: A city reports rising pedestrian injuries and proposes lower speed limits, redesigned intersections, automated enforcement, and a public-education campaign.

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Problem definitionSpecify the condition, affected population, scale, causes, evidence, jurisdiction, and difference between a symptom and underlying problem.
Agenda settingEvents, indicators, organized groups, media, elections, institutions, and available solutions influence which problems receive action.
Formulation and adoptionOfficials compare authority, effectiveness, cost, equity, feasibility, stakeholder impact, and political support before choosing a lawful instrument.
Funding and administrationBudgets, staffing, procurement, guidance, timelines, coordination, enforcement, and discretion determine what an adopted policy actually does.
Outputs and outcomesOutputs are immediate government activities; outcomes are changes in conditions. Neither alone proves the policy caused the change.
Revision or terminationEvidence, court rulings, elections, oversight, implementation problems, and changing conditions can lead to continuation, redesign, expansion, or repeal.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

  1. Step 1 — Identify the action: Underline what the government actor did. Why: rights and powers attach to actions, not broad topics.
  2. Step 2 — Select evidence: Match the action to the most specific row in the visual. Why: a nearby concept is not automatically controlling.
  3. Step 3 — Test the facts: Find one supporting fact and one limiting fact. Why: civic conclusions require both authority and constraint.
  4. Step 4 — Conclude narrowly: State what follows and identify any missing decisive fact. Why: evidence cannot support an absolute claim unless the rule is absolute.
Interpreted answer: A sound process begins with reliable crash, location, exposure, and enforcement data; identifies city and state authority; compares costs and distributional effects; funds administration; defines outputs and injury outcomes; and schedules evaluation. Choosing a popular intervention before diagnosing the pattern risks treating the wrong cause.

Worked Example 2: Repair Weak Reasoning

Incorrect: “This policy is valid because government must solve problems.”
  1. Replace “government” with the named actor.
  2. Replace “solve problems” with the exact authority or protection.
  3. Add the decisive printed fact.
  4. Add the procedure, limit, or counterclaim.

Worked Example 3: Handle Uncertainty

Problem: Suppose the scenario does not tell whether a required procedure occurred.

  1. Name the missing fact.
  2. Write: “If the procedure occurred, then ___ because ___.”
  3. Write: “If it did not occur, then ___ because ___.”
  4. Identify the record, document, or testimony that would resolve the uncertainty.

Interpretation: Conditional reasoning is more accurate than guessing. It shows exactly how new evidence would change the conclusion.
