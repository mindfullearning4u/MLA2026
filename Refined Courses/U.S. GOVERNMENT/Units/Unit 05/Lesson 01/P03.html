Unit 5 · Lesson 1: How Public Policy Is Made

Notebook Task — Part 2

Evidence Set: Policy cycle

Scenario: A city reports rising pedestrian injuries and proposes lower speed limits, redesigned intersections, automated enforcement, and a public-education campaign.

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Problem definitionSpecify the condition, affected population, scale, causes, evidence, jurisdiction, and difference between a symptom and underlying problem.
Agenda settingEvents, indicators, organized groups, media, elections, institutions, and available solutions influence which problems receive action.
Formulation and adoptionOfficials compare authority, effectiveness, cost, equity, feasibility, stakeholder impact, and political support before choosing a lawful instrument.
Funding and administrationBudgets, staffing, procurement, guidance, timelines, coordination, enforcement, and discretion determine what an adopted policy actually does.
Outputs and outcomesOutputs are immediate government activities; outcomes are changes in conditions. Neither alone proves the policy caused the change.
Revision or terminationEvidence, court rulings, elections, oversight, implementation problems, and changing conditions can lead to continuation, redesign, expansion, or repeal.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

A sound process begins with reliable crash, location, exposure, and enforcement data; identifies city and state authority; compares costs and distributional effects; funds administration; defines outputs and injury outcomes; and schedules evaluation. Choosing a popular intervention before diagnosing the pattern risks treating the wrong cause.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: The policy cycle is an analytical model, not a fixed one-way sequence. Courts, budgets, implementation feedback, elections, and new evidence often send decisions back to earlier stages.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
