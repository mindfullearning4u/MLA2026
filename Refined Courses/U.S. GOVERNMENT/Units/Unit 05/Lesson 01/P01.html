P01 Lesson Overview

Unit 5 · Lesson 1: How Public Policy Is Made

Lesson Title

How Public Policy Is Made

Standards Covered in This Lesson

MLA.USG.PCY.1: Explain how public policy problems are identified, debated, written into law, implemented, funded, and evaluated.

What You Will Learn

You will learn to identify the correct government actor, apply the mapped rule, use relevant evidence, and explain a limit or competing consideration.

What You Will Do

  1. Learn six precise terms.
  2. Study the policy cycle.
  3. Watch three complete reasoning models.
  4. Practice with feedback.
  5. Complete self-contained independent work.
  6. Submit a self-contained mastery checkpoint.

How You Will Show Mastery

You will earn at least 80% by making an accurate claim, citing two relevant details supplied with the task, explaining why each detail matters, and addressing a limit or counterclaim.

Student-Friendly Standard Connection

I can answer “How does a public concern become an implemented and evaluated government policy?” with accurate civic vocabulary, evidence, and reasoning.

Evidence Set: Policy cycle

Scenario: A city reports rising pedestrian injuries and proposes lower speed limits, redesigned intersections, automated enforcement, and a public-education campaign.

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Problem definitionSpecify the condition, affected population, scale, causes, evidence, jurisdiction, and difference between a symptom and underlying problem.
Agenda settingEvents, indicators, organized groups, media, elections, institutions, and available solutions influence which problems receive action.
Formulation and adoptionOfficials compare authority, effectiveness, cost, equity, feasibility, stakeholder impact, and political support before choosing a lawful instrument.
Funding and administrationBudgets, staffing, procurement, guidance, timelines, coordination, enforcement, and discretion determine what an adopted policy actually does.
Outputs and outcomesOutputs are immediate government activities; outcomes are changes in conditions. Neither alone proves the policy caused the change.
Revision or terminationEvidence, court rulings, elections, oversight, implementation problems, and changing conditions can lead to continuation, redesign, expansion, or repeal.
