Unit 5 · Lesson 2: Federal, State, Local, and Tribal Government Roles

Worked Example 1: Apply the Rule

Evidence Set: Authority Venn and comparison table

Scenario: A river crosses Tribal land, two states, and several cities. A chemical spill threatens drinking water, fisheries, and transportation, and officials disagree about who may issue orders and pay for cleanup.

Policy principleWhat the evidence establishes
Federal roleNational authority includes enumerated powers, spending conditions, regulation within constitutional bounds, and supremacy of valid federal law.
State roleStates exercise broad police powers over health, safety, welfare, elections, education, licensing, and local-government structure subject to federal limits.
Local roleCounties, municipalities, school districts, and special districts exercise authority delegated by state constitutions and statutes.
Tribal roleTribal Nations are distinct political communities with inherent self-government; jurisdiction may depend on land, membership, subject matter, treaties, and federal statutes.
Intergovernmental toolsGrants, waivers, compacts, mandates, cooperative administration, data sharing, mutual aid, and litigation coordinate or contest policy.
Conflict analysisIdentify the actor, source of authority, geographic and personal jurisdiction, federal floor or ceiling, funding condition, and available remedy.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

  1. Step 1 — Identify the action: Underline what the government actor did. Why: rights and powers attach to actions, not broad topics.
  2. Step 2 — Select evidence: Match the action to the most specific row in the visual. Why: a nearby concept is not automatically controlling.
  3. Step 3 — Test the facts: Find one supporting fact and one limiting fact. Why: civic conclusions require both authority and constraint.
  4. Step 4 — Conclude narrowly: State what follows and identify any missing decisive fact. Why: evidence cannot support an absolute claim unless the rule is absolute.
Interpreted answer: No single label resolves the dispute. Federal environmental and commerce authority, state health and emergency powers, delegated local responsibilities, and Tribal jurisdiction may all apply. Analysts must identify statutory authority, land and water jurisdiction, valid preemption, funding, coordination, and government-to-government consultation.

Worked Example 2: Repair Weak Reasoning

Incorrect: “This policy is valid because government must solve problems.”
  1. Replace “government” with the named actor.
  2. Replace “solve problems” with the exact authority or protection.
  3. Add the decisive printed fact.
  4. Add the procedure, limit, or counterclaim.

Worked Example 3: Handle Uncertainty

Problem: Suppose the scenario does not tell whether a required procedure occurred.

  1. Name the missing fact.
  2. Write: “If the procedure occurred, then ___ because ___.”
  3. Write: “If it did not occur, then ___ because ___.”
  4. Identify the record, document, or testimony that would resolve the uncertainty.

Interpretation: Conditional reasoning is more accurate than guessing. It shows exactly how new evidence would change the conclusion.
