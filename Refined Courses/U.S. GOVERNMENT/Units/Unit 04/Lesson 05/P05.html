Evidence Set: Participation impact chart

Scenario: A city considers weekend voting after data show lower turnout in neighborhoods with more hourly workers. A party mobilizes supporters, a business group opposes the cost, local media publish a poll, and officials predict the reform will improve representation.

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Access and turnoutEligibility, registration, administration, resources, efficacy, and mobilization affect whether preferences become ballots.
Intermediary organizationsParties, groups, campaigns, and media aggregate interests, recruit participants, frame choices, and channel demands.
Electoral rulesDistricts, ballot rules, plurality or majority systems, primaries, and the Electoral College shape strategy and vote-to-office translation.
Opinion evidenceReliable polling and careful interpretation help officials and citizens estimate preferences without confusing samples with populations.
Representation testCompare who participates, whose preferences are communicated, how officials respond, and whether policy or oversight changes.
Democratic safeguardsVoting protections, transparent administration, disclosure, independent journalism, lawful advocacy, fact checking, and civil discourse support accountability.
