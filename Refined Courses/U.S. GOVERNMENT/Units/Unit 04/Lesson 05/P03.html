Scenario: A city considers weekend voting after data show lower turnout in neighborhoods with more hourly workers. A party mobilizes supporters, a business group opposes the cost, local media publish a poll, and officials predict the reform will improve representation.

Guided Notes

  1. Claim: State a narrow answer that can be proven from the printed facts.
  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.
  3. Evidence 2: Select the exact rule from the visual.
  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

A defensible recommendation integrates legal authority, turnout denominators, barrier evidence, administrative cost, group incentives, poll quality, distributional effects, and an evaluation plan. Higher turnout may improve inclusion, but actual representational effects must be measured rather than assumed.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: Participation and representation are connected but not identical. More activity can broaden voice, yet unequal mobilization, flawed information, electoral rules, and unresponsive institutions may still shape whose preferences affect policy.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
