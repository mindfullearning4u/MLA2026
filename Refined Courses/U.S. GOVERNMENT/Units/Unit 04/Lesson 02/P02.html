Civic evidence map

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Party functionsParties recruit and nominate candidates, mobilize voters, simplify choices, coordinate governing coalitions, and create accountability labels.
Interest-group functionsGroups aggregate interests, supply expertise, lobby, litigate, mobilize members, and communicate policy preferences.
Media functionsJournalism can inform, investigate, verify, frame, and set agendas; platforms and algorithms also shape attention and exposure.
Public-opinion signalsElections, polls, meetings, communications, and organized advocacy provide imperfect signals about citizen preferences.
Representation risksUnequal resources, selective participation, misinformation, concentrated access, and weak transparency can distort perceived public demand.
Evidence tracingDistinguish source, message, audience, channel, incentive, action, and verified policy effect before claiming influence.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Evidence-write
