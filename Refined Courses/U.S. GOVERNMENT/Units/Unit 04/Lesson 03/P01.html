Lesson Overview

Unit 4 · Lesson 3: Campaigns, Elections, and the Electoral College

Standard: MLA.USG.POL.3: Analyze campaigns, elections, primaries, general elections, electoral systems, campaign finance, and the Electoral College.

Essential question: How do election rules translate votes, resources, and state results into public office?

Learning target: I can make a defensible civic claim, cite exact institutional or textual evidence, and explain why the evidence supports the claim.

Mastery: At least 80%, including accurate vocabulary, relevant evidence, and reasoning that addresses the constitutional rule rather than merely naming a branch.

Lesson roadmap

  1. Read the constitutional framework.
  2. Build precise vocabulary in your notebook.
  3. Analyze a realistic institutional scenario.
  4. Study worked examples and correct misconceptions.
  5. Practice with feedback, then complete independent evidence work.
  6. Submit the mastery checkpoint.

Election process map

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Nomination stageState laws and party rules structure primaries and caucuses, delegate selection, ballot access, and nomination timing.
Campaign strategyCandidates allocate time, money, staff, messages, data, and field operations according to electoral rules and competitive conditions.
Finance analysisContributions, independent expenditures, disclosure, public financing, and coordination rules affect participation and accountability differently.
Election administrationJurisdictions manage ballots, polling, early and absentee voting, counting, canvassing, certification, recounts, and lawful challenges.
Electoral votesMost states use a statewide winner-take-all rule; Maine and Nebraska use district-based allocations. A majority of appointed electoral votes is required.
Contingent electionIf no presidential candidate wins an electoral majority, the House chooses the President by state delegation; the Senate chooses the Vice President.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Everything needed for this task is provided on this page. Use the participation principle chart, scenario facts, definitions, and worked reasoning supplied in the lesson.

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Opening analysis: Before continuing, write a two-sentence hypothesis answering the essential question. Revisit it on Page 6 and revise it using civic evidence.
Teacher of Record support: If the constitutional text, vocabulary, case facts, or feedback remain unclear, contact your Teacher of Record. Bring the exact sentence or step causing difficulty so support can target the misconception before a new attempt.
