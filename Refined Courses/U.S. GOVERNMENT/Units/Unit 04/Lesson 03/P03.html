Scenario: Candidate A wins the national popular vote narrowly, Candidate B wins more electoral votes by carrying several closely divided states, and a commentator says the national popular vote legally decides the presidency.

  2. Evidence 1: Quote or paraphrase the decisive scenario fact.

  4. Reasoning: Explain how the fact satisfies, fails, or qualifies the rule.
  5. Limit: State what the evidence does not prove or what competing rule remains.

Model Analysis

The national popular vote is politically important but is not the direct constitutional tabulation that elects the President. State popular results determine appointed electors under state law; those electoral votes are counted through the constitutional process. Analysis should distinguish current law from proposals for reform.

Common Mistake

Incorrect: “The government may do this because it has authority.” This does not identify the source, scope, procedure, evidence, or limit.
Correct: Primary votes, general-election votes, and electoral votes occur at different stages and answer different institutional questions. Do not combine them into one total or treat every election as majority rule.

Teachable explanation: A strong answer is narrower than the topic. It matches one actor and action to one controlling rule, then explains how a printed fact changes the conclusion.
