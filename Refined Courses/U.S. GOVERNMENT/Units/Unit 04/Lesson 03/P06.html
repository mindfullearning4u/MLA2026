Evidence Set: Election process map

Scenario: Candidate A wins the national popular vote narrowly, Candidate B wins more electoral votes by carrying several closely divided states, and a commentator says the national popular vote legally decides the presidency.

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Nomination stageState laws and party rules structure primaries and caucuses, delegate selection, ballot access, and nomination timing.
Campaign strategyCandidates allocate time, money, staff, messages, data, and field operations according to electoral rules and competitive conditions.
Finance analysisContributions, independent expenditures, disclosure, public financing, and coordination rules affect participation and accountability differently.
Election administrationJurisdictions manage ballots, polling, early and absentee voting, counting, canvassing, certification, recounts, and lawful challenges.
Electoral votesMost states use a statewide winner-take-all rule; Maine and Nebraska use district-based allocations. A majority of appointed electoral votes is required.
Contingent electionIf no presidential candidate wins an electoral majority, the House chooses the President by state delegation; the Senate chooses the Vice President.

Instructions

  1. Read the complete evidence set.
  2. Complete Parts A–C in order.
  3. Use only the printed vocabulary, scenario, and visual.

Part A — Precision

Choose four terms from this page’s visual or scenario. For each, write a definition, accurate example, tempting non-example, and the evidence that distinguishes them.

Part B — Evidence Analysis

Map a hypothetical presidential campaign from nomination through certification and electoral-vote counting. Calculate pluralities, state electoral allocations, the national popular total, and the electoral majority; then explain a lawful challenge point.

Part C — Competing Claims

Claim A: the proposed government action is adequately supported. Claim B: the action requires revision or more evidence. Choose the stronger claim, cite two details printed on this page, explain each link, and identify one fact that could reverse your conclusion.
