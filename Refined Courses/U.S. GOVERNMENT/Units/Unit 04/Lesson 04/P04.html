Worked Example 1: Apply the Rule

Evidence Set: Poll interpretation table

Scenario: An online poll posted by a candidate asks followers whether they support the “job-destroying tax plan.” Eighty-eight percent select “no,” and the campaign calls the result representative of all voters.

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Sampling frameAsk who could be selected, who was excluded, how respondents were reached, and whether nonresponse may differ systematically.
Question designOrder, wording, definitions, response choices, and interviewer or mode effects can change measured opinion.
UncertaintyMargins of error describe sampling uncertainty, not every error source; small subgroup estimates usually have greater uncertainty.
Timing and contextEvents, information, campaign activity, and field dates affect what a poll measures and whether comparison across polls is valid.
Message verificationSeparate claim, evidence, source, context, emotional technique, omitted information, and independent corroboration.
Constructive discourseState claims precisely, cite evidence, represent opposing views fairly, correct errors, and distinguish factual disagreement from value conflict.

Use this evidence only: Every fact, term, and rule needed for the task is printed in this panel. No other page or lesson is required.

  1. Step 1 — Identify the action: Underline what the government actor did. Why: rights and powers attach to actions, not broad topics.
  2. Step 2 — Select evidence: Match the action to the most specific row in the visual. Why: a nearby concept is not automatically controlling.
  3. Step 3 — Test the facts: Find one supporting fact and one limiting fact. Why: civic conclusions require both authority and constraint.
  4. Step 4 — Conclude narrowly: State what follows and identify any missing decisive fact. Why: evidence cannot support an absolute claim unless the rule is absolute.
Interpreted answer: The result cannot support that population claim. The voluntary follower sample creates selection and coverage problems, and the wording is leading. Report only what the responding group selected; a representative estimate requires defensible sampling, neutral measurement, transparent methods, and uncertainty.

Worked Example 2: Repair Weak Reasoning

Incorrect: “This policy is valid because government must solve problems.”
  1. Replace “government” with the named actor.
  2. Replace “solve problems” with the exact authority or protection.
  3. Add the decisive printed fact.
  4. Add the procedure, limit, or counterclaim.

Worked Example 3: Handle Uncertainty

Problem: Suppose the scenario does not tell whether a required procedure occurred.

  1. Name the missing fact.
  2. Write: “If the procedure occurred, then ___ because ___.”
  3. Write: “If it did not occur, then ___ because ___.”
  4. Identify the record, document, or testimony that would resolve the uncertainty.

Interpretation: Conditional reasoning is more accurate than guessing. It shows exactly how new evidence would change the conclusion.
