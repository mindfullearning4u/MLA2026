Civic evidence map

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Sampling frameAsk who could be selected, who was excluded, how respondents were reached, and whether nonresponse may differ systematically.
Question designOrder, wording, definitions, response choices, and interviewer or mode effects can change measured opinion.
UncertaintyMargins of error describe sampling uncertainty, not every error source; small subgroup estimates usually have greater uncertainty.
Timing and contextEvents, information, campaign activity, and field dates affect what a poll measures and whether comparison across polls is valid.
Message verificationSeparate claim, evidence, source, context, emotional technique, omitted information, and independent corroboration.
Constructive discourseState claims precisely, cite evidence, represent opposing views fairly, correct errors, and distinguish factual disagreement from value conflict.

Lesson Evidence Anchor

Everything needed for this task is provided on this page. Use the participation principle chart, scenario facts, definitions, and worked reasoning supplied in the lesson.

Reading protocol: Identify the actor, locate the controlling words or institutional rule supplied in the lesson, separate a grant of power from a limit or procedure, and explain what the evidence proves. Naming a rule without reasoning is not analysis.

Evidence-write

  1. Select two rows from the evidence map.
  2. For each, write a claim beginning “The democratic participation system…”
  3. Cite the precise article, clause, institutional step, or case fact.
  4. Explain the link with “This matters because…”
  5. Write one limitation: what does the evidence not prove?
Accuracy checkpoint: Do not treat a branch’s general purpose as proof of a specific power. Constitutional reasoning requires the correct actor, source of authority, procedure, and limit.
