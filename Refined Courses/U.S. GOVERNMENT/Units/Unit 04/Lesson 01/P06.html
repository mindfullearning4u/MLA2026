Evidence Set: Participation barriers chart

Scenario: A state ends same-day registration and closes registration four weeks before an election. Officials cite administrative preparation; evidence shows the change disproportionately affects eligible citizens who move frequently.

Participation principleWhat the evidence establishes
Constitutional expansionThe Fifteenth, Nineteenth, Twenty-fourth, and Twenty-sixth Amendments prohibit specified voting restrictions and broaden constitutional protection.
Federal voting protectionsFederal statutes can prohibit discriminatory practices, protect ballot access, and authorize enforcement under constitutional power.
State administrationStates establish many registration and election procedures, but those rules remain subject to federal constitutional and statutory limits.
Turnout measurementVoting-eligible-population, voting-age-population, and registered-voter denominators answer different questions and produce different rates.
Participation barriersRegistration rules, identification, transportation, work schedules, language access, disability access, information, and trust can affect participation.
Responsible comparisonEvaluate both lawful administrative interests and burdens using reliable data, affected groups, implementation evidence, and available alternatives.

Instructions

  1. Read the complete evidence set.
  2. Complete Parts A–C in order.
  3. Use only the printed vocabulary, scenario, and visual.
  4. Check each response against the completion criteria.

Part A — Precision

Choose four terms from this page’s visual or scenario. For each, write a definition, accurate example, tempting non-example, and the evidence that distinguishes them.

Part B — Evidence Analysis

Analyze a supplied turnout table using two different denominators, identify one participation gap, propose two competing explanations, and state what evidence would distinguish them. Then evaluate one access reform and one election-integrity concern.

Part C — Competing Claims

Claim A: the proposed government action is adequately supported. Claim B: the action requires revision or more evidence. Choose the stronger claim, cite two details printed on this page, explain each link, and identify one fact that could reverse your conclusion.
